CWI eligibility runs on a sliding scale between education and welding work experience rather than one fixed requirement: a bachelor's degree in welding plus 1 year of experience qualifies, as does a high school diploma plus 5 years of experience, or less than an 8th-grade education plus 12 years of experience. Every candidate, regardless of path, also has to pass a vision test, a reminder that inspection work depends on being able to actually see weld quality clearly.

The certification itself is three separate exam parts, each requiring a 72% passing score on its own. Part A (Fundamentals) is a 150-question computer-based test covering destructive and nondestructive testing, fabrication math, safety, metallurgy, and welding symbols. Part B (Practical) is hands-on, applying welding procedure specifications and inspection methods, actually executing required welds, plus 46 related questions. Part C (Code Book) is open-book in a narrow sense, one specific, approved code reference allowed, covering roughly 50 to 65 questions on applying that code.

AWS's welding inspector credentials come in three levels: Associate CWI (CAWI), an entry-level credential; CWI itself, the primary certification; and Senior CWI (SCWI), requiring additional qualifications beyond CWI. It's worth not confusing this with the welding certification a structural welder earns to a specific code like AWS D1.1 (already covered elsewhere on this site under the Welder career); that verifies someone can perform welds to a code, while CWI verifies the separate skill of inspecting and judging other people's welds against that same code.

Cost runs meaningfully higher than most credentials on this site, roughly $1,220 to $1,520 for initial certification depending on AWS membership status, before factoring in any optional training seminar package. Once earned, it stays valid for 9 years, with renewal checkpoints due at years 3 and 6, each offering several valid paths to maintain it: confirming continued relevant work experience, retaking Part B, completing 80 Professional Development Hours, or earning a Certified Radiographic Interpreter credential.
