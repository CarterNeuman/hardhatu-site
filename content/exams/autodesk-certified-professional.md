Autodesk Certified Professional isn't a single exam, it's a family of tool- and discipline-specific credentials: AutoCAD for Design and Drafting, Civil 3D for Infrastructure Design, BIM Management for Building Design, and separate Revit exams for architectural, structural, mechanical, and electrical design. Each Professional-level exam runs 120 minutes with roughly 45 to 60 questions, mixing multiple-choice with drag-and-drop, hot-area, and graphic-interpretation formats, and is delivered through Pearson VUE, either in person or online with remote proctoring. Autodesk recommends real hands-on experience before attempting one, typically 400 to 1,200 hours depending on the specific track, and tests practical workflow competencies like modeling, documentation, collaboration and coordination, project standards, families, and information analysis rather than basic tool familiarity.

The credential itself is valid for 3 years before retesting is required to renew it, and a candidate who doesn't pass can retake after 7 days (up to three attempts per year, then a full year's wait), which puts some real weight behind preparing rather than treating it as low-stakes. Autodesk, like PMI, doesn't publish a fixed passing score across its exams, results come back as pass or fail only. Because the certifications are split by tool and discipline, the right one to pursue depends heavily on what someone actually does day to day, a CAD technician's AutoCAD-heavy workflow calls for a different exam than a BIM/VDC specialist coordinating Revit models across disciplines.

Unlike Procore Certification's free model, ACP is a real cost: a standard exam voucher runs around $200, though discounted pricing through education channels or promotions sometimes brings it closer to $150, and an unused voucher expires 12 months after purchase, separate from the certification's own 3-year validity once you've actually passed. ACP also sits above Autodesk's entry-level Autodesk Certified User (ACU) exams in the same family, ACU tests basic tool familiarity with no real-world-experience expectation, while ACP assumes the hundreds of hours of hands-on time discussed above and tests accordingly advanced workflows.

Currently, Professional-level exams are offered in English only, a real point of contrast with Procore's certification catalog, which spans French, Spanish, German, and Chinese in addition to English (Autodesk's entry-level ACU exams do have some localized versions, just not the Professional tier). Anyone planning to test in a non-English-speaking market should account for that gap rather than assume the same language flexibility Procore offers.
