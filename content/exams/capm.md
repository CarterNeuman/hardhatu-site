The CAPM exam is 150 questions over 180 minutes (15 of them unscored pretest questions PMI uses to field-test future material), built from PMI's current exam content outline across four domains: Project Management Fundamentals and Core Concepts (36%), Business Analysis Frameworks (27%), Agile Frameworks/Methodologies (20%), and Predictive, Plan-Based Methodologies (17%). Unlike the PMP, which was significantly rebalanced in July 2026, CAPM's outline reflects an earlier update that already broadened the exam beyond traditional waterfall planning: business analysis (gathering and documenting requirements, confirming scope before work starts) and agile/adaptive concepts both carry real weight, more combined weight than traditional predictive methodologies get on their own. That's worth knowing going in, since a construction PM's daily reality is mostly predictive-style planning, but the exam tests a broader view of project management as a profession.

What actually separates the CAPM from the PMP isn't difficulty of subject matter so much as the entry bar: CAPM requires only a secondary degree (a high school diploma, GED, or equivalent) and at least 23 hours of project management education, no professional experience required, versus the PMP's combination of a degree, years of documented experience, and 35 training hours. That makes CAPM a realistic near-term goal for someone like a Project Engineer building toward Assistant Project Manager or Project Manager, a way to show real project-management knowledge on a resume before the PMP's experience requirement is within reach. As with the PMP, PMI doesn't publish a fixed passing score, results come back against a criterion-referenced standard rather than a simple percentage.

That lighter entry bar shows up in the cost and logistics too. The exam itself runs $225 for PMI members and $300 for non-members, with a retake costing the same as the original attempt, and you can sit it either at a Pearson VUE test center or as an online proctored exam from home, whichever setup you trust more. Joining PMI first (roughly $129 a year plus a small application fee) closes that $75 member/non-member gap on the exam fee alone, the same logic that applies to the pricier PMP later if you stay in the PMI ecosystem.

CAPM also isn't a credential you earn once and forget about. It's valid for three years from the date PMI awards it, and staying certified means earning 15 Professional Development Units (PDUs) during that window, at least 9 from Education activities and up to 6 from Giving Back activities like volunteering or mentoring, plus a renewal fee ($60 for members, $150 for non-members) at the end of the cycle. That's a fraction of the PMP's 60-PDU renewal requirement, consistent with CAPM's role as the lighter-touch entry point into PMI's credential family rather than a capstone.

The practice questions below are original, written to test the same kind of applied understanding the real exam does across all four current domains, not a copy of any real PMI question.
