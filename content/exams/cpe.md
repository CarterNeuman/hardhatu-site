The CPE isn't a single multiple-choice exam the way most credentials on this site are, it's three separate components a candidate has to clear: a Technical Paper on a board-approved estimating topic (testing communication skills as much as technical knowledge), a 4-hour General Estimating Knowledge (GEK) exam covering quantities, contract terms and conditions, cost reporting, and ethics, and an 8-hour, two-part Discipline Specific Test (DST) in a chosen specialty such as general contracting, mechanical, electrical, excavation, or concrete estimating. Each exam component requires a minimum score of 70% to pass, and candidates have six months from approval to complete everything.

Getting to that point takes real experience first: ASPE requires five years of verified on-the-job estimating experience before a candidate can even apply, confirmed directly with their employer, which makes the CPE a credential built for someone already established in the field rather than an entry point into it. The GEK exam comes with an official reference (the Standard Estimating Practice, Volume I), but the Technical Paper and DST are deliberately measured against a candidate's actual accumulated knowledge, with no formal study guide standing in for real experience. Once earned, the credential has to be actively maintained through an annual renewal application, documented Professional Development Units, and a renewal fee.

Unlike most credentials on this site, the CPE doesn't charge separately for each component. It's one all-inclusive, non-refundable application fee, $655 for ASPE members or $790 for non-members, covering study materials, exam administration, and proctoring for the Technical Paper, GEK exam, and DST together, and ASPE membership isn't required to apply, it just closes that $135 gap. That single fee is also why the retake structure matters: a Technical Paper that doesn't pass gets one revise-and-resubmit chance within a tight two-week deadline, and a failed GEK exam gets one retake within a 10-to-20-day window, both timelines that still have to fit inside the overall six-month completion deadline rather than resetting it.

The DST itself flexes to match a candidate's actual specialty. ASPE maintains a list of discipline-specific exams (general contracting, mechanical, electrical, excavation, concrete, among others), and a candidate whose specialty doesn't currently have a matching DST can request an alternative "Questions and Problems" format instead of being stuck without a path to certify. Once earned, keeping the credential current is a real annual commitment, not a one-time achievement: renewal requires documented PDUs and a fee, and missing the initial deadline adds a $50-per-month late charge with March 31 as the hard final cutoff before the credential lapses.
