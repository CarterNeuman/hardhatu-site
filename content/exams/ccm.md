The CCM exam is 175 multiple-choice questions over 240 minutes (150 scored, 25 unscored pilot items), requiring a 72% overall score to pass, a fixed, published cut score, a real contrast with PMI's PMP/CAPM approach of never publishing one at all. The exam draws from 10 knowledge domains: Program & Project Management (20%, the largest single domain), Cost Management, Time Management, Contract Administration, Quality Management, Safety Management, Risk Management, and Professional Practice (10% each), and Sustainability and Technology (5% each), covering the full construction project lifecycle rather than general management theory.

What makes the CCM distinct from a credential like the PMP starts well before the exam itself: candidates have to submit a detailed application documenting "Responsible-in-Charge" (RIC) experience, real accountability for supervising construction management work, not just time spent on a job site, and get that experience verified by at least two references (clients, supervisors, or current CCMs) before being approved into a one-year candidacy window to test. Every eligibility path requires a minimum of 48 months of RIC experience, with the required years of general construction/design experience on top of that scaling down as formal education goes up (a construction-management-related bachelor's or master's degree needs the least additional experience; no degree at all requires the most). Once earned, the credential has to be actively maintained too: recertification every 3 years requires 25 professional-development points tied to construction management, with at least one of those points specifically from a safety- or ethics-related activity, and no carryover between cycles.

The two-gate structure shows up in the cost too. A non-refundable application fee (less for CMAA members than non-members) pays for the experience review itself, separate from the exam fee you pay only once that application is approved, so a rejected or withdrawn application doesn't get refunded. Once you're cleared to test, the exam is delivered as a computer-based test, either at an in-person testing center or through live remote proctoring from home, the same flexibility PMI offers on the PMP and CAPM. The credential also carries a real, independently verified credibility marker beyond CMAA's own claims: it's accredited under ANSI's National Accreditation Board (ANAB) to the ISO/IEC 17024 standard, the international standard specifically for how personnel certification programs should be developed and governed, meaning a third party has audited CMAA's process, not just the exam content.

That recertification requirement isn't optional busywork, either: a CCM who lets a three-year cycle pass without the 25 required points can instead retake and pass the current exam, but doing so locks them into exam-based renewal going forward instead of the points-based path, a real trade-off rather than a free reset.
