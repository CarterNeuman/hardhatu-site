NCCCO's mobile crane certification isn't one exam, it's a Core written exam (90 multiple-choice questions, 90 minutes) that every candidate takes regardless of specialty, plus at least one Specialty written exam chosen from three crane configurations: Lattice Boom Cranes, Telescopic Boom Cranes with a swing cab, or Telescopic Boom Cranes with a fixed cab (26 questions, 60 minutes each), plus a practical exam evaluating real hands-on operation of the specific crane type matching whichever specialty was chosen.

This matters because OSHA's crane standard, 29 CFR 1926 Subpart CC, specifically requires certification above a defined capacity and type threshold, not blanket certification for every piece of heavy equipment on a site, and NCCCO is the most common (though not the only) way employers satisfy that requirement. Independent, third-party verification of both written knowledge and hands-on skill is the actual point: crane tip-overs and failures are high-consequence events, which is why the rule didn't settle for employer-only sign-off the way most other heavy equipment is handled.

Written exams can be taken through Online Proctored Testing, at a physical Test Center, or during an Event Online Testing session, candidate's choice. Compliance with NCCCO's own Substance Abuse Policy is a stated requirement of the certification, not optional paperwork, and NCCCO also certifies other roles working around cranes, riggers and signalpersons among them, not just the operator seat.

Costs vary by exam component: a mobile crane Core exam runs roughly $140, each Specialty exam around $80 initially (slightly less at recertification), and the practical exam priced separately per attempt. Certification itself holds for 5 years, and recertifying means retaking the identical exams used for initial certification, not an abbreviated version, within the 12 months before the current certification expires.

None of this replaces building real operating experience first. Training and supervised hands-on time typically come before certification, which exists to verify and formalize competency someone has already built, not as a substitute for building it.
