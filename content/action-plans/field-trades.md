overview: >
  This plan assumes you've already read the Field & Trades Get Hired
  guide and picked a lane: union apprenticeship or open shop. From here
  it's about momentum: getting your paperwork and credentials in order,
  building a short list of real employers instead of applying into the
  void, and reaching out in a way that actually gets a callback. The
  plan below is written for a 14-day sprint at a steady pace. If you
  said you have fewer hours a week to give it, the same steps still
  happen, just spread out over more days so nothing gets skipped.
dayByDay:
  - dayStart: 1
    dayEnd: 1
    title: "Confirm your OSHA 10 status"
    detail: "If you already have your OSHA 10 card, you're done with this step, move on. If you don't, register for the next available online course today, most run under a day and clear this blocker before anything else on this list matters."
    appliesWhen: both
  - dayStart: 1
    dayEnd: 2
    title: "Pull your baseline paperwork together"
    detail: "A government-issued ID, proof you're eligible to work, and a valid driver's license if you have one. Having this ready before anyone asks for it is what lets you say yes on the spot instead of losing a day scrambling."
    appliesWhen: both
  - dayStart: 2
    dayEnd: 2
    title: "Read through two or three specific trade career pages"
    detail: "Compare entry pay, typical training length, and what a typical day actually looks like for the trades you're considering, before committing more time to one over another."
    appliesWhen: browsing
  - dayStart: 3
    dayEnd: 4
    title: "Build your target list"
    detail: "If you're going union, find your trade's local through your state or metro Building and Construction Trades Council and check its current apprenticeship application window. If you're going open shop, use the employer types below to put together 5 to 10 real names, not just \"contractors near me.\""
    appliesWhen: both
  - dayStart: 4
    dayEnd: 4
    title: "Watch a jobsite-safety lesson before you start applying"
    detail: "A few minutes on what a real jobsite expects from a new hire, so your first conversation with a foreman doesn't start from zero."
    appliesWhen: browsing
  - dayStart: 5
    dayEnd: 5
    title: "Tailor your resume or application packet"
    detail: "Use the Field & Trades Resume Guide's bullet examples as a starting point. A credentials-first, one-page packet beats a generic resume for almost every trade application."
    appliesWhen: both
  - dayStart: 6
    dayEnd: 6
    title: "Make your first contact"
    detail: "Call, walk in, or send the outreach message below to the first name on your list. Ask for the foreman or hiring manager by name if you can find it. Lead with your OSHA 10 card and that you're available to start right away."
    appliesWhen: applying
  - dayStart: 7
    dayEnd: 8
    title: "Contact two or three more on your list"
    detail: "Don't wait to hear back from the first one before reaching out to the next. Trade hiring moves fast and in parallel, so your odds go up with every real conversation you start."
    appliesWhen: applying
  - dayStart: 9
    dayEnd: 9
    title: "Decide if you're ready to move from browsing to applying"
    detail: "If the trades you've been reading about still feel right, this is the point to pick one and start reaching out for real, using the outreach guidance below."
    appliesWhen: browsing
  - dayStart: 9
    dayEnd: 10
    title: "Follow up on anything with no response"
    detail: "Three business days with no answer is the right point to follow up, not to assume it's a no. Use the follow-up template below, it takes under a minute to send."
    appliesWhen: applying
  - dayStart: 11
    dayEnd: 11
    title: "Expand your list if you're still short on responses"
    detail: "Add a construction-focused staffing agency if you haven't already, they can place you on a jobsite fast while you keep working the rest of your list."
    appliesWhen: applying
  - dayStart: 12
    dayEnd: 12
    title: "Prep for any interview or on-site conversation that's been scheduled"
    detail: "Most trade hiring conversations are short and practical, not a formal interview. The Field & Trades Interview Prep guide covers what's actually being assessed in that short conversation."
    appliesWhen: applying
  - dayStart: 13
    dayEnd: 14
    title: "Second round of follow-ups, and a real decision point"
    detail: "By now you should know which contacts are worth staying on and which ones have gone cold. Keep the warm ones going and add fresh names to replace the ones that didn't pan out."
    appliesWhen: applying
targetEmployerTypes:
  - "Union signatory contractors for your specific trade, ask your local directly which contractors are signatory in your area"
  - "ABC (Associated Builders and Contractors) member open-shop contractors, searchable by local chapter"
  - "National specialty contractors that run active apprenticeship pipelines, for example MYR Group for line work, Rosendin Electric or EMCOR for electrical and mechanical, Comfort Systems USA for HVAC"
  - "Construction-focused staffing agencies that place directly on jobsites, for example PeopleReady, Tradesmen International, or BTI"
  - "Regional general contractors large enough to run their own in-house trade crews instead of subcontracting everything out"
  - "Disaster restoration franchises, which hire trades year-round independent of new-construction cycles, for example ServiceMaster, Servpro, or BELFOR"
recruiterOutreach:
  tips:
    - "A phone call or a walk-in to the job trailer still outperforms a cold application sitting in an online queue for almost all entry-level trade hiring."
    - "Ask for the foreman or site superintendent by name if you can find it, not just whoever is hiring."
    - "Lead with your OSHA 10 card and that you can start right away. Those are the two things a foreman actually screens for first, before anything else on a resume."
    - "Follow up within three business days if you don't hear back. In this industry, persistence reads as reliability, not as pushiness."
    - "Keep it short. A foreman reading this on a jobsite phone between tasks will skim past anything longer than a few lines."
  emailTemplate: >
    Subject: [Trade] opening at [Company]


    Hi [Recruiter or Hiring Manager Name],


    My name is [Your Name], and I'm looking for [Trade] work in the
    [Location] area. I have my OSHA 10 card and I'm available to start
    right away.


    I'd like to apply for any open [Trade] positions at [Company], or be
    considered for future openings if nothing is open right now. I'm
    reliable, I show up on time, and I'm ready to learn.


    You can reach me at [Your Phone Number] or by replying to this email.
    Thanks for your time.


    [Your Name]
  linkedinTemplate: >

    10 card and I'm available to start right away. Would you be open to a
    quick call about any current or upcoming openings?
  followUpTemplate: >
    Hi [Recruiter or Hiring Manager Name], following up on the message I
    sent on [Date Sent] about [Trade] openings at [Company]. Still very
    interested and still available to start right away. Let me know if
    there's anything else you need from me.
