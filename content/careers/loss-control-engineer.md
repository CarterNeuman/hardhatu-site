title: Loss Control / Risk Engineer
category: "Insurance & Claims"
tagline: Inspects jobsites on the insurer's behalf to manage risk before a loss happens, distinct from a Safety Manager who does similar work but for the contractor, not the insurance company.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Loss Control Engineer, also called a Risk Engineer, inspects
  construction sites and facilities on behalf of an insurance company or
  broker, identifying hazards and risk factors that could lead to a claim
  and recommending changes to reduce that risk. It sounds similar to a
  Safety Manager's job, and the underlying hazard knowledge overlaps, but
  the two roles sit on opposite sides of the relationship: a Safety
  Manager works for the contractor building the project, while a Loss
  Control Engineer works for (or on behalf of) the insurer that's
  covering it, and their inspection findings can directly affect a
  project's insurance pricing and terms.
whatTheyDo:
  - Inspect construction sites and facilities to identify hazards and risk factors
  - Write loss control reports documenting findings and recommended corrective actions
  - Advise underwriters on a project or contractor's risk profile based on site inspections
  - Track a contractor's safety performance and loss history over time
  - Recommend risk improvement measures to policyholders as a condition of coverage
  - Investigate the circumstances behind significant claims to identify prevention opportunities
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review a contractor's safety records ahead of a scheduled site inspection
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Inspect an active jobsite, documenting hazards and safety program adherence
  - time: "12:00 PM"
    activity: Write up a loss control report with recommended corrective actions
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Advise an underwriter on a contractor's risk profile ahead of a policy renewal
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Follow up with a policyholder on whether previously recommended safety improvements were made
whereTheyWork:
  - Field (jobsite and facility inspections)
  - Office (report writing and underwriter coordination)
skills:
  - Jobsite hazard identification
  - Report writing and documentation
  - Understanding of OSHA and general safety standards
  - Risk assessment and data analysis
  - Clear communication with both underwriters and contractor safety teams
software:
  - Risk management information system (RMIS) software
education:
  required: Bachelor's degree in occupational safety, engineering, or a related field, plus safety or risk-related field experience; no license is legally required for this role
  preferred: >
    Board of Certified Safety Professionals' Certified Safety
    Professional (CSP) credential, the same designation many contractor
    Safety Managers pursue, and the Associate in Risk Management (ARM)
    designation from The Institutes
  notNecessary: A construction-specific degree; many loss control engineers come from a general safety or engineering background
progression:
  - Loss Control Field Representative
  - Loss Control / Risk Engineer
  - Senior Risk Engineer
  - Risk Engineering Manager
