category: "Field & Trades"
tagline: The most common way into the entire industry, no prior experience required, and a front-row seat to every trade before choosing which one to specialize in.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Construction Laborer performs the general physical work a jobsite
  needs, material handling, site cleanup, basic demolition, and support
  for skilled trades, and is usually the single easiest entry point into
  construction, since it requires no prior experience, schooling, or
  certification to start. Many people who go on to become electricians,
  carpenters, or superintendents started as laborers, using the role to
  see multiple trades up close before choosing a specialty. No license is
  legally required for this work.
whatTheyDo:
  - Load, unload, and move materials and equipment around a jobsite
  - Clean and organize work areas, removing debris and keeping walkways clear
  - Assist skilled trades with basic tasks (holding materials, basic tool use, simple demolition)
  - Set up and break down basic site equipment, barricades, and signage
  - Dig, backfill, and grade by hand where machinery can't reach
  - Follow jobsite safety procedures and use required personal protective equipment
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Arrive on site, put on PPE, and get the day's task assignment from the foreman
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Move and stage materials for the trades working that day
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Assist a carpentry crew with basic framing support tasks
  - time: "11:30 AM"
    activity: Clean up debris and organize a work area ahead of an inspection
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Help set up equipment and materials for the next day's work
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
skills:
  - Physical stamina and willingness to do demanding physical work
  - Basic tool familiarity
  - Following instructions and safety procedures precisely
  - Reliability and punctuality
  - Willingness to learn, since this role is often a stepping stone to a specific trade
software:
  - Generally none, since this is a hands-on field role
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent is common but not always required; no license or certification is legally required for this role
  preferred: None, since this is designed as an entry-level role with on-the-job training
  helpful: OSHA 10 certification, which many employers value even though it isn't a nationwide legal requirement for this role
  notNecessary: Any prior construction experience, schooling, or certification, since this is the industry's most accessible entry point
progression:
  - Construction Laborer
  - Apprentice in a chosen trade (electrician, carpenter, plumber, etc.) or Skilled Laborer/Crew Lead
  - Journeyman tradesperson or Foreman
  - Superintendent or trade-specific advancement
hirerTypes:
