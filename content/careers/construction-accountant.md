whatIs: >
  A Construction Accountant manages the financial side of construction
  projects and companies using accounting methods specific to the
  industry, job costing by project, percentage-of-completion revenue
  recognition, retention tracking, and work-in-progress (WIP) reporting
  that shows whether each active job is running ahead of or behind its
  billed value. No license is legally required to work in construction
  accounting generally; the "Certified Public Accountant" (CPA) title and
  the ability to perform certain regulated services, audits and other
  attest engagements, are legally restricted to people holding a state
  CPA license, which becomes relevant if the role involves those specific
  services rather than internal company accounting.
whatTheyDo:
  - Track job costs against budget for every active project
  - Prepare work-in-progress (WIP) reports showing over/under-billing on each job
  - Manage retention tracking and billing for pay applications
  - Apply percentage-of-completion revenue recognition across active projects
  - Support cash flow forecasting tied to project billing and payment cycles
  - Prepare financial statements and reports for company leadership, bonding companies, and lenders
