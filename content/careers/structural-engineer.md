title: Structural Engineer
category: "Technology & Design"
tagline: Designs the structural steel, concrete, and foundation systems that keep a building standing, pairing with the trades that actually build what the drawings specify.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Structural Engineer designs the systems that carry a building's
  loads, foundations, structural steel or concrete framing, and the
  connections that hold it all together, then produces the calculations
  and drawings that prove the design meets code. It's a licensed
  profession like civil engineering, and in a meaningful number of
  states the licensing is a level stricter: some states require a
  separate Structural Engineer (SE) license, beyond a general PE, to
  stamp drawings for taller buildings or higher-risk structures, while
  others treat SE as an optional add-on credential to an existing PE.
whatTheyDo:
  - Design structural systems (foundations, framing, connections) to meet code-required loads
  - Perform structural calculations for gravity, wind, and seismic loads
  - Produce structural drawings and specifications for construction
  - Review shop drawings and submittals from steel fabricators and concrete subcontractors
  - Respond to RFIs about structural conditions discovered during construction
  - Coordinate with architects, civil engineers, and MEP engineers on how systems fit together
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review structural calculations for a foundation design against updated geotechnical data
  - time: "10:00 AM"
    activity: Review structural steel shop drawings submitted by the fabricator
  - time: "12:30 PM"
    activity: Coordinate with the architect on a structural conflict with a mechanical shaft location
  - time: "2:00 PM"
    activity: Respond to an RFI about an unexpected structural condition uncovered during demolition
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Update structural drawings to reflect an approved change
whereTheyWork:
  - Office (structural engineering or design firm)
  - Occasional site visits during construction for structural observation
skills:
  - Structural analysis and design calculations
  - Reading and producing structural drawings
  - Understanding of building codes and seismic/wind design requirements
  - Reviewing shop drawings and submittals
  - Coordination with architects and other engineering disciplines
software:
  - Structural analysis and design software (e.g. structural modeling and BIM tools)
education:
  required: >
    A bachelor's degree in civil or structural engineering from an
    ABET-accredited program, then passing the Fundamentals of
    Engineering (FE) exam, then typically four years of progressive
    experience under a licensed PE before sitting for the Professional
    Engineer (PE) Structural exam. A PE license is legally required to
    stamp structural drawings, and some states also require a separate
    Structural Engineer (SE) license, a stricter credential, for taller
    buildings or higher-risk essential facilities
  notNecessary: A background in construction trades; most structural engineers come directly from an engineering degree program
progression:
  - Engineer in Training (EIT)
  - Structural Engineer
  - Senior Structural Engineer or Project Engineer (design firm side)
  - Principal Engineer or Engineering Manager
hirerTypes:

  - General Contractors (some larger firms keep in-house structural engineers)
  - Developers and Owners (for structural feasibility and due diligence)
