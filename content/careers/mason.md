whatIs: >
  A Mason builds and repairs structures using brick, concrete masonry
  units (CMU), stone, and mortar, exterior brick veneer, structural CMU
  walls, and decorative stonework. Unlike electrical or plumbing work,
  masonry generally isn't a separately state-licensed trade in most
  places, so the path into it runs through apprenticeship and on-the-job
  skill-building rather than a licensing exam.
whatTheyDo:
  - Lay brick, block, and stone according to drawings and specifications
  - Mix and apply mortar, matching the correct type and ratio for the material and application
  - Read drawings and details to plan coursing, bond patterns, and control joint locations
  - Build and remove scaffolding as work progresses up a wall
  - Cut and fit masonry units around openings, corners, and other details
  - Tuckpoint and repair existing masonry as part of restoration work

  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Cut and fit brick around a window opening per the detail drawings
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Build scaffolding to reach the next lift of the wall
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Clean tools and protect the day's completed work before weather exposure
whereTheyWork:

software:

education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, then a registered apprenticeship (typically 3-4 years of paid on-the-job training plus classroom hours), since masonry isn't a separately state-licensed trade in most places the way electrical or plumbing work is
  helpful: >
    OSHA 10 or 30 certification (not a nationwide legal requirement,
    though several states, including New York, Massachusetts,
    Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri, and Rhode Island, legally require it
    for workers on public construction projects) and scaffold-specific
    safety training
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree, since the apprenticeship-to-journeyman route is the standard path
