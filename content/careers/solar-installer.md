whatIs: >
  A Solar PV Installer mounts and wires photovoltaic panel systems on
  roofs, ground mounts, and carports, connecting them into a building's
  electrical system. The role sits somewhere between roofing and
  electrical work: installers handle the physical racking and panel
  mounting themselves, but the electrical interconnection, especially
  anything past the panel's DC output, often has to be done or signed off
  by a licensed electrician depending on the state and the scope of work.
  Licensing for the trade itself is inconsistent nationwide, which is
  part of what makes it confusing to newcomers: some states have a
  dedicated solar contractor license, most simply fold solar work under
  existing electrical or general contractor licensing, and a national
  certification (NABCEP) has become the de facto credential installers
  use to prove their skills regardless of which state they're in.
whatTheyDo:
  - Install racking and mounting systems for rooftop or ground-mount solar arrays
  - Mount and wire photovoltaic panels per the system design
  - Connect PV systems to a building's electrical panel and the utility grid, working alongside or under a licensed electrician depending on state rules
  - Test and commission completed systems to confirm they perform as designed
  - Read solar system design drawings and site surveys to plan panel layout
  - Perform maintenance and troubleshooting on existing solar installations
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Review the day's install plan and confirm panel and racking materials are staged
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Install racking and mounting hardware on a residential or commercial roof
  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Mount and wire solar panels onto the completed racking
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Coordinate with an electrician on the interconnection to the building's main electrical panel
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Test and commission a completed system, checking output against the design specs
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite, mostly rooftops, with some ground-mount and carport installations
skills:
  - Electrical fundamentals (DC and AC systems)
  - Roofing and racking installation
  - Reading solar system design drawings
  - Working safely at height
  - System testing and commissioning
software:
  - Solar design and monitoring software (varies by installer/manufacturer)
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, then on-the-job training or a
    trade school solar program; licensing varies significantly by state.
    A minority of states, plus Puerto Rico, have a dedicated solar
    contractor license, most other states instead require installers to
    work under a licensed electrical or general contractor, and a
    smaller number have no state-level licensing requirement at all
  preferred: >
    NABCEP (North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners)
    PV Installation Professional certification, a voluntary national
    credential that several states (including Maine, Minnesota, and
    Wisconsin) require for a project to qualify for state solar
    incentive programs, that Utah requires as a prerequisite for its
    state solar contractor license, and that Rhode Island accepts as
    one of several qualifying credentials for its Renewable Energy
    Professional license
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; this is a hands-on, certification-driven trade
progression:
  - Solar Installer (entry-level)
  - Lead Installer or Crew Lead
  - Solar Electrician (with electrical licensure) or Site Supervisor
  - Solar Contractor or Renewable Energy Project Manager
