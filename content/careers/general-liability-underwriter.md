whatIs: >
  A General Liability Underwriter evaluates a construction company's
  operations, safety record, and loss history to decide whether to offer it
  general liability insurance, and at what price and terms. Where a Builder's
  Risk Underwriter prices the risk to a specific project under construction, a
  General Liability (or casualty) Underwriter prices the risk of a whole
  contracting business over a policy year: third-party bodily injury and
  property damage claims arising from its work, often bundled with workers'
  compensation, auto, and umbrella coverage into one casualty program. An
  Insurance Broker brings the account to the underwriter and negotiates on the
  contractor's behalf; the underwriter represents the insurance carrier's side
  of that negotiation.
whatTheyDo:
  - Review a contractor's loss history, safety programs, and operations to assess liability risk
  - Price general liability policies and determine coverage limits, terms, and exclusions
  - Evaluate a contractor's subcontractor-management practices, since uninsured subs are a major liability driver
  - Coordinate with brokers to gather the underwriting information needed to quote an account
  - Monitor a book of contractor accounts for loss trends that could require re-pricing at renewal
  - Work with claims teams to understand how past claims should affect a renewal's pricing
