whatIs: >
  A Tile Setter installs ceramic, porcelain, and stone tile on floors,
  walls, countertops, and in wet areas like showers, cutting tile to fit
  and setting it to precise layout and leveling tolerances. BLS tracks
  Tile and Stone Setters (SOC 47-2044) as its own occupation, distinct
  from the general Flooring Installer occupation on this site. No
  personal license is generally required to work as an employee tile
  setter in most states; a handful require a specialty tile or general
  contractor license at the business level. Waterproofing in wet areas
  (showers, tub surrounds) has to be properly installed and tested before
  tile covers it, since a leak behind finished tile is expensive to trace
  and repair.
whatTheyDo:
  - Lay out tile patterns and establish reference lines before setting any tile
  - Cut tile to fit around fixtures, corners, and edges using a wet saw or tile cutter
  - Set tile using thinset mortar or mastic, maintaining consistent spacing with tile spacers
  - Apply grout and sealant once the tile has cured, and clean excess grout from the surface
  - Confirm waterproofing membranes are installed and tested in wet areas before covering them with tile
  - Set natural stone, which requires different cutting tools and setting materials than ceramic or porcelain

whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite (indoors, in finished and near-finished spaces, especially kitchens and bathrooms)
skills:
  - Precision and attention to detail (an uneven line or inconsistent grout joint is highly visible)
  - Spatial reasoning for laying out patterns around fixtures and room irregularities
  - Comfort using wet saws and tile cutters safely
  - Physical stamina for kneeling and repetitive cutting and setting
  - Basic math for layout, spacing, and material takeoffs
software:

education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent; no personal license is generally required to work as an employee tile setter
  preferred: A formal tile-setting apprenticeship (BAC-affiliated programs are common) or several years of on-the-job training
  helpful: OSHA 10 certification, Ceramic Tile Education Foundation (CTEF) certification
  notNecessary: A college degree, since this trade is learned almost entirely on the job
