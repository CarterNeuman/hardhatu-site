whatIs: >
  A Claims Examiner reviews insurance claims from a desk rather than the
  field, checking an Insurance Adjuster's scope and estimate against the
  policy's actual coverage terms, approving payment, or flagging issues that
  need further investigation before a check goes out. Where an adjuster
  inspects the physical damage, an examiner's job is coverage and compliance:
  confirming the loss is covered, the estimate is reasonable, the reserve set
  aside for the claim is adequate, and nothing about the claim suggests fraud
  or a coverage dispute that needs escalating. On larger or more complex
  construction claims, examiners often also directly handle the claim
  themselves rather than just reviewing someone else's work.
whatTheyDo:
  - Review adjusters' claim files, estimates, and supporting documentation for accuracy and coverage
  - Verify a claim falls within the policy's coverage terms, limits, and exclusions before approving payment
  - Set and adjust claim reserves, the funds an insurer sets aside for an expected payout
  - Handle larger or more complex claims directly, including coordinating with coverage counsel when a dispute arises
  - Identify claims that show signs of fraud or inflated estimates and refer them for further investigation
  - Communicate coverage decisions to adjusters, policyholders, and sometimes construction attorneys
