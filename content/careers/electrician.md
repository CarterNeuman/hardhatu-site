title: Electrician
category: "Field & Trades"
tagline: Installs and wires the systems that bring power, light, and data into a building, and keeps them from becoming a fire hazard.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  An Electrician installs, maintains, and repairs the electrical systems in a
  building, wiring, panels, outlets, lighting, and increasingly low-voltage
  data and security systems. On a commercial job they usually work for an
  electrical subcontractor, not the general contractor directly, and their
  work has to pass inspection against the National Electrical Code (NEC)
  before it can ever be covered up by drywall.
whatTheyDo:
  - Run conduit and pull wire through framed walls before drywall goes up (rough-in)
  - Install panels, breakers, outlets, switches, and fixtures (trim-out)
  - Read electrical drawings and one-line diagrams to plan a layout before cutting anything
  - Troubleshoot circuits that aren't working and trace the fault back to its source
  - Coordinate with plumbers and HVAC crews so nobody's rough-in occupies the same wall cavity at the same time
  - Test and inspect completed work before the local electrical inspector signs off on it
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Review the day's task list and pull materials for the panels being wired that day
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Rough-in wiring on the second floor, following the drawings' outlet and switch locations
  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Stop to answer a question from the superintendent about a light fixture location that isn't clear on the plans
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Terminate wiring into a newly set panel and label each circuit
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Walk finished rough-in with the inspector before the wall gets closed up
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
  - Occasionally a shop for prefabricating assemblies
skills:
  - Reading electrical drawings and code (NEC)
  - Wiring and conduit installation
  - Troubleshooting
  - Blueprint math (load calculations, conduit fill)
  - Working safely around live circuits
software:
  - Bluebeam
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, then a registered apprenticeship
    (typically 4-5 years of paid on-the-job training plus classroom
    hours); a Journeyman Electrician license is legally required by most
    states/municipalities before working unsupervised; an apprentice
    works under a licensed electrician until then
  helpful: >
    OSHA 10 or 30 certification, not a nationwide legal requirement,
    though several states (including New York, Massachusetts,
    Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri, and Rhode Island) legally require it
    for workers on public construction projects specifically
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree isn't part of this path; the apprenticeship-to-license route is the standard one, and it pays during training rather than costing tuition
progression:
  - Apprentice Electrician
  - Journeyman Electrician
  - Master Electrician
  - Electrical Foreman or Electrical Contractor (running your own crew/business)
hirerTypes:
  - Electrical Subcontractors
  - General Contractors (on projects that self-perform electrical work)
  - Facilities/Maintenance departments
