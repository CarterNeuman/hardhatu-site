title: Commissioning Agent
category: "Consultants & Advisory"
tagline: Independently verifies that a building's MEP systems actually work as designed before turnover, distinct from QA/QC and unrepresented on this site despite commissioning existing as a concept since the original build.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Commissioning Agent independently verifies that a building's
  mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and life-safety systems are
  installed, tested, and actually perform the way they were designed to
  before a building gets turned over to its owner. It's a distinct
  quality function from a QA/QC Inspector's day-to-day inspection work:
  a commissioning agent typically reports independently of the
  contractor and design team specifically so their testing and
  verification isn't influenced by whoever installed the systems, and
  the role is often required outright on projects pursuing LEED or
  similar green building certification.
whatTheyDo:
  - Develop a commissioning plan defining what systems will be tested and how
  - Review design documents early in a project to catch commissioning issues before construction
  - Witness and verify functional performance testing of HVAC, electrical, and life-safety systems
  - Document deficiencies found during testing and track them through resolution
  - Train building operations staff on the systems being turned over to them
  - Prepare a final commissioning report required for LEED and similar green building certifications
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review design documents for an upcoming project to identify commissioning requirements
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Witness functional performance testing of a building's HVAC control system
  - time: "12:00 PM"
    activity: Document a deficiency found during testing and issue it to the responsible subcontractor
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Verify a previously identified deficiency has been corrected
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Train the building's facilities team on the newly commissioned systems
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite (testing and verification)
  - Office (commissioning plan development and reporting)
skills:
  - Deep knowledge of mechanical, electrical, and life-safety systems
  - Functional performance testing procedures
  - Independent, detail-oriented verification
  - Clear technical documentation and reporting
  - Training and knowledge transfer to building operations staff
software:
  - Building automation and commissioning management software
education:
  required: Bachelor's degree in mechanical or electrical engineering, or significant relevant field experience; no license is legally required for this role, though the person performing commissioning is often a licensed PE on larger projects
  preferred: >
    ASHRAE's Building Commissioning Professional (BCxP) certification,
    or the Certified Building Commissioning Professional (CBCP)
    credential from the Association of Energy Engineers, both tiered by
    education and years of hands-on commissioning experience
  notNecessary: A background specifically in construction trades; many commissioning agents come from an MEP engineering or facilities operations background
progression:

  - Commissioning Agent
  - Senior Commissioning Agent or Commissioning Manager
  - Commissioning Practice Lead
