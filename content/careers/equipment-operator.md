whatIs: >
  An Equipment Operator runs heavy machinery on a construction site,
  excavators, bulldozers, loaders, graders, and cranes. Which certification
  is legally required depends heavily on the specific equipment: most
  heavy equipment (excavators, dozers, loaders) has no federal
  certification mandate, just an OSHA requirement that operators be
  trained and evaluated as competent by their employer. Cranes are the
  major exception: OSHA's crane standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart CC)
  requires anyone operating a crane above a certain capacity on a
  construction site to be certified by an accredited crane operator
  testing organization (most commonly through the National Commission for
  the Certification of Crane Operators, NCCCO), employer-qualified under
  an audited program, or licensed by a state/local government with its own
  qualifying program; this is a real, federally enforced requirement, not
  optional for that equipment type.
whatTheyDo:
  - Operate excavators, bulldozers, loaders, or graders to move earth and materials per the site plan
  - Operate cranes to lift and place materials, following signal person direction and load charts
  - Perform pre-operation inspections and basic maintenance on assigned equipment
  - Read grading and site plans to execute cuts, fills, and grading to the correct elevations
  - Coordinate with ground crews and signal persons for safe, precise material placement
  - Maintain daily logs of equipment hours and any maintenance issues
