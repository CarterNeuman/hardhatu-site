title: Restoration Project Manager
category: "Specialized Construction"
tagline: Runs the actual rebuild after property damage, the project management counterpart to the estimators and adjusters who scope and price the claim.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Restoration Project Manager manages the reconstruction and repair work
  after property damage, fire, water, storm, or other loss, coordinating
  crews, subcontractors, and schedule the same way a general construction
  Project Manager would, but working from an insurance-approved scope and
  budget rather than a standard construction bid. No personal license or
  certification is legally required to hold this role as an employee; a
  restoration company itself, like any general contractor, typically needs
  its own state contractor's license to legally perform and bill for the
  work, but that license attaches to the business, not to each individual
  project manager it employs.
whatTheyDo:
  - Manage restoration projects from the approved insurance scope through completion
  - Coordinate subcontractors and crews across multiple simultaneous restoration jobs
  - Track project costs against the insurance-approved budget
  - Communicate progress to the property owner and, often, the insurance adjuster or public adjuster
  - Manage change orders when concealed damage is discovered mid-project
  - Ensure completed work passes final inspection and closes out the insurance claim
typicalDay:
  - time: "7:30 AM"
    activity: Review overnight updates from active restoration jobs and prioritize site visits
  - time: "9:00 AM"
    activity: Walk a fire-damage restoration site with the lead subcontractor
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Document concealed water damage found during demolition and prepare a supplement request
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Update the property owner on progress and revised timeline
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Coordinate final walkthrough scheduling for a nearly complete project
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
  - Office (for scheduling, budgeting, and coordination)
skills:
  - Project management and scheduling
  - Reading insurance scopes and estimates
  - Subcontractor coordination
  - Client communication, often with owners in a stressful situation
  - Change order and supplement documentation
software:

education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, since no personal license or certification is legally required for this role as an employee; the restoration company itself typically needs its own state contractor's license to perform the work
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in construction management or a related field, or equivalent field experience
  helpful: IICRC restoration certifications, Project Management Professional (PMP) credential
  notNecessary: A construction management degree, since many restoration PMs move up from estimating or field supervision within restoration work
progression:

  - Restoration Project Manager
  - Senior Project Manager
  - Director of Operations (at a restoration contractor)
