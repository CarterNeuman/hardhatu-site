whatIs: >
  A Commercial Manager owns the financial and contractual outcome of a
  project or program, negotiating change orders and claims to a resolution
  rather than simply documenting them, managing cost and margin against the
  contract value as the job proceeds, and representing the company's
  commercial interests directly in conversations with the owner or client.
  It's a more senior and more negotiation-driven role than a Contracts
  Administrator, who manages the paperwork and compliance side of
  contracts, change orders, and insurance; a Commercial Manager has the
  authority to actually settle a disputed change order or claim and carries
  real responsibility for the project's bottom line. It's also broader than
  a Quantity Surveyor, whose work centers on measurement and valuation: a
  Commercial Manager oversees that measurement work as part of a wider
  commercial strategy. The title is especially common at large commercial
  general contractors and international infrastructure contractors working
  in the US, and less common at smaller firms, where a Project Manager or
  Project Executive typically absorbs this responsibility instead.
whatTheyDo:
  - Negotiate change orders and claims to resolution on behalf of the company
  - Manage project cost and margin against the contract value throughout construction
  - Represent the company's commercial position directly to the owner or client
  - Review and advise on contract terms and commercial risk before and during a project
  - Oversee subcontractor commercial administration, including change orders and final accounts
  - Report project-level commercial performance to company leadership
