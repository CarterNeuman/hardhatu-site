id: career-superintendent
title: Superintendent
category: "Project & Operations"
tagline: Runs the jobsite day-to-day so the schedule, the safety, and the actual sequence of work all hold together.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Superintendent ("super") is the general contractor's senior person
  physically on site every day, responsible for the means and methods of
  construction: which trade works where, in what order, and whether what
  just got built actually matches the drawings before it gets covered up.
  Where a Project Manager owns the budget and the paperwork, the
  superintendent owns the field: safety, sequencing, and quality control on
  the ground.
whatTheyDo:
  - Walk the site daily to check progress against the schedule and catch problems early
  - Coordinate the day-to-day sequencing of subcontractors so trades aren't fighting over the same space
  - Enforce jobsite safety rules and run daily safety briefings
  - Inspect completed work against the drawings before it's covered by the next trade
  - File RFIs from the field when the drawings don't match what's actually buildable
  - Escalate anything that will affect cost or schedule to the project manager, usually as the first step toward a change order
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Walk the site before crews arrive, checking yesterday's work and today's material staging
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Run the morning safety briefing and hand out the day's work assignments to each trade
  - time: "9:00 AM"
    activity: Resolve a sequencing conflict between two trades scheduled for the same space; one gets moved
  - time: "12:00 PM"
    activity: Inspect framing before the drywall crew closes up a wall, catch a dimension issue, file an RFI
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Update the project manager on progress and flag a schedule risk from a delayed material delivery
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
skills:
  - Field coordination and sequencing
  - Reading construction drawings
  - Safety management (often OSHA 30 certified)
  - Conflict resolution
  - Scheduling
software:
  - Procore
  - Bluebeam
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent. No OSHA certification is legally
    mandated nationwide for this role, but OSHA 30 becomes legally
    required for a construction supervisor on a public project in a
    state that mandates it (including New York, Massachusetts,
    Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri, and Rhode Island); most employers
    expect it as standard practice everywhere else too
  helpful: A construction management degree helps but is far from universal in this role
  notNecessary: A four-year degree; most superintendents are promoted up through the trades (starting as a tradesperson or foreman) rather than hired straight out of school
progression:
  - Foreman
  - Assistant Superintendent
  - Superintendent
  - Senior or General Superintendent
  - Director of Field Operations
hirerTypes:
  - General Contractors
  - Construction Management Firms
