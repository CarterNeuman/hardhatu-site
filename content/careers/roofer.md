title: Roofer
category: "Field & Trades"
tagline: Installs and repairs the one system every building depends on to keep water out, physically demanding work done almost entirely outdoors, at height.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Roofer installs, repairs, and replaces roofing systems, shingles, tile,
  metal, or low-slope membrane roofing, on new construction and existing
  buildings. No personal license is legally required to work as an employee
  roofer in most of the country; roofing licensing in the states that
  require it (including California, Nevada, and Utah) applies at the
  business/contractor level, the person who contracts directly with
  customers and pulls permits, not to every roofer on a crew. A number of
  states (including Georgia, Missouri, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Texas, and
  others) have no state-level roofing license requirement at all, though
  local jurisdictions can impose their own.
whatTheyDo:
  - Install roofing materials (shingles, tile, metal panels, or membrane systems) per manufacturer specifications
  - Remove and dispose of old roofing during a re-roof or repair
  - Inspect roofs for leaks, damage, and wear, and identify the cause of a failure
  - Install flashing, underlayment, and ventilation components that keep a roof system watertight
  - Follow fall-protection procedures required for work at height on a sloped or low-slope roof
  - Coordinate with insurance adjusters or estimators when the work is tied to a storm-damage claim
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Load materials and equipment for the day's job, check the weather forecast
  - time: "7:30 AM"
    activity: Set up fall protection and begin tearing off old roofing on a re-roof job
  - time: "10:00 AM"
    activity: Install underlayment and begin laying the new roofing material
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Install flashing around a chimney and vents to keep those transitions watertight
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Do a final walk of the completed sections and clean up debris and materials
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite (on rooftops, almost entirely outdoors)
skills:
  - Comfort and safety awareness working at height on sloped surfaces
  - Physical stamina, especially working outdoors in heat
  - Attention to detail (a small missed detail in flashing or underlayment causes a leak)
  - Reading manufacturer installation specifications
  - Basic carpentry for repairing roof decking
software:
  - Generally none, since this is a hands-on field trade; some roofers use estimating apps for measurements and quotes
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, since no personal license is legally
    required to work as an employee roofer in most states. A person who
    wants to independently contract roofing work (sign contracts, pull
    permits) typically needs a state roofing or general contractor license
    in states that require one; that requirement is business-level, and
    which states require it varies widely
  preferred: On-the-job training or a formal roofing apprenticeship
  helpful: OSHA 10 or OSHA 30 certification, manufacturer-specific installation certifications for particular roofing systems
  notNecessary: A college degree, since this trade is learned almost entirely on the job
progression:
  - Roofing Laborer
  - Roofer
  - Crew Lead or Foreman
  - Roofing Superintendent or own a roofing contracting business
