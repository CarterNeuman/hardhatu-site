title: Deep Foundation / Pile-Driving Specialist
category: "Specialized Construction"
tagline: Runs the specialized equipment that drives piles and caissons deep into the ground to support a structure where a standard foundation won't work, a real, well-paid niche most newcomers never see.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Deep Foundation Specialist, often called a Pile Driver, operates the
  specialized equipment that installs piles, caissons, and other deep
  foundation elements, driving or drilling structural support deep into
  the ground where soil conditions or building loads make a standard
  shallow foundation impractical. The equipment involved (pile driving
  rigs) is specialized and regulated enough that OSHA classifies it
  separately from general cranes, and the National Commission for the
  Certification of Crane Operators built a dedicated certification for
  operating it.
whatTheyDo:
  - Operate pile driving rigs to install driven piles to specified depth and capacity
  - Read geotechnical reports and foundation drawings to plan pile layout and installation sequence
  - Monitor pile driving records (blow counts, refusal criteria) to confirm piles meet design capacity
  - Perform equipment inspections and maintenance on specialized pile driving rigs
  - Coordinate with structural engineers on unexpected subsurface conditions
  - Work alongside crews installing caissons or drilled shaft foundations on larger projects
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:00 AM"
    activity: Inspect the pile driving rig and confirm it's ready for the day's installation
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Drive piles per the day's layout, monitoring blow counts against the design criteria
  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Document pile driving records for the engineer of record's review
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Coordinate with the structural engineer on an unexpected soil condition at one pile location
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Perform end-of-day equipment maintenance and log completed pile counts
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite (typically early in the construction sequence, before the rest of the foundation)
skills:
  - Pile driving and deep foundation equipment operation
  - Reading geotechnical reports and foundation drawings
  - Understanding of pile capacity and blow count criteria
  - Heavy equipment inspection and maintenance
  - Working safely around heavy, specialized equipment
software:
  - Generally none; this is a hands-on field trade
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, then on-the-job training and equipment-specific experience; OSHA's cranes and derricks rule (1926 Subpart CC) legally requires anyone operating this equipment to be certified or licensed through one of three qualifying paths, a certification from an accredited testing organization, a state or local license that meets the rule's own criteria, or an employer's audited certification program, and NCCCO's Dedicated Pile Driver (DPD) Operator certification is one accredited-organization option satisfying that requirement, covering equipment setup, inspection, load charts, and hands-on operating proficiency
  helpful: OSHA 10 or 30 certification, and rigging or signal-person certification
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; this is an equipment-operation specialty built on hands-on experience
progression:
  - Pile Driving Crew Member
  - Certified Pile Driver Operator
  - Pile Driving Foreman
  - Deep Foundation Contractor or Superintendent
hirerTypes:
  - Deep Foundation and Pile Driving Subcontractors
  - Heavy Civil and Marine Construction Contractors
  - General Contractors (on projects requiring deep foundations)
