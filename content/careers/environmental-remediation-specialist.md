whatIs: >
  An Environmental/Remediation Specialist manages environmental hazards
  connected to a construction or demolition project, testing for
  contamination, coordinating remediation, and keeping the work compliant
  with environmental regulations so it doesn't create a hazard for workers
  or the public. This role sits at the intersection of several different
  federal and state regulatory schemes, each triggering its own mandatory
  certification depending on the specific hazard: OSHA's 40-hour HAZWOPER
  training (29 CFR 1910.120) is required for workers handling hazardous
  waste site cleanup or working at certain regulated hazardous waste
  facilities; EPA's Lead-Safe Certification is required by federal law for
  anyone disturbing lead-based paint during renovation or repair of a
  pre-1978 residence or child-occupied facility; and most states require a
  separate asbestos abatement license before a worker can perform asbestos
  removal. A specialist working across multiple hazard types over a career
  typically ends up holding more than one of these, since each applies
  only to the specific hazard it covers.
whatTheyDo:
  - Conduct or coordinate environmental testing (lead, asbestos, soil/groundwater contamination) before demolition or renovation
  - Develop remediation plans and oversee abatement or cleanup work for compliance
  - Ensure work complies with the specific federal or state rule that applies to the hazard present
  - Coordinate with regulatory agencies on permitting, notifications, and inspections
  - Train site workers on hazard-specific safety procedures
  - Document remediation work for regulatory and liability purposes
