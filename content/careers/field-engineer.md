whatIs: >
  A Field Engineer is the entry-level engineering role on the field side
  of a project rather than the office side, reporting to a Superintendent
  or General Superintendent rather than a Project Manager. Where a
  Project Engineer's work is mostly documentation and coordination from
  an office or jobsite trailer, submittals, RFIs, and drawing logs, a
  Field Engineer spends most of the day actually out on the work:
  performing layout and surveying checks to confirm what's being built
  matches the drawings, tracking daily field progress and quantities,
  and supporting the Superintendent's crew with technical problem-solving
  in real time. It's especially common in heavy civil and infrastructure
  construction, where tight engineering tolerances on grading, utilities,
  and structures make a dedicated field-side technical role worth having
  on every project, and it's a frequent first job for a civil engineering
  or construction management graduate who wants to learn the field before
  the office.
whatTheyDo:
  - Perform layout and surveying checks to confirm work in progress matches the drawings
  - Track daily field quantities and progress for cost and schedule reporting
  - Support the Superintendent's crew with technical questions and problem-solving in the field
  - Document field conditions, as-built changes, and daily activity through site photos and reports
  - Coordinate material deliveries and staging with the field team
  - Assist with quality control checks on completed work before it's covered up
