whatIs: >
  A Workers' Compensation Claims Adjuster manages a construction worker's
  injury claim from the first incident report through medical treatment,
  wage-replacement benefits, and return to work. Unlike a Property/Casualty
  Insurance Adjuster who writes an estimate for physical damage, a workers'
  comp adjuster manages an ongoing medical and disability case: approving
  treatment, calculating indemnity (wage-replacement) benefits, tracking a
  worker's recovery, and working with employers and medical providers toward a
  safe return to work, all under state-specific workers' comp statutes that
  vary significantly by jurisdiction. In construction specifically, these
  claims also feed directly into a contractor's experience modification rate,
  so how a claim is handled affects that contractor's future insurance costs
  for years.
whatTheyDo:
  - Investigate and establish coverage for newly reported workplace injury claims
  - Approve and coordinate medical treatment for injured workers within state workers' comp guidelines
  - Calculate and administer indemnity (wage-replacement) benefit payments
  - Coordinate with employers and medical providers on modified-duty and return-to-work plans
  - Monitor claim reserves and case progress, closing claims once a worker reaches maximum medical improvement
  - Communicate with attorneys on contested or litigated claims
