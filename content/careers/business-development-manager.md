id: career-business-development-manager
title: Business Development Manager
category: "Business"
tagline: Builds the relationships and pursuit strategy behind a contractor's biggest wins, the senior counterpart to Construction Sales focused on major, long-cycle pursuits rather than direct selling.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Business Development Manager builds and maintains the relationships
  that win a contractor major work, developers, owners, architects, and
  repeat clients, and leads the strategy behind pursuing large projects
  long before a bid is ever due. It overlaps with Construction Sales, but
  where sales is often about closing individual deals, business
  development is usually a longer game: staying visible with the right
  people for years so that when a major project comes up, the firm gets
  invited to pursue it at all.
whatTheyDo:
  - Build and maintain relationships with developers, owners, architects, and repeat clients
  - Identify and track upcoming project opportunities well before they go out to bid
  - Lead go/no-go decisions on which pursuits are worth the firm's time and resources
  - Develop proposal strategy and messaging for major pursuits, working with marketing and estimating
  - Represent the company at industry events, associations, and client meetings
  - Track win/loss data and refine pursuit strategy over time
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:30 AM"
    activity: Review the pipeline of upcoming project opportunities and update pursuit priorities
  - time: "10:00 AM"
    activity: Meet with a developer client to discuss an upcoming project still in early planning
  - time: "12:00 PM"
    activity: Have lunch with a developer the firm has cultivated for years, ahead of a major project still a year or two from breaking ground
  - time: "2:00 PM"
    activity: Lead a go/no-go meeting on whether to pursue a major upcoming project
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Work with the marketing team on proposal strategy for a pursuit due next month
whereTheyWork:
  - Company office
  - Client meetings, industry events, and site visits
skills:
  - Relationship building and networking
  - Strategic thinking and pursuit prioritization
  - Presentation and communication skills
  - Understanding of the construction industry and project delivery methods
  - Proposal strategy and messaging
software:
  - Customer relationship management (CRM) software
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, plus significant industry relationship-building experience; no license is legally required for this role
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in construction management, business, marketing, or a related field
  helpful: >
    The Society for Marketing Professional Services' Certified
    Professional Services Marketer (CPSM) credential, which requires
    four or more years of relevant marketing/business development
    experience (with a bachelor's degree) and passing a 150-question exam
  notNecessary: A construction-specific degree; many business development managers come from sales, marketing, or account management backgrounds in other industries
progression:
  - Business Development Coordinator
  - Business Development Manager
  - Director of Business Development
  - Vice President of Business Development or Chief Growth Officer
hirerTypes:
  - General Contractors
  - Specialty Subcontractors (on larger firms with dedicated BD roles)
  - Construction Management Firms
