whatIs: >
  A Waterproofing Installer applies waterproofing membranes and systems
  to below-grade foundation walls, parking structures, plaza decks, and
  other surfaces where water intrusion has to be stopped before the area
  is backfilled, built over, or otherwise sealed from access. It's
  considered a specialty aspect of the roofing trade, and many
  waterproofers cross-train or work alongside roofers, since both trades
  install membrane systems and share union representation. No personal
  license is generally required to work as an employee waterproofer; a
  specialty waterproofing or general contractor license may be required
  at the business level in some states. The stakes on this work are
  unusually high: a waterproofing failure behind a backfilled foundation
  wall or under a finished plaza deck is essentially impossible to fix
  without excavating or demolishing what was built over it.
whatTheyDo:
  - Prepare the substrate (concrete, masonry) before applying a waterproofing membrane
  - Apply sheet, liquid-applied, or bentonite waterproofing membranes to foundation walls, plaza decks, and below-grade structures
  - Install flashing and terminations at penetrations, corners, and transitions where membrane failures are most common
  - Coordinate with the concrete crew so waterproofing goes on after the concrete has cured but before backfill or decking covers it
  - Perform flood testing or water testing on some installations to confirm the membrane holds before it's covered
  - Follow manufacturer specifications closely, since most waterproofing warranties require documented, inspected installation
