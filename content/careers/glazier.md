whatIs: >
  A Glazier cuts, fits, and installs glass and metal glazing systems on a
  project, from full curtain wall and storefront systems on commercial
  buildings to windows, mirrors, and interior glass partitions. Much of
  the work happens well into a project, once the structural frame is up
  and the building needs its exterior skin, and a lot of it happens at
  height, on scaffolding or a swing stage, coordinating tight tolerances
  with the structural steel or framing crew that set the openings.
whatTheyDo:
  - Install curtain wall panels and storefront systems per shop drawings and manufacturer specs
  - Cut, fit, and set glass panes to exact frame dimensions
  - Apply sealants and glazing compounds to create a weathertight seal around installed glass
  - Install mirrors, glass partitions, and skylights
  - Read curtain wall and storefront shop drawings to plan sequencing and material needs
  - Coordinate with structural steel and framing crews so openings come in at the tolerances glazing requires
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Review the day's curtain wall installation sequence and stage glass panels at the hoist
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Set and secure curtain wall units on the level currently underway, checking plumb and level as each panel goes in
  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Apply sealant and weatherproofing around a completed section of storefront glazing
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Fit and install interior glass partitions on a lower, already-enclosed floor
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Walk completed glazing with the foreman, checking for gaps, scratches, or seal defects before the next trade moves in
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite, often at height on scaffolding, a swing stage, or a boom lift
  - Occasionally a shop for pre-glazing frames before they ship to site
skills:
  - Reading curtain wall and storefront shop drawings
  - Precise measurement and cutting
  - Sealant and glazing compound application
  - Working safely at height
  - Math for angles, dimensions, and material takeoff
software:
  - Generally none; this is a hands-on field trade
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, then a registered apprenticeship (typically three years or more, with a minimum of 144 hours of classroom instruction per year plus paid on-the-job training through a union program like IUPAT); most states don't separately license glaziers the way they do electricians or plumbers
  helpful: >
    OSHA 10 or 30 certification (not a nationwide legal requirement,
    though several states, including New York, Massachusetts,
    Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri, and Rhode Island, legally require it
    for workers on public construction projects), plus fall protection
    or aerial/scissor lift certification given how much of the work
    happens at height
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; the apprenticeship-to-journeyman route is the standard path into the trade
progression:
  - Apprentice Glazier
  - Journeyman Glazier
  - Glazing Foreman
  - Glazing Contractor or Superintendent
hirerTypes:
