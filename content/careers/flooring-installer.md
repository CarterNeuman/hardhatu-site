whatIs: >
  A Flooring Installer installs the finished floor covering, resilient
  sheet and tile (vinyl, linoleum), engineered and laminate wood, carpet,
  or specialty systems like epoxy-resinous and access flooring, over a
  prepared subfloor. No personal license is generally required to work
  as an employee flooring installer; a handful of states require a
  specialty flooring or general contractor license at the business level
  for anyone contracting directly with customers. The job starts with
  testing and preparing the subfloor, since moisture or flatness problems
  that aren't caught before installation show up later as bubbling,
  curling, or adhesive failure that's expensive to fix after the floor is
  down. Tile and stone setting is tracked by BLS as a separate occupation
  and has its own career page on this site.
whatTheyDo:
  - Test subfloor moisture and flatness before installation, and prepare the surface (patching, leveling, grinding)
  - Measure and cut flooring material to fit the room, including transitions, thresholds, and obstacles
  - Install resilient sheet, vinyl tile, laminate, or engineered wood flooring using the manufacturer's specified adhesive or click-lock method
  - Install carpet, including stretching, seaming, and pattern matching
  - Apply specialty systems like epoxy-resinous flooring, following the manufacturer's surface-prep and cure-time requirements
  - Document moisture test results for the quality control team before installation is approved to proceed
typicalDay:
  - time: "7:00 AM"

  - time: "1:00 PM"

whereTheyWork:

skills:

  - Measuring and cutting accuracy to minimize waste
  - Knowledge of moisture testing and subfloor prep requirements for different flooring types
  - Physical stamina for kneeling, bending, and repetitive cutting
  - Basic math for material takeoffs and layout
software:
