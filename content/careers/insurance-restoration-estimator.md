whatIs: >
  An Insurance Restoration Estimator prepares detailed repair estimates for
  property damage tied to an insurance claim, usually working for a
  restoration or reconstruction contractor, translating the scope of
  damage into a detailed, insurer-acceptable estimate, almost always using
  Xactimate, the estimating software most insurers standardize on. The
  role is distinct from an Insurance Adjuster or Public Adjuster: those
  roles determine or negotiate what the policy actually covers and pays,
  while an estimator's job is producing an accurate cost estimate of the
  repair, not deciding or advocating what should be paid. No state license
  is legally required specifically for this estimating work, as long as
  the person isn't also adjusting the claim or negotiating coverage on
  someone's behalf; those specific activities are what trigger the state
  adjuster or public adjuster licensing requirements covered elsewhere on
  this site.
whatTheyDo:
  - Inspect and document property damage in detail, including photos and measurements
  - Write a comprehensive line-item estimate of repair costs using industry-standard estimating software
  - Reconcile estimate discrepancies between the contractor's and insurer's numbers
  - Coordinate with adjusters or public adjusters to align on the scope of covered repairs
  - Track material and labor pricing to keep estimates current and defensible
  - Support the contractor's production team by handing off an accurate estimate to work from
