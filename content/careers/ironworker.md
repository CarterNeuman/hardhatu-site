title: Ironworker
category: "Field & Trades"
tagline: Erects the structural steel skeleton a building hangs from, often working at height on beams before any floor or safety net exists below.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  An Ironworker erects and connects structural steel (and, in a related
  specialty, places reinforcing steel/"rebar" in concrete). Structural
  ("erecting") ironworkers set beams and columns with a crane and bolt or
  weld them into place, frequently working at significant height. No
  government-issued personal license is required to work as an ironworker,
  but two things become legally or contractually mandatory depending on the
  specific work: OSHA's steel erection standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart R)
  requires specific fall-protection and connector training for structural
  steel erection work, and AWS D1.1 structural welding certification is
  required, by code or contract, not by the ironworker trade itself,
  specifically for ironworkers who perform structural steel welding
  connections, not for those doing bolted connections only.
whatTheyDo:
  - Erect structural steel beams and columns using a crane, guiding pieces into position
  - Bolt or weld steel connections according to structural drawings
  - Place and tie reinforcing steel (rebar) in concrete forms before a pour, on the reinforcing side of the trade
  - Read structural drawings to verify correct steel placement and connection details
  - Rig loads for the crane operator and work as part of a coordinated erection crew
  - Follow fall-protection procedures required for steel erection work at height
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Review the day's erection sequence and rigging plan with the crew
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Guide steel beams into position as the crane sets them, connecting temporarily with bolts
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Make final structural connections, bolting or welding per the drawings
  - time: "12:00 PM"
    activity: Rig the next load and coordinate with the crane operator on placement
  - time: "2:30 PM"
    activity: Verify plumb and alignment of the day's erected steel before securing it permanently
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite (frequently at height)
skills:
  - Comfort and safety awareness working at significant height
  - Rigging and signaling to crane operators
  - Reading structural steel drawings
  - Physical strength and stamina
  - Precision bolting or welding to structural tolerances
software:
  - Generally none, since this is a hands-on field trade
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, typically followed by a formal
    apprenticeship, no personal government license is required to work as
    an ironworker. Ironworkers doing structural steel erection are legally
    required to follow OSHA's steel erection fall-protection and connector
    training rules (29 CFR 1926 Subpart R), and an ironworker who performs
    structural steel welding connections needs AWS D1.1 structural welding
    certification for that specific scope of work
  preferred: Completion of a formal ironworker apprenticeship (typically 3-4 years, often through a union)
  helpful: OSHA 30 certification, crane signal person certification for those who rig and signal regularly
  notNecessary: A college degree, since this trade is learned entirely through apprenticeship
progression:
  - Apprentice Ironworker
  - Journeyman Ironworker
  - Foreman
  - Superintendent or own an ironworking/steel erection business
hirerTypes:
  - Steel Erection Subcontractors
  - General Contractors (on structural steel projects)
