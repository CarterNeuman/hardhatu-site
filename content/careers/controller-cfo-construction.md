whatIs: >
  A Controller or Chief Financial Officer (CFO) at a construction company owns
  the financial side of the business at the strategic level, cash flow
  forecasting, bonding capacity and the surety relationship, bank covenant
  compliance, multi-entity financial consolidation, and the numbers ownership
  uses to decide how aggressively to grow. Where a Construction Accountant
  handles job costing, WIP reporting, and day-to-day financial transactions
  for active projects, a Controller sits above that work, reviewing and
  consolidating it into company-wide financial statements and controls, while
  a CFO adds a layer of strategic finance on top: capital planning, banking
  and surety relationships, and advising ownership on acquisitions, major
  equipment purchases, or expansion. At many mid-size construction companies,
  Controller is explicitly the stepping-stone role to CFO as the company
  grows, which is why job postings for this role routinely advertise "a clear
  path to CFO".
whatTheyDo:
  - Consolidate project-level job costing and WIP reports into company-wide financial statements
  - Manage cash flow forecasting and banking relationships
  - Prepare the financial statements a surety reviews annually to set the company's bonding capacity
  - Oversee internal controls, GAAP compliance, and the annual audit or review process
  - Advise ownership on capital planning, major purchases, and growth strategy (CFO-level responsibility)
  - Manage the accounting team and set company-wide financial policy
