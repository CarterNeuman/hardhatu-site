whatIs: >
  A Credit Manager sets and manages a construction company's credit policy,
  deciding how much credit to extend a given customer or contractor,
  monitoring accounts receivable aging, and running the collections process on
  overdue accounts. In construction, this job carries extra weight because of
  mechanic's lien and bond-claim rights: a Credit Manager has to understand
  lien priority and preliminary notice deadlines well enough to preserve a
  company's right to file a lien if an account goes unpaid, since missing a
  notice deadline can mean losing that leverage entirely. This role is most
  common at construction materials suppliers, equipment dealers, and specialty
  subcontractors who extend trade credit to contractors, rather than at the
  general contractor level, where payment typically flows the other direction.
  The National Association of Credit Management (NACM) offers
  industry-recognized credit certifications, including one specific to
  commercial construction credit.
whatTheyDo:
  - Evaluate new customer credit applications and set credit limits
  - Monitor accounts receivable aging and flag accounts falling behind
  - Run the collections process on overdue accounts, including placing credit holds
  - Track mechanic's lien and bond-claim preliminary notice deadlines to preserve legal remedies
  - Negotiate payment plans with slow-paying customers
  - Report credit risk and AR aging trends to company leadership
