title: Facilities Manager
category: "Specialized Construction"
tagline: Keeps a building running well after the construction crew leaves, maintenance, repairs, and the small renovation projects that never really stop.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Facilities Manager oversees the ongoing maintenance, repair, and small
  renovation work in an already-built facility, an office building, a
  hospital, a school, a manufacturing plant. Where every other role on this
  site focuses on getting a project built, a Facilities Manager picks up
  where construction leaves off: keeping building systems running,
  managing vendors and in-house maintenance staff, and planning the
  capital improvement projects that eventually bring construction crews
  back in.
whatTheyDo:
  - Oversee preventive maintenance schedules for HVAC, electrical, plumbing, and life-safety systems
  - Manage vendor and contractor relationships for repairs and small renovation projects
  - Respond to and prioritize facility issues (leaks, equipment failures, life-safety concerns)
  - Plan and budget capital improvement projects (roof replacement, HVAC upgrades, renovations)
  - Ensure the building stays compliant with fire, safety, and accessibility codes
  - Manage in-house maintenance staff and coordinate their work with outside contractors
typicalDay:
  - time: "7:30 AM"
    activity: Review overnight maintenance tickets and prioritize anything urgent
  - time: "9:00 AM"
    activity: Walk the building with a mechanical contractor bidding on an HVAC replacement
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Review quotes for a roof repair against the facility's capital budget
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Coordinate a fire alarm inspection with the testing vendor and building occupants
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Meet with in-house maintenance staff to plan next week's preventive maintenance tasks
whereTheyWork:
  - The facility itself, full-time
  - Office (for budgeting and vendor management)
skills:
  - Building systems knowledge (HVAC, electrical, plumbing, life safety)
  - Vendor and contractor management
  - Budgeting for capital and operating expenses
  - Code and compliance knowledge
  - Problem-solving under time pressure
software:
  - Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS) software
  - Procore
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in facilities management, construction management, or engineering
  helpful: Certified Facility Manager (CFM) credential
  notNecessary: A specific facilities degree; many facilities managers come up through building maintenance, engineering, or property management rather than starting in this role
progression:
  - Maintenance Technician or Coordinator
  - Assistant Facilities Manager
  - Facilities Manager
  - Director of Facilities
hirerTypes:
  - Building Owners
  - Property Management Companies
  - Institutions (hospitals, schools, universities, corporate campuses)
