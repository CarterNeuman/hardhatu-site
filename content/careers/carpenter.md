title: Carpenter
category: "Field & Trades"
tagline: Builds the wood and metal framing that gives a structure its shape, and the finish work that makes it look done.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Carpenter builds and installs wood, metal-stud, and composite
  structures on a project, from framing walls, floors, and roofs early in
  a project (rough carpentry) to installing trim, cabinetry, and doors near
  the end (finish carpentry). Commercial carpenters usually work for a
  framing or general carpentry subcontractor, and their work ranges from
  physically demanding structural framing to precise, detail-oriented
  finish work depending on the phase of the project.
whatTheyDo:
  - Frame walls, floors, and roof structures according to structural drawings
  - Install metal stud framing for interior partitions on commercial projects
  - Build and set concrete formwork that holds concrete in place until it cures
  - Install doors, trim, cabinetry, and other finish carpentry near project closeout
  - Read structural and architectural drawings to plan layouts before cutting material
  - Coordinate with other trades so framing leaves the right openings for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Review the day's framing layout and stage lumber or metal studs for the crew
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Frame interior partition walls on the level currently under construction
  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Coordinate with the electrician on stud spacing near a panel location
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Build and set concrete formwork for an upcoming pour
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Check completed framing against the drawings before the next trade moves in
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
  - Occasionally a shop for prefabricating wall panels or trim assemblies
skills:
  - Reading architectural and structural drawings
  - Framing and formwork
  - Precise measurement and cutting
  - Blueprint math (layout, angles, material takeoff)
  - Working safely at height and around power tools
software:
  - Bluebeam
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, then a registered apprenticeship (typically 3-4 years of paid on-the-job training plus classroom hours); carpentry isn't separately state-licensed in most places the way electrical or plumbing work is
  helpful: >
    OSHA 10 or 30 certification (not a nationwide legal requirement,
    though several states, including New York, Massachusetts,
    Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri, and Rhode Island, legally require it
    for workers on public construction projects) and fall protection or
    scaffold certifications for framing at height
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; the apprenticeship-to-journeyman route is the standard path, and many carpenters move up through the trade rather than through school
progression:
  - Apprentice Carpenter
  - Journeyman Carpenter
  - Lead Carpenter or Carpentry Foreman
  - Carpentry Contractor (running your own crew/business) or Superintendent
hirerTypes:
  - Carpentry and Framing Subcontractors
  - General Contractors (on projects that self-perform framing)
  - Custom home builders and remodeling companies
