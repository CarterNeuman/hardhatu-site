title: Drone/UAV Specialist
category: "Technology & Design"
tagline: Flies drones to capture aerial imagery, topographic data, and progress documentation for a project, one of the few construction roles with a hard federal license requirement.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Drone/UAV Specialist operates unmanned aircraft to capture aerial
  photos, video, and survey-grade topographic data for a construction
  project, site progress documentation, volumetric measurements of
  earthwork, and increasingly data that feeds directly into a BIM model.
  Unlike most roles on this site, this one has a clear-cut federal
  requirement: the FAA requires anyone flying a drone for commercial
  purposes in the United States, which covers virtually all construction
  use, to hold a Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate, earned by passing the
  FAA's aeronautical knowledge test. There's no ambiguity or workaround
  here; flying commercially without it is a real regulatory violation.
whatTheyDo:
  - Plan and fly drone missions to capture site progress photos and video
  - Capture aerial data used to calculate earthwork volumes and verify grading
  - Process captured imagery into orthomosaic maps and 3D point clouds
  - Coordinate flights with site safety requirements and, near airports, FAA airspace authorization
  - Deliver processed data to project teams, estimators, or the BIM/VDC team for use in the model
  - Maintain and inspect drone equipment and keep required flight logs
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Check weather and airspace conditions before planning the day's flight
  - time: "9:00 AM"
    activity: Fly a site progress mission, capturing imagery of the week's earthwork
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Process captured imagery into an updated site orthomosaic
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Deliver volumetric earthwork data to the estimating team
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Inspect and maintain drone equipment, and log the day's flight hours
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
  - Office (for data processing)
skills:
  - Drone piloting and mission planning
  - Aerial data processing (photogrammetry, point clouds)
  - Understanding of FAA airspace rules
  - Basic surveying and earthwork measurement concepts
  - Attention to safety around active construction operations
software:
  - Drone flight planning and photogrammetry software
  - Building information modeling software (for handoff to BIM/VDC)
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, plus an FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot
    Certificate, legally required to fly a drone commercially in the
    U.S., including for construction site work; there is no substitute or
    workaround for this one
  preferred: Background in surveying, photography, or GIS
  notNecessary: A four-year degree; many drone specialists come from a surveying, photography, or field construction background and add the Part 107 certificate on top
progression:
  - Drone Pilot / Operator
  - Drone/UAV Specialist
  - UAV Program Manager (for companies running fleets across multiple projects)
