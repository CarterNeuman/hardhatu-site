whatIs: >
  An Architect designs buildings: translating an owner's program, budget, and
  site into a design that satisfies building codes, zoning, and the client's
  intent, then producing the construction documents a contractor builds from.
  On most projects, architecture work starts well before a General Contractor
  is even hired, and the Architect's drawings are what a Project Engineer,
  Superintendent, and every trade on site ultimately build against. Unlike
  most careers on this site, architecture is a licensed profession: calling
  yourself an architect and stamping drawings for permit legally requires a
  state license.
whatTheyDo:
  - Develop a design concept that satisfies an owner's program, budget, and site constraints
  - Produce construction documents (drawings and specifications) a contractor builds from
  - Coordinate structural, mechanical, electrical, and civil engineering consultants into one cohesive design
  - Navigate zoning, building code, and permitting requirements with the local jurisdiction
  - Review submittals and respond to RFIs during construction to clarify design intent
  - Visit the site during construction to confirm the work matches the design (contract administration)

education:
  required: >
    A professional degree from a NAAB-accredited architecture program (a
    5-year Bachelor of Architecture or a 2-3 year Master of Architecture after
    a non-accredited bachelor's), then the NCARB Architectural Experience
    Program (AXP), a structured record of supervised work experience,
    completed alongside or after the degree, then passing all divisions of the
    Architect Registration Examination (ARE). A state architecture license is
    legally required to stamp drawings submitted for permit.
  notNecessary: A background in construction trades; most architects come directly from an accredited design program
