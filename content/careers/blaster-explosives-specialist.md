whatIs: >
  A Blaster, also called an Explosives Specialist, plans and executes the
  controlled use of explosives to break rock during excavation, clear material
  at a quarry or mine, or bring down a structure during demolition. The work
  is licensed and tightly regulated: most states require a separate blaster's
  license on top of any employer training, and federal rules under the Bureau
  of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) govern the purchase,
  storage, and transport of explosive material. A blaster calculates the
  explosive charge, drilling pattern, and timing sequence needed to achieve a
  specific result (breaking rock to a usable size, or collapsing a structure
  in a controlled direction) while keeping flyrock, vibration, and air blast
  within limits that protect workers, nearby structures, and the public.
whatTheyDo:
  - Calculate drilling patterns, charge weights, and detonation sequencing for a specific blast
  - Load and wire explosive charges according to the blast plan
  - Monitor and control vibration, air blast, and flyrock to stay within permitted limits
  - Coordinate site evacuation and exclusion zones before a blast
  - Maintain required explosives storage, transport, and inventory records under ATF and state rules
  - Work with a Civil Engineer or Demolition Supervisor to plan blasting as part of a larger excavation or demolition sequence
