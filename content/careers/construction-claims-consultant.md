title: Construction Claims Consultant
category: "Consultants & Advisory"
tagline: Gets brought in when a project dispute is heading toward, or already in, a legal claim, to untangle what actually happened and what it's worth.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Construction Claims Consultant analyzes construction disputes,
  delay claims, cost overrun claims, defect claims, for whichever party
  hired them (owner, contractor, or their attorneys), then builds the
  documented, defensible position that side takes into negotiation,
  mediation, or litigation. Unlike an Insurance Adjuster, who works
  specifically within insurance claims, a Claims Consultant works across
  any construction dispute regardless of whether insurance is involved,
  often serving as an expert witness when a case goes to litigation.
whatTheyDo:
  - Analyze project schedules to determine the cause and length of a delay and who's responsible for it
  - Review cost documentation to substantiate or challenge a cost overrun claim
  - Reconstruct a timeline of project events from RFIs, change orders, daily reports, and correspondence
  - Prepare detailed claim reports and exhibits used in negotiation, mediation, or litigation
  - Serve as an expert witness, testifying to findings in depositions or at trial
  - Advise clients on claim strategy and the strength of their position before committing to litigation
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review a contractor's daily reports to build a timeline for a delay claim
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Analyze the project's critical path schedule to determine what actually drove the delay
  - time: "11:30 AM"
    activity: Draft a section of a claim report quantifying additional costs caused by the delay
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Call with the client's attorney to discuss claim strategy ahead of mediation
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Review a prior expert report for a case heading toward deposition
whereTheyWork:
  - Office
  - Occasional site visits and depositions/court appearances
skills:
  - Schedule analysis (critical path method)
  - Cost analysis and documentation review
  - Technical writing for claim reports
  - Reading contracts and construction documentation closely
  - Communicating complex technical findings clearly to attorneys and juries
software:
  - Scheduling software (e.g. Primavera P6, Microsoft Project)
  - Bluebeam
education:
  required: Bachelor's degree in construction management, engineering, or a related field
  preferred: Several years of construction project management or scheduling experience before moving into consulting
  helpful: PMP or CCM certification, PMI Scheduling Professional (PMI-SP) credential
  notNecessary: A law degree; claims consultants provide technical/schedule/cost expertise, not legal representation, though they work closely with attorneys
progression:
  - Project Engineer or Scheduler
  - Claims Analyst
  - Construction Claims Consultant
  - Senior Consultant or Expert Witness (Construction Litigation)
hirerTypes:

  - Law Firms (construction litigation practices)
  - Owners and General Contractors (directly, for major disputes)
