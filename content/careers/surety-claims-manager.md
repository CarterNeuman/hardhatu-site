whatIs: >
  A Surety Claims Manager investigates and resolves claims made against a
  performance or payment bond after a contractor defaults or a subcontractor
  goes unpaid. Where a Surety Bond Underwriter decides upfront whether to
  issue a bond and how much bonding capacity to extend, a Surety Claims
  Manager gets involved only once something has gone wrong: confirming whether
  a default actually occurred under the bond's terms, calculating the surety's
  cost-to-complete exposure, and deciding whether to finance the original
  contractor through completion, hire a replacement contractor, or pay out a
  settlement. Many surety claims are technical and legally complex enough that
  this role is filled by people with a construction management, engineering,
  or legal background rather than a traditional insurance-claims path.
whatTheyDo:
  - Investigate reported bond claims to determine whether a contractor actually defaulted under the bond's terms
  - Perform cost-to-complete analyses to estimate the surety's financial exposure on a defaulted project
  - Decide whether to finance the original contractor, hire a completion contractor, or negotiate a settlement
  - Review construction documents, schedules, and payment records to evaluate contested claims
  - Coordinate with construction attorneys and consulting engineers on disputed or litigated claims
  - Negotiate directly with obligees (project owners) and claimants toward claim resolution
