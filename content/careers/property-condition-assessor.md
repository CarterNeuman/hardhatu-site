whatIs: >
  A Property Condition Assessor inspects a building's physical condition,
  structure, roof, building systems, and site, as part of a real estate
  transaction's due diligence, then writes a Property Condition Assessment
  (PCA) report that identifies existing deficiencies and forecasts future
  capital repair and replacement costs. Lenders, institutional investors, and
  property owners commission these assessments, most often following the ASTM
  E2018 standard, before closing on a purchase, originating a loan, or
  planning a capital budget. This work is distinct from a home inspection:
  PCAs cover commercial, multifamily, and industrial properties, follow a
  formal industry standard, and feed directly into a transaction's financial
  underwriting rather than a single buyer's walk-through.
whatTheyDo:
  - Conduct on-site physical inspections of a commercial or multifamily property's structure, systems, and site
  - Review available property documentation, maintenance records, and prior reports before a site visit
  - Identify existing physical deficiencies and immediate repair needs
  - Forecast capital expenditure needs over a multi-year reserve period based on building component life expectancy
  - Write Property Condition Assessment (PCA) reports to ASTM E2018 standards for lenders and investors
  - Coordinate with specialists (roofing, MEP, structural) on more complex or higher-risk properties
