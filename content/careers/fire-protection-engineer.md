whatIs: >
  A Fire Protection Engineer designs and reviews the systems that protect a
  building and its occupants from fire: automatic sprinklers, fire alarms,
  smoke control, fire-rated construction assemblies, and egress design. On a
  typical project, fire protection work runs alongside the Architect's and MEP
  Engineer's design, since fire protection decisions affect ceiling heights,
  wall assemblies, and the mechanical system's smoke-control duties, and the
  Fire Protection Engineer's drawings become part of the permit set a fire
  marshal or building official reviews before construction. Independent
  practice and stamping fire protection drawings for permit in most
  jurisdictions requires a Professional Engineer (PE) license in fire
  protection engineering.
whatTheyDo:
  - Design automatic sprinkler, standpipe, and fire alarm systems for new and renovated buildings
  - Perform fire and smoke modeling to design smoke-control systems for atriums and high-rise buildings
  - Review architectural and MEP drawings for code compliance with NFPA standards and the applicable building code
  - Coordinate with architects and MEP engineers on how fire-rated assemblies and fire protection equipment affect the overall design
  - Prepare life-safety and egress analyses for complex or high-occupancy buildings
  - Respond to RFIs during construction and inspect installed systems for code compliance
