title: "Human Resources Manager (Construction)"
category: "Business"
tagline: Handles hiring, employee relations, and compliance for a construction company's workforce, with construction-specific wrinkles a general HR role doesn't have to deal with.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Human Resources Manager in construction runs the same core HR
  functions as any industry: recruiting, onboarding, benefits, employee
  relations, compliance, but with construction-specific complications
  layered on top: certified payroll requirements on public projects,
  managing a workforce spread across multiple active jobsites, union labor
  agreements at some companies, and high safety-incident stakes that touch
  workers' compensation and OSHA recordkeeping. No HR-specific certification
  is legally required to do this job; professional credentials exist, but
  they're optional recognition, not a licensing requirement the way a
  journeyman trade license is.
whatTheyDo:
  - Recruit and hire for both office and field positions, often across multiple active jobsites
  - Manage employee relations issues and ensure compliance with labor law
  - Administer benefits and payroll, including certified payroll reporting requirements on public/prevailing-wage projects
  - Support safety and workers' compensation processes alongside the safety team
  - Manage union labor agreements and related compliance, at companies that use union labor
  - Develop policies and training that account for a largely field-based, multi-site workforce
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review open field positions and coordinate with project teams on hiring needs
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Resolve an employee relations issue raised by a jobsite supervisor
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Prepare certified payroll documentation for a prevailing-wage public project
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Review a workers' compensation claim alongside the safety manager
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Update onboarding materials for new field hires starting the following week
whereTheyWork:
  - Office
  - Occasional jobsite visits for hiring events or employee relations issues
skills:
  - Employment law and compliance knowledge
  - Recruiting across office and field roles
  - Conflict resolution
  - Benefits and payroll administration
  - Communication across a dispersed, multi-site workforce
software:
  - Human resources information system (HRIS) and payroll software
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent; no certification or license is
    legally required to work in construction HR
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in human resources, business, or a related field
  helpful: Professional HR credentials such as SHRM-CP or PHR are widely respected in the field but remain optional, not a legal requirement
  notNecessary: A construction-specific degree; most construction HR managers come from a general HR background and pick up the industry-specific parts (certified payroll, union agreements) on the job
