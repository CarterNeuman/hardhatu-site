title: Demolition Supervisor
category: "Specialized Construction"
tagline: Runs the high-risk process of taking a structure down safely, a distinct specialty most newcomers don't realize is its own career track until they see the certification behind it.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Demolition Supervisor plans and oversees the safe demolition of
  structures, sequencing the work, managing hazardous material concerns
  alongside an Environmental/Remediation Specialist, and directing the
  crews and equipment that bring a building down without endangering
  workers, neighboring structures, or the public. Demolition carries
  enough distinct risk, structural collapse sequencing, utility
  disconnection, hazardous material exposure, that the industry built its
  own credential for it: the National Demolition Association's Certified
  Demolition Supervisor (CDS), a newer, rigorous exam-based certification
  that only launched in the past few years.
whatTheyDo:
  - Plan the sequence and method for safely demolishing a structure
  - Coordinate utility disconnection before demolition begins
  - Supervise demolition crews and heavy equipment operators during the work
  - Coordinate with environmental specialists on hazardous material abatement ahead of demolition
  - Monitor for structural instability and adjust the demolition plan as conditions change
  - Ensure demolition debris is properly sorted, recycled, and disposed of
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:00 AM"
    activity: Walk the site to confirm utility disconnections are complete before work begins
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Brief the crew on the day's demolition sequence and safety requirements
  - time: "9:00 AM"
    activity: Supervise mechanical demolition of a structural section, monitoring for instability
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Coordinate with the environmental specialist on a newly discovered hazardous material
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Inspect debris sorting and confirm recycling and disposal documentation
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
skills:
  - Demolition sequencing and structural risk assessment
  - Heavy equipment and crew supervision
  - Hazardous material awareness
  - Jobsite safety enforcement
  - Coordination with utility companies and environmental specialists
software:
  - Generally none; this is a hands-on field role
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, plus significant field demolition experience; no license is legally required nationwide, though local jurisdictions sometimes require permits or licensing for demolition contractors
  preferred: >
    The National Demolition Association's Certified Demolition
    Supervisor (CDS) credential, a newer exam-based certification
    requiring five years of hands-on demolition experience, OSHA 30,
    8-hour fall protection training, asbestos or HAZWOPER awareness
    training, and current First Aid/CPR/AED certification before sitting
    for a 110-question exam
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; this role is built on hands-on demolition and safety experience
progression:
  - Demolition Laborer or Equipment Operator
  - Demolition Foreman
  - Demolition Supervisor
  - Demolition Superintendent or Demolition Contractor
hirerTypes:
  - Demolition Subcontractors
  - General Contractors (on projects involving significant demolition)
  - Environmental Remediation Firms (on combined abatement-and-demolition projects)
