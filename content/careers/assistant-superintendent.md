id: career-assistant-superintendent
title: Assistant Superintendent
category: "Project & Operations"
tagline: Runs the field alongside the Superintendent, the natural next step for someone who wants to manage a jobsite rather than move into the office.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  An Assistant Superintendent supports the Superintendent in running the
  day-to-day field operations of a construction site, supervising crews,
  coordinating subcontractors, and enforcing safety and quality on a
  portion of the project while learning to run the whole thing. It's the
  standard field-side path into project leadership: someone who's spent
  years in a trade or as a laborer and wants to run the jobsite rather
  than move into project management's office-based track typically comes
  up through this role rather than through an Assistant Project Manager
  position.
whatTheyDo:
  - Supervise subcontractor crews on a defined portion of the project, verifying work matches drawings and specifications
  - Coordinate daily trade sequencing to keep work areas clear and conflict-free
  - Enforce jobsite safety requirements and lead toolbox talks
  - Track daily progress and prepare daily reports
  - Walk completed work with subcontractors to identify and resolve punch list items
  - Step in to run the full site when the Superintendent is offsite or on another project
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:00 AM"
    activity: Walk the site before crews arrive to check yesterday's work and flag any issues
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Lead a toolbox talk and confirm the day's trade sequencing with subcontractor foremen
  - time: "9:00 AM"
    activity: Supervise an active work area, checking installed work against drawings
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Resolve a sequencing conflict between two trades trying to work the same space
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Complete the daily report and update the Superintendent on progress and any open issues
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
skills:
  - Reading construction drawings and specifications
  - Crew and subcontractor supervision
  - Scheduling and sequencing coordination
  - Jobsite safety enforcement
  - Problem-solving under time pressure
software:
  - Procore
  - Bluebeam
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, plus field experience (commonly several years as a tradesperson, laborer, or foreman); no license is legally required for this role
  preferred: Associate's or bachelor's degree in construction management (helpful for advancement, not required to enter the role)
  helpful: OSHA 30 certification, widely expected for anyone in a field supervisory role even where it isn't a legal requirement
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; this role is typically earned through field experience rather than schooling
progression:
  - Foreman or Lead Tradesperson
  - Assistant Superintendent
  - Superintendent
  - General Superintendent or Operations Manager
