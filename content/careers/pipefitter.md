whatIs: >
  A Pipefitter, also called a Steamfitter, installs and welds piping
  systems that carry steam, high-pressure fluids, and process materials,
  work found in commercial HVAC systems, hospitals, power plants, and
  industrial facilities. It's a separate trade from a Plumber, who
  focuses on domestic water, drainage, and fixtures; pipefitters work
  with higher pressures and a heavier emphasis on welding, and the two
  trades often work side by side on the same commercial mechanical
  systems without doing each other's scope.
whatTheyDo:
  - Read piping and instrumentation drawings to plan pipe routing and fabrication
  - Cut, thread, and weld pipe to connect mechanical systems
  - Install and pressure-test high-pressure steam, hydronic, and process piping systems
  - Install fire sprinkler mains and standpipe systems on larger commercial projects
  - Fabricate pipe assemblies in a shop ahead of jobsite installation
  - Coordinate with HVAC technicians and sheet metal workers on mechanical room layout

software:

education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, then a registered apprenticeship
    through the United Association (UA), typically five years combining
    roughly 2,000 hours of paid on-the-job training per year with about
    216 hours of annual classroom instruction; welding certification
    (commonly shielded metal arc and gas tungsten arc welding) is built
    into the later years of the standard UA curriculum rather than being
    a separate add-on
  helpful: >
    OSHA 10 or 30 certification (not a nationwide legal requirement,
    though several states, including New York, Massachusetts,
    Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri, and Rhode Island, legally require it
    for workers on public construction projects), and an AWS welding
    certification for pipefitters who want to specialize further in
    pressure-pipe welding
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; the apprenticeship-to-journeyman route is the standard path into the trade
