title: MEP Engineer
category: "Technology & Design"
tagline: Designs the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems that make a building livable, work behind three of the site's most concept-dense trades with no design-side career representing any of them until now.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  An MEP Engineer designs the mechanical (HVAC), electrical, and plumbing
  systems inside a building, sizing equipment, laying out distribution,
  and producing the drawings that HVAC Technicians, Electricians,
  Plumbers, Sheet Metal Workers, and Pipefitters actually build from.
  Some MEP engineers specialize in just one discipline (mechanical,
  electrical, or plumbing), while others work across all three,
  especially at smaller firms. Like civil and structural engineering,
  it's a licensed profession, and coordinating three interdependent
  systems in the same limited ceiling space is a big part of the job.
whatTheyDo:
  - Design HVAC systems, sizing equipment and laying out ductwork and piping
  - Design electrical distribution systems, from service entrance to branch circuits
  - Design plumbing systems, from domestic water supply to sanitary and storm drainage
  - Produce MEP drawings and specifications, and coordinate them against each other and the structural and architectural drawings
  - Review shop drawings and submittals from mechanical, electrical, and plumbing subcontractors
  - Respond to RFIs and field coordination issues during construction
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review a clash detection report flagging a conflict between ductwork and a structural beam
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Size HVAC equipment for a building addition based on updated load calculations
  - time: "11:30 AM"
    activity: Review electrical panel schedules and circuit submittals from the electrical subcontractor
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Coordinate plumbing riser locations with the structural engineer's floor plan
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Respond to an RFI about a mechanical room clearance issue discovered in the field
whereTheyWork:
  - Office (MEP or multidisciplinary design firm)
  - Occasional site visits during construction for system commissioning and observation
skills:
  - Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing system design
  - Load calculations and equipment sizing
  - Reading and producing MEP drawings
  - Coordination across disciplines (clash detection, shared space conflicts)
  - Understanding of mechanical, electrical, and plumbing codes
software:
  - Building information modeling software (e.g. Revit)
  - Load calculation and energy modeling software
education:
  required: >
    A bachelor's degree in mechanical or electrical engineering (or a
    related field) from an ABET-accredited program, then passing the
    Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam, then typically four years of
    progressive experience under a licensed PE before sitting for a
    discipline-specific Professional Engineer (PE) exam (mechanical or
    electrical). A PE license is legally required to stamp MEP design
    drawings.
  notNecessary: A background in the HVAC, electrical, or plumbing trades; most MEP engineers come directly from an engineering degree program, though some do cross over from a trade background
progression:
  - Engineer in Training (EIT)
  - MEP Engineer
  - Senior MEP Engineer or Project Engineer (design firm side)
  - Principal Engineer or Engineering Manager
hirerTypes:
  - MEP and Multidisciplinary Engineering Firms
  - General Contractors (design-build firms keep in-house MEP engineers)
  - Developers and Owners (for system feasibility and energy analysis)
