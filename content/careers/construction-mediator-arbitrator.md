whatIs: >
  A Construction Mediator or Arbitrator is the neutral third party who
  actually resolves a construction dispute outside of court: a mediator
  facilitates a negotiated settlement between the parties without deciding the
  outcome, while an arbitrator hears evidence and issues a binding decision,
  much like a judge, but in a private, contractually agreed-upon process.
  Where a Construction Claims Consultant builds one side's technical case and
  a Construction Attorney argues it, a mediator or arbitrator sits in the
  middle, impartial to both sides. This is almost never a first career: nearly
  everyone serving in this role spent 15-20+ years first as a construction
  attorney, licensed engineer or architect, or senior project executive, then
  trained and was accepted onto a panel (most commonly through the American
  Arbitration Association's Construction Panel) once their subject-matter
  expertise and reputation were established.
whatTheyDo:
  - Review submitted evidence, expert reports, and legal briefs from both sides of a dispute
  - Facilitate settlement discussions between disputing parties (mediation) without imposing a decision
  - Conduct evidentiary hearings, question witnesses, and weigh expert testimony (arbitration)
  - Issue a binding arbitration award, or help parties reach a voluntary settlement (mediation)
  - Manage case scheduling, procedural rulings, and communication between parties and counsel
  - Maintain the training, continuing education, and panel credentials required to stay active on a roster like the AAA's

  - Clear, well-reasoned written decisions (arbitration awards)
  - Managing a multi-party process without favoring either side

education:
  required: >
    No specific degree is required to become a mediator or arbitrator itself,
    but nearly every construction neutral built their underlying subject-
    matter credibility first, most often as a licensed attorney, Professional
    Engineer (PE), Registered Architect (RA), or Certified Construction
    Manager (CCM) with 15-20+ years of construction-specific experience.
    Formal mediator or arbitrator training programs exist and are typically
    required for admission onto a panel like the American Arbitration
    Association's Construction Panel, which also requires nomination and a
    demonstrated track record in the field.
  notNecessary: >
