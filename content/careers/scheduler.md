title: Scheduler
category: "Project & Operations"
tagline: Builds and maintains the project schedule everyone else's work depends on, the person who can tell you exactly what happens if today's delay ripples forward.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Scheduler builds, updates, and analyzes a construction project's
  schedule: sequencing thousands of activities, identifying the critical
  path (the chain of tasks that directly controls the project's finish
  date), and modeling the impact of delays or changes before they happen.
  On smaller projects, scheduling often falls to the project manager or
  superintendent alongside their other responsibilities; larger and more
  complex projects bring in a dedicated Scheduler instead.
whatTheyDo:
  - Build the project schedule from scope, sequencing logic, and durations provided by the project team
  - Identify and monitor the critical path, the activities that directly control the finish date
  - Update the schedule regularly with actual progress and re-forecast the completion date
  - Model the schedule impact of proposed changes, delays, or acceleration options
  - Prepare schedule narratives and reports for project leadership and the owner
  - Support delay analysis when disputes arise over who caused a schedule slip
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Update the schedule with the previous week's actual progress
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Review the updated critical path with the project manager and superintendent
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Model the schedule impact of a proposed design change
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Prepare a schedule narrative for the monthly owner report
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Meet with a subcontractor to align their schedule with the master schedule
whereTheyWork:
  - Office
  - Occasional site visits to verify actual progress against the schedule
skills:
  - Critical path method (CPM) scheduling
  - Scheduling software proficiency
  - Analytical thinking and delay analysis
  - Communication across project stakeholders
  - Attention to detail
software:
  - Scheduling software (e.g. Primavera P6, Microsoft Project)
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent; no certification or license is legally required to work as a construction scheduler
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in construction management, civil engineering, or a related field
  helpful: PMI Scheduling Professional (PMI-SP) or AACE International's Planning & Scheduling Professional (PSP) credential
  notNecessary: A scheduling-specific degree; many schedulers move into the role from project engineering or estimating
progression:

  - Scheduling Manager or Director of Project Controls
