title: Sheet Metal Worker
category: "Field & Trades"
tagline: Fabricates and installs the ductwork, flashing, and metal roofing systems that move air through a building and keep water out of it.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Sheet Metal Worker fabricates and installs sheet metal components on a
  project, most commonly HVAC ductwork, but also roofing panels,
  flashing, and architectural metal. A lot of the fabrication happens in
  a shop ahead of time, cutting and forming metal to exact duct-run
  dimensions from drawings, before the finished pieces move to the
  jobsite for installation and connection to air handling equipment. The
  trade has its own apprenticeship and union (SMART) distinct from an
  HVAC Technician, who installs and services the equipment those ducts
  connect to rather than fabricating the ductwork itself.
whatTheyDo:
  - Fabricate ductwork and sheet metal components in a shop from drawing dimensions
  - Install ductwork runs on the jobsite and connect them to air handling equipment
  - Install metal roofing panels, flashing, and architectural sheet metal
  - Seal and insulate ductwork joints to prevent air leakage
  - Read HVAC drawings and shop drawings to plan duct runs and fabrication sequencing
  - Coordinate with HVAC technicians and other trades on routing through ceilings and walls
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:00 AM"
    activity: Review the day's duct fabrication list and set up the shop's cutting and forming equipment
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Fabricate a run of rectangular ductwork to the dimensions on the shop drawing
  - time: "10:00 AM"
    activity: Move to the jobsite to install a completed duct run above a finished ceiling grid
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Seal ductwork joints and connections to meet the project's air-leakage requirements
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Coordinate with the HVAC technician on final connections between ductwork and a rooftop unit
whereTheyWork:
  - Shop (fabrication)
  - Jobsite (installation)
skills:
  - Reading HVAC and sheet metal shop drawings
  - Metal cutting, forming, and fabrication
  - Precise measurement and layout
  - Sealing and insulating ductwork
  - Working safely at height for duct and roofing installation
software:
  - Generally none; this is a hands-on field trade
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, then a registered apprenticeship through the SMART union, typically around four years combining paid on-the-job training with roughly 160 hours of classroom instruction per year; sheet metal work isn't separately state-licensed in most places
  helpful: >
    OSHA 10 or 30 certification (not a nationwide legal requirement,
    though several states, including New York, Massachusetts,
    Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri, and Rhode Island, legally require it
    for workers on public construction projects)
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; the apprenticeship-to-journeyman route is the standard path into the trade
progression:
  - Apprentice Sheet Metal Worker
  - Journeyman Sheet Metal Worker
  - Shop or Field Foreman
  - Sheet Metal Contractor or Superintendent
hirerTypes:
  - Sheet Metal and HVAC Subcontractors
  - Mechanical Contractors
  - Roofing Contractors (for metal roofing specialty work)
