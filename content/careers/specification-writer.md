whatIs: >
  A Specification Writer (also called a specifier) writes and coordinates the
  written technical specifications that accompany a project's drawings,
  together making up the construction documents a contractor bids and builds
  from. Where a drawing shows what and where, a specification says exactly
  which products, materials, installation methods, and quality standards
  apply, organized by CSI MasterFormat division. Working closely with the
  Architect and engineering consultants, a specifier keeps the project manual
  coordinated with the evolving drawings, resolving conflicts between what's
  drawn and what's specified before they become an expensive RFI during
  construction.
whatTheyDo:
  - Write and edit technical specifications organized by CSI MasterFormat division
  - Coordinate specifications with the architect's and engineers' evolving drawings to catch conflicts early
  - Research and specify products, materials, and installation standards appropriate to a project
  - Review manufacturer data and substitution requests for compliance with the specified standard
  - Maintain and update a firm's master specification library across projects
  - Respond to RFIs and submittal reviews that involve interpreting specification requirements

education:

    A bachelor's degree in architecture, engineering, or construction

    experience before specializing into specification writing. The CSI
    Construction Documents Technologist (CDT) certification is a common entry
    credential, and the Certified Construction Specifier (CCS) certification,
    also from the Construction Specifications Institute, is the recognized
    advanced credential for this specialty.
  notNecessary: >
