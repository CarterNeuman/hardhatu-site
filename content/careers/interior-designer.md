whatIs: >
  An Interior Designer designs the interior spaces within a building's
  architectural shell: space planning, finishes, lighting, furniture, and
  fixtures, balancing a client's aesthetic and functional goals against
  building codes, accessibility requirements, and life-safety egress rules. On
  a commercial project, interior design work typically starts once an
  Architect has set the building's shell and structure, and the Interior
  Designer's drawings and specifications become part of what a General
  Contractor and finish trades build from. Licensure requirements vary
  significantly by state; many states require a license (via the NCIDQ exam)
  to use the title 'interior designer' or to stamp certain construction
  documents, while others have no such requirement.
whatTheyDo:
  - Develop interior space plans, finish selections, and furniture layouts that satisfy a client's program and budget
  - Specify finishes, fixtures, furniture, and lighting, and prepare the documents a contractor builds from
  - Verify designs meet building code, accessibility (ADA), and life-safety egress requirements
  - Coordinate with architects and MEP engineers on how interior layouts affect lighting, HVAC, and electrical plans
  - Present design concepts and budgets to clients for approval
  - Visit the site during construction to confirm finishes and installations match the design intent
