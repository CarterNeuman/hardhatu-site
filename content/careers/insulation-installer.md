whatIs: >
  An Insulation Installer places batt, blown-in, rigid foam, or spray foam
  insulation into walls, ceilings, floors, and attics so a building holds
  heat in winter and keeps it out in summer. BLS tracks this work as two
  related occupations: Insulation Workers, Floor, Ceiling, and Wall
  (building insulation) and Insulation Workers, Mechanical (wrapping pipes,
  ducts, and mechanical equipment, usually a higher-paid specialty).
  Residential batt and blown-in insulation generally requires no personal
  license, though a handful of states require a specialty contractor
  license at the business level, and installers working with spray
  polyurethane foam often need manufacturer-specific certification. The
  work is easy to do badly in ways that don't show up until an energy bill
  or a mold problem arrives, since gaps, compression, and missing vapor
  barriers are invisible once drywall or siding covers the wall.
whatTheyDo:
  - Install batt, blown-in, rigid foam, or spray foam insulation to the R-value called for in the plans
  - Install vapor barriers and air-sealing materials alongside insulation to control moisture and drafts
  - Wrap ductwork, pipes, and mechanical equipment with insulation to reduce energy loss (mechanical insulation specialty)
  - Coordinate with the framer or HVAC trades so insulation goes in after rough framing and ductwork but before drywall closes the wall
  - Use a blower or spray rig for blown-in and spray foam applications, following manufacturer mixing and thickness specifications
  - Perform blower-door or infrared checks on some jobs to confirm there are no major gaps before the wall is closed
typicalDay:
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Load insulation materials and equipment, review which rooms passed framing and HVAC rough-in inspection
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Install batt insulation in exterior wall cavities, fitting tightly around outlets and plumbing penetrations
  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Run the blower hose into the attic and blow in loose-fill insulation to the specified depth
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Install a vapor barrier and seal penetrations before the area is cleared for drywall
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Walk the completed area with the superintendent to confirm coverage before it's closed in
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite (inside unfinished walls, attics, and crawlspaces; mechanical insulation is often inside mechanical rooms and along ductwork)
skills:
  - Attention to detail (a gap or compressed section loses most of its insulating value)
  - Comfort working in tight, hot, or dusty spaces like attics and crawlspaces
  - Reading plans for required R-values by wall and ceiling assembly
  - Basic math for calculating material coverage and blown-in depth
  - Physical stamina for repetitive overhead and crouching work
software:
  - Generally none, since this is a hands-on field trade; some mechanical insulation contractors use takeoff software for bidding
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent; most installers learn on the job with no personal license required for standard insulation work
  preferred: A formal insulation apprenticeship or manufacturer training for spray foam and mechanical insulation systems
  helpful: OSHA 10 certification, manufacturer certification for spray polyurethane foam (SPFA-affiliated training), asbestos-awareness training for renovation work in older buildings
  notNecessary: A college degree, since this trade is learned almost entirely on the job
