whatIs: >
  A Tenant Improvement (TI) Construction Manager, also called an Interior
  Construction Manager, runs the build-out or renovation of interior
  commercial space, office suites, retail stores, restaurants, medical suites,
  inside a building that already exists, and often while neighboring tenants
  remain occupied and operating. The work looks like general construction
  project management, budget, schedule, subcontractors, but with its own
  distinct pressures: TI projects run on compressed timelines (often tied to a
  tenant's lease start date), require constant coordination with a building's
  landlord and property management team, and demand careful control of noise,
  dust, and access so ongoing business in the rest of the building isn't
  disrupted. A general contractor that specializes exclusively in tenant
  improvement work is a common and recognized business model in commercial
  real estate markets.
whatTheyDo:
  - Manage interior build-out schedules tied to a tenant's lease commencement date
  - Coordinate construction access, noise, and dust control with a building's landlord and property management
  - Manage subcontractors across interior trades (drywall, electrical, HVAC, flooring, millwork)
  - Track the [[concept-tenant-improvement-allowance|tenant improvement allowance]] budget against actual costs
  - Coordinate permit and inspection timing with the local building department for occupied-building work
  - Walk the completed space with the tenant and landlord for final punch list and sign-off
