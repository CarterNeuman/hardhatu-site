whatIs: >
  A Project Engineer (PE, not to be confused with a licensed Professional
  Engineer) supports a Project Manager by owning the day-to-day
  documentation and coordination that keeps a project moving: submittals,
  RFIs, drawing logs, and subcontractor coordination. It's usually the
  entry-level role on the project management track, and the fastest way
  for someone without trade experience to learn how a project actually
  runs before stepping into the PM seat themselves.
whatTheyDo:
  - Track and log submittals, making sure the right subcontractor submits the right product data on time
  - Draft and route RFIs from the field to the architect or engineer, then log and distribute the response
  - Maintain the current drawing set and distribute revisions to the field and subcontractors
  - Attend site walks and take notes on progress and open issues for the project manager
  - Help assemble closeout documentation (warranties, as-builts, O&M manuals) near the end of a project
  - Research materials, code requirements, or subcontractor options the PM needs answers on
typicalDay:
  - time: "7:30 AM"

  - time: "9:00 AM"

  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Walk the site with the PM and superintendent, taking notes on progress
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Distribute a drawing revision to the affected subcontractors
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Research a substitute material a subcontractor proposed and summarize it for the PM's approval
