tagline: Designs the grading, drainage, and utility systems that make a site buildable before anyone can pour a foundation, the single biggest missing career on a site full of concepts that assume this role exists.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Civil Engineer designs the site-level systems a building depends on
  before construction can start: grading and earthwork, storm and
  sanitary drainage, water and utility service, and paving. On a typical
  project, civil engineering work happens early, during design and
  permitting, producing the site plans and utility drawings that a Site
  Superintendent, Surveyor, and Heavy Civil Estimator all work from once
  construction begins. Unlike most careers on this site, civil
  engineering is a licensed profession: stamping site design drawings
  legally requires a Professional Engineer (PE) license.
whatTheyDo:
  - Design grading and drainage plans that route stormwater safely off a site
  - Design water, sanitary sewer, and storm utility systems and their connections to public infrastructure
  - Prepare site plans and civil drawings for permitting and construction
  - Perform hydrology and hydraulic calculations to size drainage and detention systems
  - Coordinate with geotechnical engineers, surveyors, and architects on site constraints
  - Review RFIs and field conditions during construction that affect the civil design
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review a geotechnical report to confirm site soil assumptions for a grading design
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Run hydraulic calculations to size a stormwater detention pond for a new site plan
  - time: "11:30 AM"
    activity: Coordinate with a surveyor on updated topographic data for an active design
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Respond to an RFI from the field about an unexpected utility conflict
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Review permit comments from the local jurisdiction and revise the site plan accordingly
whereTheyWork:
  - Office (design firm or engineering department)
  - Occasional site visits during design and construction
skills:
  - Site grading and drainage design
  - Hydrology and hydraulic calculations
  - Reading and producing civil construction drawings
  - Understanding of local permitting and utility standards
  - Coordination across design and construction teams
software:
  - Computer-aided design and civil engineering software (e.g. AutoCAD Civil 3D)
education:
  required: >
    A bachelor's degree in civil engineering from an ABET-accredited
    program, then passing the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam to
    become an Engineer in Training, then typically four years of
    progressive experience under a licensed PE before sitting for the
    Professional Engineer (PE) exam. A PE license is legally required to
    stamp civil design drawings submitted for permit.
  notNecessary: A background in construction trades; most civil engineers come directly from an engineering degree program
progression:
  - Engineer in Training (EIT)
  - Civil Engineer
  - Senior Civil Engineer or Project Engineer (design firm side)
  - Principal Engineer or Engineering Manager
hirerTypes:
  - Civil Engineering and Design Firms
  - General Contractors (some larger firms keep in-house civil engineers)
  - Developers and Owners (for site feasibility work)
