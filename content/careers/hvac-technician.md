whatIs: >
  An HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning) Technician installs,
  maintains, and repairs the systems that heat, cool, and ventilate a
  building: furnaces, rooftop units, ductwork, and increasingly building
  automation controls. Unlike most trades, working with the refrigerants
  used in most HVAC equipment isn't optional to certify for: federal law
  (the Clean Air Act's Section 608 rule) requires anyone who handles
  regulated refrigerants to hold an EPA Section 608 certification, so this
  is one credential nearly every HVAC technician earns early on, not just a
  nice-to-have.
whatTheyDo:
  - Install ductwork, rooftop units, furnaces, and air handlers according to mechanical drawings
  - Charge, recover, and service refrigerant in cooling systems, following EPA-regulated handling procedures
  - Diagnose and repair failures in heating, cooling, and ventilation equipment
  - Test and balance airflow through a finished system before it's turned over
  - Read mechanical drawings and manufacturer specs to plan installation and troubleshoot problems
  - Coordinate with electricians and plumbers so rough-in work doesn't conflict in shared wall or ceiling space

education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, then trade school or a registered
    apprenticeship (typically 3-5 years); an EPA Section 608 certification
    is legally required before working with regulated refrigerants, and
    a Journeyman HVAC license is legally required by most
    states/municipalities before working unsupervised
  helpful: >
    NATE (North American Technician Excellence) certification, and OSHA
    10 or 30 (not a nationwide legal requirement, though several states,
    including New York, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri,
    and Rhode Island, legally require it for workers on public
    construction projects)
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree isn't part of this path; trade school or apprenticeship leading to licensure is the standard route
