title: Estimator
category: "Preconstruction & Estimating"
tagline: Prices the job before anyone breaks ground, so the bid actually makes money instead of just winning the work.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  An Estimator figures out what a project will cost before construction
  starts, so the company can submit a competitive bid that still leaves room
  for profit. That means measuring quantities directly off the drawings (a
  "takeoff": how much concrete, how many linear feet of wall, how many
  doors), then pricing each one using current material costs, labor rates,
  and subcontractor quotes.
whatTheyDo:
  - Perform quantity takeoffs from architectural and engineering drawings
  - Solicit and compare pricing quotes from subcontractors and material suppliers
  - Build a complete bid, choosing lump sum, unit price, or cost-plus pricing depending on what the owner asked for
  - Track historical unit costs (cost per square foot, per linear foot, etc.) to sanity-check new bids
  - Identify scope gaps or ambiguities in the drawings before they become expensive change orders later
  - Hand off the winning bid's assumptions to the project manager so the budget they inherit is accurate
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review a new project's drawings and specs to scope out what needs a takeoff
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Measure quantities off the drawings using takeoff software
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Call two subcontractors to clarify quotes that came in with different scope assumptions
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Build the bid summary, checking the total against a similar recently-completed project
  - time: "4:00 PM"
    activity: Walk the near-final bid with a senior estimator before it goes out the door
