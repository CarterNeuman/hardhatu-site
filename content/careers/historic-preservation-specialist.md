title: Historic Preservation Specialist
category: "Specialized Construction"
tagline: Makes sure a landmark or historic building gets restored the right way rather than just the fast way, pairing with a Restoration Project Manager but for buildings where the standards are legal, not just practical.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Historic Preservation Specialist plans and oversees restoration and
  rehabilitation work on historic and landmark-designated buildings,
  making sure the work meets the Secretary of the Interior's Standards
  and whatever local landmark commission or historic district
  regulations apply. It's a more regulated, research-heavy specialty
  than general restoration work: getting it wrong doesn't just mean a
  worse repair, it can mean a project gets rejected by a landmark
  commission or loses eligibility for historic tax credits that were
  funding it in the first place.
whatTheyDo:
  - Research a historic building's original materials, construction methods, and architectural history
  - Prepare historic structures reports documenting existing conditions and recommended treatment
  - Ensure restoration work complies with the Secretary of the Interior's Standards and local landmark regulations
  - Specify appropriate historic materials and restoration techniques (matching mortar, replicating millwork, and similar work)
  - Coordinate with landmark commissions and preservation review boards on project approval
  - Oversee contractors performing specialized restoration work to confirm it meets preservation standards
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review archival photographs and records to confirm a building's original facade details
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Walk the site with a mason to confirm mortar matching meets the historic specification
  - time: "11:30 AM"
    activity: Prepare documentation for an upcoming landmark commission review
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Present a proposed restoration approach to a local historic preservation board
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Inspect completed restoration work against the approved treatment plan
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
  - Office (research and documentation)
  - Public hearings and landmark commission meetings
skills:
  - Historic research and documentation
  - Knowledge of historic construction materials and methods
  - Understanding of preservation standards and regulatory review processes
  - Coordination with specialized restoration contractors
  - Public presentation to review boards
software:

  - Reality capture and documentation software
education:
  required: >
    A professional degree in architecture (or state architectural
    licensure) combined with either one year of graduate study in
    architectural preservation or a closely related field, or one year
    of full-time professional experience on historic preservation
    projects, per the Secretary of the Interior's Professional
    Qualification Standards; this standard governs eligibility for many
    federally involved historic preservation projects specifically,
    rather than being a universal license
  notNecessary: A license beyond standard architectural licensure; historic preservation itself isn't separately state-licensed the way architecture is
