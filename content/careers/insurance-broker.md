whatIs: >
  An Insurance Broker (also called an agent or producer) helps a contractor
  find, structure, and buy the coverage it needs: general liability, builder's
  risk, workers' compensation, umbrella, and surety bonding. A broker
  represents the buyer, shopping a contractor's risk across multiple insurance
  carriers to find the best combination of price and terms, where a Builder's
  Risk Underwriter or General Liability Underwriter represents the carrier
  deciding whether and how to price that same risk. On a typical account, the
  broker gathers a contractor's loss history and operations details, packages
  it for underwriters, negotiates terms, and stays the client's point of
  contact for certificates of insurance, mid-term changes, and claims support
  all year, not just at renewal.
whatTheyDo:
  - Gather a contractor's operations, loss history, and financial information to market to carriers
  - Shop a contractor's risk across multiple insurance carriers to find the best price and terms
  - Negotiate coverage terms, limits, and exclusions directly with underwriters on a client's behalf
  - Issue certificates of insurance and verify subcontractors' coverage meets contract requirements
  - Advise contractors on coverage gaps, such as missing additional-insured endorsements or inadequate umbrella limits
  - Support a client through the claims process, coordinating between the contractor and the insurer's claims team
