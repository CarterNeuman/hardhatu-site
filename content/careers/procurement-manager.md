id: career-procurement-manager
title: Procurement Manager
category: "Business"
tagline: Makes sure the right materials and equipment show up on the right jobsite at the right time, a job that's mostly invisible when it goes well and a schedule crisis when it doesn't.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Procurement Manager sources and purchases the materials, equipment, and
  long-lead items a construction company needs across its projects,
  negotiating with suppliers, tracking lead times, and managing the supply
  chain relationships that keep a project from stalling while waiting on a
  delivery. On larger projects, procurement can start well before
  construction itself, since some equipment (switchgear, elevators,
  specialty mechanical equipment) can take many months to manufacture and
  ship.
whatTheyDo:
  - Source and negotiate pricing and terms with material suppliers and equipment manufacturers
  - Track lead times for long-lead items and flag anything that threatens the schedule
  - Issue and manage purchase orders across multiple active projects
  - Build and maintain supplier relationships to secure reliable pricing and priority during shortages
  - Coordinate delivery logistics and staging with project teams and superintendents
  - Monitor material market pricing to inform estimating and budget assumptions
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review lead-time updates on long-lead equipment ordered for an upcoming project
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Negotiate pricing with a supplier for a large material order across two projects
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Coordinate a delivery schedule with a superintendent who has limited site staging space
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Issue purchase orders for materials an estimator just finalized pricing on
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Update project teams on a supplier delay affecting a long-lead mechanical item
whereTheyWork:
  - Office
skills:
  - Negotiation
  - Supply chain and logistics coordination
  - Vendor relationship management
  - Market awareness (material pricing trends)
  - Organization across multiple simultaneous projects
software:
  - Procore
  - Enterprise resource planning (ERP) / procurement software
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in supply chain management, business, or construction management
  helpful: Certified Professional in Supply Management (CPSM) credential from the Institute for Supply Management (ISM), not construction-specific, but widely recognized across industries that do procurement work
  notNecessary: A supply-chain-specific degree, since many procurement managers move into the role from estimating, purchasing, or operations
progression:
  - Buyer or Procurement Coordinator
  - Procurement Manager
  - Senior Procurement Manager
  - Director of Procurement or Supply Chain
