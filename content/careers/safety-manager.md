id: career-safety-manager
title: Safety Manager
category: "Project & Operations"
tagline: Owns the jobsite's safety program, training, inspections, and incident tracking, on projects large enough to need a dedicated safety role beyond what the superintendent handles day to day.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Safety Manager builds and runs a project's (or a company's) safety
  program, training workers, conducting jobsite inspections, investigating
  incidents, and keeping the project in compliance with OSHA regulations.
  On smaller jobs, safety responsibility often falls to the superintendent
  alongside everything else they manage; larger projects and companies with
  a serious safety record to protect bring in a dedicated Safety Manager
  instead.
whatTheyDo:
  - Conduct regular jobsite safety inspections and document hazards for correction
  - Train workers and subcontractors on jobsite-specific safety requirements
  - Investigate incidents and near-misses, and track corrective actions
  - Maintain OSHA-required recordkeeping (injury and illness logs) and prepare for regulatory inspections
  - Develop site-specific safety plans for high-risk activities (excavation, fall protection, confined space)
  - Track the company's safety metrics (incident rates) that affect its insurance costs and bid eligibility
typicalDay:
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Walk the site before crews start, checking fall protection and housekeeping
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Deliver a toolbox talk to a crew starting a high-risk excavation task
  - time: "10:00 AM"
    activity: Investigate a near-miss reported the previous day and document corrective steps
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Update the OSHA injury and illness log and review upcoming high-risk work for the week
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Meet with the superintendent to review a subcontractor's safety plan for an upcoming crane pick
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
  - Office (for documentation, training prep, and reporting)
skills:
  - OSHA regulation knowledge and compliance
  - Incident investigation
  - Training and communication
  - Risk assessment for high-hazard activities
  - Recordkeeping and reporting
software:
  - Procore
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent. No OSHA certification is legally
    mandated nationwide for this role, but OSHA 30 becomes legally
    required for a safety supervisor on a public project in a state that
    mandates it (including New York, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Nevada,
    Missouri, and Rhode Island); most employers require it as standard
    practice everywhere else too
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in occupational safety, construction management, or a related field
  helpful: Associate Safety Professional (ASP) or Construction Health and Safety Technician (CHST) credential, both via the Board of Certified Safety Professionals (BCSP); Certified Safety Professional (CSP) as a longer-term goal
  notNecessary: A four-year degree, since many safety managers move into the role from the trades or from a superintendent track, backed by BCSP credentials rather than a specific degree
progression:
  - Safety Coordinator
  - Safety Manager
  - Senior Safety Manager or Regional Safety Director
  - VP of Safety / Corporate Safety Director
hirerTypes:

  - Construction Management Firms
  - Large Subcontractors
