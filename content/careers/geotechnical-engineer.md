whatIs: >
  A Geotechnical Engineer investigates the soil, rock, and groundwater
  conditions beneath a site and determines what the ground can actually
  support: foundation type and depth, retaining wall and slope stability
  design, and whether a site needs special treatment like deep foundations or
  soil improvement. On a typical project, geotechnical work happens early,
  producing the geotechnical report that a Structural Engineer's foundation
  design and a Civil Engineer's grading plan both depend on, and that a
  Superintendent and QA/QC Inspector later verify in the field during site
  work. Geotechnical engineering is a specialty within civil engineering: a
  standard PE (Civil) license covers the legal requirement to stamp
  geotechnical reports, though a post-PE geotechnical certification signals
  deeper specialization.
whatTheyDo:
  - Plan and oversee subsurface investigations: soil borings, test pits, and in-situ testing
  - Analyze soil and rock samples to determine bearing capacity, settlement, and slope stability
  - Recommend foundation types (shallow versus deep) and design parameters for structural engineers
  - Design retaining walls, slope stabilization, and ground improvement measures
  - Write geotechnical reports that become part of the design and permitting record
  - Observe foundation and earthwork construction in the field to confirm conditions match the report's assumptions

education:
  required: >
    A bachelor's degree in civil engineering with a geotechnical focus from an
    ABET-accredited program, often followed by a master's degree in
    geotechnical engineering, then passing the Fundamentals of Engineering
    (FE) exam to become an Engineer in Training, then several years of
    progressive experience before sitting for the Professional Engineer (PE)
    exam in civil engineering. A standard PE (Civil) license is legally
    required to stamp geotechnical reports; a post-PE geotechnical
    certification (such as the Academy of Geo-Professionals' Board Certified
    Geotechnical Engineer credential) signals deeper specialization but isn't
    required to practice.
  preferred: A master's degree in geotechnical engineering
  notNecessary: A background in construction trades; most geotechnical engineers come directly from an engineering degree program
