title: "Construction Consultant (Cost/General)"
category: "Consultants & Advisory"
tagline: Brought in for independent expertise on a specific problem, a troubled budget, a stalled schedule, a process that isn't working, rather than to run the day-to-day project.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Construction Consultant provides independent, specialized expertise to
  owners, contractors, or attorneys on a specific project challenge,
  commonly cost, budget, process, or general project advisory work that
  falls short of a formal litigation dispute (which is closer to a
  Construction Claims Consultant's territory). Rather than managing a
  project day to day, a consultant is typically engaged for a defined
  scope: reviewing a troubled budget, advising on procurement strategy, or
  assessing whether a project's processes are set up to succeed.
whatTheyDo:
  - Review project budgets, schedules, or processes and identify the root cause of problems
  - Advise ownership or contractors on procurement strategy, contract structure, or risk allocation
  - Prepare independent reports and recommendations, often for use in decision-making at the executive level
  - Benchmark a project or company's practices against industry standards
  - Support due diligence on acquisitions or major capital projects
  - Occasionally serve on a project temporarily to stabilize a specific problem area before handing it back to the regular team
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review a client's project budget and schedule ahead of an on-site assessment
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Interview the client's project team to understand where a budget overrun originated
  - time: "11:30 AM"
    activity: Draft findings and recommendations for an independent project review
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Present findings to the client's executive team
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Research industry benchmarks to support a recommendation on a different client's procurement strategy
whereTheyWork:
  - Office
  - Client sites (varies significantly by engagement)
skills:
  - Broad construction knowledge across cost, schedule, and operations
  - Analytical and diagnostic thinking
  - Technical writing and executive-level presentation
  - Independent judgment and objectivity
  - Client relationship management
software:
  - Bluebeam
  - Scheduling and cost management software (varies by engagement)
education:
  required: Bachelor's degree in construction management, engineering, or a related field
  preferred: Significant experience (often 10+ years) in project management, estimating, or cost engineering before moving into consulting
  helpful: Certified Construction Manager (CCM) or Certified Cost Professional (CCP) credential, depending on specialty
  notNecessary: A consulting-specific degree; this role is almost always reached after a long track record in a hands-on construction role, not through a direct entry path
progression:
  - Project Manager, Estimator, or Cost Engineer
  - Construction Consultant
  - Senior Consultant or Practice Lead
  - Partner or Principal at a consulting firm
hirerTypes:
  - Consulting Firms
  - Owners and Developers (directly, for specific engagements)
  - Law Firms (for non-litigation advisory work)
