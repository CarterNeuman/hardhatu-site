title: BIM/VDC Specialist
category: "Technology & Design"
tagline: Builds the 3D digital model of a project before it's built, so conflicts between trades get caught on a screen instead of on the jobsite.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A BIM (Building Information Modeling) or VDC (Virtual Design and
  Construction) Specialist builds and manages the 3D digital model that
  represents a project, with structure, mechanical, electrical, and plumbing
  systems all modeled together. Their core value is clash detection:
  finding the spot where a duct and a structural beam are modeled to occupy
  the same space, for example, before it becomes an expensive problem in
  the field where cutting into installed work is far costlier than fixing
  a model.
whatTheyDo:
  - Build and coordinate 3D models from architectural, structural, and MEP drawings
  - Run clash detection between different trades' models and route conflicts back to the right designer or subcontractor
  - Extract accurate quantities directly from the model to support estimating
  - Produce coordinated shop drawings and layout points for the field from the finished model
  - Train project teams and subcontractors on how to use and interpret the model
  - Maintain the model as changes happen throughout design and construction
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Run a clash detection report across the updated mechanical and structural models
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Meet with the mechanical subcontractor to resolve a ductwork conflict flagged in the model
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Update the model with a structural revision that came in from the engineer
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Export coordinated layout points for the field team to use for hanger placement
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Walk a superintendent through the model to clarify a sequencing question
whereTheyWork:
  - Office
  - Occasional site visits to verify as-built conditions against the model
skills:
  - 3D modeling and BIM software proficiency
  - Clash detection and coordination
  - Reading architectural, structural, and MEP drawings
  - Spatial reasoning
  - Communication across multiple trades and design disciplines
software:
  - Bluebeam
  - Building information modeling software (e.g. Revit, Navisworks)
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent
  preferred: Associate's or bachelor's degree in construction management, architecture, or a related design/technology field
  helpful: Autodesk Certified Professional credential
  notNecessary: A design or architecture degree; many BIM specialists come from a CAD or drafting background and learn coordination on the job
