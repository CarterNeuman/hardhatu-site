title: Permit Expediter
category: "Consultants & Advisory"
tagline: Navigates building permits, plan review, and zoning approvals on an owner's or GC's behalf, a specialized, low-visibility career built for someone detail-oriented who'd rather not work a jobsite.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Permit Expediter manages the process of getting a project through
  local building permit approval, plan review, and zoning sign-off,
  preparing and submitting permit applications, tracking their status
  through the jurisdiction's review process, and resolving plan review
  comments so a project can start or continue construction on schedule.
  Large cities in particular have complex, slow-moving permitting
  bureaucracies with their own procedures and relationships that matter;
  some jurisdictions, including New York City, formally require permit
  expediters to register with the local building department (as a
  "filing representative" in NYC's case) before they can file on a
  client's behalf.
whatTheyDo:
  - Prepare and submit building permit applications and supporting documentation
  - Track permit and plan review status across multiple projects and jurisdictions
  - Respond to plan review comments and coordinate revisions with the design team
  - Maintain relationships with local building department staff and plan reviewers
  - Advise clients on a jurisdiction's specific permitting requirements and likely timeline
  - Manage zoning variance and special approval submissions when a project needs them
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Check the status of permit applications currently in review across several projects
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Submit a new building permit application with the required drawings and documentation
  - time: "11:00 AM"
    activity: Respond to plan review comments, coordinating a revision with the architect
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Meet with building department staff to discuss an application stuck in review
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Update a client on the projected timeline for their permit approval
whereTheyWork:
  - Office
  - Building department offices for filings and in-person follow-up
skills:
  - Deep familiarity with local permitting processes and requirements
  - Organization and tracking across many applications at once
  - Reading construction drawings well enough to spot code issues before submission
  - Relationship management with building department staff
  - Patience and persistence with bureaucratic processes
software:
  - Bluebeam
  - Permitting and project tracking software
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent; some jurisdictions, including New York City, require permit expediters to register with the local building department (NYC calls this a "filing representative," with two registration classes depending on scope of work) before filing on a client's behalf
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in construction management, architecture, or a related field
  helpful: Prior experience working inside a local building department, which gives real insight into how plan reviewers actually think
  notNecessary: A construction-specific degree; many permit expediters build expertise through years of hands-on experience with a specific jurisdiction's process rather than through formal education
progression:
  - Permit Coordinator
  - Permit Expediter
  - Senior Permit Expediter or Expediting Firm Owner
  - Director of Entitlements (at a larger development or consulting firm)
hirerTypes:
