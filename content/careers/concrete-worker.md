title: "Concrete Worker (Cement Mason / Finisher)"
category: "Field & Trades"
tagline: Places, shapes, and finishes concrete before it sets, a narrow window to get a permanent, highly visible surface exactly right.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Concrete Worker (also called a Cement Mason or Concrete Finisher)
  places, consolidates, and finishes concrete, floors, sidewalks,
  driveways, foundations, and structural slabs, working within the short
  window before concrete cures. The finish has to be right the first time;
  once concrete sets, mistakes usually mean cutting it out and starting
  over rather than sanding or repainting. No government license is legally
  required to do this work.
whatTheyDo:
  - Set forms that define the shape and boundaries of a concrete pour
  - Place and consolidate wet concrete, working it into forms and around reinforcing steel
  - Screed, float, and trowel the surface to the specified finish (broom, smooth, exposed aggregate, and more)
  - Cut control joints to manage cracking as the concrete cures
  - Apply curing compounds and protect fresh concrete from weather and early traffic
  - Read plans and specifications for slab thickness, reinforcement, and finish requirements
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:00 AM"
    activity: Arrive early to set forms and check reinforcing steel placement before the concrete truck arrives
  - time: "7:30 AM"
    activity: Place and consolidate concrete as it's poured, working quickly before it begins to set
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Screed and float the surface to bring it to grade and remove high spots
  - time: "11:30 AM"
    activity: Apply the specified finish once the concrete has set up enough to work without marring it
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Cut control joints and apply curing compound before leaving the pour to cure
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
skills:
  - Timing and judgment (reading when concrete is ready for each finishing step)
  - Physical stamina and comfort working in awkward positions for extended periods
  - Reading plans and specifications for slab and finish requirements
  - Precision (a slab has to be level and to grade)
  - Teamwork and coordination during a pour, which moves fast and can't pause
software:
  - Generally none; this is a hands-on field trade
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent. No license is legally required to work as a concrete finisher
  preferred: Completion of a formal apprenticeship (typically through a union or trade association) rather than a college degree
  helpful: >
    ACI (American Concrete Institute) certifications such as Concrete
    Flatwork Finisher/Technician are optional, but some project
    specifications require ACI-certified personnel for certain concrete
    work, worth pursuing if a specific job or employer calls for it
  notNecessary: A college degree; this trade is learned through apprenticeship and hands-on experience
progression:
  - Concrete Laborer
  - Concrete Finisher
  - Lead Finisher or Foreman
  - Concrete Superintendent or own a concrete finishing business
