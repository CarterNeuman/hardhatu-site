title: QA/QC Inspector
category: "Project & Operations"
tagline: Physically inspects and tests completed work against the specifications, the hands-on counterpart to a Quality Control Manager's oversight role.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A QA/QC Inspector performs the hands-on inspection and testing that a
  project's quality control program requires, checking completed work
  against drawings and specifications, running or witnessing material
  tests, and documenting the results. It's distinct from a Quality
  Control Manager, who designs and oversees the quality program for the
  whole project; an inspector is the person actually walking the work,
  pulling concrete cylinders, checking weld quality, and writing up the
  non-conformance reports when something doesn't meet spec.
whatTheyDo:
  - Inspect completed work against drawings, specifications, and applicable codes
  - Witness or perform material testing (concrete, soils, welds) and document results
  - Complete special inspection reports required by the building code for structural and life-safety systems
  - Write non-conformance reports when work doesn't meet specification
  - Verify corrective work before it's covered up by the next trade
  - Maintain inspection and testing records for the project's quality and closeout files
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:30 AM"
    activity: Review the day's inspection schedule and required tests
  - time: "7:30 AM"
    activity: Witness concrete placement and pull test cylinders for a structural pour
  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Inspect completed structural welds against the project's welding specification
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Write a non-conformance report for a completed wall assembly that doesn't match the fire-rated spec
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Verify a subcontractor's corrective work before it gets covered by drywall
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite
skills:
  - Reading drawings and specifications in detail
  - Material testing procedures
  - Building code and special inspection requirements
  - Precise documentation and report writing
  - Diplomatic but firm communication with subcontractor crews
software:
  - Procore
  - Bluebeam
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, plus relevant field or trade experience; specific requirements depend on the type of inspection being performed
  preferred: >
    A discipline-specific certification depending on specialty, such as
    the American Welding Society's Certified Welding Inspector (CWI) for
    welding inspection, ACI's Concrete Field Testing Technician
    certification for concrete testing, or NICET's Construction
    Materials Testing certification for soils, asphalt, or concrete work
  helpful: OSHA 10 or 30 certification, and an ICC certification for code-related inspections
  notNecessary: A four-year engineering degree; this role is built on hands-on inspection and testing expertise, often gained in a trade before moving into inspection
progression:
  - Field Technician or Inspector-in-Training
  - QA/QC Inspector
  - Senior Inspector or Multi-Discipline Inspector
  - Quality Control Manager
hirerTypes:

  - Independent Testing and Inspection Firms
  - Owners and Owner's Representatives (on projects requiring third-party inspection)
