whatIs: >
  A Plasterer or Stucco Mason applies interior plaster, exterior stucco,
  cement, or similar finish materials to walls and ceilings, and may also
  set ornamental plaster. BLS tracks this as its own occupation (SOC
  47-2161), distinct from masonry work with brick and block, even though
  both trades are sometimes grouped together loosely as "masonry." No
  personal license is generally required to work as an employee
  plasterer in most states; a specialty plastering or general contractor
  license may apply at the business level in states that require one.
  Exterior stucco work is weather-dependent and applied in coats, so
  timing and curing conditions between coats matter as much as the
  application itself.
whatTheyDo:
  - Prepare surfaces (lath, masonry, or existing substrate) before applying plaster or stucco
  - Mix and apply plaster or stucco in multiple coats, following required cure times between coats
  - Apply exterior stucco finishes and textures to building facades
  - Apply interior plaster finishes, including smooth and textured finish coats
  - Install and finish ornamental plaster details on some projects
  - Repair cracked or damaged plaster and stucco on renovation and restoration work

  - time: "11:00 AM"

  - time: "1:00 PM"

  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Clean tools and equipment, plan tomorrow's coat schedule around the weather forecast
whereTheyWork:

skills:
  - Hand-finishing skill for smooth, consistent plaster and stucco surfaces
  - Knowledge of coat sequencing and cure times for different plaster and stucco systems
  - Physical stamina for repetitive overhead and reaching work
  - Comfort working from scaffolding or lifts on exterior work
  - Basic math for mixing ratios and material coverage
software:

  notNecessary: A college degree, since this trade is learned almost entirely on the job
