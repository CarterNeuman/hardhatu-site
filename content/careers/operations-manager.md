title: Operations Manager

tagline: Oversees multiple project teams and their Project Managers at once, the role that scales a contractor's capacity beyond what one PM can run.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  An Operations Manager oversees a portfolio of active projects and the
  Project Managers running them, focusing on company-wide execution,
  staffing, and process rather than any single project's day-to-day
  details. No personal license is legally required to hold this title.
  There's a related but distinct legal wrinkle worth knowing: many states
  require a licensed contractor's business to designate one specific
  person, a "qualifying individual" or "Responsible Managing Employee",
  who holds the personal license the company's contractor license is built on.
  An Operations Manager can be the person a company designates for that
  role, but it's a single, specific legal designation tied to the business
  license, not a requirement attached to the Operations Manager job title
  itself.
whatTheyDo:
  - Oversee multiple Project Managers and their project teams across the company's active work
  - Monitor project performance (schedule, budget, safety) across the full portfolio and intervene where projects are at risk
  - Support staffing decisions, allocating project management talent across active and upcoming projects
  - Standardize and improve company-wide project management processes and tools
  - Report portfolio-level performance to company leadership or ownership
  - Mentor and develop Project Managers moving up through the organization

  - time: "9:30 AM"

  - time: "11:00 AM"

  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Review and standardize a project management process that's been applied inconsistently
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Present portfolio-wide performance metrics to company ownership

  preferred: Bachelor's degree in construction management, business, or engineering, plus significant experience as a Project Manager
