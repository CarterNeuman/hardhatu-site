title: Millwright
category: "Field & Trades"
tagline: Installs, aligns, and maintains the heavy industrial machinery inside a facility, an entry point into manufacturing and industrial construction most newcomers never hear about.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Millwright installs, aligns, and maintains heavy industrial
  equipment, conveyors, pumps, gearboxes, turbines, and production
  machinery, inside manufacturing plants, power facilities, and other
  industrial buildings. It's precision work: aligning a piece of rotating
  equipment can come down to thousandths of an inch, checked with dial
  indicators or laser alignment tools, because equipment installed out of
  tolerance wears out or fails early. Millwrights typically get involved
  once a building's structural shell and utilities are in place and it's
  time to install the equipment the building was actually built to house.
whatTheyDo:
  - Rig, move, and set heavy industrial equipment using cranes and rigging
  - Align rotating equipment (motors, pumps, gearboxes) to precise tolerances using dial indicators and laser alignment tools
  - Install and level conveyors, production machinery, and process equipment
  - Read mechanical drawings to plan equipment placement and anchoring
  - Troubleshoot and repair mechanical equipment failures
  - Perform preventive maintenance on installed machinery
typicalDay:
  - time: "6:00 AM"
    activity: Review the day's equipment installation plan and rigging requirements
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Rig and set a piece of production machinery into its final position
  - time: "10:00 AM"
    activity: Laser-align a motor and pump coupling to the tolerances specified by the manufacturer
  - time: "1:00 PM"
    activity: Read mechanical drawings to plan anchor bolt layout for an upcoming equipment set
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Perform preventive maintenance checks on previously installed conveyor equipment
whereTheyWork:
  - Industrial and manufacturing jobsites
  - Existing plants and facilities for maintenance and equipment upgrades
skills:
  - Precision alignment (dial indicators, laser alignment)
  - Rigging and heavy equipment moving
  - Reading mechanical drawings
  - Hydraulic and pneumatic systems knowledge
  - Craft-related math and trigonometry
software:
  - Generally none; this is a hands-on field trade
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, then a registered apprenticeship
    (through a union such as the United Brotherhood of Carpenters or the
    International Union of Operating Engineers, depending on region) or
    an NCCER Millwright curriculum, a four-level, roughly 670-800 hour
    program covering rigging, alignment, and mechanical systems that's
    built to meet U.S. Department of Labor apprenticeship standards;
    millwright work isn't separately state-licensed
  helpful: >
    OSHA 10 or 30 certification (not a nationwide legal requirement,
    though several states, including New York, Massachusetts,
    Connecticut, Nevada, Missouri, and Rhode Island, legally require it
    for workers on public construction projects), and rigging or crane
    signal-person certification given how much of the work involves
    moving heavy equipment
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; the apprenticeship-to-journeyman route is the standard path into the trade
progression:
  - Apprentice Millwright
  - Journeyman Millwright
  - Millwright Foreman
  - Maintenance Superintendent or Industrial Contractor
hirerTypes:
  - Industrial and Millwright Subcontractors
  - Manufacturing Plants and Facilities (direct hire for maintenance)
  - General Contractors (on industrial and manufacturing projects)
