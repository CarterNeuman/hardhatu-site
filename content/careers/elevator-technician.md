whatIs: >
  An Elevator & Escalator Technician, also called an elevator constructor
  or mechanic, installs new elevator and escalator systems as a building
  goes up and maintains, inspects, and repairs existing ones for the rest
  of a building's life. The work is safety-critical and specialized
  enough that it runs its own apprenticeship track separate from
  electricians or millwrights, and most states require a state elevator
  mechanic license on top of it before someone can work unsupervised.
whatTheyDo:
  - Install elevator cars, rails, motors, and control systems per manufacturer specifications
  - Wire and test elevator control panels and safety systems before an elevator goes into service
  - Perform scheduled maintenance and required safety testing on existing elevators and escalators
  - Diagnose and repair mechanical and electrical elevator malfunctions
  - Respond to entrapments and emergency service calls
  - Coordinate with electricians and structural crews during new-building elevator shaft installation
typicalDay:
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Review the day's service and installation schedule, and check parts/tools needed for each stop
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Respond to a maintenance contract's scheduled inspection on an existing building's elevators
  - time: "10:30 AM"
    activity: Test safety systems (governor, brakes, door interlocks) and log the results per code
  - time: "12:30 PM"
    activity: Move to a new-construction site to continue wiring an elevator control panel mid-installation
  - time: "3:00 PM"
    activity: Troubleshoot an entrapment call and get the car back in service
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite (elevator shaft and machine room) during new construction
  - Existing buildings for maintenance contracts and service calls
skills:
  - Electrical and mechanical troubleshooting
  - Reading elevator wiring diagrams and understanding elevator code (ASME A17.1)
  - Working safely in confined shafts and at height
  - Hydraulics and control systems knowledge
  - Safety-critical precision under time pressure (entrapment calls)
software:
  - Generally none; this is a hands-on field trade
education:
  required: >
    High school diploma or equivalent, then a union apprenticeship
    through the International Union of Elevator Constructors (IUEC),
    delivered by NEIEP, typically four to five years combining roughly
    2,000 hours of paid on-the-job training per year with 100-200 hours
    of annual classroom instruction (about 8,000 OJT hours total).
    Completion leads to a mechanic exam, and most states then require a
    separate state elevator mechanic license; a number of states,
    including Idaho, Iowa, and Wyoming, don't mandate one at the state
    level, and licensing rules in this trade change often enough that
    it's worth confirming current requirements for any specific state.
  helpful: The National Association of Elevator Contractors' Certified Elevator Technician (CET) credential, which some states accept in place of a state licensing exam
  notNecessary: A four-year college degree; this is an apprenticeship-and-license trade, not a degree-based one
progression:
  - Apprentice Elevator Constructor
  - Journeyman Mechanic
  - Lead Mechanic or Adjuster (fine-tunes elevator ride quality and performance)
  - Superintendent or Elevator Contractor
hirerTypes:

  - General Contractors (subcontract this work almost universally)
  - Building Owners and Facilities Companies (for ongoing maintenance contracts)
