title: Insurance Adjuster (Property/Casualty)
category: "Insurance & Claims"
tagline: Investigates property damage claims and decides what the insurance company will actually pay to fix it.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Property/Casualty Insurance Adjuster inspects damage from events like
  fires, storms, and water leaks, then writes a scope of loss, an itemized
  estimate of repair costs, that determines what the policyholder gets
  paid. Adjusters sit on the insurer's side of a claim (a separate role,
  Public Adjuster, represents the property owner instead); construction
  knowledge is what lets them tell the difference between a reasonable
  repair estimate and one that's padded or missing damage entirely.
whatTheyDo:
  - Inspect damaged property in person and document the damage with photos and measurements
  - Write a detailed scope of loss covering every item that needs repair or replacement
  - Interpret the policy to determine what's actually covered and what isn't
  - Negotiate the final settlement with the policyholder or their contractor when the two sides disagree on scope or price
  - Coordinate with contractors and restoration companies to verify a proposed repair matches the actual damage
  - Manage a caseload of claims simultaneously, prioritizing by severity and how long a claim has been open
typicalDay:
  - time: "8:00 AM"
    activity: Review the day's inspection schedule and prior notes on each claim
  - time: "9:00 AM"
    activity: Inspect a storm-damaged roof, photograph the damage, and take measurements
  - time: "11:30 AM"
    activity: Write the scope of loss for that morning's inspection, itemizing materials and labor
  - time: "1:30 PM"
    activity: Call a policyholder's contractor to reconcile a difference between two competing estimates
  - time: "3:30 PM"
    activity: Close out a finished claim's paperwork and update the file before moving to the next one
whereTheyWork:
  - Field (inspecting damaged properties)
  - Home office or a company office for paperwork and scope-writing
skills:
  - Damage assessment
  - Policy interpretation
  - Estimating (Xactimate proficiency is close to a job requirement)
  - Negotiation
  - Documentation and photography
software:
  - Xactimate
education:
  required: High school diploma or equivalent, plus a state adjuster license (requirements vary by state, and many states have reciprocity agreements with each other)
  preferred: Bachelor's degree in business, construction management, or a related field
  helpful: IICRC certifications for water/fire/mold restoration knowledge
  notNecessary: A construction-specific degree; many adjusters come from an insurance or claims background rather than construction, and pick up the technical side on the job
progression:
  - Trainee or Staff Adjuster
  - Independent or Catastrophe (CAT) Adjuster (deployed to disaster areas as needed, often higher-paying but less predictable)
  - Senior Adjuster or Claims Manager
  - Some adjusters instead move to the policyholder's side as a Public Adjuster
hirerTypes:
  - Insurance Companies
  - Independent Adjusting Firms
  - Restoration Contractors (some keep estimators on staff who work alongside adjusters)
