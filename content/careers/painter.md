tagline: Applies the finish coat that's the most visible part of almost every building, and, on older buildings, has to know exactly what's under that old paint before touching it.
tier: free
whatIs: >
  A Painter prepares surfaces and applies paint, stain, and coatings on
  new construction and renovation projects, both interior and exterior. No
  personal painting license is legally required in most states. One
  federal requirement does apply to a specific, common scope of work: the
  EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) Rule legally requires firm
  certification and individual "certified renovator" training for anyone
  paid to perform work, including painting, that disturbs paint in housing
  or child-occupied facilities built before 1978, since that paint may
  contain lead.
whatTheyDo:
  - Prepare surfaces, scraping, sanding, patching, and priming, before painting
  - Apply paint, stain, and specialty coatings by brush, roller, or sprayer
  - Match colors and finishes to specifications or existing conditions
  - Test for and safely manage lead-based paint on pre-1978 buildings per EPA RRP requirements
  - Protect finished surfaces, flooring, and fixtures from overspray and drips
  - Estimate paint and material quantities needed for a job
typicalDay:
  - time: "7:00 AM"
    activity: Set up drop cloths and protect surfaces not being painted
  - time: "7:30 AM"
    activity: Prepare surfaces, sanding, patching, and priming as needed
  - time: "9:30 AM"
    activity: Apply the first coat of paint to prepared surfaces
  - time: "12:00 PM"
    activity: Allow the first coat to dry while prepping the next area
  - time: "2:00 PM"
    activity: Apply a second coat and do detail/trim work with a brush
whereTheyWork:
  - Jobsite (interior and exterior)
skills:
  - Surface preparation
  - Color matching and finish consistency
  - Attention to detail and clean lines
  - Physical stamina, including working on ladders or lifts
  - Knowledge of lead-safe work practices for older buildings
software:

education:

    renovation work that disturbs paint in housing or child-occupied
    facilities built before 1978 is legally required, under the EPA's RRP
    Rule, to work for an EPA-certified firm and have completed the
    certified renovator training for that specific scope of work
  preferred: On-the-job training or a formal painting apprenticeship
  helpful: Manufacturer-specific coating certifications, spray finishing experience
  notNecessary: A college degree, since this trade is learned almost entirely on the job
