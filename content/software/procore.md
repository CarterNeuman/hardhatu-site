category: "Project management"
whatItIs: >
  A cloud-based construction management platform that many general
  contractors, owners, and specialty contractors use to run a project from
  one place: budget, schedule, drawings, RFIs, submittals, safety, and
  daily field reports, all connected instead of living in separate
  spreadsheets and email threads.
whatItDoes: >
  Procore centers on a single project record everyone on the job can see at
  the same permission level they're supposed to have: the superintendent
  in the field, the project manager in the office, and the owner checking
  progress remotely are all looking at the same live drawings, the same
  RFI log, and the same budget, instead of whoever has the latest version
  emailed to them. When a superintendent logs a daily report from a
  tablet on-site, or a subcontractor uploads a photo of a completed
  inspection, that update is immediately visible to everyone else with
  access, which is the specific problem it's built to solve: construction
  projects generate huge amounts of paperwork (RFIs, submittals, change
  orders, inspection reports) across dozens of people, and most disputes
  and delays trace back to someone not seeing the right version of
  something at the right time.
keyFeatures:
  - Drawing and specification management with version control, so the
    field always opens the current set instead of an outdated PDF
  - RFI and submittal tracking with due dates and automatic reminders
  - Daily log / field report tool for superintendents, from a phone or
    tablet on-site
  - Budget and change order tracking tied to the project's actual cost
    codes
  - Photo and document storage tied to specific locations on the drawings
  - Scheduling tools that integrate with the project's RFIs and submittals
commonUses:
  - A superintendent photographs a completed wall before it's covered by
    drywall and tags the photo to that exact location on the floor plan,
    so it's easy to find months later if a question comes up about what's
    behind that wall
  - A project engineer submits an RFI about a dimension conflict directly
    from the drawings, and the architect's response becomes part of the
    permanent project record instead of a buried email
  - A project manager tracks every change order against the original
    budget in real time, instead of reconciling a spreadsheet at the end
    of the month
