title: QuickBooks
tier: premium
category: Accounting
whatItIs: >
  A general small-business accounting platform that has become the
  default bookkeeping tool for a large share of construction companies,
  especially smaller subcontractors.
whatItDoes: >
  QuickBooks wasn't built specifically for construction, but its wide
  ecosystem of bank, payroll, and industry-tool integrations has made it
  the practical default anyway. A contractor can track income and
  expenses by individual job rather than just for the company as a whole,
  send invoices and collect payment electronically, and run payroll
  through an add-on that also handles year-end contractor tax reporting.
  Setting it up to actually reflect how a construction company operates,
  with a proper job-costing structure, takes real configuration rather
  than working correctly out of the box.
keyFeatures:
  - Per-job profit and loss tracking, so a contractor can see whether a
    specific job actually made money rather than just the company as a
    whole
  - Invoicing and online payment collection, including credit card and
    bank transfer options
  - An optional payroll add-on that handles wage payments, tax filings,
    and year-end contractor tax reporting
  - Integration with many construction-specific scheduling and customer
    management tools that plug into QuickBooks for the accounting side
commonUses:
  - A construction accountant sets up class tracking so the company's
    income and expenses can be broken out by crew or division instead of
    one company-wide total
  - A small contractor invoices a client directly from a completed job
    and collects payment electronically without a separate payment
    processor
