tagline: "Estimating hiring managers want to see the CSI divisions you've priced and the software you priced them in, not a general description of your attention to detail."
sections:
  - heading: "What recruiters actually look for"
    content: >
      Specific estimating software named directly: Bluebeam, On-Screen
      Takeoff, ProEst, HCSS HeavyBid. Which CSI divisions you've actually
      taken off and priced, since a resume that shows range across
      multiple divisions reads as more capable than one that's vague
      about scope. Real numbers on bid volume or win rate if you have
      them. And familiarity with the pricing method that matches the work
      you're applying for, unit price, lump sum, or GMP, since they're
      genuinely different skills.
  - heading: "How to structure it"
    content: >
      Open with your estimating software and the CSI divisions or project
      types you cover, so a reviewer can tell in five seconds whether
      you're a fit. Use real numbers wherever you have them: contract
      values, win rates, subcontractor counts managed. One page for
      someone early in the field, up to two pages once you've estimated
      larger or more varied project types.
  - heading: "What to leave off"
    content: >
      Generic "detail-oriented" or "strong analytical skills" claims with
      no number attached to back them up. Vague references to "pricing
      experience" without naming the divisions, project types, or pricing
      method involved. And don't list software you've only seen someone
      else use, an interviewer will ask you to walk through a takeoff, and
      that gap shows immediately.
exampleBullets:

  - "Estimated unit-price heavy civil work alongside lump-sum commercial buildouts"
