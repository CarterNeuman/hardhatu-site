tagline: "Here, a portfolio link and named software fluency carry more weight than a degree, and a resume without either one is missing what reviewers check first."
sections:
  - heading: "What recruiters actually look for"
    content: >
      Software fluency named specifically: Revit, Navisworks, AutoCAD
      Civil 3D, not a generic "CAD/BIM software" line. Real clash
      detection, coordination, or modeling experience described in
      concrete terms (how many models federated, how many conflicts
      resolved, what discipline you coordinated). A portfolio or work
      sample link, since design-adjacent roles are judged visually more
      than almost any other category on this site, and a resume without
      one invites the reviewer to assume there isn't one. Relevant
      certifications like Autodesk Certified Professional.
  - heading: "How to structure it"
    content: >
      Put your portfolio or work-sample link near the top, not buried in
      a footer. List software fluency by name and by proficiency level
      rather than a flat list with no distinction between "used it once"
      and "coordinate models in it daily." One page early career, closer
      to two once you have multiple real projects and a specific
      technical specialty to show.
  - heading: "What to leave off"
    content: >
      A software list with no indication of actual skill level; naming
      ten tools you've each opened once reads worse than naming three
      you're genuinely fluent in. Applying for a design-adjacent role
      with no portfolio or sample link at all, when one is the standard
      expectation. And don't describe modeling or data work only in
      vague terms ("worked with BIM") when you could name the specific
      software, project type, and outcome instead.
exampleBullets:

  - "Processed drone-captured site survey data into as-built topographic models"
