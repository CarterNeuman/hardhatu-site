sections:
  - heading: "What recruiters actually look for"
    content: >
      Current state adjuster license status, and which states you're
      licensed or reciprocity-eligible in, right at the top. Real claims
      volume and type (property, catastrophe, liability), since a
      specific number reads as far more credible than "extensive claims
      experience." Xactimate fluency named directly. Catastrophe or CAT
      deployment experience if you have it, since it signals you can
      handle high-volume, high-pressure claim surges.
  - heading: "How to structure it"
    content: >
      Licensing status goes in the first few lines, not buried in a
      certifications section near the bottom; it's often the literal
      first filter a reviewer applies. Follow with claims volume and
      type, named software, and any catastrophe deployment history. One
      page is standard here, since the credential and the numbers are
      doing most of the persuading, not lengthy narrative descriptions.
  - heading: "What to leave off"
    content: >
      Vague "customer service" framing that doesn't mention claims
      volume, type, or outcomes at all. Leaving licensing status
      ambiguous or omitting which states you're covered in, forcing a
      reviewer to ask a question your resume should have already
      answered. And don't inflate claim counts or cycle times; adjusting
      work is heavily documented, and a number that doesn't hold up in
      an interview is worse than a smaller, accurate one.
exampleBullets:
