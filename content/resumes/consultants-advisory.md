tagline: "A firm's principal is reading this, not a recruiter, and they're screening for specific engagement outcomes and dollar figures, not a description of your trusted-advisor style."
sections:
  - heading: "What recruiters and principals actually look for"
    content: >
      Specific engagement outcomes with real numbers attached: dispute
      value resolved, value engineering savings delivered, claims
      defended. Relevant credentials (bar admission for attorneys, CCM
      or CCP for consultants, PE for technical experts), since these
      roles are reached after a long track record, and the credential
      signals that track record quickly. A track record of engagement
      types that match what the firm actually does, not a generalized
      construction background.
  - heading: "How to structure it"
    content: >
      Lead with your most significant engagement and its number. Since
      this resume is usually read by a firm's principal rather than an
      HR screener, it can afford to read more like a professional
      biography, still concrete and numbers-driven, but with room for
      the kind of engagement narrative a pure HR resume would cut. One
      to two pages, with credentials and bar admissions or licenses
      clearly visible near the top.
  - heading: "What to leave off"
    content: >
      Generic "trusted advisor" or "strategic partner" language with no
      concrete engagement example or number attached to it. A resume
      that reads like a general contracting or project management
      resume without the advisory-specific outcomes that got you hired
      into this kind of role in the first place. And don't understate
      real engagement value out of caution; specificity is what this
      category's hiring actually rewards.
exampleBullets:

  - "Negotiated schedule delay disputes totaling $14M in contested claims to settlement"
