tagline: "A lot of trade roles don't require a formal resume at all. When one helps, lead with your certifications and safety record, not a chronological work history."
sections:
  - heading: "What foremen and hiring managers actually look for"
    content: >
      Current certifications first: an OSHA 10 or 30 card, NCCER Core
      Curriculum, or journeyman status, since these are often the literal
      first screening filter before anyone reads further. After that,
      specific equipment, tools, and materials experience named directly
      (which excavator model, which welding process, which systems you've
      run), not a generic "skilled laborer" description. A clean safety
      record matters more here than almost anywhere else in construction,
      since one incident report can end a jobsite placement. Reliability
      and availability (can you start this week, do you have your own
      transportation) often outweigh years of experience for an entry-level
      opening.
  - heading: "How to structure it"
    content: >
      Keep it to one page. Certifications and safety training go at the
      top, above work history, not buried at the bottom. Follow with a
      short, specific skills and equipment list. Work history comes last,
      in reverse chronological order, and each line should name what you
      actually did (which tasks, which tools, which conditions) rather
      than just a job title. Put your phone number somewhere obvious. A lot
      of trade hiring moves fast by phone, and a resume that's hard to
      reach you from doesn't get a callback.
  - heading: "What to leave off"
    content: >
      A generic objective statement at the top. Unrelated non-trade jobs
      padded in just to fill space, unless they genuinely show up-on-time
      reliability. Soft-skill buzzwords like "hard worker" or "team player"
      with nothing backing them up. And never list a certification as
      current when it's actually expired; getting caught overstating your
      status is one of the fastest ways to lose credibility once you're
      actually on a jobsite.
exampleBullets:
  - "OSHA 10 Construction certified (current), NCCER Core Curriculum completed"
  - "Operated skid steer, mini-excavator, and forklift on active commercial jobsites"
  - "Zero safety incidents across 18 months on a 200-person high-rise jobsite"
  - "Completed 3-year apprenticeship, journeyman status since 2023"
