questions:
  - question: "What do you already know about how construction differs from other industries you've worked in?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Whether you understand you're not just doing the same job with a
      different logo, since construction has its own accounting methods,
      compliance requirements, and rhythms that differ meaningfully from
      most industries.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Be specific about something real, such as job costing, retention, certified
      payroll, lien waivers, or the fact that a project can span years, rather
      than a vague "construction is more hands-on."
  - question: "Tell me about a time you had to learn an entirely new domain quickly for a job."
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Whether you can actually ramp up on construction-specific knowledge,
      especially if you're coming from a different industry.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Use a real example with a concrete outcome, and if you're new to
      construction, be upfront about what you're still learning rather than
      overstating existing knowledge.
  - question: "How comfortable are you working across an office team and a field team that rarely see each other in person?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Whether you can communicate effectively with people whose daily
      reality (a jobsite versus a desk) is very different from yours.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Give a real example of adapting your communication style for a
      field-based audience, shorter, more direct, less email-dependent,
      versus an office one.
  - question: "What's the difference between a WIP report showing over-billing versus under-billing, and why does it matter?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Construction Accountant) Real fluency with construction-specific
      accounting concepts, not just general accounting competence.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Explain both directions clearly and give a concrete example of why
      each matters to cash flow or to a bonding company reviewing the
      numbers.
  - question: "How do you build a relationship with a developer or owner before there's even a project to bid on?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Construction Sales / Business Development) Real relationship-building
      skill over a long, often multi-year sales cycle, not transactional
      sales tactics.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Describe a genuine relationship you built over time and what it
      eventually led to, rather than a generic networking philosophy.
  - question: "How do you decide which contract clauses are worth flagging for leadership versus standard boilerplate?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Contracts Administrator) Risk judgment, since flagging everything is as
      unhelpful as flagging nothing.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Name a specific clause type you'd always flag (liquidated damages,
      indemnification) and explain why, showing you understand which terms
      carry real financial exposure.
  - question: "How would you handle certified payroll reporting on a prevailing-wage public project?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (HR Manager, Construction) Familiarity with a compliance requirement
      unique to public construction work, which general HR experience alone
      doesn't cover.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Speak concretely about the actual process if you've done it before,
      or be upfront that it's new to you but explain how you'd learn it
      quickly and accurately, since errors here carry real legal exposure.
  - question: "How do you decide which of several struggling projects needs your attention first?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Operations Manager) Prioritization across a portfolio, since this
      role can't dive deep on every project at once.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Give a real framework, such as dollar exposure, schedule criticality, or how
      recoverable the situation still is, along with a specific example if you
      have one.
  - question: "How do you handle a long-lead item that's suddenly at risk of missing the schedule?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Procurement Manager) Real supply-chain problem-solving under time
      pressure, not just vendor management in the abstract.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Walk through concrete options (expediting, substitution, resequencing
      other work around it) and how you'd communicate the risk to the
      project team early rather than after it becomes a crisis.

Hiring for these roles looks more like a typical corporate process than a jobsite one; expect a standard multi-round interview (a recruiter screen, a hiring manager interview, sometimes a panel), often including someone from outside construction entirely, like a general HR or finance leader. Because the core skillset here (accounting, HR, sales, procurement, contracts) exists in every industry, what actually differentiates candidates is construction-specific fluency: certified payroll, retention, lien waivers, job costing, prevailing wage rules, and the fact that "the customer" is often a repeat developer relationship built over years rather than a one-time transaction.

If you're coming from outside construction, lead with your relevant industry-agnostic skill, but be upfront that you're actively learning the construction-specific wrinkles rather than pretending you already know them, because interviewers in this category generally respect honesty about the gap far more than an attempt to bluff through it, since the gap becomes obvious quickly on the job anyway.
