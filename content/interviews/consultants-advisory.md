questions:
  - question: "Walk me through a complex technical finding you had to explain to someone without your technical background."
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Whether you can actually communicate expertise clearly to attorneys,
      juries, or executives, the core skill these roles are built on,
      separate from the underlying technical knowledge itself.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Use a specific, real example and describe how you simplified the
      explanation without distorting the substance; that balance is
      exactly what's being tested.
  - question: "Tell me about a time your independent findings weren't what the client hoped to hear. How did you handle it?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Genuine independence, since a consultant who always tells the client what
      they want to hear isn't providing real value, and experienced
      interviewers know to probe for this directly.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Give a real example and be honest about how the conversation with the
      client actually went, including any tension, rather than a tidy
      story where everyone was immediately satisfied.
  - question: "How do you maintain objectivity when a client is paying for your opinion?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Whether you understand this as a genuine professional tension rather
      than a solved problem, since courts and clients alike scrutinize
      consultant objectivity closely.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Speak to real practices (following the evidence wherever it leads,
      being willing to deliver an unfavorable finding) rather than just
      asserting that you're objective.
  - question: "Walk me through how you'd reconstruct a project timeline from incomplete records for a delay claim."
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Construction Claims Consultant) Real technical method for a task
      that's often the hardest part of a claims engagement, piecing
      together what actually happened from imperfect documentation.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Describe an actual method (cross-referencing daily reports,
      correspondence, RFIs, and change orders against each other) and be
      honest about how you handle genuine gaps in the record.
  - question: "Tell me about an engagement where you had to diagnose a problem the client's own team hadn't identified."
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Construction Consultant) The independent-diagnosis value this role
      is supposed to add, beyond confirming what the client already
      suspected.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Use a specific, real example, and be clear about how you found the
      actual root cause rather than just the symptom the client originally
      flagged.
  - question: "How would you prepare for a deposition where opposing counsel is trying to discredit your qualifications?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Expert Witness, Construction Litigation) Composure and preparation
      under genuinely adversarial questioning, which is a real, testable
      part of this specific role.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Talk through concrete preparation (reviewing your own report
      thoroughly, anticipating likely lines of attack with retaining
      counsel) and emphasize staying calm and factual rather than
      defensive.
  - question: "Walk me through the difference between a time impact analysis and an as-planned-versus-as-built analysis."
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Scheduling & Delay Consultant) Real technical depth in delay
      analysis methodology, since this distinction is central to the work
      and easy to fake with surface-level familiarity.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Give a precise, correct technical explanation of both methods and,
      ideally, a real example of choosing one over the other for a specific
      situation.
  - question: "How do you handle a project team that's resistant to the extra documentation LEED certification requires?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      (Sustainability/LEED Consultant) Practical project-management skill
      on top of technical sustainability knowledge, since credit tracking only
      works if the project team actually cooperates with it.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Describe how you'd make the documentation burden concrete and
      manageable for a resistant team, and connect it back to something
      they actually care about (the credits or certification level at
      stake), rather than just asserting its importance.

These are senior, expertise-driven roles, and the hiring process reflects it: expect the interview to feel more like a conversation or a case-study discussion than a procedural HR process, often conducted directly by a firm's principal or partner rather than a recruiter. These roles are also frequently sourced through professional networking and referral rather than a public job posting, so the interview itself may follow an introduction that already established some credibility before you sat down.

Because your credibility on paper, such as years of hands-on experience, a relevant credential, or a track record of specific projects or cases, is usually what got you the interview in the first place, the conversation tends to probe judgment and communication rather than re-litigate your resume: can you explain a complex technical finding clearly to a non-technical audience, and can you maintain genuine independence when a client is paying you for your opinion. If the role involves litigation support specifically, expect direct questions about your composure under adversarial questioning, since that's a real, testable part of the job rather than just a personality trait.
