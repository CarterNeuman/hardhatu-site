questions:
  - question: "Walk me through how you'd respond to an RFI from the field."
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Whether you actually understand the day-to-day PM workflow, not just
      the job title. RFIs are one of the most constant parts of the role.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Reference the real process: log it, understand what's actually being
      asked and why, get the right person (architect, engineer, owner)
      the correct information to answer it, track the response, and
      communicate the answer back to the field before it holds up work.
      Mentioning that a slow RFI response is a common source of schedule
      delay shows you understand the stakes, not just the paperwork.
  - question: "Tell me about a time you had to manage a difficult subcontractor relationship."
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Negotiation and conflict-resolution skill: a PM spends a large
      share of their time managing relationships between parties who don't
      report to each other.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Use a real, specific example (from construction or elsewhere) and
      focus on how you kept the relationship workable while still holding
      the line on cost, schedule, or quality, not just how the conflict
      got "won."
  - question: "How do you track a project's budget day to day, and what do you do the moment you see it slipping?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Financial literacy and whether you escalate problems early or let
      them compound. A PM who hides a budget problem until it's a crisis
      is a real liability.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Talk through a concrete process, such as tracking committed costs
      against budget by cost code, reviewing pay applications, or flagging
      variance early, and be explicit that you'd raise a slipping budget
      immediately rather than hoping it self-corrects.
  - question: "Describe your experience with construction management software like Procore."
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Practical readiness: most firms run their RFIs, submittals, budget,
      and schedule through one platform, and ramp-up time matters.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      If you have direct experience, be specific about what you used it
      for (RFI tracking, budget, daily logs). If you don't, say so plainly
      and mention any similar tools you've used (project management
      software from another industry) and that you're comfortable
      learning a new platform quickly.
  - question: "How do you handle a change order the owner doesn't want to approve?"
    whatTheyreAssessing: >
      Negotiation under pressure, and whether you understand the
      contractual and financial mechanics of a change order, not just the
      conversation.
    strongAnswerTips: >
      Show that you'd document the scope and cost clearly, explain why the
      change is necessary (versus discretionary), and work toward a
      resolution that protects the schedule without just capitulating on
      price. Mentioning that you'd loop in the right people (legal,
      senior PM) for a change order that's stalling matters too: knowing
      when to escalate is part of the job.
