howHiringWorks: >
  This is the one category on this site where the job search barely
  resembles a job search: these roles are reached through a track record
  and a professional network, not a resume dropped into an applicant
  tracking system, and they're almost never truly entry-level. The good
  news for anyone already building toward this category is that the
  market itself is genuinely heating up. Since late 2025, new specialist
  consultancies have launched, senior experts have moved firms, and
  demand for experienced quantum, delay, and technical specialists has
  increased significantly, according to industry recruiters tracking the
  space. The construction dispute consulting field alone counts roughly
  550 practitioners across more than a hundred firms globally,
  concentrated in a handful of leading names, which tells you both that
  it's a real, defined career path and that it rewards depth over
  breadth. LEED and sustainability consulting is on a similar trajectory
  as green building requirements keep expanding. What gets you in the
  door is almost always the same thing: a specific engagement, a real
  dollar figure attached to it, and a credential (bar admission, PE,
  CCM) that signals the track record is real. It's worth saying plainly:
  nobody starts their career as a consultant. Everyone in this category
  spent years in the field or the office first, building the judgment
  and the relationships that eventually get them called for a specific
  engagement. If you're early in your career and this is where you want
  to end up, the fastest path there is doing excellent,
  relationship-building work wherever you are right now. See [[resume-consultants-advisory|the Consultants & Advisory Resume Guide]] for
  the way a firm principal actually wants that track record written up.
checklist:
  - step: "Build your track record inside a GC, owner, or specialty firm first"
    detail: "Almost nobody is hired directly into consulting or advisory work; the credibility these roles require comes from years of hands-on experience somewhere else in the industry first."
  - step: "Get the credential your specialty actually requires"
    detail: "Bar admission for construction attorneys and claims consultants, a PE for technical experts, CCM or CCP for construction consultants, LEED credentials for sustainability consulting. These signal the track record before anyone reads the resume."
  - step: "Get known inside your specialty's network before you need the job"
    detail: "Conferences, professional associations like AACE International for cost and schedule specialists or USGBC for sustainability consulting, and published case studies or articles are how this category's hiring actually happens, far more than job boards."
  - step: "Target the specific firms actively expanding right now"
    detail: "The dispute consulting market has been in a genuine growth phase since late 2025, with new specialist consultancies launching and rising demand for experienced quantum, delay, and technical specialists specifically."
  - step: "Lead with one engagement and a real number attached to it"
    detail: "Dispute value resolved, value engineering savings delivered, claims defended. A specific number is what separates a credible advisory background from a generalized one, and the Resume Guide below covers how to phrase it."
  - step: "Prepare to discuss one engagement in real depth, not in summary"
    detail: "These are relationship-driven hires, usually with a firm principal rather than an HR screener, and the interview typically goes deep into how you actually handled one real situation."
whereToLook:
  - "Major construction dispute and claims consulting firms (names like HKA, Secretariat, Ankura, FTI Consulting, Kroll, and J.S. Held lead the field) checked directly for openings"
  - "AACE International for cost engineering, quantum, and delay specialists"
  - "USGBC and LEED-focused professional networks for sustainability consulting"
  - "Referrals from GC, owner, or specialty-firm relationships built earlier in your career, which drive more hiring in this category than any job board"
  - "Expert witness and construction consulting directories, if you're pursuing litigation-support or testifying-expert work"
credentialsToHaveReady:
  - "Bar admission, for construction attorneys and claims consultants"
  - "A Professional Engineer (PE) license, for technical expert and forensic engineering roles"
  - "Certified Construction Manager (CCM) or Certified Cost Professional (CCP)"
  - "LEED credentials, for sustainability and green-building consulting"
  - "A documented track record with specific engagement outcomes and dollar figures attached"
commonMistakes:
  - "Trying to shortcut into a consulting or advisory title without the years in the field or office that usually come first. Firms in this category hire almost entirely on judgment built over time, and a resume that skips straight to 'consultant' reads as a red flag, not a shortcut."
  - "Writing generic 'trusted advisor' or 'strategic partner' language with no concrete engagement example or number attached to it."
  - "Understating real engagement value out of caution, when specificity is exactly what this category's hiring rewards."
  - "Ignoring the referral and association network that actually drives hiring here, in favor of only applying through general job boards."
firstStepToday: "Identify one past engagement with a real number attached to it, and write the one sentence that leads with it."
