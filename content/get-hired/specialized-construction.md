howHiringWorks: >
  This category is gated almost entirely by the specific credential tied
  to the specialty, more than any other on this site, but two of its
  subfields are growing for genuinely durable reasons. Solar power
  generation is the single fastest-growing occupation tracked by federal
  labor projections, up roughly 153% through 2035, and wind power
  generation is projected to grow around 62% over the same period, both
  driven in large part by data centers pushing US electricity demand
  sharply higher. That's real, sustained construction demand for [[career-renewable-energy-project-manager|renewable energy project managers]]
  specifically. Separately, the global disaster restoration market is
  projected to grow at roughly 5.7% a year through 2033, driven by a
  rising frequency of natural disasters, which has climbed from about 3
  a year in the 1980s to over 20 a year in the 2020s. That's steady,
  recession-resistant demand for restoration and remediation
  specialists. The rest of this category, demolition, pile driving,
  historic preservation, facilities management, hires more narrowly, but
  the same rule holds everywhere in it: the credential comes first,
  general construction experience second. Getting the credential is the
  entry ticket, but staying on past your first job comes down to
  something less official: whether the supervisor trusts you around real
  hazards. That trust gets built fast when you show up, follow the
  safety protocol without being told twice, and ask when you're not
  sure, and it's what turns a one-time placement into a standing spot on
  the crew. See [[resume-specialized-construction|the Specialized Construction Resume Guide]] to see exactly where that credential should sit
  on the page.
checklist:
  - step: "Identify the exact credential your target specialty requires before you apply"
    detail: "OSHA's 40-hour HAZWOPER for environmental remediation, an NCCCO operator certification for pile driving, an IICRC certification for restoration work. Confirm which one first; it changes where and how you apply."
  - step: "If you're coming from general construction, look hard at renewable energy and restoration specifically"
    detail: "Solar alone is projected to add roughly 26,000 jobs through 2035 as the fastest-growing occupation tracked federally, and restoration is growing steadily on rising disaster frequency. Both are actively recruiting from general construction backgrounds."
  - step: "Stack credentials rather than collecting just one"
    detail: "These roles often require more than one certification at once, HAZWOPER plus OSHA 10, for example, and showing up with the full stack already in hand is a real differentiator."
  - step: "Go through the specialty's own trade association, not a general job board"
    detail: "The Pile Driving Contractors Association, the National Demolition Association, the IICRC, and state historic preservation offices all post and refer roles that rarely show up on general listings."
  - step: "Get hands-on time with the specific hazard, system, or equipment before you interview"
    detail: "Even a short apprenticeship, internship, or entry-level stint gives you a real, specific story to tell, which matters more here than a general construction resume."
  - step: "Be ready to describe a specific hazard, compliance, or equipment scenario in the interview"
    detail: "These specialties involve real hazard exposure, and interviewers routinely probe for whether you actually understand the risk, not just whether you hold the certificate."
whereToLook:
  - "The Pile Driving Contractors Association (PDCA) for pile driving roles"
  - "The National Demolition Association (NDA) for demolition roles"
  - "The IICRC and major restoration franchises and firms directly for water, fire, and mold restoration roles"
  - "State Historic Preservation Offices and the Association for Preservation Technology for historic preservation roles"
  - "Solar and wind developers and EPC contractors directly for renewable energy project management roles"
  - "Environmental consulting and remediation firms directly for HAZWOPER-credentialed remediation roles"
credentialsToHaveReady:
  - "OSHA 40-Hour HAZWOPER certification for environmental remediation and hazardous materials work"
  - "NCCCO operator certification for pile driving and crane-adjacent equipment roles"
  - "IICRC certification (water damage, fire/smoke, or mold) for restoration roles"
  - "A current, unexpired copy of whichever credential your specialty requires; a lapsed certification is treated as a real liability here, not a technicality"
commonMistakes:
  - "Treating the credential as the finish line instead of the starting point. It gets you considered; whether you get kept on the crew comes down to how you actually handle the hazard and how easy you are for a supervisor to trust with it."
  - "Writing a generic trade resume that never actually names the specialty credential, forcing the reviewer to guess whether you have it."
  - "Letting a relevant certification lapse and not disclosing it, which reads far worse than being honest that it needs renewal."
  - "Treating every specialized construction role as interchangeable with general construction experience, when the credential is what's actually being hired for."
  - "Overlooking renewable energy and restoration specifically, the two fastest-growing corners of this category, in favor of only applying to more familiar specialties."
  - "Padding a resume with unrelated general-construction experience instead of leading with the specific hazard, system, or equipment background the role actually needs."
