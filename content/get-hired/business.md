howHiringWorks: >
  Business-side roles, accounting, HR, contracts administration,
  procurement, sales, hire more like a normal corporate job search than
  any other category on this site: a careers page, LinkedIn, a general
  or industry-specific recruiter. What's different is what gets you
  through the screen once you apply. Nearly a fifth of the construction
  workforce is over 55, and a meaningful share of 2026's hiring demand
  is driven by retirements, which includes experienced back-office
  staff, not just field labor. That's real opportunity if you can show
  up with construction-specific context: certified payroll and
  prevailing wage for accounting and payroll roles, subcontract and lien
  law familiarity for contracts roles, jobsite safety awareness for HR.
  A resume that reads like it could be for any industry, with nothing
  construction- specific in it, is exactly what gets passed over here,
  even when the underlying skills are strong. The construction-specific
  vocabulary above isn't something you're expected to arrive fluent in,
  either. Most people pick up certified payroll, lien waivers, or
  subcontract administration from a controller or contracts manager
  willing to explain why it's done differently here, and getting pulled
  into that kind of mentoring usually comes down to asking good
  questions and being easy to work with, not already knowing the
  answers. See [[resume-business|the Business Roles Resume Guide]] for how to make that construction context visible
  instead of assumed.
checklist:
  - step: "Translate your background into construction-specific language"
    detail: "Certified payroll instead of just 'payroll,' subcontract administration instead of just 'contracts,' lien waivers, prevailing wage, and named software like Procore or Sage 300 CRE, exactly as the Business Roles Resume Guide below lays out."
  - step: "Apply directly to GC, subcontractor, and owner back-office openings"
    detail: "General contractors, construction management firms, and large subcontractors all run their own accounting, HR, contracts, and procurement functions, and applying directly to them beats a generic corporate job board search."
  - step: "Use an industry-specific association for your function"
    detail: "The Construction Financial Management Association (CFMA) for accounting and finance roles, SHRM's construction-focused communities for HR, are real networking channels most general job seekers never think to use."
  - step: "Lead with any relevant credential you already hold"
    detail: "A CPA license, SHRM-CP or PHR, a payroll certification. These matter here just as much as they would anywhere else, and they're an easy way to signal seriousness fast."
  - step: "Research what's actually different about the specific company you're applying to"
    detail: "Union versus open shop payroll, public versus private contract work, these change what the role actually involves, and showing up with that context already understood is a real differentiator."
  - step: "Be ready to answer what you already know about how construction differs from other industries"
    detail: "This is a real, commonly asked interview question in this category, and having a genuine, specific answer ready matters more than a generic one."

commonMistakes:
  - "Assuming you need to already understand every construction-specific process before you apply. Most of it is learned from a mentor in your department, and showing real curiosity about why it's done differently here reads better at the interview stage than pretending you already know."
  - "Submitting a fully generic resume with zero construction context, which reads as not yet understanding what's different about the work."
  - "Making vague compliance claims like 'ensured compliance' without naming which regulation or process, certified payroll, lien waivers, prevailing wage, you actually handled."
  - "Burying a real, relevant credential near the bottom of the resume instead of near the top where it gets seen first."
  - "Walking into the interview without a real answer for how construction differs from other industries, which is often one of the very first questions asked in this category."
firstStepToday: "Find one open accounting, HR, contracts, or procurement role at a local general contractor or subcontractor, and identify specifically what construction context the posting is asking for before you apply."
