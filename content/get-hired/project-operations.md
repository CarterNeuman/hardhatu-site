howHiringWorks: >
  Hiring for project managers, superintendents, schedulers, and project
  engineers runs through fairly traditional channels, a company's own
  careers page, LinkedIn, and a construction-specific recruiter, but the
  market behind those channels looks better than a lot of people assume
  in 2026. The Associated General Contractors' 2026 outlook found that
  63 percent of firms plan to grow headcount, yet 80 percent report real
  difficulty filling salaried openings, and the Bureau of Labor
  Statistics projects roughly 49,700 construction management openings a
  year through 2035. That combination, growing demand and a genuine
  supply gap, means an experienced or well-prepared candidate has more
  leverage here than the general "tough job market" headlines suggest.
  What gets you through the door is almost always a specific, quantified
  project track record rather than a job title alone: the general
  contractors, construction management firms, and owners with in-house
  construction teams who hire for this category want to see the dollar
  value, delivery method, and software you've actually worked in. You
  also don't need a decade of this before it counts. Most project
  managers and superintendents started as an assistant PM or field
  engineer on someone else's job, learning how a schedule or a budget
  actually holds up under pressure by watching it happen in real time,
  and the ones who move up fastest are usually the ones who stayed
  level-headed, asked good questions, and made the super or PM above
  them want to bring them onto the next project. See [[resume-project-operations|the Project & Operations Resume Guide]], which
  breaks down how to put that on the page.
checklist:
  - step: "Quantify your project track record before you apply anywhere"
    detail: "Dollar value, square footage, delivery method, specific software. This is the single biggest thing that separates a resume that gets a callback from one that doesn't in this category, and the Project & Operations Resume Guide below breaks down exactly how to phrase it."
  - step: "Go directly to general contractors, CM firms, and owner in-house teams"
    detail: "These are the three types of companies that actually hire this category, and a lot of their current openings never make it to a generic job board. Check their own careers pages directly."
  - step: "Work with a construction-specific recruiter"
    detail: "Construction has its own niche of staffing and recruiting firms that work these roles specifically, often with access to openings that aren't posted publicly. One real relationship with a recruiter who knows your market is worth more than a dozen cold applications."
  - step: "Get active in AGC or CMAA, even before you have the job"
    detail: "Associated General Contractors and the Construction Management Association of America both run local chapter events built for exactly this kind of networking, and a lot of hiring in this category still happens through a warm introduction."
  - step: "Prepare for a scenario-based interview, not a generic one"
    detail: "Expect real questions about schedule changes, budget overruns, and subcontractor conflicts, not just a resume walkthrough."
  - step: "Follow up with something specific, not a generic thank-you note"
    detail: "Reference a real detail from the conversation, a project they mentioned, a challenge they described, since it signals you were actually listening, not just going through the motions."
whereToLook:

  - "Construction Management Association of America (CMAA) local chapter networking"
  - "LinkedIn, specifically following and engaging with construction companies and industry groups rather than just browsing postings"
credentialsToHaveReady:
  - "A resume built around quantified project scope, not just job titles"
  - "PMP or CAPM certification, if you're pursuing the formal project management track"
  - "OSHA 30, especially for a field-facing role like superintendent or assistant superintendent"
  - "Named software fluency: Procore, Primavera P6, Microsoft Project, Bluebeam"
commonMistakes:
  - "Believing you need years of experience before this category will take you seriously. Most people break in as an assistant PM or field engineer with one real project behind them and a superintendent willing to walk them through the rest."
  - "Applying only through generic job boards and skipping the direct outreach and recruiter relationships that actually fill most of these roles."
  - "Listing job titles and responsibilities without a single number attached, no project value, no team size, no outcome."
  - "Treating a GC, a CM firm, and an owner's in-house team as interchangeable employers. They screen differently, and knowing which one you're talking to changes how you should frame your experience."
  - "Walking into a scenario-based interview prepared only to talk about what you did, instead of how you'd handle a specific problem."
firstStepToday: "Pick 5 general contractors or construction management firms in your target market, look at their current openings directly on their own site, and reach out to one construction-specific recruiter this week."
