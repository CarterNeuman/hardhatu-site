---
id: concept-building-deconstruction
title: Building Deconstruction
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "02, Existing Conditions"
definition: >
  The systematic disassembly of a building to recover reusable materials --
  lumber, brick, fixtures, structural steel -- rather than demolishing it in
  place and sending the debris to a landfill.
whyItMatters: >
  Deconstruction costs more in labor than straight demolition because it's
  slower and more careful, but it diverts material from landfills and can
  recover valuable material, which some jurisdictions and
  sustainability-focused projects now require or incentivize.
example: >
  Before demolishing a 1920s warehouse, a contractor deconstructs its
  heavy-timber framing and recovers the old-growth lumber for resale to a
  salvage yard, rather than crushing it along with the rest of the structure.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-demolition-supervisor|Demolition Supervisor]] sequences the
  disassembly to protect recoverable material, and a
  [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]]
  tracks the diverted material toward a project's waste-diversion goals.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew is told to deconstruct a building for material recovery but works at
  demolition speed, damaging lumber and fixtures that could have been resold,
  turning what was supposed to be a cost-offsetting recovery effort into a net
  loss.
