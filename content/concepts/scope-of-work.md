---
id: concept-scope-of-work
title: Scope of Work
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The detailed written description in a contract of exactly what work is included,
  and, just as importantly, what isn't, that both sides use to determine whether
  something is part of the original price or a separate, billable addition.
whyItMatters: >
  Almost every payment dispute on a project eventually comes back to the same question:
  was this actually in the original scope of work, or not? A clear scope of work is what
  gives both sides a real answer instead of a disagreement based on memory or
  assumptions.
example: >
  A renovation contract's scope of work explicitly lists "replace kitchen flooring" but
  does not mention the adjacent hallway. When the owner assumes the hallway floor was
  included too, the written scope is what settles the disagreement: it wasn't.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] writes the scope of work to match exactly what was
  priced, since a vague or incomplete scope becomes the estimator's problem the moment a
  dispute happens. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] refers back to it
  constantly during construction to decide whether a request is a legitimate
  [[concept-change-order|change order]] or something that was already included.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contract's scope of work is written loosely, listing "site work" without spelling
  out specifics. When unexpected utility relocation turns out to be needed, the owner
  insists it was already covered under "site work," while the contractor insists it
  wasn't, a disagreement a more specific scope of work would have prevented from ever
  starting.
