---
id: concept-come-along
title: Come-Along
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  A hand-operated ratchet puller, hooked between two points and cranked to
  generate pulling force, used during steel erection to pull a slightly
  out-of-square or out-of-plumb member back into its correct position before
  it's permanently bolted or welded.
whyItMatters: >
  Structural steel members don't always land in perfect position the instant
  they're set, due to small fabrication tolerances or the weight of the piece
  itself. A come-along gives an ironworker enough controlled, adjustable force
  to nudge a member into alignment without needing powered equipment for a
  small correction.
example: >
  An ironworker finds a steel beam sitting slightly out of square after it's
  set by the crane, and uses a come-along hooked to an adjacent column to pull
  it gradually into alignment before the connection is bolted up permanently.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] keeps a come-along as standard hand tool
  equipment during steel erection, using it constantly for the small alignment
  corrections that come up on nearly every piece set.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew bolts up a slightly misaligned connection permanently rather than
  taking the time to pull it into true alignment with a come-along first. The
  small misalignment compounds as more steel is erected on top of it, becoming
  a much larger and more expensive problem to fix later in the sequence.
