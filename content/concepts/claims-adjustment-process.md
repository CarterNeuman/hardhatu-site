title: Claims Adjustment Process
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The overall sequence a property insurance claim moves through, from
  [[concept-first-notice-of-loss|first notice of loss]] through investigation,
  scoping, negotiation, payment, and any later
  [[concept-supplement-claims|supplements]], that every insurance-side role on a
  restoration project touches at a different stage.
whyItMatters: >
  Understanding where a specific claim sits in this overall process is what lets
  everyone involved, the policyholder, the adjuster, the contractor, know what
  should happen next and who's actually responsible for making it happen, rather
  than each side guessing at the other's timeline.
example: >
  A water damage claim moves from first notice of loss, to an adjuster's inspection
  and scope of loss, through negotiation with a public adjuster over disputed items,
  to a settlement payment, and finally to a supplement once concealed damage is
  found during reconstruction, each stage handled by a different party with a
  different role.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] manages the process end to end
  from the insurer's side, moving a claim through each stage. A
  [[career-public-adjuster|Public Adjuster]] represents the policyholder through
  that same process, often pushing back at the stages where the two sides' scopes
  diverge.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A claim stalls for weeks because nobody clearly owns the handoff between the
  emergency mitigation stage and the full scope-of-loss inspection, with the
  restoration company assuming the adjuster would schedule it and the adjuster
  assuming the contractor would request it. The delay itself lets damage get worse
  while the claim sits unassigned to either party's next step.
