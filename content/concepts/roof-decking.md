---
id: concept-roof-decking
title: Roof Decking
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal and Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  The wood sheathing layer installed directly over roof framing that shingles
  or other roofing material get fastened to, distinct from steel decking,
  which is a completely different material used in commercial structural floor
  and roof systems.
whyItMatters: >
  Roof decking has to be structurally sound for shingles to stay properly
  fastened and for the roof to perform at all. Water damage that gets past a
  failing roof covering often soaks into the decking underneath, causing it to
  go soft or spongy, a condition restoration crews specifically check for
  since it means more than just the surface shingles need replacing.
example: >
  During a roof replacement after storm damage, a restoration crew finds a
  section of roof decking that's gone soft and spongy from prior water
  intrusion, and replaces that section of decking entirely before installing
  new shingles over it.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]] checks
  the condition of roof decking during a storm-damage assessment, since
  decking that's deteriorated changes the scope of repair well beyond just the
  visible shingle damage.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A roofing crew installs new shingles directly over decking that's already
  gone soft from prior water damage, without checking the decking's condition
  first. The new roof doesn't hold its fasteners properly, and the problem
  resurfaces as a leak within a relatively short time.
