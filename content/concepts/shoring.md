id: concept-shoring
title: Shoring
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  A temporary support system, such as trench boxes, hydraulic shores, or sloped/benched
  walls, that protects workers from a cave-in while they're inside an excavation,
  selected and installed according to the specific soil type and excavation depth
  rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.
whyItMatters: >
  OSHA requires a designated competent person to select the shoring or protective
  system appropriate for the actual soil conditions present, since a protective
  system rated for one soil classification can be inadequate for a different, less
  stable soil type even at the same excavation depth.
example: >
  A trench dug through unstable, previously disturbed soil is protected with a trench
  box rated for that specific soil classification, selected by the site's competent
  person after evaluating the actual soil conditions rather than assuming a standard
  trench box would automatically be sufficient.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] places trench boxes and
  installs shoring equipment to the competent person's specification. A
  [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] verifies the selected protective system
  actually matches the excavation's documented soil classification, since choosing
  the wrong protection class is a common and serious excavation safety failure.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew reuses a trench box from an earlier, more stable section of a project
  without re-evaluating soil conditions at a new location where the soil is
  previously disturbed and less stable. The trench box, rated for the earlier soil
  type, isn't adequate for the new conditions, and the mismatch isn't caught until
  the site's competent person performs the required inspection before work resumes.
