---
id: concept-apprenticeship-program
title: Apprenticeship Program
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A structured, employer-sponsored training path combining paid on-the-job
  work with related classroom instruction, typically registered with the
  Department of Labor or a state apprenticeship agency, leading to
  journey-level certification in a specific trade over several years.
whyItMatters: >
  Many public contracts require a minimum percentage of apprentice labor
  hours on the job, which turns apprenticeship from just a workforce
  pipeline into a compliance obligation a contractor has to actively track,
  not something it can leave informal.
example: >
  A registered apprentice works under a journeyworker electrician for four
  years, combining paid field hours with required classroom coursework, before
  qualifying to test for journey-level certification.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-hr-manager-construction|HR Manager]] runs a contractor's
  apprenticeship program and tracks apprentice-to-journeyworker ratios against
  whatever public contracts require them.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor bidding public work that requires a set percentage of
  apprentice labor hours doesn't actually track its apprentice-to-journeyworker
  ratio on site day to day. It falls short of the required ratio without
  noticing, and a competing bidder's protest challenging the contractor's
  compliance on its current public project surfaces the shortfall,
  jeopardizing its eligibility for the next one.
