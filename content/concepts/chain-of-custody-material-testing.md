id: concept-chain-of-custody-material-testing
title: Chain of Custody (Material Testing)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The documented record tracking a material sample, like a concrete cylinder or
  a soil sample, from the moment it's taken on site through transport,
  storage, and lab testing, establishing who handled it and when at every
  step.
whyItMatters: >
  A test result is only as credible as the chain of custody behind it; a sample
  with a gap in its documented handling, or one that sat improperly stored for
  an unexplained stretch of time, can have its test result challenged or
  thrown out entirely regardless of how the actual test came back.
example: >
  Concrete cylinders taken during a structural pour are logged with the exact
  time and location they were cast, then tracked through curing, transport to
  the lab, and testing, with each handoff documented. When a later dispute
  questions the pour's strength, the unbroken chain of custody is what lets
  the test results actually be relied on as evidence.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] documents the chain of custody
  when samples are taken on site, and the
  [[career-construction-claims-consultant|Construction Claims Consultant]]
  relies on an intact chain of custody when using test results as evidence in
  a dispute.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A material sample is taken on site but its handling afterward isn't properly
  documented, and when a later dispute turns on that sample's test result, the
  gap in custody gives the other side grounds to challenge whether the result
  can be trusted at all.
