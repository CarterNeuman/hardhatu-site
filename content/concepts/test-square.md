---
id: concept-test-square
title: Test Square (Roof Hail Inspection)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A marked-off section of roof, commonly ten feet by ten feet, used during a
  hail damage inspection to count the number of hail hits within that known
  area, giving a consistent, repeatable way to measure damage severity across
  the whole roof.
whyItMatters: >
  Counting hail hits across an entire roof by eye isn't reliable or consistent
  from one inspector to another. A test square gives everyone involved, the
  insurance adjuster, the contractor, and the homeowner, a shared, measurable
  standard: a specific number of hits within a specific, known area, rather
  than a subjective impression of how damaged a roof looks.
example: >
  An insurance adjuster marks off several ten-by-ten test squares at different
  spots on a roof and counts the hail hits within each one, using that count
  to determine whether the damage meets the threshold the insurance policy
  considers reportable.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] uses test squares as the
  standard method for documenting and justifying a hail damage assessment on a
  roof claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An inspection counts hail hits across the whole roof without using a defined
  test square, giving an impression of damage severity that isn't tied to any
  measurable, defensible standard. The resulting damage assessment is harder
  to support if the insurer disputes the claim later.
