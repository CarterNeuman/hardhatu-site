id: concept-life-safety-code-nfpa-101
title: Life Safety Code / NFPA 101
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  NFPA's consensus standard covering egress and fire/life safety in buildings,
  widely adopted by reference into state and local building codes, and separately
  required for accreditation by healthcare, assisted-living, and other
  institutional bodies regardless of what the local building code otherwise
  requires.
whyItMatters: >
  A building can meet its local building code and still fail an NFPA 101 survey,
  because an accrediting body's own life-safety inspection applies the code's more
  detailed occupancy-specific chapters, healthcare in particular, that a general
  building permit review doesn't always dig into as deeply.
example: >
  A hospital renovation gets a building permit under the general building code, but
  still has to separately satisfy NFPA 101's healthcare occupancy chapter before its
  accrediting body will sign off, a stricter, more detailed set of requirements
  layered on top of the permit.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-permit-expediter|Permit Expediter]] working on institutional projects
  has to track both the building department's requirements and NFPA 101's own
  occupancy-specific rules, since satisfying one doesn't guarantee satisfying the
  other.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A hospital renovation team designs to the general building code alone, assuming
  that covers life safety, and only discovers NFPA 101's stricter healthcare
  occupancy requirements during the accreditation survey, well after construction
  is finished, forcing costly rework to correct.
