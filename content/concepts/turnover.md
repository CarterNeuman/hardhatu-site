---
id: concept-turnover
title: Turnover
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The formal handover of a completed project, or a completed phase of one on a project
  turned over in stages, from the contractor to the owner, transferring keys, operating
  responsibility, and usually insurance coverage for the space from the builder to the
  people who'll actually run it.
whyItMatters: >
  A building that's physically finished still needs someone to actually take
  responsibility for it: who's insuring it, who's paying utilities, who's on the hook
  if something breaks. Turnover is the specific moment that responsibility legally and
  practically shifts from the contractor to the owner.
example: >
  On a hotel renovated one floor at a time while the rest stays open, each finished
  floor is turned over to hotel management individually: keys handed over, systems
  demonstrated, and that floor placed back into service, well before the entire project
  reaches final [[concept-closeout|closeout]].
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] coordinates turnover, timing it against
  substantial completion and making sure the owner has what they need to actually take
  over. A [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] is often the one on the
  receiving end, since it's their team that starts running and maintaining the space the
  moment turnover happens.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A hotel wing is turned over to management before the fire alarm system is fully
  commissioned, because the schedule called for it and everyone assumed the remaining
  punch list items were minor. Guests move in before the safety systems are fully
  verified, an outcome that should have blocked turnover entirely, regardless of how
  good the rest of the schedule looked.
