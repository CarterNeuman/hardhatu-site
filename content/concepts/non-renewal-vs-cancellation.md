---
id: concept-non-renewal-vs-cancellation
title: Non-Renewal vs. Cancellation (Policy)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  Two distinct ways coverage can end: cancellation terminates a policy before
  its scheduled expiration (usually for nonpayment or material
  misrepresentation), while non-renewal lets the policy run to its expiration
  date but the insurer declines to offer a new term.
whyItMatters: >
  The two carry different notice periods and different legal protections, so
  confusing them can cause a property owner to misjudge how much time they
  have to find replacement coverage before a repair project is left uninsured
  mid-job.
example: >
  A homeowner receives a non-renewal notice 60 days before their policy expires
  because the insurer is pulling out of wind-prone coastal zip codes, giving
  them a window to shop for a new carrier before the current policy lapses.
careerAngle: >
  Restoration contractors scheduling multi-week jobs should ask whether the
  property's policy is being renewed or cancelled, since a lapse mid-project
  leaves nobody covering further damage.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People often assume non-renewal implies some fault on their part, like a
  cancellation for nonpayment would, when insurers frequently non-renew entire
  blocks of policies for reasons that have nothing to do with the individual
  policyholder.
