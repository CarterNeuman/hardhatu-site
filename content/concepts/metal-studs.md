id: concept-metal-studs
title: Metal Studs
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  Light-gauge, C-shaped steel framing members used to build interior partition walls
  and, in some cases, exterior wall backup framing, the modern standard replacement
  for wood studs in most commercial construction due to steel's dimensional
  consistency and fire resistance.
whyItMatters: >
  Metal stud spacing, gauge (thickness), and bracing all have to match what the
  drawings specify for a given wall's height and loading; a wall that looks the same
  from the outside can be framed to very different structural standards depending on
  what it's actually required to support.
example: >
  A commercial interior buildout frames partition walls with metal studs spaced at the
  specification's required interval, with additional bracing and heavier-gauge studs
  used specifically at walls tall enough to need the extra support.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] typically frames metal stud walls in commercial
  construction, a skill set that overlaps with but differs somewhat from wood framing.
  A [[career-drywall-installer|Drywall Installer]] depends on properly spaced and
  plumb stud framing underneath, since drywall applied over an out-of-plumb wall
  telegraphs every framing flaw straight through to the finished surface.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framing crew uses a lighter stud gauge than specified on a tall partition wall to
  save material cost, and the substitution isn't caught before drywall covers it. The
  wall develops a visible bow over time under normal building movement, a defect that
  traces directly back to framing that didn't meet the wall's actual height and
  loading requirements.
