id: concept-daylighting-design
title: Daylighting Design
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Designing a building's windows, skylights, and interior layout specifically
  to maximize     the use of natural light for occupied spaces, reducing
  dependence on artificial lighting     during daytime hours while managing
  glare and solar heat gain that excessive daylight can     also introduce.
whyItMatters: >
  Daylighting credits are a scoring category in most green building
  certifications and also     correlate with occupant wellbeing and
  productivity, but daylighting has to be balanced     against solar heat
  gain, since a design that floods a space with light without controlling
  heat gain can actually increase cooling loads enough to offset the lighting
  energy savings.
example: >
  An office building's design team positions interior workstations within
  daylight-reach of     perimeter windows and adds light shelves above the
  glazing to bounce daylight deeper into the     floor plate, reducing how
  often the automated lighting controls need to switch on overhead
  fixtures during business hours.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] balances daylighting goals against
  solar heat gain,     often designing automated shading or dimming controls
  that respond to how much natural light     is actually reaching a space
  rather than running on a fixed schedule.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A design maximizes window area for daylighting without addressing solar heat
  gain, and the     resulting glare and cooling load force occupants to keep
  blinds permanently closed, defeating     the daylighting strategy the large
  windows were installed to achieve in the first place.
