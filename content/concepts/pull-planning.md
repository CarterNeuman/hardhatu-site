---
id: concept-pull-planning
title: Pull Planning
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A collaborative scheduling technique, done by getting foremen and trade partners
  around a wall of sticky notes, where the team works backward from a target milestone
  to plan the next several weeks of work together, rather than a scheduler dictating
  the sequence alone from an office.
whyItMatters: >
  The people who actually execute a schedule's assumptions are the trades doing the work
  day to day, and pull planning gets their input before a plan is finalized instead of
  after it's already failing. It tends to surface sequencing conflicts and unrealistic
  durations that an office-built schedule alone would miss.
example: >
  Before a building's mechanical, electrical, and plumbing rough-in phase, the
  superintendent runs a pull-planning session where each trade foreman places sticky
  notes representing their tasks on a wall, working backward from the drywall start
  date until every trade agrees on a sequence that actually works for all of them.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] typically facilitates pull-planning
  sessions, since building buy-in from the trades who'll execute the plan is the whole
  point of the exercise. A [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] takes the results of a
  pull-planning session and folds the agreed sequence into the
  [[concept-look-ahead-schedule|look-ahead schedule]] and master schedule.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A superintendent runs a pull-planning session but then builds the official schedule
  differently from what the trades actually agreed to in the room, prioritizing the
  sequence the office originally wanted. The trades show up expecting the plan they
  helped build, and the mismatch causes the same coordination conflicts pull planning
  was supposed to prevent.
