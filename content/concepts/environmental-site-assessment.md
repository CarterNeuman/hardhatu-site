id: concept-environmental-site-assessment
title: Environmental Site Assessment (Phase I/II ESA)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A standardized process for evaluating a property's environmental contamination
  risk before purchase or development. A Phase I ESA is a non-intrusive review of
  records, historical use, and a site visit, if it flags a real concern, a Phase II
  ESA adds actual soil and groundwater sampling to confirm or rule out
  contamination.
whyItMatters: >
  A lender or buyer typically won't finance a purchase without a clean Phase I on
  file, which makes the assessment a financing gate as much as an environmental
  one, skip it and the deal itself may not be able to close, independent of whether
  contamination actually exists.
example: >
  A developer purchasing a former gas station site commissions a Phase I ESA,
  which flags historical fuel storage as a concern, triggering a Phase II ESA with
  actual soil sampling before the lender will finance the purchase.
careerAngle: >
  An
  [[career-environmental-remediation-specialist|Environmental/Remediation Specialist]]
  conducts or oversees the assessment, while a
  [[career-risk-manager-construction|Risk Manager]] uses the results to evaluate
  the deal's real risk before the owner commits.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A developer skips a Phase I ESA on a former industrial site to save time and
  money, then discovers contaminated soil mid-excavation. The project halts for
  remediation, and the resulting cost and delay dwarf what the assessment would
  have cost upfront.
