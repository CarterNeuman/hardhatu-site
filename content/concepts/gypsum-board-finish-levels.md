id: concept-gypsum-board-finish-levels
title: Gypsum Board Finish Levels
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  A standardized scale, Level 0 through Level 5, defined by the Gypsum
  Association's GA-214 standard, describing how much taping, compound, and
  sanding is applied to a drywall installation before it's considered
  finished, ranging from Level 0, no finishing at all, to Level 5, a full skim
  coat for the most demanding paint finishes and lighting conditions.
whyItMatters: >
  Specifying the wrong finish level for a space's actual use either wastes
  money finishing a back-of-house storage room to a showroom standard, or
  leaves a highly visible space with a finish level too rough for the paint
  sheen and lighting it's actually getting, a mismatch that only becomes
  obvious once the space is painted and lit.
example: >
  A general contractor specifies Level 1 finish for drywall above a suspended
  ceiling, where it's never seen, but Level 5 finish for a lobby wall
  receiving glossy paint under wall-wash lighting that would show every
  imperfection a lower finish level would leave visible.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-drywall-installer|Drywall Installer]] finishes each area to the
  specified level, since each level requires a genuinely different number of
  compound coats and amount of sanding, not just more effort applied
  uniformly everywhere.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A drywall contractor finishes an entire building's walls to a uniform Level
  4, the common default, without checking which specific walls the
  specification called out for Level 5. The lobby's glossy paint under
  wall-wash lighting reveals visible seams and fastener patterns the Level 4
  finish wasn't meant to hide, and the wall has to be re-finished before the
  owner will accept it.
