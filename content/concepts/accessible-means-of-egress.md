---
id: concept-accessible-means-of-egress
title: Accessible Means of Egress
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  An exit path specifically designed so a person who cannot use stairs can
  evacuate independently or wait safely for assistance, typically through an
  accessible route to an exit, an area of refuge, or an elevator designated
  for emergency use by trained responders.
whyItMatters: >
  Standard means of egress requirements, covering general exit width and travel
  distance, don't by themselves guarantee a wheelchair user can actually get
  out of a multi-story building during a fire, which is exactly the gap
  accessible means of egress requirements exist to close.
example: >
  A multi-story building's design includes a fire-rated area of refuge adjacent
  to an exit stairwell, where a wheelchair user can wait safely with a two-way
  communication system to alert responders, since the stairwell itself isn't
  usable without that assistance.
careerAngle: >
  Architects and code consultants need to verify accessible egress separately
  from general egress requirements, since a building can satisfy standard
  exit-width and travel-distance rules while still failing to provide any way
  for a mobility-impaired occupant to actually evacuate.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume that meeting standard egress requirements for exit
  width and number of exits automatically covers accessibility too, when a
  mobility-impaired occupant needs a specifically designed accessible route,
  refuge area, or evacuation elevator that standard egress calculations don't
  address.
