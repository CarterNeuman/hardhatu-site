id: concept-concurrent-delay
title: Concurrent Delay
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A situation where both the owner and the contractor cause independent
  delays to the project's critical path at the same time, complicating who's
  responsible for the resulting schedule impact, since neither party's delay
  alone fully explains the overrun.
whyItMatters: >
  A concurrent delay finding doesn't split responsibility evenly, it
  typically limits what either side can recover from the other for that
  overlapping period, which is exactly why an owner's forensic scheduler
  actively looks for it in every delay claim.
example: >
  A contractor's own labor shortage delays framing during the same two-week
  window an owner-caused design change is also working its way through
  approval on the project's critical path, a genuine concurrent delay that
  complicates the contractor's claim for that period.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-scheduling-delay-consultant|Scheduling & Delay Consultant]]
  investigates whether a claimed delay period actually overlaps with a
  separate, independent delay the other party caused.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor claims full compensation for an owner-caused delay without
  acknowledging that its own separate, independent delay was happening on
  the critical path during that same period. The owner's forensic scheduler
  catches the concurrency, substantially reducing what the contractor can
  actually recover.
