---
id: concept-fragnet
title: Fragnet (Schedule Fragment)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A small, self-contained piece of schedule logic, a handful of new or modified
  activities with their own durations and relationships, inserted into an
  existing schedule to model the time impact of a specific change, like a
  design revision or an added scope item.
whyItMatters: >
  Rather than rebuilding an entire schedule from scratch to evaluate one change,
  a fragnet isolates just that change's activities and logic, which makes it
  the standard building block for a formal delay or time-impact analysis,
  since it shows precisely how much time the specific change actually added.
example: >
  An owner issues a late design change requiring additional structural
  reinforcement. The scheduler builds a fragnet modeling the new reinforcement
  activities and their logic ties to the existing schedule, then inserts it to
  calculate exactly how many days that specific change pushes back the
  project's finish date.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] builds the fragnet representing a change's
  added activities and logic ties, and the
  [[career-scheduling-delay-consultant|Scheduling Delay Consultant]] uses the
  resulting fragnet as the core evidence in a formal delay claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A party claims a change caused a certain number of days of delay without
  building an actual fragnet showing the added activities and their logic ties
  into the existing schedule, leaving the claim without the structured
  evidence a delay analysis actually requires.
