id: concept-competent-person
title: Competent Person
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  An OSHA term for a worker who is capable of identifying existing and predictable
  hazards in a specific area of work and who has the authority to take prompt corrective
  action to eliminate them, a designation tied to demonstrated knowledge and authority,
  not a job title or a certificate.
whyItMatters: >
  Several of OSHA's highest-consequence standards, such as excavation, scaffolding, and
  fall protection, specifically require a designated competent person to inspect conditions
  and make judgment calls, rather than relying on a generic checklist anyone could apply.
  Naming someone competent person for an area they aren't actually qualified for defeats
  the entire purpose of the requirement.
example: >
  Before workers enter a trench deeper than five feet, the crew's designated competent
  person inspects the soil conditions and confirms the protective system in place is
  adequate, a judgment call OSHA specifically requires that named individual to make.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] often works directly under a
  competent person's direction when placing shoring or trench protection. A
  [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] is typically the one who formally designates
  competent persons across a project and verifies their inspections are actually
  happening and documented.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A company designates a crew lead as the competent person for excavation work mainly
  because of seniority, without confirming they actually have the training to evaluate
  soil conditions. An inspection that should have caught an inadequate protective system
  gets signed off anyway, because the person signing it wasn't equipped to catch the
  problem in the first place.
