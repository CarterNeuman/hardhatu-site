---
id: concept-backflow-preventer
title: Backflow Preventer
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "22, Plumbing"
definition: >
  A plumbing device installed on a water supply line specifically to stop
  contaminated water from flowing backward into a building's or municipality's clean
  drinking water supply, required at any connection point where a cross-connection
  to a non-potable source (an irrigation system, a fire sprinkler system, a boiler)
  creates a realistic risk of backflow.
whyItMatters: >
  Backflow preventers are one of the few plumbing devices that require regular,
  code-mandated testing by a certified tester after installation, not just at
  initial inspection, precisely because a device that fails silently could allow
  contamination into a drinking water system without anyone noticing until people are
  already affected.
example: >
  A building's irrigation system, which draws from the same water supply as the
  drinking water system, includes a backflow preventer at the connection point,
  certified and tested annually to confirm it's still functioning to prevent
  contaminated irrigation water from siphoning back into the potable water supply.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] installs backflow preventers at every required
  cross-connection point per code and the local water authority's requirements. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] confirms the required
  initial certification testing is completed and documented before the system is
  accepted.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project installs an irrigation system tied into the building's potable water
  supply without a required backflow preventer, treating it as a minor detail since
  the irrigation system itself doesn't seem hazardous. A local water authority
  inspection catches the missing device, and the project has to add it after the fact,
  a straightforward fix, but one that should have been caught in the original
  plumbing design and permit review.
