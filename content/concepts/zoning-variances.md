id: concept-zoning-variances
title: Zoning & Variances
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  Zoning regulates how land within a jurisdiction can be used and built on, such as
  permitted use, building height, setbacks from property lines, and parking requirements, separate
  from [[concept-building-code|building code]], which governs how a building is
  constructed rather than what can be built where. A variance is a specific, formally
  granted exception when a project can't meet a zoning requirement as written.
whyItMatters: >
  A project can be fully compliant with building code and still be legally impossible to
  build if it doesn't fit the site's zoning, such as a height limit, a setback, or a use
  restriction. Because zoning is decided locally and case by case, a design that would be
  routine in one jurisdiction can require a variance, or be outright prohibited, in
  another.
example: >
  A proposed building's design exceeds the site's zoning height limit by a small margin.
  Rather than redesigning the entire building, the owner applies for a height variance,
  presenting the case to the local zoning board before construction can proceed as
  designed.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] often manages the variance
  application process on the owner's behalf, since it typically involves public hearings
  and negotiation well outside normal construction scope. A
  [[career-preconstruction-manager|Preconstruction Manager]] flags a likely zoning
  conflict early, during constructability review, before the design is far enough along
  that a variance becomes the only option.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A design team finalizes a building's height and footprint without confirming it fits
  the site's actual zoning limits, assuming the parcel's use was the only zoning
  consideration that mattered. The project later has to pursue a variance it could have
  avoided entirely with an earlier zoning check, adding real schedule risk to a
  process outside the design team's control.
