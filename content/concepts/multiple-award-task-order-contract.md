id: concept-multiple-award-task-order-contract
title: Multiple Award Task Order Contract (MATOC)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A procurement arrangement, common on public and government work, in which an
  owner awards a pool of pre-qualified contractors an indefinite-quantity
  contract vehicle, then issues individual task orders to specific contractors
  in that pool as actual project work comes up, similar in spirit to
  [[concept-job-order-contracting|Job Order Contracting]] but spread across a
  pool of contractors instead of one pre-priced catalog with a single
  contractor.
whyItMatters: >
  It lets an owner skip a full competitive procurement for every single project,
  since the pool of contractors has already been qualified once, but
  individual task orders are still typically awarded based on some combination
  of price and capacity among the pool.
example: >
  A military installation awards a MATOC to five pre-qualified general
  contractors for routine facility repairs and small renovations. When a roof
  replacement project comes up, the installation issues a task order to
  whichever contractor in that pool best fits the scope and current workload,
  without running an entirely new competitive bid.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] manages the pool of
  pre-qualified contractors and the task order award process under a MATOC,
  and the [[career-business-development-manager|Business Development Manager]]
  pursues getting a company qualified into relevant MATOC pools as a
  business-development strategy.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor assumes winning a spot in a MATOC pool guarantees actual project
  work, when it only guarantees the chance to be considered for task orders as
  they come up, with no work volume promised.
