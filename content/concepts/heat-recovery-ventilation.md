id: concept-heat-recovery-ventilation
title: Heat-Recovery Ventilation (HRV/ERV)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "23, HVAC"
definition: >
  Mechanical ventilation equipment that captures heat, and in some units
  moisture, from the air being exhausted out of a building and uses it to
  pre-warm or pre-cool the fresh air being brought in, rather than simply
  exhausting that energy to the outside.
whyItMatters: >
  A tightly sealed, energy-efficient building still needs fresh air brought in
  continuously, but bringing in untreated outside air would waste much of the
  energy savings the tight envelope was built to achieve. Heat-recovery
  ventilation is what lets a building get fresh air without giving up most of
  that efficiency, which is why it's central to standards like Passive House.
example: >
  A Passive House project installs an energy-recovery ventilator that captures
  heat and humidity from stale indoor air being exhausted and transfers it to
  the incoming fresh air stream, maintaining comfortable, efficient
  ventilation without the energy loss untreated outside air would otherwise
  cause.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] installs and balances
  heat-recovery ventilation equipment, a different system from a standard
  furnace or air handler, since its entire purpose is energy recovery rather
  than simply heating or cooling air.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A tightly sealed, energy-efficient building is ventilated with a standard
  exhaust fan system instead of heat-recovery ventilation, since the two look
  similar on paper. The building ends up losing much of the energy efficiency
  its tight envelope was designed to achieve, since all that conditioned air's
  energy simply vents straight outside.
