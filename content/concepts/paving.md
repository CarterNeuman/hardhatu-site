id: concept-paving
title: Paving
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "32, Exterior Improvements"
definition: >
  Asphalt or concrete surface construction for a site's parking lots, drives, and
  walkways, built up in layers (compacted subgrade, aggregate base, then the
  finished asphalt or concrete surface) where each layer's proper compaction matters
  as much as the visible top layer's quality.
whyItMatters: >
  Pavement failures, such as potholes, cracking, or settling, almost always trace back to
  inadequate compaction or drainage in the layers underneath, not a defect in the
  visible asphalt or concrete itself, which is why testing the base layers is as
  important as inspecting the finished surface.
example: >
  Before asphalt paving begins, the contractor compacts the aggregate base to the
  specified density and confirms it with field density testing, since a well-compacted
  base is what keeps the finished pavement from settling and cracking under traffic
  loads later.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] runs the paving and compaction
  equipment to the plan's grades and specified compaction. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] reviews compaction test
  results on the base layers before the paving surface goes down, since a compaction
  problem is essentially impossible to verify or fix once it's paved over.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A paving crew places asphalt over a base layer that wasn't compacted to the
  specified density, under pressure to keep the schedule moving before a scheduled
  inspection. Within the pavement's first year, the under-compacted base settles
  unevenly under vehicle traffic, causing cracking and depressions that trace directly
  back to a base layer that should have been tested and re-compacted before paving.
