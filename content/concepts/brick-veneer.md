id: concept-brick-veneer
title: Brick Veneer
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "04, Masonry"
definition: >
  A single layer of brick attached to the outside of a building's actual structural
  wall (wood frame, steel, or CMU) purely as an exterior finish: the brick carries its
  own weight but isn't part of the structure holding the building up, unlike a solid
  masonry wall.
whyItMatters: >
  Because brick veneer isn't structural, the connection details behind it, the air
  gap, flashing, weep holes, and ties that anchor it back to the structural wall, are
  what actually keep water from getting behind the brick and into the building, and
  those details are entirely hidden once the veneer is finished.
example: >
  A wood-framed house has brick veneer installed with a required air gap behind it,
  flashing at the base and above openings to direct any water that gets behind the
  brick back out through weep holes, rather than letting it soak into the wood
  framing.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mason|Mason]] installs brick veneer and its ties, but coordinating the
  flashing behind it, often installed by a different trade, is what determines
  whether the assembly actually keeps water out. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] inspects flashing and
  weep hole placement before the brick veneer covers it up for good.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A mason installs brick veneer over flashing that wasn't properly lapped by the trade
  that installed it, creating a gap water can pass through. Years after occupancy,
  water intrusion rots the wood framing behind the veneer, discovered only once the
  damage is extensive enough to show up as staining or a musty smell inside the
  building.
