id: concept-percentage-of-completion-accounting
title: Percentage-of-Completion Accounting
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The standard revenue-recognition method for long-duration construction
  contracts, recognizing revenue and profit gradually as a job progresses,
  based on costs incurred so far against total estimated costs, rather than
  waiting until the project finishes to recognize anything at all.
whyItMatters: >
  A multi-year project would otherwise show zero revenue for years and then
  one enormous number the month it finishes, a distorted picture that tells a
  bank, surety, or owner almost nothing useful about how the company is
  actually doing in any given year. Recognizing revenue as the work actually
  happens matches the financials to reality.
example: >
  A $2,000,000 contract has incurred $600,000 of its total estimated
  $1,200,000 in costs, putting it at 50 percent complete. Percentage-of-
  completion accounting recognizes $1,000,000 of revenue for that period, half
  of the total contract, not zero and not the full amount.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-construction-accountant|Construction Accountant]] applies
  percentage-of-completion revenue recognition across every active project
  each reporting period. A [[career-controller-cfo-construction|Controller/CFO]]
  relies on it to produce the company-wide financial statements a surety
  reviews each year to set the company's bonding capacity, since a sloppy or
  inconsistent application of this method directly undermines a surety's
  confidence in the numbers.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A company underestimates a project's total remaining costs partway through
  the job, which makes the project look further along, and therefore more
  profitable, than it actually is. Revenue gets over-recognized for several
  quarters before the real cost overrun surfaces, and the correction then hits
  all at once as a sudden, hard-to-explain loss in a single reporting period.
