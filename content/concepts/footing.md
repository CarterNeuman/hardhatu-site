---
id: concept-footing
title: Footing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  The widened base at the bottom of a [[concept-foundation|foundation]], usually
  concrete, that spreads a building's structural loads over enough soil area to keep
  the structure from settling or sinking, sized specifically for both the building's
  weight and the soil's bearing capacity at that location.
whyItMatters: >
  A footing that's undersized for the actual soil conditions or building loads can lead
  to differential settlement, where different parts of a structure sink at different
  rates, cracking walls and floors in ways that are expensive and disruptive to fix
  after the fact.
example: >
  A structural engineer sizes footings for a building based on a geotechnical report
  showing the soil's bearing capacity, then a concrete crew excavates and forms each
  footing to those exact dimensions before the foundation walls are poured on top of
  them.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] forms and pours footings to the
  engineer's exact dimensions, since even a small deviation in size or depth can
  undermine the load calculations behind it. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] verifies footing
  dimensions and rebar placement against the structural drawings before the concrete
  pour, since it's one of the last chances to catch a problem before it's buried.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew pours a footing slightly shallower than the drawings specify, not realizing
  the soil at that exact depth has a lower bearing capacity than what was tested
  elsewhere on the site. Months later, that corner of the building settles more than
  the rest, cracking the slab and drywall above it in a pattern that traces directly
  back to the undersized footing.
