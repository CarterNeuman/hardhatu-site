---
id: concept-mock-up
title: Mock-Up
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A full-scale sample of a building assembly, such as a section of exterior wall, a
  typical hotel room, or a curtain wall corner, built and approved before that
  assembly is constructed throughout the rest of the project, used to verify
  appearance, quality, and performance while changes are still cheap to make.
whyItMatters: >
  Discovering that a brick pattern or window installation detail doesn't look or
  perform right after it's been repeated across an entire building's facade is far more
  expensive to fix than catching it in a single approved sample first.
example: >
  Before a hotel's exterior brick veneer work begins, the mason builds a 10-foot-square
  mock-up panel showing the exact brick pattern, mortar color, and control joint
  spacing. The architect and owner review and approve it on-site, and that approved
  panel becomes the standard every subsequent wall is compared against.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] tracks mock-up approvals
  as a required milestone before full production work on that assembly can start. A
  [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] coordinates building the mock-up itself,
  usually with the actual subcontractor and materials that will be used for the real
  work, so the approved sample is a true representation of what's coming.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor builds a mock-up using a subcontractor's best installer, gets it
  approved, then has different, less experienced installers execute the actual work
  across the building. The quality gap between the approved mock-up and the real
  installation becomes an ongoing dispute with the architect, who expected every wall
  to match the sample that was actually approved.
