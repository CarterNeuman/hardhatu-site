id: concept-general-conditions
title: General Conditions
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The section of a construction contract, often a standard form like AIA A201,
  that sets the general rules, responsibilities, and procedures governing the whole
  project: how RFIs and change orders get handled, insurance requirements, dispute
  resolution, and dozens of other administrative provisions that apply regardless of
  what's actually being built.
whyItMatters: >
  General conditions are where most of the procedural "rules of the game" for a
  project actually live, separate from the specific scope of work. A dispute over how
  a change order should be priced, or how much notice a delay claim requires, usually
  gets resolved by checking exactly what the general conditions say, not by common
  sense alone.
example: >
  A contract's general conditions specify that any RFI must receive an architect's
  response within 10 business days. When a response takes three weeks, the contractor
  can point to that specific general conditions provision as grounds for a schedule
  impact.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] knows a project's
  general conditions in detail, since they govern nearly every procedural dispute
  that comes up. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] refers back to them
  constantly for things like notice requirements and payment timing.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team assumes a specific procedural question, like how many days the
  owner has to respond to a submittal, works the same way it did on their last
  project. The general conditions on this particular contract set a different
  timeline entirely, and assuming the old rule applied costs the team a missed
  deadline they didn't realize existed.
