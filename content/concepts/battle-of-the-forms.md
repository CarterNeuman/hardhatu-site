---
id: concept-battle-of-the-forms
title: Battle of the Forms
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A contract-formation problem that arises when a buyer's purchase order and a
  seller's acknowledgment or invoice contain conflicting standard terms, and
  neither party ever signs a single unified document.
whyItMatters: >
  Material and equipment purchases in construction are often handled through a
  stack of forms rather than one negotiated contract, so conflicting
  boilerplate, like different warranty terms or dispute-resolution clauses,
  can end up controlling the deal without either party realizing it.
example: >
  A contractor's purchase order for custom millwork states that disputes go to
  arbitration, but the supplier's order acknowledgment states that disputes go
  to litigation in the supplier's home state. Neither party reads the other's
  fine print, and a dispute later turns into an argument over whose terms
  actually govern.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] reviews supplier
  acknowledgments against the original purchase order terms to catch
  conflicting language before it becomes a problem, and the
  [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] sorts out which terms
  actually control when a battle-of-the-forms dispute reaches a claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A buyer assumes their purchase order terms automatically control the deal,
  without noticing that the seller's returned acknowledgment added or changed
  terms that may now govern instead.
relatedIds:
  - career-procurement-manager
  - career-construction-attorney
---
