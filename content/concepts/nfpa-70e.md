id: concept-nfpa-70e
title: Electrical Safety Standard / NFPA 70E
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  NFPA's consensus standard for electrical safety in the workplace, covering
  arc-flash and shock hazard analysis, required PPE categories, and
  [[concept-lockout-tagout|lockout/tagout]] procedures for electrical work,
  distinct from the National Electrical Code, which governs how systems are
  installed rather than how workers safely work on them.
whyItMatters: >
  Following NFPA 70E is how an electrician's arc-flash PPE category and approach
  boundaries actually get determined for a given panel or piece of equipment, it
  isn't a generic safety guideline, it's the calculation behind exactly how close a
  worker can get and what they have to wear to do it.
example: >
  Before an electrician opens an energized panel, a 70E-based hazard analysis
  determines the arc-flash boundary and the specific PPE category required for
  that panel's incident energy level, not a blanket rule applied to every panel on
  the job.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]]'s day-to-day PPE requirements and approach
  boundaries around energized equipment come directly from a 70E-based hazard
  analysis specific to each piece of equipment.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrician works on an energized panel assuming it's low-risk without
  following the 70E hazard analysis for that specific equipment, and an arc flash
  event occurs causing severe burns that the correctly-rated PPE the analysis
  would have called for could have limited.
