id: concept-concrete-admixtures
title: Concrete Admixtures
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  Chemical additives mixed into concrete at the batch plant or jobsite to modify
  how it behaves: accelerators to speed curing in cold weather, retarders to slow
  it in hot weather or for long pours, water reducers, and air-entraining agents
  for freeze-thaw resistance, each selected for the specific conditions and
  performance a given pour needs.
whyItMatters: >
  The wrong admixture, or the right one at the wrong dosage, can change a pour's
  working time and final strength enough to cause real problems, concrete that
  sets faster than the crew can finish it, or concrete that doesn't develop its
  design strength on the schedule the project needs.
example: >
  A contractor pouring a large reinforced slab in cold weather specifies a
  non-chloride accelerator to keep the concrete curing on schedule despite
  freezing overnight temperatures, avoiding the corrosion risk a calcium chloride
  accelerator would pose to the embedded rebar, rather than pouring a standard
  mix that could freeze before gaining enough strength.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] approves which admixtures
  a mix design can use. A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] or ready-mix
  supplier doses and places the mix in the field.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A ready-mix supplier adds an accelerator to a large pour without confirming it
  against the structural engineer's approved mix design. The accelerated concrete
  sets faster than the finishing crew can work it, and several sections are left
  with a rough, improperly finished surface that has to be ground down.
