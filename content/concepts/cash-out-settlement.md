---
id: concept-cash-out-settlement
title: Cash Out Settlement
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  An insurance settlement in which the insurer pays the policyholder the agreed
  claim value directly, without requiring the funds to be used for actual
  repairs, letting the policyholder choose not to rebuild at all or to use a
  different contractor than originally estimated.
whyItMatters: >
  This option changes a contractor's negotiating position, since a homeowner who
  has already cashed out a settlement has full discretion over the budget and
  isn't constrained to a scope and price that matches the original insurance
  estimate.
example: >
  After settling a hail-damage roof claim, a homeowner takes a cash-out
  settlement instead of having the insurer pay a contractor directly, then
  shops the repair to several contractors and chooses one willing to do the
  work for less than the insurer's original estimate, keeping the difference.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors bidding on cash-out jobs should expect more price negotiation than
  on a direct-pay insurance job, since the homeowner is spending their own
  settlement money and has no obligation to stick to the insurer's original
  scope or price.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Contractors sometimes assume an insurance settlement figure is the fixed price
  for the job, when a cash-out homeowner is free to negotiate a lower price,
  choose cheaper materials, or skip some of the originally estimated scope
  entirely.
relatedIds:

---
