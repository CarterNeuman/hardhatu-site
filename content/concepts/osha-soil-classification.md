id: concept-osha-soil-classification
title: OSHA Soil Classification (Type A, B, C)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's three-tier system for rating how stable excavated soil is, Type A
  being the most stable, Type C the least, which determines what kind of
  protective system, sloping, benching, shoring, or a trench box, a given
  excavation legally requires.
whyItMatters: >
  The same trench depth can be safe or deadly depending on the soil type,
  since Type C soil, like loose sand or previously disturbed ground, can
  collapse far more easily and with far less warning than stable Type A clay.
  A competent person has to classify the soil correctly before deciding what
  protection the trench actually needs.
example: >
  A competent person evaluating a trench finds the soil has been previously
  disturbed and has water seeping into it, both signs that rule out
  classifying it as the more stable Type A, so the crew sets up a trench box
  rated for the less stable classification instead of relying on sloped walls
  alone.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]], acting as the jobsite's
  competent person, classifies soil on every excavation before crews are
  allowed to enter it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew assumes soil is stable Type A because the excavation looks similar to
  one they dug the week before, without actually testing it. The soil turns
  out to be unstable Type C, and the trench wall collapses under a load it was
  never classified to resist.
