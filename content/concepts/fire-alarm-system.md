id: concept-fire-alarm-system
title: Fire Alarm System
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "28, Electronic Safety and Security"
definition: >
  The network of smoke and heat detectors, pull stations, and notification devices
  (horns, strobes, speakers) that detects a fire and alerts building occupants,
  wired to a central fire alarm control panel and, on most commercial buildings,
  monitored by a central station that automatically notifies the fire department.
whyItMatters: >
  A fire alarm system is one of the few building systems that legally requires
  third-party acceptance testing before occupancy, every device has to be tested and
  documented as functioning correctly, since a fire alarm system that looks complete
  but doesn't actually work is arguably worse than no system at all, in that it gives
  false confidence.
example: >
  A fire alarm contractor completes installation of detectors and notification
  devices throughout a building, then performs a full acceptance test with the local
  fire marshal present, verifying every device activates and reports correctly to the
  control panel before the building can be occupied.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] or specialty fire alarm technician installs
  and wires the system's devices and control panel to code. A
  [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] coordinates the required acceptance
  testing and ongoing inspection schedule, since a fire alarm system's compliance
  doesn't end at installation; it requires periodic re-testing for the life of the
  building.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A fire alarm contractor installs a system and it passes a basic functional check,
  but several devices are wired to the wrong zone on the control panel, a mistake that
  doesn't affect whether the alarm sounds but does affect what location it reports.
  The wiring error isn't caught until a full acceptance test cross-checks every device
  against its intended zone, delaying occupancy until the wiring is corrected.
