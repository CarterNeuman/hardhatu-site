id: concept-rough-in-hvac
title: Rough-In HVAC
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "23, HVAC"
definition: >
  The mechanical installation performed inside walls, ceilings, and floors before
  they're closed up, such as ductwork runs, refrigerant piping, and condensate drain
  lines, installed and pressure-tested while it's still visible, alongside the equivalent
  rough-in work happening for plumbing and electrical in the same spaces.
whyItMatters: >
  HVAC rough-in competes for the exact same ceiling and wall cavity space that
  plumbing, electrical, and fire sprinkler rough-in also need, so a project that lets
  one trade rough-in without coordinating with the others can end up with a physical
  conflict that isn't discovered until someone tries to close up the ceiling.
example: >
  A mechanical contractor completes ductwork and refrigerant line rough-in on a floor,
  coordinating routing with the electrician and plumber ahead of time using a
  combined coordination drawing, then has the completed work inspected before drywall
  and ceiling grid close over it.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] installs rough-in mechanical work to
  the mechanical drawings, routing around conflicts identified during coordination. A
  [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] sequences HVAC rough-in against electrical
  and plumbing rough-in in the same space, since all three trades often want the same
  ceiling cavity at the same time.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A mechanical crew roughs in ductwork through a corridor ceiling without checking a
  recently issued coordination drawing showing a fire sprinkler main now routed through
  the same space. The conflict isn't caught until the sprinkler crew arrives and finds
  no room left to run their line, forcing a rework of ductwork that had already passed
  its own inspection.
