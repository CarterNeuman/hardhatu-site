---
id: concept-radiation-protection-rooms
title: Radiation Protection Rooms
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "13, Special Construction"
definition: >
  Rooms shielded with lead-lined walls, lead glass, or high-density concrete
  to contain ionizing radiation from medical imaging or treatment equipment
  (X-ray, CT, linear accelerators), with shielding thickness calculated from
  the specific equipment's output and the room's occupancy on the other side
  of each wall.
whyItMatters: >
  Shielding requirements are calculated by a qualified medical physicist based
  on the exact equipment and what's adjacent to each wall, since a hallway
  versus an occupied office requires different shielding, so the design can't
  be generalized from one radiation room to another.
example: >
  A contractor building a linear accelerator vault follows the medical
  physicist's shielding report exactly, including concrete wall thickness that
  varies by wall depending on whether an occupied space sits on the other
  side.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] coordinates the construction with the
  medical physicist's shielding report, and the [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC
  Inspector]] verifies shielding thickness against that report before
  equipment installation.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A radiation room's wall is built to a uniform thickness rather than the
  physicist's wall-by-wall shielding report, and post-construction radiation
  survey testing finds a wall adjacent to an occupied office under-shielded,
  requiring it to be reinforced before the equipment can go live.
relatedIds:
  - career-mep-engineer
  - career-qaqc-inspector
---
