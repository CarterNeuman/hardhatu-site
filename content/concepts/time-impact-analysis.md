---
id: concept-time-impact-analysis
title: Time Impact Analysis
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A forensic scheduling technique that inserts a specific delay event into a
  copy of the project schedule at the point it actually occurred, to isolate
  and measure exactly how many days that one event pushed out the project's
  completion date, distinct from simply comparing the original and final
  schedules after the fact.
whyItMatters: >
  Isolating one event's actual impact matters most when several delays
  happened close together, without a time impact analysis, it's easy to
  credit or blame the wrong event for a schedule slip that several things
  contributed to at once.
example: >
  A scheduling consultant inserts a differing site condition, an unmapped
  utility line that stalled excavation for ten working days, into a copy of
  the project's baseline schedule at the exact point it occurred, showing
  that specific event pushed the completion date back by exactly ten working
  days, isolated from any other delays that happened later.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-scheduling-delay-consultant|Scheduling & Delay Consultant]]
  performs the time impact analysis, the technical basis behind most serious
  delay claims.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor's delay claim asserts a specific owner-caused event pushed the
  whole project back by six weeks, but without a time impact analysis
  actually isolating that one event's effect, the claim can't distinguish
  that owner-caused delay from other, contractor-caused slippage happening
  around the same time. The owner's counsel picks the claim apart on exactly
  that gap.
