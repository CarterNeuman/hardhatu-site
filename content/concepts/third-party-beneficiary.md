---
id: concept-third-party-beneficiary
title: Third-Party Beneficiary
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  Someone who isn't a party to a contract but who the contract was intended to
  benefit, and who may in some circumstances be able to enforce rights under
  that contract.
whyItMatters: >
  It matters most in construction when an owner enters a contract with a general
  contractor and a question arises over whether a subcontractor, lender, or
  future building owner can enforce a promise made in that owner-contractor
  agreement, even though they never signed it.
example: >
  A condominium association was not a party to the original contract between the
  developer and general contractor, but courts in some jurisdictions treat
  future unit owners as intended third-party beneficiaries of workmanship
  warranties written into that contract, letting the association pursue a
  defect claim.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] reviews contract
  language to determine whether it was written to create third-party
  beneficiary rights or expressly disclaims them, since many construction
  contracts now include language stating no third-party beneficiaries are
  intended.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A party assumes that because a contract clearly benefits someone outside the
  deal, that person automatically has the right to sue on it, when many
  contracts expressly disclaim third-party beneficiary status to prevent
  exactly that outcome.
relatedIds:
  - career-construction-attorney
---
