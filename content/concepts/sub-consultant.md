id: concept-sub-consultant
title: Sub-Consultant
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A specialized design professional, such as a structural, mechanical, or
  electrical engineer, retained by the project's architect rather than
  directly by the owner or general contractor, forming a design team under the
  architect's own contract with the owner.
whyItMatters: >
  Who hires whom in a design-build-contract chain actually matters for
  liability: a structural engineer working as the architect's sub-consultant
  is generally answerable to the architect, not directly to the contractor,
  which shapes how a design dispute gets resolved if something in that
  engineer's design goes wrong.
example: >
  An architect retains a structural engineer as a sub-consultant under the
  architect's own contract with the owner, rather than the owner or general
  contractor hiring the structural engineer directly, which is the typical
  arrangement on a design-bid-build project.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] is frequently retained
  as a sub-consultant to the architect rather than contracted directly by the
  owner, which shapes the reporting and liability relationships on the
  project.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor assumes it can go directly to a structural engineer to resolve
  a design question, not realizing the engineer is a sub-consultant to the
  architect and contractually answers to them instead. The contractor's
  request gets rerouted through the architect, adding delay that could have
  been avoided by understanding the actual contractual chain from the start.
