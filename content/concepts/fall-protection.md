id: concept-fall-protection
title: Fall Protection
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  Systems and procedures, guardrails, safety nets, and personal fall-arrest harnesses
  tied to an anchor point, that prevent or arrest a fall from height, legally required
  by OSHA once a construction worker is exposed to a fall of six feet or more.
whyItMatters: >
  Falls are consistently one of construction's leading causes of fatality, which is why
  OSHA's fall protection standard is one of its most heavily enforced, and one of the
  most commonly cited violations, since the required protection has to actually be used,
  not just available on site.
example: >
  An ironworker connecting steel at height ties off to an anchor point with a
  fall-arrest harness before moving beyond a completed floor's edge, rather than relying
  only on careful footing on an open beam.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] works at height routinely, often before any floor
  or safety net exists below, making correctly used fall protection a daily necessity
  rather than an occasional precaution. A [[career-roofer|Roofer]] faces the same
  exposure on sloped, often wet or uneven surfaces, where a slip can turn into a fall
  with very little warning.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A worker ties off to a convenient piece of equipment instead of an engineered anchor
  point, assuming it's sturdy enough to hold a fall. The anchor fails under the sudden
  load of an actual fall, since it was never rated or evaluated as an anchor point in the
  first place.
