id: concept-lead-safe-work-practices
title: Lead-Safe Work Practices / RRP Rule
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule, requiring lead-safe work
  practices, containment, a certified renovator on-site, and verified cleanup, on
  any renovation disturbing painted surfaces in housing or child-occupied
  facilities built before 1978. A narrow exemption covers very minor repair work
  under roughly six square feet of interior paint per room, or twenty square feet
  of exterior paint.
whyItMatters: >
  The rule applies to any trade disturbing old paint, not just painters, sanding,
  scraping, cutting, or drilling into a pre-1978 painted surface all trigger it, so
  a carpenter or electrician working in an old house can be just as subject to it as
  the painting crew.
example: >
  A contractor renovating a kitchen built in 1965 has an EPA-certified renovator on
  site, sets up plastic containment around the work area, and verifies cleanup with
  a dust-clearance test before the family moves back into the space.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-painter|Painter]] working on pre-1978 housing is directly subject to
  the rule whenever sanding or scraping old paint, which is central to much of the
  job, while an
  [[career-environmental-remediation-specialist|Environmental/Remediation Specialist]]
  handles the more extensive lead abatement work beyond a renovation's scope.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A painter sands old exterior trim on a pre-1978 home well beyond the small
  exempt area, without EPA lead-safe certification or proper containment. That
  exposes the family, especially any children, to lead dust and risks a real EPA
  fine on top of the health hazard.
