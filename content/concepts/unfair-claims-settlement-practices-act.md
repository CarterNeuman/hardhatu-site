id: concept-unfair-claims-settlement-practices-act
title: Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A model law, developed by the National Association of Insurance Commissioners
  and adopted in some form by nearly every state, that defines specific
  insurer conduct, like unreasonable claim delays or failing to explain a
  denial, as an unfair claims practice subject to regulatory penalty.
whyItMatters: >
  This law gives contractors and homeowners dealing with a slow or evasive
  insurer a regulatory standard to point to, and in many states grounds for a
  bad-faith complaint, when a carrier's handling of a claim crosses from
  merely frustrating into legally unfair.
example: >
  A homeowner whose water-damage claim sits unanswered for months files a
  complaint with the state insurance department citing the Unfair Claims
  Settlement Practices Act's prohibition on unreasonable delay, prompting the
  department to open an inquiry into the carrier's handling.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors advising clients through a stalled claim should know this law
  exists as a regulatory backstop, since the model act sets specific standards
  for how an insurer must investigate and respond to a claim, and nearly every
  state has adopted some version of it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume filing a regulatory complaint under this act directly
  gets their claim paid, when the act generally functions as a basis for a
  state insurance department investigation or a private bad-faith action, not
  an automatic payment order.
