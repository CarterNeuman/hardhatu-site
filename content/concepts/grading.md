id: concept-grading
title: Grading
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  Reshaping a site's ground surface to the elevations shown on the civil drawings,
  cutting high areas down and filling low areas in, establishing both the pad a
  building sits on and the slopes that direct stormwater away from it rather than
  toward it.
whyItMatters: >
  Grading mistakes are expensive specifically because they're often invisible until
  it rains; a slope that's off by a small amount can direct water toward a
  foundation instead of away from it, a problem that may not show up until well after
  the project is complete and the original grading contractor is long gone.
example: >
  A site is graded to the elevations and slopes shown on the civil engineer's grading
  plan, verified against a fresh site survey, so that finished grade slopes stormwater
  away from the building toward the designed drainage system rather than pooling
  against the foundation.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] executes cuts and fills to the
  grading plan's exact elevations, often using GPS-guided equipment for precision. A
  [[career-surveyor|Surveyor]] verifies as-graded elevations against the design
  before the next phase of work builds on top of the graded surface.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A grading crew finishes a site close to the design elevations but slightly off at
  one corner near the building, not realizing the small discrepancy actually creates a
  low point right against the foundation. The first significant rain after backfill is
  complete pools water against the foundation wall instead of draining away, a costly
  problem to fix once landscaping and paving have already covered the area.
