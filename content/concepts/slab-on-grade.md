id: concept-slab-on-grade
title: Slab-on-Grade
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  A concrete floor system poured directly on top of prepared ground, rather than
  suspended over a crawl space or basement, typically used for the ground floor of a
  building where the site's soil and drainage conditions support it.
whyItMatters: >
  Because it sits directly on the ground, a slab-on-grade's performance depends heavily
  on what's underneath it: proper soil compaction, a vapor barrier, and adequate
  drainage all have to be right before the concrete goes down, since fixing a problem
  underneath a finished slab means breaking it out.
example: >
  Before pouring a warehouse's slab-on-grade floor, the crew compacts the sub-base
  soil, installs a vapor barrier to block moisture from wicking up through the
  concrete, and sets reinforcing steel, all inspected before the concrete truck
  arrives.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] places and finishes the slab itself,
  but the site prep underneath it, such as compaction, vapor barrier, and reinforcing,
  is just as critical to the finished product's performance. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] inspects that sub-slab
  prep work before the pour, since none of it can be verified once the concrete is
  down.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew pours a slab-on-grade without a vapor barrier to save a day of schedule,
  assuming the site's soil is dry enough. Months after the building is occupied,
  moisture wicking up through the slab causes a moisture-sensitive flooring finish to
  bubble and delaminate, a problem that traces directly back to a step skipped before
  the pour.
