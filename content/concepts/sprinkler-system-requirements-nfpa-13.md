title: Automatic Sprinkler System Requirements (NFPA 13)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The NFPA 13 standard governing the design and installation of automatic fire
  sprinkler systems, covering sprinkler head spacing, water supply adequacy,
  and the specific hazard classification a building's contents require,
  referenced by the building code as the baseline for any sprinkler system a
  project installs.
whyItMatters: >
  A sprinkler system designed to the wrong hazard classification can fail to
  control a fire even though every sprinkler head technically fired, since the
  actual water density and coverage needed depends heavily on what's actually
  stored or occurring in the space below.
example: >
  A warehouse storing high-piled combustible materials requires a sprinkler
  system designed to a much higher hazard classification and water density
  than an office building would, even though both buildings might look similar
  from the outside.
careerAngle: >
  Fire protection engineers need to classify a building's actual use and
  contents correctly before designing to NFPA 13, since under-classifying the
  hazard produces a system that passes inspection but may not actually control
  a real fire.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume a sprinkler system is a single, interchangeable
  product, when NFPA 13 requires the design itself, head spacing, water
  supply, pipe sizing, to be matched to the specific hazard classification of
  what the building actually contains.
