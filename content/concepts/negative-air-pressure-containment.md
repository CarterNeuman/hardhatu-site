id: concept-negative-air-pressure-containment
title: Negative Air Pressure / Containment
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A technique used during mold or hazardous-material remediation where an air
  scrubber pulls air out of a sealed work area faster than it can leak in,
  keeping contaminated air and spores from escaping into the rest of the
  building while work is underway.
whyItMatters: >
  This is often a documented requirement for larger mold or biohazard jobs, and
  failing to maintain it can spread contamination to previously clean areas,
  creating a much bigger claim and a liability problem for the contractor.
example: >
  A remediation crew seals a basement with poly sheeting, runs a negative air
  machine venting through a HEPA filter to the exterior, and uses a manometer
  to confirm the sealed area stays at lower pressure than the rest of the
  house throughout the job.
careerAngle: >
  Remediation technicians need to understand containment setup and verification,
  since an improperly sealed containment that leaks spores into HVAC systems
  can turn one room's problem into a whole-building claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Crews sometimes assume sealing the doorway with plastic sheeting alone creates
  containment, when true negative pressure containment requires active air
  pressure differential, not just a physical barrier.
