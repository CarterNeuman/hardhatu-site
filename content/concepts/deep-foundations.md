id: concept-deep-foundations
title: Deep Foundations (Piles & Caissons)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  Foundation systems, driven piles, drilled caissons or shafts, or similar
  deep elements, that transfer a structure's load down through weak or
  unstable near-surface soil to a stronger bearing layer or bedrock well
  below the surface, used when a shallow spread footing foundation can't
  provide adequate bearing capacity for the site's actual soil conditions.
whyItMatters: >
  Choosing between pile types, driven versus drilled, and confirming actual
  capacity depends heavily on site-specific geotechnical data and often
  requires field verification, such as a pile load test, since a deep
  foundation that doesn't reach adequate bearing capacity fails in a way
  that's extremely expensive to correct once the structure above it is
  built.
example: >
  A high-rise's structural engineer specifies drilled caissons extending to
  bedrock, sized and verified through a pile load test performed on a
  sacrificial test pile before production piles are installed across the
  site.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-pile-driving-specialist|Deep Foundation / Pile-Driving Specialist]]
  operates the specialized equipment that installs piles or
  drilled shafts. A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]]
  designs the foundation system based on the site's geotechnical report.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor installs driven piles to the depth shown on the original
  drawings without confirming each pile actually reaches the required
  bearing capacity through driving resistance measurements. Several piles
  refuse well short of the specified depth, and the discrepancy isn't
  investigated until post-installation testing reveals a foundation that
  doesn't meet its design capacity, requiring supplemental piles to be
  added.
