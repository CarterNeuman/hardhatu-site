id: concept-wet-dry-sprinkler-systems
title: Wet vs. Dry Sprinkler Systems
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "21, Fire Suppression"
definition: >
  The two primary configurations for a fire sprinkler system's piping, a wet
  pipe system that keeps water in the pipes at all times for the fastest
  possible response, and a dry pipe system that holds pressurized air in the
  piping until a sprinkler activates, used specifically in unheated spaces,
  such as parking garages, attics, and freezers, where standing water would
  freeze.
whyItMatters: >
  A dry pipe system responds more slowly than a wet system since the pipes
  have to fill with water after a sprinkler activates before any water
  actually discharges, which is exactly why codes limit how large a single dry
  pipe system can be, keeping that trip time within an acceptable range.
example: >
  An unheated parking garage is protected with a dry pipe sprinkler system
  sized within the code's maximum volume limit for that system type, keeping
  the delay between sprinkler activation and actual water discharge within the
  trip time the design allows for.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-pipefitter|Pipefitter]] installs both wet and dry sprinkler
  piping, following different installation details for each, since a dry
  system also needs a means of draining any condensation that collects in the
  piping.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor installs a dry pipe sprinkler system sized larger than code
  allows for that system type to save on valve and equipment costs. The
  oversized system passes a basic pressure test, but fails its required
  trip-time acceptance test once fully commissioned, taking far longer than
  code allows to deliver water after activation, forcing the piping to be
  reconfigured into smaller zones after it's already installed.
