id: concept-confined-space-entry
title: Confined Space Entry
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A regulated procedure for entering a space that's large enough to work in but not
  designed for continuous occupancy and has limited means of entry or exit, a utility
  vault, a tank, a crawlspace, with a permit-required confined space adding the further
  risk of a hazardous atmosphere requiring air testing, ventilation, and a rescue plan
  before anyone goes in.
whyItMatters: >
  A space that seems merely cramped can also be one where a hazardous atmosphere builds
  up silently, with no way to tell just by looking. OSHA's confined space standard exists
  because that combination, hard to get out of and potentially dangerous to breathe in,
  has caused a disproportionate number of fatalities relative to how often construction
  workers actually enter these spaces.
example: >
  Before entering an underground utility vault, a crew tests the air for oxygen levels
  and toxic gases, sets up continuous ventilation, and confirms a rescue plan is in place,
  all required before the permit-required entry is authorized.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] regularly works in utility vaults and other confined
  spaces where hazardous atmospheres can accumulate. A
  [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] issues and oversees the entry permit process,
  since a confined space entry without the required testing and rescue plan is one of
  construction's more serious regulatory exposures.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew enters a space they've used many times before without re-testing the air,
  assuming it's the same as always. A buildup of gas from a source that wasn't present on
  previous visits creates a hazardous atmosphere that goes undetected until someone is
  already inside and affected by it.
