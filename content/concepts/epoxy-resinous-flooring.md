id: concept-epoxy-resinous-flooring
title: Epoxy & Resinous Flooring
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  A seamless, high-performance floor coating system, epoxy, polyurethane, or
  polyaspartic resin applied in liquid form directly over a properly prepared
  concrete slab, used in industrial, commercial kitchen, and laboratory
  settings where chemical resistance, cleanability, or heavy traffic
  durability matter more than the look or feel of a standard floor finish.
whyItMatters: >
  Resinous flooring's bond to the slab depends entirely on the concrete
  surface preparation, typically mechanical grinding or shot-blasting to open
  the surface's pores, skipping or shortcutting that prep step is the single
  most common cause of a resinous floor delaminating from the slab underneath
  it.
example: >
  An installer shot-blasts a warehouse's concrete slab to the specified
  surface profile before applying an epoxy coating system, confirming the
  prepared surface meets the coating manufacturer's required profile rather
  than applying the coating directly over the slab's original finished
  surface.
careerAngle: >
  A specialty flooring subcontractor, often working alongside a
  [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] who finished the slab beneath
  it, prepares the surface and applies the resinous coating system,
  following the manufacturer's specified surface preparation and cure time
  between coats.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A kitchen installer substitutes a lower-grade epoxy system to save on
  material cost, rating it acceptable for light-duty use without confirming
  it matches the manufacturer's chemical-resistance data sheet for the
  specific cleaning agents and grease the space will actually see. Within a
  few months, the substituted coating starts softening and discoloring
  wherever the kitchen's degreaser is used, and the floor has to be
  recoated with the originally specified system.
