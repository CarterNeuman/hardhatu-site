id: concept-soil-compaction-testing
title: Soil Compaction Testing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  Field and laboratory testing that verifies compacted fill soil has reached
  a specified percentage of its maximum density, typically established in the
  lab through a Proctor test and confirmed in the field with a nuclear density
  gauge or sand cone test, required before a foundation, slab, or pavement can
  be built on top of that fill.
whyItMatters: >
  Fill soil that hasn't been compacted to spec keeps settling under load
  after construction is complete, showing up later as a cracked slab or a
  settled foundation, which is exactly why compaction testing exists as a
  required checkpoint before building on top of fill rather than an optional
  quality check.
example: >
  A geotechnical testing firm runs a nuclear density gauge test on each lift
  of compacted fill beneath a new slab, verifying it meets the specified 95
  percent of the laboratory's maximum dry density before the contractor is
  allowed to place the next lift.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] specifies the required
  compaction percentage based on the geotechnical report. A
  [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] or geotechnical testing firm
  verifies each lift meets that requirement before construction proceeds.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor places several lifts of fill without waiting for compaction
  test results between each one, assuming the soil is compacting adequately
  based on how it looks and feels underfoot. A later test reveals a lower
  lift never actually met the required density, and the contractor has to
  remove and recompact several completed lifts to reach the one that failed.
