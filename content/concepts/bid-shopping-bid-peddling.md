id: concept-bid-shopping-bid-peddling
title: Bid Shopping / Bid Peddling
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Two related, generally frowned-upon practices: bid shopping is when a general
  contractor discloses a subcontractor's bid price to competing subs after
  winning the job, trying to squeeze a lower price out of someone else; bid
  peddling is when a subcontractor, after bids are already in, approaches the
  GC directly offering to underbid the number already submitted.
whyItMatters: >
  Both practices undermine the competitive bidding process the original bid list
  was supposed to represent, and many public contracts specifically prohibit
  bid shopping once a general contractor has used a subcontractor's price to
  win its own bid, since using a number to win work and then shopping it
  afterward isn't playing by the same rules everyone else bid under.
example: >
  A general contractor wins a public project using a steel subcontractor's
  submitted bid price as part of its own winning number. After award, the GC
  discloses that subcontractor's price to a competing steel sub, trying to
  squeeze out a lower number, a practice the project's contract documents
  specifically prohibit once that price was used to win the bid.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-estimator|Estimator]] keeps subcontractor bid pricing
  confidential through the bidding process specifically to prevent bid
  shopping, and the [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]]
  advises on whether a specific contract's anti-bid-shopping language has
  actually been violated.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A subcontractor assumes its submitted bid price is safely confidential once
  bids are in, without realizing a general contractor facing cost pressure
  after award may try to shop that exact price to a competitor to squeeze a
  lower number.
relatedIds:
