---
id: concept-offsite-manufacturing
title: Offsite/Prefabrication Manufacturing
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  The broader industry shift toward fabricating building components in a
  factory rather than building everything in the field, spanning a spectrum
  from simple panelized wall systems and structural insulated panels to
  prefabricated MEP racks, corridor kits, and bathroom pods, distinct from
  full [[concept-modular-construction|volumetric modular construction]]
  since offsite manufacturing covers partial factory fabrication of
  individual components as often as it covers complete finished modules.
whyItMatters: >
  A persistent skilled-labor shortage and the desire for tighter quality
  control are pushing more contractors to evaluate which parts of a project,
  not necessarily whole rooms, could be built in a factory instead of the
  field, so the real decision most companies face isn't whether to go fully
  modular but which specific components, wall panels, MEP racks, bathroom
  pods, are worth shifting offsite for a given project type.
example: >
  A mechanical contractor fabricates an entire corridor's overhead ductwork,
  piping, and conduit as a single prefabricated rack in an offsite shop,
  then ships and lifts it into place as one unit instead of running each
  system separately in the field, cutting overhead work at height to a
  fraction of what stick-built installation would require.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-construction-technology-manager|Construction Technology Manager]]
  evaluates which components are worth shifting to offsite fabrication
  across a company's project types and builds the shop relationships to
  support it. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] coordinates a
  prefabricated component's factory delivery date against the site's actual
  readiness to receive and set it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A mechanical contractor scales up its use of prefabricated overhead racks
  without first confirming a specific building's loading dock size and
  interior corridor clearances can actually accommodate the larger
  assemblies. The racks arrive too large to maneuver into their final
  position, forcing the shop-built assemblies to be partially disassembled
  and reworked on site, erasing much of the labor savings the prefabrication
  was meant to capture.
