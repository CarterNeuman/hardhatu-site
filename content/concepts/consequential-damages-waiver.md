title: Consequential Damages Waiver
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A contract clause in which both parties agree to give up the right to recover
  indirect, consequential losses, like lost profits, lost financing, or
  reputational harm, from each other, limiting recoverable damages to direct
  costs actually tied to the breach itself.
whyItMatters: >
  Consequential damages, like an owner's lost revenue from a delayed hotel
  opening, can dwarf the direct cost of the underlying construction problem,
  so a mutual waiver protects both parties from catastrophic, hard-to-predict
  exposure, which is why these waivers are standard in most major industry
  contract forms.
example: >
  A hotel owner's delayed opening due to a late-finishing contractor causes
  significant lost revenue during the delay period. Because the contract
  includes a mutual consequential damages waiver, the owner can still pursue
  direct costs tied to the delay, but cannot recover the lost revenue itself,
  since that loss is specifically excluded as a consequential damage.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] negotiates the
  scope of a consequential damages waiver, including which specific loss
  categories are carved back in or out, and the [[career-risk-manager-
  construction|Risk Manager]] evaluates how much real protection the waiver
  actually provides against the project's biggest financial risks.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A party assumes a consequential damages waiver eliminates all exposure beyond
  direct repair costs, without checking whether the contract carved specific
  categories, like liquidated damages for delay, back out of the waiver's
  scope, leaving that exposure very much still in place.
