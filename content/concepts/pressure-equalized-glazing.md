---
id: concept-pressure-equalized-glazing
title: Pressure-Equalized Glazing System
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  A curtain wall or window design that deliberately lets a small amount of
  water past the outer seal into a drainage cavity, equalizes air pressure
  inside that cavity to match outside pressure, and drains any water back out
  through weep holes, rather than trying to stop every drop of water at a
  single outer seal.
whyItMatters: >
  A single seal that has to be perfect everywhere, all the time, for decades
  is an unrealistic design goal. Pressure-equalized design assumes some water
  will get past the first line of defense and gives it a controlled,
  intentional way to drain back out, which is a fundamentally more reliable
  strategy than relying on one perfect barrier. The same drainage principle
  shows up in masonry as
  [[concept-masonry-flashing-weeps|Masonry Flashing and Weeps]], applied to an
  entirely different wall system.
example: >
  A curtain wall system is designed with a drainage cavity behind the outer
  glazing seal, equalized to outside air pressure through small vents, so that
  any water that gets past the outer seal drains back out through weep holes
  at the base of each unit instead of migrating further into the wall.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-glazier|Glazier]] installs curtain wall components in a sequence
  that preserves the system's pressure equalization and drainage path, since
  blocking a weep hole or drainage cavity during installation defeats the
  whole design strategy.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An installer accidentally blocks a curtain wall's weep holes with excess
  sealant while finishing a joint, trapping water that was designed to drain
  out instead. The trapped water has nowhere to go and eventually finds its
  way further into the wall assembly than the original seal failure ever would
  have on its own.
