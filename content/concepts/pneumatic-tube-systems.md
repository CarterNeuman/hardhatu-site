---
id: concept-pneumatic-tube-systems
title: Pneumatic Tube Systems
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "14, Conveying Equipment"
definition: >
  A network of tubes using air pressure to move small canisters, carrying
  documents, lab samples, or medications between departments, most common in
  hospitals, requiring dedicated chase space and station locations planned
  during design rather than retrofitted later.
whyItMatters: >
  Pneumatic tube routing needs continuous chase space through walls and floors
  with minimum bend radius requirements, which is far easier to coordinate
  with structural and MEP design than to route through an already-built
  building.
example: >
  A contractor building a new hospital coordinates the pneumatic tube system's
  chase routing with the structural engineer early, since the tubing's minimum
  bend radius requirements can conflict with structural beam locations if not
  planned together.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] coordinates the tube routing with
  other building systems competing for the same chase space.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A pneumatic tube system's routing is finalized without checking it against
  the structural beam layout, and a conflict discovered mid-construction
  forces an expensive reroute through an already-framed area.
relatedIds:
  - career-mep-engineer
