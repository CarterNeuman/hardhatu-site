id: concept-piercing-the-corporate-veil
title: Piercing the Corporate Veil
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A legal theory that lets a court hold a company's owners personally liable for
  business debts or judgments, bypassing the normal legal separation between a
  corporation or LLC and the individuals who own it.
whyItMatters: >
  Contractors who treat the business as a true separate entity, with its own
  bank accounts, insurance, and formalities, keep personal assets shielded;
  contractors who commingle personal and business funds or undercapitalize the
  company invite a court to disregard that separation entirely.
example: >
  A small contracting LLC loses a construction-defect lawsuit, but the company
  has no meaningful assets and the owner has been paying personal expenses
  straight out of the business account for years. The injured party's attorney
  argues successfully to pierce the corporate veil and collect the judgment
  from the owner's personal assets.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] advises contractors
  on the corporate formalities that keep the liability shield intact, and the
  [[career-construction-accountant|Construction Accountant]] keeps business
  and personal finances properly separated to avoid giving a future plaintiff
  grounds to pierce the veil.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner treats the company's checking account like a personal wallet, skips
  corporate formalities, and assumes the LLC label alone protects personal
  assets no matter how the business is actually run.
relatedIds:
