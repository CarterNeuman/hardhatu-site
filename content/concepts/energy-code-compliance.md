id: concept-energy-code-compliance
title: Energy Code Compliance / IECC
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  Compliance with the International Energy Conservation Code, adopted with local
  amendments by most states, setting minimum requirements for building envelope
  insulation, window performance, and mechanical system efficiency, verified
  through one of a few compliance paths, a prescriptive checklist, a whole-building
  performance calculation, or an energy-rating index score.
whyItMatters: >
  Energy code compliance is checked separately from the rest of the building
  code plan review, so a design can satisfy every structural and life-safety
  requirement and still fail its own dedicated energy-code review if the envelope
  or mechanical systems don't meet the minimum efficiency the code sets for that
  climate zone.
example: >
  A design team runs a whole-building energy performance calculation instead of
  following the prescriptive checklist, since a slightly less efficient window
  package is offset by better wall insulation, letting the project still pass
  energy code compliance through the performance path.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] designs mechanical systems to meet the
  code's minimum efficiency requirements, while an
  [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] installs and commissions the
  equipment those calculations assumed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A builder specs a cheaper HVAC system that fits the owner's budget but falls
  short of the IECC's minimum efficiency requirement for the local climate zone.
  The project fails its energy-code plan review, and the system has to be
  re-specified and repriced before the permit can be issued.
