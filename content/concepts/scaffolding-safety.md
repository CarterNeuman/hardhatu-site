id: concept-scaffolding-safety
title: Scaffolding Safety
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA requirements governing scaffold erection, use, and inspection, such as a
  competent person inspecting the scaffold before each work shift, guardrails at
  required heights, proper access (not climbing the frame itself), and load limits
  matched to the actual work being done on it.
whyItMatters: >
  A scaffold that looks stable can still be missing a required guardrail, sitting on
  unstable footing, or overloaded beyond its rated capacity in ways that aren't obvious
  from a quick glance. The inspection requirement exists because scaffold-related falls
  and collapses are common enough, and preventable enough, to warrant a mandatory
  daily check rather than an occasional one.
example: >
  Before a crew begins work on a multi-level scaffold, the site's competent person
  inspects the guardrails, planking, and base footing, confirming the scaffold is safe to
  use for that day's specific task before anyone climbs on.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-laborer|Construction Laborer]] often erects and dismantles scaffolding as
  part of routine work and needs to build it correctly to spec, not just quickly. A
  [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] verifies scaffold inspections are actually
  happening before each shift and that the scaffold matches the load it's being used for.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew adds an extra plank level to a scaffold to reach a higher work area without
  re-evaluating the scaffold's rated load capacity for the additional height and weight.
  The scaffold becomes unstable under a load it was never inspected or rated for at that
  configuration.
