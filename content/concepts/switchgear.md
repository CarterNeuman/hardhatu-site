id: concept-switchgear
title: Switchgear
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  The assembly of switches, breakers, and protective devices that controls and
  protects a building's electrical distribution at a higher voltage and capacity than
  a standard panel board, the point where utility or generator power is switched,
  metered, and distributed to a large building's electrical system.
whyItMatters: >
  Switchgear is one of the longest-lead procurement items on many commercial
  projects, custom-configured to a specific project's electrical design, so ordering
  it late can delay the entire building's ability to receive permanent power, a
  schedule risk that shows up months before the equipment itself ever arrives.
example: >
  A project's electrical engineer finalizes the switchgear configuration early in
  design specifically so it can be ordered as a long-lead item, since the
  manufacturer's fabrication time for a custom-configured unit is longer than nearly
  any other piece of equipment on the project.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] terminates and commissions switchgear once
  it's set, a task requiring specialized high-voltage training beyond typical panel
  work. A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] tracks switchgear submittals
  and delivery dates closely, since a delay here can push back the date permanent
  power becomes available for every other trade finishing their work.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team treats switchgear procurement like any other electrical equipment
  order, submitting it on the standard submittal schedule rather than flagging it
  immediately as a long-lead item. The switchgear's actual fabrication lead time turns
  out to be several months longer than assumed, and the building can't get permanent
  power until well after the original schedule called for it.
