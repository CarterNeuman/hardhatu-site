id: concept-slump-test
title: Slump Test
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  A quick field test, filling a cone-shaped mold with wet concrete, lifting
  the mold, and measuring how far the concrete slumps down, used to check
  whether a concrete mix is workable enough to pour and finish properly.
whyItMatters: >
  Concrete that's too stiff is difficult to place and consolidate around
  rebar, while concrete that's too wet loses strength as it cures. The slump
  test gives a fast, repeatable way to catch a mix that's out of spec before
  it ever gets poured, rather than discovering the problem after it's already
  in the forms.
example: >
  A ready-mix truck arrives at a jobsite, and before the pour begins, a crew
  member runs a slump test on a sample from the load, confirming the slump
  falls within the range the mix design calls for before approving the truck
  to discharge.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] or quality technician runs a
  slump test on nearly every concrete delivery, since it's the fastest way to
  catch a bad mix before it's committed to the forms.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew under time pressure skips the slump test on a delivery and pours
  concrete that turns out to be far too wet. The extra water weakens the cured
  concrete's final strength, a problem that isn't discovered until strength
  testing weeks later, long after the pour can be undone.
