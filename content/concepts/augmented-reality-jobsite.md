id: concept-augmented-reality-jobsite
title: Augmented Reality on the Jobsite
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Technology that overlays a digital model, such as MEP rough-in locations
  or structural elements, directly onto a worker's real-world view of the
  jobsite through a headset or tablet, letting a crew see where a wall,
  duct run, or embedded item should go without translating a 2D drawing
  into the physical space themselves.
whyItMatters: >
  AR's usefulness on a jobsite depends entirely on the accuracy of the
  underlying model it's overlaying, and on the AR device's own real-world
  positioning accuracy, an AR overlay that's off by even a small margin
  can be more misleading than a printed drawing, since it presents itself
  with a false sense of precision the worker has less reason to question.
example: >
  A mechanical contractor uses an AR tablet to overlay the exact location
  of concealed conduit and piping onto a finished wall before drilling
  anchor points for equipment, avoiding a costly strike on a concealed line
  a printed drawing alone might not have prevented.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-bim-vdc-specialist|BIM/VDC Specialist]] prepares and maintains
  the underlying model AR tools overlay onto the jobsite. An
  [[career-electrician|Electrician]] or other trade uses the AR device in
  the field to confirm concealed locations before cutting or drilling.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew relies on an AR overlay to locate a concealed structural element
  without recalibrating the device's positioning at the start of the work
  area, trusting the overlay's apparent precision. The AR system's
  positioning has drifted slightly since setup, and a worker drills into
  the actual structural element a few inches off from where the overlay
  showed it, an error a quick manual verification against a physical
  reference point would have caught.
