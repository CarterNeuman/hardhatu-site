id: concept-excavation
title: Excavation
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  Digging into the ground to create the space a foundation, utility line, or basement
  needs, one of the most heavily OSHA-regulated activities in construction, since an
  excavation deep enough for a worker to enter creates a real cave-in risk that
  ordinary jobsite hazards don't.
whyItMatters: >
  OSHA's excavation standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart P) legally requires a "competent
  person" to inspect an excavation daily and before workers enter it, and to protect
  workers in excavations five feet or deeper with sloping, benching, shoring, or a
  trench box: cave-ins happen fast and without warning, which is why this specific
  hazard gets its own dedicated OSHA standard rather than falling under general safety
  rules.
example: >
  Before workers enter a utility trench deeper than five feet, the crew's designated
  competent person inspects the trench and confirms it has adequate protection,
  properly sloped walls or a trench box, before anyone is allowed inside it.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] digs the excavation to the plan
  dimensions, working closely with whoever is designated as the site's competent
  person for excavation safety. A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] verifies
  the required daily excavation inspections are actually happening and documented,
  since a cave-in is one of construction's most survivable-if-prevented, fatal-if-not
  hazards.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew works in an unshored trench deeper than five feet, judging the soil
  "stable enough" without a documented competent-person inspection or any protective
  system in place. A section of the trench wall collapses without warning while a
  worker is inside it, a hazard that OSHA's excavation protection requirements exist
  specifically to prevent.
