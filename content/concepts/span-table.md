---
id: concept-span-table
title: Span Table
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics, and Composites"
definition: >
  An engineering reference chart that tells a framer what size and species of
  lumber, or what engineered lumber product, is required to safely span a
  given distance while carrying a specified load, used to size headers, floor
  joists, ceiling joists, and roof rafters.
whyItMatters: >
  A beam or joist that looks strong enough by eye can still be badly
  undersized for its actual span and load. Span tables take the guesswork out
  of framing by translating building code requirements into a simple lookup:
  given this span and this load, use at least this size member.
example: >
  A carpenter framing a floor checks a span table to confirm that a floor
  joist spanning twelve feet, under the load the code requires for that room's
  use, needs at least a specific depth and spacing, rather than relying on
  whatever lumber size was used on a previous, different job.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] references span tables constantly while
  framing, since the correct lumber size changes with every different span,
  load, and spacing combination.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framing crew reuses a joist size from a previous project on a new floor
  with a longer span, without checking a span table for the new dimensions.
  The floor ends up with noticeable deflection, bouncing underfoot, because
  the joists were never actually sized for this particular span.
