id: concept-ordinance-or-law-coverage-tiers
title: Ordinance or Law Coverage Tiers (A/B/C)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The three components that make up ordinance or law coverage: Coverage A
  reimburses the value of an undamaged portion of a building that must be
  demolished to comply with current code, Coverage B pays the added cost of
  demolishing and clearing debris, and Coverage C pays the increased
  construction cost of rebuilding to meet current code rather than the
  building's original, often older, standard.
whyItMatters: >
  Knowing which tier applies to which cost is critical on a partial-loss rebuild
  in an older building, since code upgrades, like bringing electrical or
  structural systems up to current standards, can add substantial cost that
  only this specific coverage, not standard dwelling coverage, will pay for.
example: >
  A contractor rebuilding a partially fire-damaged 1960s home must demolish an
  undamaged wing because current code no longer allows the original floor
  plan's egress layout, with Coverage A paying the undamaged wing's value,
  Coverage B paying its demolition cost, and Coverage C paying the added cost
  of rebuilding it to current code.
careerAngle: >
  Estimators on older-building losses should check whether ordinance or law
  coverage exists and at what limit, since a code-driven cost that standard
  dwelling coverage won't pay can otherwise fall entirely on the property
  owner.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume standard dwelling coverage pays for any code-required
  upgrade during a repair, when the added cost of meeting current code on an
  older building typically requires this separate, often limited, ordinance or
  law coverage.
relatedIds:
