---
id: concept-concrete-control-joint
title: Concrete Control Joint (Slabs)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  A planned, tooled or saw-cut groove placed into a concrete slab at regular
  intervals, giving the slab's inevitable shrinkage crack a predictable,
  controlled place to happen rather than cracking randomly across the surface.
whyItMatters: >
  All concrete shrinks slightly as it cures and will crack somewhere. A
  control joint doesn't prevent that crack, it decides where it happens,
  keeping the crack hidden in a straight, intentional line instead of a jagged
  crack running across an otherwise finished surface. This is a different
  concept from [[concept-masonry-control-joint|Masonry Control Joint]], which
  does a similar job but for a completely different material and movement
  pattern.
example: >
  A crew saw-cuts control joints into a large interior slab in a regular grid
  pattern shortly after the concrete has set enough to cut without raveling,
  so that as the slab shrinks while curing, the cracking happens neatly along
  those cut lines instead of randomly.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] times the saw-cutting of
  control joints carefully, since cutting too early tears the surface and
  cutting too late means the slab may have already cracked on its own,
  somewhere other than the planned joint line.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A large slab is poured without a control joint plan laid out in advance. The
  slab shrinks as it cures and cracks randomly across its surface instead of
  along a controlled line, leaving a cosmetic and sometimes structural defect
  that has no clean fix after the fact.
