id: concept-grease-interceptor
title: Grease Interceptor
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "22, Plumbing"
definition: >
  A plumbing device installed in a commercial kitchen's wastewater line that
  separates and traps fats, oils, and grease before the wastewater reaches the
  municipal sewer system, sized based on the kitchen's actual fixture flow
  rates rather than a single standard size, and required by most local codes
  for any food-service establishment.
whyItMatters: >
  An undersized grease interceptor can't handle a kitchen's actual peak flow,
  letting grease pass through into the municipal sewer system where it
  accumulates and causes blockages, which is exactly the problem the code
  requirement exists to prevent, making correct sizing a real compliance
  issue, not just a design preference.
example: >
  A restaurant's plumbing engineer sizes its grease interceptor based on the
  total fixture flow rate of every connected kitchen fixture, sink,
  dishwasher, floor drain, rather than a generic size assumed to work for any
  restaurant.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] installs the grease interceptor and connects
  the kitchen's fixtures to it. A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]]
  calculates the required sizing based on the kitchen equipment's actual flow
  demand.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A restaurant's plumbing contractor installs a standard-sized grease
  interceptor without calculating the specific kitchen's actual fixture flow
  rate, assuming one size fits most restaurants. The undersized interceptor
  lets grease pass through and cause a sewer blockage within the first year,
  and the local utility requires the restaurant to install a properly sized
  replacement.
