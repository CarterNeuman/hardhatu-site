---
id: concept-moisture-mapping
title: Moisture Mapping
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The systematic use of moisture meters and often thermal imaging to document
  exactly how far water has migrated through a structure, creating a documented,
  defensible boundary between what actually needs to be dried or removed and what
  wasn't affected at all.
whyItMatters: >
  Without documented moisture mapping, a dispute over how much of a structure was
  actually affected by water damage comes down to one side's word against the
  other's; mapping turns that argument into a measurement both sides can review.
example: >
  A restoration technician uses a moisture meter to test drywall at regular
  intervals around a water-damaged room, marking and photographing exactly where
  readings return to normal, establishing a documented boundary for what actually
  needs to be removed versus dried in place.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-restoration-estimator|Insurance Restoration Estimator]]
  uses moisture mapping data to write an estimate scoped to the actual documented
  extent of damage. An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] relies on
  moisture maps to verify a claimed scope isn't larger than what the water damage
  actually reached.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A restoration company skips moisture mapping and removes drywall well beyond the
  visibly damaged area "to be safe," without documentation to support the extra
  scope. The insurer disputes and denies payment for the additional removal, since
  nothing shows that area was actually affected by the loss.
