---
id: concept-hydronic-systems
title: Hydronic Systems
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "23, HVAC"
definition: >
  Heating or cooling systems that move water or a water-glycol mixture through
  piping using pump pressure to transfer heat, as distinct from a gravity-fed
  plumbing system that simply relies on elevation to move water.
whyItMatters: >
  Hydronic systems show up throughout a building's mechanical systems, from
  boilers and radiators to the chilled water loops that feed large HVAC
  equipment, and they require their own design considerations, pump sizing,
  pipe sizing for flow rate, that are different from the considerations a
  gravity-fed domestic water system needs.
example: >
  A building's heating system circulates hot water from a boiler through a
  closed hydronic loop, using a pump to push the water through radiators
  throughout the building and back to the boiler, rather than relying on
  gravity to move the water anywhere.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-pipefitter|Pipefitter]] installs the pressurized piping a
  hydronic system depends on, distinct from the gravity-fed piping a plumber
  typically installs for domestic water and drainage.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A hydronic system is piped with fittings and pipe sizing appropriate for a
  gravity-fed system instead of one designed for pump pressure, creating
  excessive flow resistance in the loop. The system struggles to circulate
  enough water to heat the building evenly, and the pump has to work harder
  than it should.
