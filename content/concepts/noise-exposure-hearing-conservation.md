title: Noise Exposure Standard / Hearing Conservation Program
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's construction noise standard, which requires hearing protection and
  administrative controls once worker exposure reaches an 8-hour time-weighted
  average of 90 decibels, and a broader hearing conservation program,
  including audiometric testing, once exposure regularly approaches that
  level.
whyItMatters: >
  Hearing loss from jobsite noise is permanent, cumulative, and often goes
  unnoticed until years of exposure have already done the damage, which is why
  the standard focuses on exposure averaged over a full shift rather than just
  loud moments.
example: >
  A crew running jackhammers and saws for most of a shift gets issued hearing
  protection and rotated through quieter tasks periodically, while the
  company's hearing conservation program schedules annual audiometric testing
  to catch any hearing loss trend early.
careerAngle: >
  Superintendents scheduling noisy work should think about total shift-long
  exposure, not just peak noise level, since a worker bouncing between a loud
  saw and a quiet task all day can still average under the trigger level.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Workers sometimes think hearing protection is only needed right next to the
  loudest tool, when the standard is based on an averaged exposure over the
  full workday, so moving away from one loud source doesn't necessarily mean
  exposure is under control.
