---
id: concept-bioswale
title: Bioswale
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A shallow, landscaped drainage channel, typically planted with vegetation,
  designed to slow down, filter, and partially absorb stormwater runoff as it
  moves across a site, rather than routing that runoff directly into a storm
  drain untreated.
whyItMatters: >
  Stormwater that runs straight off a site carries pollutants, oil, sediment,
  debris, directly into waterways. A bioswale is a natural filtering step that
  slows the water down and lets vegetation and soil absorb some of it and
  filter out pollutants before the water continues on, often as part of a
  broader sustainable site design strategy.
example: >
  A parking lot's stormwater is routed through a landscaped bioswale running
  along its edge before it reaches the site's storm drain system, slowing the
  water down and filtering it through planted vegetation along the way.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] designs a bioswale's size, slope,
  and plantings to handle a site's expected stormwater volume, coordinating
  with landscape design.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A bioswale is installed as part of a project's stormwater design but isn't
  maintained afterward, letting sediment build up and vegetation die off. The
  swale stops functioning as an effective filter, and the site's stormwater
  runoff ends up reaching the storm drain largely untreated anyway.
