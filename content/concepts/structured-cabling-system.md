id: concept-structured-cabling-system
title: Structured Cabling System
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "27, Communications"
definition: >
  The organized network of data and telecommunications cabling, backbone
  cabling between telecom rooms, horizontal cabling to each workstation, patch
  panels, and cross-connects, installed to an industry standard, most commonly
  TIA-568, so the resulting network infrastructure is documented, labeled, and
  usable regardless of what specific equipment eventually connects to it.
whyItMatters: >
  A structured cabling system is designed to outlast any single piece of
  equipment connected to it, a building's cabling infrastructure typically has
  a much longer service life than the computers or phone systems plugged into
  it, so cutting corners on labeling and documentation during installation
  creates a maintenance headache that outlives the original installer by
  years.
example: >
  A low-voltage contractor labels and documents every cable run in a new
  office building's structured cabling system according to the TIA-568
  standard, so facilities staff years later can identify exactly which patch
  panel port corresponds to which workstation without having to trace cables
  physically.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] or specialty low-voltage contractor
  installs and terminates structured cabling. A
  [[career-construction-technology-manager|Construction Technology Manager]]
  often specifies the cabling standard and documentation requirements for a
  project.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A low-voltage contractor installs a building's structured cabling quickly
  to meet the schedule but skips proper labeling at the patch panels,
  planning to document it later. The documentation never actually gets
  completed, and facilities staff spend hours tracing individual cables by
  hand every time they need to troubleshoot a connectivity issue after
  occupancy.
