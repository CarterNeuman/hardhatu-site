---
id: concept-impact-wrench
title: Impact Wrench (Structural Bolting)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  A high-torque power tool that delivers rapid rotational impacts rather than
  continuous force, used during structural steel erection to drive and fully
  tension the final structural bolts once a connection has been aligned and
  temporarily held in place.
whyItMatters: >
  Structural bolts have to be tensioned to a specific, code-required level to
  perform correctly, and simply spinning a bolt tight by hand can't reliably
  reach that tension. An impact wrench provides the torque needed to properly
  tension a high-strength structural bolt, a step that's actually inspected
  and signed off on, not just a convenience over hand tools.
example: >
  Once an ironworker crew has aligned a connection with a spud wrench and set
  a few bolts by hand to hold it temporarily, they switch to an impact wrench
  to bring every bolt in that connection up to its required final tension.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] finishes every permanent structural
  connection with an impact wrench, since the earlier hand-tool alignment
  steps are only meant to hold a connection temporarily, not to its final
  required strength.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew leaves a connection's bolts snug but not fully tensioned with an
  impact wrench, planning to come back and finish it later, then loses track
  of which connections still need final tensioning. A special inspection finds
  bolts that were never actually brought up to their required tension.
