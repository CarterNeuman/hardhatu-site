id: concept-rooftop-unit
title: Rooftop Unit (RTU)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "23, HVAC"
definition: >
  A packaged heating and cooling unit mounted on a building's roof, combining the
  compressor, coils, and fan into a single factory-built unit that connects to the
  building's ductwork, the standard cooling approach for most single-story commercial
  and retail buildings.
whyItMatters: >
  An RTU has to be set on a properly engineered curb that both supports its weight and
  seals the roof penetration around it, so this single piece of equipment sits at the
  intersection of structural, mechanical, and roofing work, a coordination point where
  a mistake in any one trade's scope can compromise the other two.
example: >
  A crane sets a new RTU onto a roof curb built to the manufacturer's exact dimensions
  and structural load requirements, and the roofing crew flashes and seals the curb
  into the roofing membrane before the mechanical contractor connects ductwork and
  power.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] sets, connects, and commissions the
  unit, verifying it operates to its rated capacity before turnover. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] confirms the curb's
  structural support and roof flashing details are complete and inspected before the
  unit is set, since re-flashing under an already-set unit is far harder than doing it
  right the first time.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A roofing crew flashes an RTU curb using a generic detail rather than the
  manufacturer's specific curb dimensions, leaving a slight gap at one corner. The gap
  isn't obvious during a dry-weather walkthrough, but the first heavy rain finds it,
  and water tracks into the ceiling below before the leak is traced back to the
  improperly flashed curb.
