title: Trench Box / Protective System
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's excavation standard requires a protective system, sloping the trench
  walls back, shoring them, or using a trench box or shield, for any excavation
  five feet deep or greater, unless the excavation is entirely in stable rock. The
  system has to be sized to the soil's actual classification, Type A, B, or C,
  determined on site by a competent person.
whyItMatters: >
  A trench that looks stable can still collapse suddenly and without warning, soil
  doesn't hold up predictably just because it hasn't moved yet, which is exactly
  why the standard doesn't let anyone skip the protective system based on how a
  trench looks that day.
example: >
  Before workers enter a seven-foot utility trench, a competent person classifies
  the soil type and a properly sized trench box is lowered into place, sized to
  that specific classification rather than assumed to be adequate.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] sets the trench box in
  place, while a [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] or the jobsite's
  designated competent person makes the soil classification call that determines
  what system is actually required.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew works in an unshielded six-foot trench because the trench box "wasn't
  available that day" and the schedule was tight. The trench wall collapses,
  causing a fatality that a protective system, costing a small fraction of the
  incident's real cost, would have prevented.
