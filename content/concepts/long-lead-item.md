id: concept-long-lead-item
title: Long-Lead Item
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A material or piece of equipment, such as switchgear, elevators, custom windows, or
  specialty mechanical units, whose manufacturing and delivery time is long enough that it has to
  be ordered early, often before other design or construction details are finalized, or
  it will delay the whole project.
whyItMatters: >
  A project schedule built without accounting for a 40-week equipment lead time is a
  schedule built on a false assumption. Identifying long-lead items early, and ordering
  them before they'd normally be needed, is often the difference between hitting a
  completion date and missing it by months.
example: >
  A hospital project's medical gas system requires custom equipment with a 30-week lead
  time. The project team places the order during design development, months before the
  mechanical room is even framed, so the equipment arrives right when the space is ready
  for it instead of holding up the schedule.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] builds long-lead items into the schedule as their own
  tracked activities, often starting before construction itself does. A
  [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] pushes to get long-lead items ordered
  during [[concept-buyout|buyout]], sometimes before the full subcontract is even
  finalized, specifically to protect the schedule.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team waits until a mechanical subcontractor is fully under contract to order
  a custom air handling unit, not realizing it carries a 24-week lead time. The unit
  arrives four months late, and by then the mechanical room built to receive it has been
  finished and closed up around empty space, delaying the entire building's opening.
