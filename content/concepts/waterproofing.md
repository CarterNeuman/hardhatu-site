---
id: concept-waterproofing
title: Waterproofing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  Membrane or coating systems applied to building elements below grade (foundation
  walls, below-grade slabs) or in wet areas to create a continuous barrier against
  water intrusion, distinct from "damp-proofing," a lighter-duty treatment that
  resists moisture but isn't rated to hold back standing water or hydrostatic
  pressure.
whyItMatters: >
  Confusing waterproofing with damp-proofing is a common and costly mistake: a
  below-grade wall subject to real hydrostatic pressure (water actively pushing
  against it) needs a true waterproofing system, and a lighter damp-proofing treatment
  applied in that situation will eventually fail once groundwater conditions create
  real pressure against the wall.
example: >
  A building with an occupied basement below the water table uses a fully adhered
  waterproofing membrane on the foundation walls, rated specifically to resist
  hydrostatic pressure, rather than a lighter damp-proofing coating that wouldn't hold
  up under those groundwater conditions.
careerAngle: >
  A specialty waterproofing crew, often working alongside a
  [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]], applies the membrane before the
  foundation is backfilled, since it's essentially impossible to fix once soil covers
  it. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] inspects the
  waterproofing membrane for continuity and proper detailing at penetrations and
  corners before backfill is approved.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project substitutes a damp-proofing coating for the specified waterproofing
  membrane on a below-grade wall to save cost, reasoning that the site "doesn't seem
  that wet." A few years after occupancy, seasonal groundwater rises high enough to
  create real hydrostatic pressure against the wall, and water finds its way through
  the damp-proofing into the finished basement space below, a failure specifically
  avoidable by using the originally specified system.
