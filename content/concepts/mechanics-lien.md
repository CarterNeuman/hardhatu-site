id: concept-mechanics-lien
title: Mechanic's Lien
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A legal claim placed against a property by a contractor, subcontractor, or supplier
  who hasn't been paid for work or materials, one that can cloud the property's title
  and, in some cases, force a sale to satisfy the debt.
whyItMatters: >
  It's one of the strongest tools a contractor has to actually get paid, since it
  attaches to the property itself rather than just being a claim against a company that
  might have no money left. For an owner, an unresolved lien can block a sale or a
  refinance until it's cleared.
example: >
  A drywall subcontractor doesn't get paid by a general contractor who's run into cash
  problems. Rather than just suing the GC, the subcontractor files a mechanic's lien
  directly against the owner's property, since the unpaid work is physically part of
  that building.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] tracks lien deadlines and
  requirements closely, since missing a filing window can mean losing the right to file
  one at all. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] wants to avoid liens
  entirely; one filed against the owner's property, even by a subcontractor the GC
  owes money to, damages the relationship with the owner.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A general contractor pays a subcontractor in full, but the subcontractor never pays
  their own material supplier. The unpaid supplier files a mechanic's lien against the
  owner's property, even though the owner already paid the GC for that same work, and
  the owner is left fighting to clear a lien for money they've technically already paid
  once.
