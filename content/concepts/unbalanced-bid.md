---
id: concept-unbalanced-bid
title: Unbalanced Bid
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A bid where a contractor shifts pricing between line items, overpricing items
  performed early in a project and underpricing items performed later, so the
  total bid stays competitive while early pay applications bring in more cash
  than that early work actually earned.
whyItMatters: >
  An owner evaluating unit-price bids has to watch for this, since an unbalanced
  bid can look identical in total price to a balanced one while quietly
  front-loading the contractor's cash flow at the owner's expense, and some
  public agencies specifically reject bids found to be materially unbalanced.
example: >
  A unit-price highway bid prices early excavation work well above its actual
  cost and late paving work well below cost, keeping the total bid
  competitive. Once significant excavation work is complete, the contractor
  has already been paid a disproportionate share of the total contract value
  relative to the work actually finished.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-estimator|Estimator]] has to balance unit pricing carefully to
  avoid triggering a responsiveness challenge, and the
  [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] reviews line-item pricing
  for signs of material unbalancing before recommending an award.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner awards a unit-price contract based on the lowest total price alone,
  without checking whether the line-item pricing underneath that total is
  unbalanced, and ends up paying out a disproportionate share of the contract
  value before a disproportionate share of the actual work is done.
relatedIds:
  - career-estimator
  - career-procurement-manager
---
