id: concept-named-insured-vs-additional-insured
title: Named Insured vs. Additional Insured
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The foundational distinction in a liability policy between the named insured,
  the party the policy is written for and who controls it, and an additional
  insured, a party added to share in that same policy's protection for claims
  arising from the named insured's work, without taking on the named insured's
  own obligations.
whyItMatters: >
  Who counts as which determines what coverage actually responds to a claim: a
  general contractor added as an additional insured on a sub's policy is
  protected for the sub's work, but it's still the sub's policy, with the
  sub's limits and the sub's claims history driving renewal.
example: >
  A general contractor is named as an additional insured on a drywall sub's
  general liability policy; when a worker is hurt on the drywall sub's scope,
  the GC has coverage under that policy, but if the sub's policy lapses or
  hits its aggregate limit, the GC's protection goes with it.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors negotiating subcontracts should understand that being added as an
  additional insured is not the same as carrying their own policy, and
  shouldn't be treated as a substitute for it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume an additional insured has the same rights as the named
  insured, like control over settlement decisions or defense counsel
  selection, when those rights generally stay with the named insured.
