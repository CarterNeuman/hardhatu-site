id: concept-temporary-facilities
title: Temporary Facilities
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The non-permanent infrastructure set up during [[concept-mobilization|mobilization]]
  to support construction (site trailers, temporary power and water, perimeter
  fencing, portable toilets, and temporary site lighting), none of which is part of the
  finished building but all of which the job can't run without.
whyItMatters: >
  Crews can't safely or legally work on a site with no power, no fencing, and no
  sanitation. Temporary facilities are easy to overlook when planning a schedule or
  budget because they don't show up in the finished building, but skipping or
  under-budgeting them stalls a project before real construction can even start.
example: >
  A project's temporary facilities package includes a job trailer for the site office, a
  temporary electrical panel to power tools before permanent power is energized, and
  chain-link fencing around the entire site perimeter for security and public safety.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] sets up and maintains temporary facilities
  throughout construction, since a site power outage or a damaged fence becomes their
  problem to fix immediately. A [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] on the
  owner's side sometimes gets involved when temporary facilities interact with an
  occupied building nearby, like tying temporary power into an existing structure.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team underestimates the cost of temporary facilities in the original bid,
  treating it as a minor line item. Temporary power ends up needing a larger service
  than budgeted once several trades are running equipment simultaneously, and the extra
  cost comes out of the project's [[concept-contingency|contingency]] instead of being
  priced in from the start.
