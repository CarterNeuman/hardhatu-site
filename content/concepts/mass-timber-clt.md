---
id: concept-mass-timber-clt
title: Mass Timber & Cross-Laminated Timber (CLT)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Large, engineered wood structural panels and members, most commonly CLT,
  made by gluing     layers of lumber together in alternating directions,
  strong and dimensionally stable enough     to replace steel or concrete in a
  building's primary structure, now permitted up to 18     stories under the
  2021 IBC's new Type IV-A construction classification.
whyItMatters: >
  Mass timber sequesters carbon in the building's own structure rather than
  emitting it the     way steel and concrete production do, but it also
  changes the fire-protection strategy     entirely: Type IV-A buildings
  require a specific combination of exposed-timber limits and
  noncombustible protection the code didn't need to address before mass timber
  could go this     tall.
example: >
  A 12-story apartment building is designed under the 2021 IBC's Type IV-B
  classification,     which caps exposed mass timber surface area and requires
  the rest of the structural CLT to     be protected with gypsum wallboard, a
  fire-rating tradeoff a conventional steel building     never has to make.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] designing a mass timber
  building has to     coordinate the structural design with the specific IV-A,
  IV-B, or IV-C classification the     project is pursuing, since each caps
  height and exposed-timber area differently.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A design team specifies mass timber assuming it automatically qualifies for
  the tallest     18-story IBC allowance, when the taller classifications
  (Type IV-A) require nearly all     structural timber to be fully protected,
  and only the shorter IV-C classification allows     broad timber exposure.
