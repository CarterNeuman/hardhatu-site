id: concept-depreciation-schedule-by-trade
title: Depreciation Schedule by Trade
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A reference table insurers use to estimate how much value a building component
  has lost to age and wear, broken out by trade category, such as roofing,
  flooring, or HVAC, each with its own typical useful life and depreciation
  rate.
whyItMatters: >
  This schedule drives how much of a claim is paid as actual cash value versus
  held back as recoverable depreciation, so a contractor estimating a claim
  needs to understand which trade-specific depreciation rate the insurer
  applied and whether it's reasonable for the component's actual age and
  condition.
example: >
  An adjuster applies a 15-year useful-life depreciation schedule to a
  10-year-old asphalt roof, while the roofing contractor pushes back by
  documenting that the specific manufacturer's product, now well-maintained,
  has a longer typical service life than the schedule's default assumption.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors disputing a depreciation figure should be ready to show
  documentation, like manufacturer specs or maintenance records, that supports
  a different useful-life assumption than the insurer's default trade
  schedule.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume a component's depreciation is based solely on its
  calendar age, when actual condition, maintenance history, and the specific
  material can all be grounds to argue for a different rate than the
  schedule's default.
