---
id: concept-resource-leveling
title: Resource Leveling
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Adjusting a schedule's activity timing, within the float already available, to
  smooth out spikes and dips in labor or equipment demand, so a project
  doesn't call for twice the available crew one week and almost none the next.
whyItMatters: >
  [[concept-resource-loading|Resource loading]] shows whether a schedule's
  demand for people and equipment is realistic at all; resource leveling is
  the follow-up step that actually adjusts activity timing, using available
  float, to flatten out an uneven demand curve the loading step revealed.
example: >
  Resource loading reveals that a schedule calls for twenty-five electricians in
  one week but only six the next, a staffing spike the company can't
  realistically crew. The scheduler levels the schedule by shifting some
  non-critical electrical activities, using their available float, into the
  lighter week, smoothing the demand without affecting the project's finish
  date.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] performs resource leveling once resource
  loading exposes an unrealistic demand spike, and the
  [[career-operations-manager|Operations Manager]] relies on a leveled
  schedule to plan crew assignments across multiple active projects at once.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A schedule passes a basic logic check and shows an achievable finish date, but
  nobody levels the resource demand underneath it, and the project discovers
  mid-stream that no crew the size the unleveled schedule actually calls for
  in a given week is realistically available.
