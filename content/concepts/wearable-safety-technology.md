---
id: concept-wearable-safety-technology
title: Wearable Safety Technology
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Sensor-equipped devices worn by jobsite workers, such as a vest or hard
  hat insert that detects a fall or a worker entering a hazardous zone, or
  a proximity sensor that alerts both a worker and equipment operator when
  they get too close to each other, feeding real-time safety alerts rather
  than relying solely on visual awareness.
whyItMatters: >
  Wearable technology works alongside, not instead of, a jobsite's existing
  safety program and PPE requirements, a proximity sensor alerting an
  equipment operator doesn't replace the spotter and exclusion zone
  procedures already required around heavy equipment, so it's an added
  layer of protection rather than a substitute for established practices.
example: >
  A highway contractor equips workers near active equipment with
  proximity-sensing wearables that alert both the worker and the equipment
  operator with an audible warning when they come within a set distance of
  each other, layered on top of the crew's existing spotter procedures.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] evaluates and rolls out
  wearable safety technology, monitoring the data it generates for
  patterns, such as a specific area of the site triggering frequent
  proximity alerts.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project equips its crew with fall-detection wearables and assumes the
  technology itself reduces fall risk, cutting back on its usual fall
  protection inspections since the wearables provide a safety net. A worker
  falls from height wearing a device that alerts responders quickly after
  the fact, but the underlying missing guardrail that caused the fall was
  never actually caught because the routine inspection it would have been
  found in had been scaled back.
