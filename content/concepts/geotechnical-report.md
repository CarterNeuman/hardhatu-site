id: concept-geotechnical-report
title: Geotechnical Report
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  A report prepared by a geotechnical engineer based on soil borings,
  laboratory testing, and site investigation, documenting a site's actual
  soil and groundwater conditions and providing specific recommendations for
  foundation design, allowable bearing capacity, and any special
  considerations, such as expansive soil or a high water table, the project
  needs to account for.
whyItMatters: >
  A structural engineer's foundation design depends entirely on the
  geotechnical report's soil recommendations, designing a foundation before
  the geotechnical investigation is complete, or on a generic assumption
  instead of site-specific data, risks a foundation that doesn't actually
  match the soil conditions it's built on.
example: >
  A structural engineer designs a building's foundation using the specific
  allowable bearing capacity and groundwater depth documented in the
  project's geotechnical report, rather than a generic assumption based on
  the region's typical soil conditions.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] or geotechnical engineer
  prepares the report based on soil borings and lab testing. A
  [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] designs the foundation
  directly from the report's specific recommendations.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A developer starts foundation design on a fast-tracked project before the
  geotechnical report is complete, using a generic bearing capacity
  assumption to keep the schedule moving. The actual geotechnical report,
  once finished, reveals significantly weaker soil than assumed, forcing a
  redesign of the foundation after structural work has already begun.
