id: concept-life-cycle-assessment
title: Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A method for calculating a building's total environmental impact across
  its entire life span, from raw material extraction and manufacturing
  through construction, operation, and eventual demolition, combining a
  building's embodied carbon with its operational energy use into one
  comprehensive picture rather than evaluating each separately.
whyItMatters: >
  Optimizing a building's operational energy performance in isolation can
  sometimes increase its embodied carbon, thicker insulation or additional
  mechanical equipment both carry their own manufacturing footprint, an LCA
  is what actually reveals whether a given design decision reduces the
  building's total lifetime impact or just shifts it from one category to
  another.
example: >
  A design team runs a whole-building LCA comparing two envelope options,
  finding that a proposed high-performance assembly's manufacturing
  footprint outweighs its operational energy savings within the building's
  expected service life, information neither an embodied carbon calculation
  nor an energy model alone would have shown.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]]
  runs or reviews the LCA to guide major design decisions, typically
  working alongside the project's [[concept-energy-modeling|energy modeling]]
  to see the complete picture.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A design team runs an LCA comparing two structural systems but sets
  inconsistent system boundaries between them, including one option's
  end-of-life demolition and transportation stages while leaving those same
  stages out of the other option's analysis. The mismatched comparison isn't
  caught until a third-party reviewer reruns the assessment with matched
  boundaries and finds the original conclusion actually reverses once both
  systems are evaluated on the same basis.
