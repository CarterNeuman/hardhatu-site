title: ADA Title III Accessibility Compliance
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The section of the Americans with Disabilities Act requiring places of public
  accommodation to be accessible. New construction and most alterations must
  follow the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design in full, while an existing
  building that isn't being altered only has to remove barriers when doing so is
  "readily achievable," a lower, more flexible bar.
whyItMatters: >
  The compliance bar shifts dramatically the moment a project crosses from a minor
  repair into an "alteration," a renovation that trips that line can suddenly owe
  full accessibility upgrades to areas the project never intended to touch, like the
  path of travel leading to the work.
example: >
  A restaurant renovating its kitchen doesn't have to touch its dining room
  restrooms under the readily-achievable standard, but if that same renovation
  also alters the dining room itself, the restrooms along the path of travel to the
  kitchen may now have to meet full 2010 Standards.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-permit-expediter|Permit Expediter]] flags ADA scope early in the
  permitting process, since a plan reviewer catching a missed accessibility
  requirement late can stall a permit for weeks.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A renovation crosses the line from minor repair into a full alteration without
  anyone updating the accessible path of travel to match, and a plan reviewer
  rejects the permit application over the gap, forcing a redesign after the
  contractor had already priced and scheduled the original scope.
