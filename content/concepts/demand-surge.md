---
id: concept-demand-surge
title: Demand Surge (Post-Catastrophe Pricing)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A temporary spike in labor and material costs that follows a major disaster,
  driven by overwhelming local demand for repairs combined with a limited
  supply of contractors and materials, which insurers and estimators account
  for separately from normal pricing.
whyItMatters: >
  A claim priced using pre-storm cost data can significantly underfund the
  actual repair if demand surge isn't factored in, since material and labor
  costs can rise sharply in the weeks after a major regional disaster.
example: >
  After a hurricane damages thousands of roofs in a region, estimating software
  applies a demand-surge adjustment factor on top of standard local pricing,
  reflecting the temporary spike in roofing material and labor costs the storm
  itself caused.
careerAngle: >
  Estimators and adjusters working catastrophe claims need to apply demand-surge
  pricing adjustments, since using baseline pricing after a major regional
  disaster typically underfunds the repair.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume contractors raising prices after a disaster are simply
  price-gouging, when a documented demand-surge adjustment reflecting genuine
  local cost increases is standard practice that estimating platforms build
  into catastrophe pricing.
