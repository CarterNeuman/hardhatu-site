---
id: concept-site-specific-safety-plan
title: Site-Specific Safety Plan (SSSP)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A written safety plan developed for one particular jobsite, addressing that
  site's actual hazards, emergency procedures, and site-specific rules, often
  required by an owner or general contractor before work can begin, distinct
  from a company's generic, company-wide safety program.
whyItMatters: >
  A company's general safety manual can't anticipate every hazard on every
  jobsite, so many owners and GCs specifically require a plan tailored to that
  site's actual conditions, like its specific fall hazards, site traffic
  patterns, or nearby utilities, before allowing work to start.
example: >
  A subcontractor's generic company safety manual gets supplemented with a
  site-specific plan addressing the particular project's unique hazards, like
  a nearby active roadway and an adjacent excavation, before the general
  contractor will approve the sub to mobilize on site.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers should treat the SSSP as a living document specific to that
  one project, updating it as site conditions change, rather than reusing one
  generic plan across multiple different jobs.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Companies sometimes submit their standard company safety manual when a
  site-specific plan is actually required, missing that an SSSP has to address
  that particular site's actual hazards and conditions, not just restate
  generic company policy.
