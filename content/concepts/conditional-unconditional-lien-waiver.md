id: concept-conditional-unconditional-lien-waiver
title: Conditional vs. Unconditional Lien Waiver
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  Two forms of [[concept-lien-waiver|lien waiver]] a payee can sign. A
  conditional waiver only takes effect once payment actually clears, while an
  unconditional waiver takes effect immediately upon signing, regardless of
  whether the check clears, making it far riskier for whoever signs it.
whyItMatters: >
  Signing an unconditional waiver in exchange for a check that later bounces
  means the lien rights are already gone, a conditional waiver is what actually
  protects a payee until the money is confirmed in hand.
example: >
  A subcontractor receives a progress payment check and signs a conditional
  lien waiver rather than an unconditional one, so the waiver only takes effect
  once that specific check actually clears the bank.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] tracks which
  form of lien waiver each payment requires, since signing the wrong one can
  waive lien rights before payment is actually confirmed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A subcontractor signs an unconditional lien waiver in exchange for a check
  that later bounces, and has now waived its lien rights on that payment even
  though it was never actually paid, a mistake a conditional waiver would have
  prevented.
