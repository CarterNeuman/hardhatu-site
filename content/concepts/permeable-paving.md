id: concept-permeable-paving
title: Permeable Paving
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Pavement specifically engineered to let rainwater soak through its surface
  and into the ground below, rather than shedding water off into storm drains
  the way conventional solid pavement does.
whyItMatters: >
  Conventional pavement increases stormwater runoff, which can overwhelm storm
  drainage systems and carry pollutants directly into waterways. Permeable
  paving is one of the tools a sustainable site design uses to reduce that
  runoff at the source, letting water soak back into the ground roughly where
  it fell instead.
example: >
  A project's parking area is built using permeable paving instead of standard
  asphalt, letting rainwater filter down through the surface and into a gravel
  base below rather than running off into the site's storm drainage system.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] specifies permeable paving as
  part of a site's overall stormwater management strategy, often alongside
  other features like a bioswale.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project installs permeable paving but doesn't maintain it, letting fine
  sediment and debris clog the surface over time. The paving gradually loses
  its ability to let water through, and it ends up performing almost like
  conventional pavement despite the extra cost of installing it.
