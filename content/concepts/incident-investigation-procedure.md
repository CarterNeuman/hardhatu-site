id: concept-incident-investigation-procedure
title: Incident Investigation Procedure
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The structured process a company follows after a workplace injury or near miss
  to determine its root cause, distinct from simply recording the incident on
  the OSHA 300 Log, typically involving witness interviews, a scene review,
  and identifying the underlying conditions that allowed the incident to
  happen, not just its most immediate trigger.
whyItMatters: >
  Stopping at the obvious, immediate cause of an incident, like a worker
  tripping, often misses the real underlying condition, like a cluttered
  walkway that's been an issue for weeks, that will just cause the same kind
  of incident again if it isn't actually addressed.
example: >
  After a worker trips over debris and is injured, the investigation doesn't
  stop at "worker tripped": it traces back to find housekeeping on that area
  of the site had been neglected for over a week, and the resulting corrective
  action targets that underlying scheduling and cleanup gap, not just the
  individual trip.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers should resist stopping an investigation at the most obvious
  proximate cause, since the real corrective action usually needs to address
  the underlying condition that let the incident happen in the first place.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Companies sometimes treat recording an incident on the OSHA 300 Log as the end
  of the process, when a real investigation into root cause is a separate step
  that's what actually prevents the same incident from happening again.
