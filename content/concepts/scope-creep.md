id: concept-scope-creep
title: Scope Creep
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The gradual expansion of a project's work beyond what the contract originally
  specified, usually through a series of small requests or field decisions that
  accumulate without ever being formally captured in a
  [[concept-change-order|change order]], as opposed to a single large addition that
  obviously requires one.
whyItMatters: >
  Scope creep is dangerous specifically because no individual addition looks big enough
  to fight over, so a contractor absorbs it request by request until the cumulative cost
  and schedule impact is significant, and by then, proving each small addition was
  actually outside the original [[concept-scope-of-work|scope of work]] is much harder
  than it would have been in the moment.
example: >
  An owner's representative makes a series of small verbal requests over several weeks,
  such as moving an outlet here or adding a shelf there, each one seemingly too minor to bother
  with a formal change order. Two months later, the contractor tallies the cumulative
  labor cost of all those small additions and finds it adds up to a meaningful, unpaid
  expansion of the original scope.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] has to enforce a discipline of
  documenting every scope addition in writing, no matter how small, specifically to
  prevent scope creep from becoming an unrecoverable cost. A
  [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] reviews field logs and
  correspondence to catch scope creep before it accumulates into a dispute at closeout.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A superintendent agrees to a string of small field changes verbally, trusting that
  "we'll sort out the paperwork later," and never documents them as they happen. By the
  time the contractor tries to recover the cost at the end of the project, the owner
  disputes that the changes were ever agreed to, and without contemporaneous written
  documentation, the contractor has little leverage to prove the scope actually grew.
