id: concept-constructability-review
title: Constructability Review
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A structured review of a project's design, before construction starts, by the
  people who will actually build it, looking for details that would be difficult,
  inefficient, or unnecessarily expensive to construct as drawn.
whyItMatters: >
  An architect designs for appearance and function; they don't always know what's
  awkward or slow to actually build. A constructability review catches those problems
  on paper, while they're still cheap to fix, instead of discovering them mid-
  construction when a crew is already standing in front of the wall.
example: >
  During a constructability review, a superintendent points out that a designed ceiling
  detail would require ductwork and sprinkler piping to physically occupy the same
  space. The design team revises it before construction starts, avoiding a costly
  field conflict later.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-preconstruction-manager|Preconstruction Manager]] typically organizes
  constructability reviews, pulling in the trades whose input actually matters for a
  given design. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] brings the field perspective
  that catches problems a design team sitting in an office would never notice.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project skips a formal constructability review to save time during preconstruction.
  Midway through construction, the framing crew discovers a structural beam was
  designed to run directly through the space where a stairwell needs to go, a conflict
  a field-experienced reviewer would have caught on paper, months before it became an
  expensive, in-progress redesign.
