---
id: concept-ev-charging-infrastructure
title: EV Charging Infrastructure
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  The electrical infrastructure, conduit, panels, and charging stations,
  installed to support electric vehicle charging, ranging from simple
  Level 2 chargers in a parking garage to high-power DC fast chargers,
  sized and designed around the building's available electrical capacity
  and how many vehicles need to charge at once.
whyItMatters: >
  EV charging demand can add a substantial new electrical load to a
  building that wasn't originally designed for it, and retrofitting
  adequate capacity after the fact, upgrading a building's electrical
  service or transformer, is far more disruptive and expensive than
  planning for future EV charging capacity during original construction,
  even if only a fraction of the parking is wired for charging on day one.
example: >
  A new parking garage is designed with the electrical conduit and panel
  capacity for EV charging at every space already roughed in, even though
  chargers are only being installed at a fraction of spaces initially, so
  future demand can be met by adding chargers without a disruptive
  electrical infrastructure upgrade.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] installs the charging stations and
  their electrical connections. A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]]
  plans the building's electrical capacity to accommodate both current and
  anticipated future EV charging demand.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A developer installs EV chargers at only the handful of spaces
  immediately needed without roughing in conduit or reserving panel
  capacity for future expansion. When tenant demand for EV charging grows
  within a few years, adding capacity requires a disruptive and expensive
  electrical service upgrade that could have been avoided by planning for
  it during original construction.
