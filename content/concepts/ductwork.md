id: concept-ductwork
title: Ductwork
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "23, HVAC"
definition: >
  The sheet metal or fiberglass-duct-board network that carries conditioned air from a
  building's HVAC equipment to each room, sized and routed based on airflow
  calculations, not just whatever path happens to fit above the ceiling.
whyItMatters: >
  Ductwork that's undersized, poorly sealed, or routed with too many sharp turns can
  quietly undermine an otherwise well-designed HVAC system: airflow that never reaches
  the rooms it was calculated for shows up as uneven temperatures and higher energy use
  that's hard to diagnose once the ceiling is closed.
example: >
  A sheet metal crew fabricates and hangs ductwork through a building's ceiling plenum
  following the mechanical drawings' exact routing and sizing, sealing every joint with
  mastic or tape rated for the ductwork's pressure class before the system is tested.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] installs and seals ductwork to the
  mechanical drawings, a task where duct leakage directly undercuts the system's
  designed performance. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] coordinates
  ductwork's large physical footprint above the ceiling against competing space needs
  from electrical, plumbing, and fire sprinkler rough-in happening in the same plenum.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A ductwork installer leaves several joints unsealed to save time, assuming the
  system's overall airflow will still average out. Once the ceiling closes and the
  system is balanced, rooms at the far end of the run receive noticeably less airflow
  than designed, and the leakage isn't found until an energy audit or comfort complaint
  traces it back to the unsealed joints.
