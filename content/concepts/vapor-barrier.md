---
id: concept-vapor-barrier
title: Vapor Barrier
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  A material, typically polyethylene sheeting or a specially rated coating,
  installed within a wall, floor, or roof assembly specifically to block water vapor
  from migrating through it and condensing inside the assembly, where it can't dry out
  and causes hidden moisture damage over time.
whyItMatters: >
  Where a vapor barrier goes in an assembly (interior side vs. exterior side) depends
  entirely on climate: installing it on the wrong side for a given climate zone can
  trap moisture inside the wall instead of keeping it out, which is a more damaging
  mistake than installing no vapor barrier at all.
example: >
  In a cold climate, a vapor barrier is installed on the warm, interior side of
  exterior wall insulation, stopping interior humidity from migrating outward into the
  wall cavity where it would condense against the colder outer sheathing.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] installs the vapor barrier as part of the wall
  assembly, typically right before drywall closes up the wall. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] checks that the vapor
  barrier is installed on the correct side of the assembly for the project's climate
  zone, since the same detail that's correct in one region is a defect in another.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew builds a wall assembly using the same vapor barrier placement they always
  use, without checking that this particular project is in a different climate zone
  than their usual work. Moisture gets trapped on the wrong side of the insulation,
  and the wall cavity develops hidden condensation and eventually mold, a problem
  invisible until it's serious enough to show through the finished wall.
