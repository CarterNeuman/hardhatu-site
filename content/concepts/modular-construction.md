---
id: concept-modular-construction
title: Modular/Prefabricated Construction
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "13, Special Construction"
definition: >
  A construction method where entire building sections, such as modules, panelized
  walls, or fully assembled rooms, are manufactured in an off-site factory under controlled
  conditions, then transported to the site and set into place, rather than being
  built piece by piece in the field.
whyItMatters: >
  Modular construction shifts a large share of the actual building work off the
  critical path of site conditions and weather, often compressing the overall
  schedule significantly, but it also requires design decisions to be finalized much
  earlier than conventional construction, since a factory can't easily accommodate a
  late field change the way an on-site crew might.
example: >
  A hotel's guest rooms are manufactured as complete modules, already framed, wired,
  plumbed, and finished, in a factory, then transported to the site and stacked and
  connected by crane, dramatically compressing the time needed compared to building
  each room from scratch on site.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] manages the tight coordination
  required between the factory's production schedule and the site's readiness to
  receive modules, since a module arriving before its foundation or utility
  connections are ready creates an expensive bottleneck. A
  [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] manages a very different sequence on a
  modular project, set and connect rather than build in place, that requires
  different site logistics planning.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project orders modular units without finalizing certain interior finish
  selections in time for the factory's production schedule, assuming those details
  could still be adjusted the way they could on a conventional job. The factory
  proceeds with its own default finishes to stay on schedule, and the owner ends up
  with units that don't match what they expected, a mismatch caused by treating
  modular design decisions with the same late-stage flexibility as conventional
  construction.
