---
id: concept-successor-liability
title: Successor Liability
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The risk that a company which buys or merges with another contracting business
  inherits that business's existing legal liabilities, including pending
  construction-defect claims, warranty obligations, or unpaid judgments.
whyItMatters: >
  Buyers who skip proper due diligence before acquiring a contracting company
  can end up on the hook for problems on projects they had nothing to do with,
  which makes liability review as important to a construction-company sale as
  reviewing its backlog and balance sheet.
example: >
  A larger contractor acquires a smaller specialty subcontractor without digging
  into its project history, and six months later discovers the acquired
  company has an open construction-defect lawsuit tied to a project completed
  two years earlier. Depending on how the deal was structured, the acquiring
  company may now be defending that claim.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] structures
  acquisitions to limit successor liability exposure, and the
  [[career-construction-accountant|Construction Accountant]] reviews a target
  company's project history and open claims as part of due diligence before a
  purchase closes.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A buyer structures an acquisition as a straightforward purchase of company
  stock without realizing that, unlike a clean asset purchase, a stock
  purchase typically carries forward the acquired company's existing
  liabilities intact.
