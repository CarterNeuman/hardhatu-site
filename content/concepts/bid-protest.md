id: concept-bid-protest
title: Bid Protest
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A formal challenge filed by a losing or disqualified bidder against a public
  agency's award decision, arguing the process was flawed, usually because a
  competitor's bid was actually non-responsive, the evaluation criteria
  weren't properly followed, or the process otherwise violated procurement
  law.
whyItMatters: >
  A pending bid protest can delay a project's start significantly, since many
  public agencies have to pause moving forward with the award until the
  protest is resolved, which makes avoiding a successful protest, through a
  clean and well-documented evaluation process, as important to an agency as
  picking the right winning bidder in the first place.
example: >
  A losing bidder on a public building project files a bid protest arguing the
  winning bidder's proposal failed to meet a mandatory qualification
  requirement stated in the solicitation. The agency has to pause the award
  and formally review the protest's merits before construction can begin,
  delaying the project's start by several weeks regardless of how the protest
  is ultimately decided.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] represents a bidder
  filing or defending against a bid protest, and the
  [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] documents the evaluation
  process thoroughly enough in advance to withstand a protest if one is filed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An agency runs an informal, loosely documented evaluation process, assuming no
  one will actually challenge the award, and finds itself unable to defend the
  decision once a bid protest forces it to show exactly how and why the
  winning bid was selected.
