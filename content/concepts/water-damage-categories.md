id: concept-water-damage-categories
title: Water Damage Categories (1-3)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The IICRC S500 standard's three-tier classification of a water loss by how
  contaminated the source water is, Category 1 (clean), Category 2 (gray, containing
  significant contamination), and Category 3 (black, grossly contaminated), which
  determines how a restoration crew is required to handle drying, PPE, and material
  removal.
whyItMatters: >
  Category isn't just a label, it drives real decisions: a Category 1 loss can often
  be dried in place, while a Category 3 loss requires full protective equipment and
  removal of affected porous materials rather than drying them, since they can't be
  safely decontaminated.
example: >
  A burst supply line (Category 1, clean water) is dried in place with fans and
  dehumidifiers, while a sewage backup into the same room (Category 3, black water)
  requires removing the affected carpet, pad, and drywall entirely rather than
  attempting to dry and save them.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-restoration-estimator|Insurance Restoration Estimator]]
  prices a job completely differently depending on category, since Category 3 work
  requires containment, PPE, and material replacement that Category 1 work never
  needs.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A restoration crew treats a sewage backup, Category 3, black water, using standard
  Category 1 drying procedures to save time, leaving contaminated porous materials in
  place instead of removing them. That creates a real health hazard for the crew and
  the homeowner, and the job later fails a clearance inspection.
