---
id: concept-qualified-person
title: Qualified Person
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  An OSHA designation for someone who holds a recognized degree, certificate, or
  professional standing, or has extensive knowledge, training, and experience,
  that qualifies them to design, evaluate, or resolve a specific technical
  problem, distinct from a Competent Person, who is capable of identifying
  hazards on site and has the authority to correct them but doesn't
  necessarily hold the formal credential a qualified person does.
whyItMatters: >
  Several OSHA standards specifically require sign-off from a qualified person,
  not just a competent one, for design or engineering decisions, so a site
  relying only on its competent person for a task that actually calls for a
  qualified person's sign-off is out of compliance.
example: >
  A site's competent person identifies a trench's soil condition as unstable and
  has the authority to stop work immediately, but the specific protective
  system design for that trench still requires sign-off from a qualified
  person, in this case a licensed professional engineer.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers should know which specific standards on their project call for
  a qualified person's sign-off versus a competent person's judgment, since
  using the wrong designation for a required task is itself a compliance gap.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes use "competent person" and "qualified person"
  interchangeably, when OSHA treats them as genuinely different designations,
  a qualified person's status resting on a formal credential or extensive
  documented experience that a competent person's field authority alone
  doesn't require.
