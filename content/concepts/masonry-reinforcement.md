id: concept-masonry-reinforcement
title: Masonry Reinforcement, Ties & Anchors
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "04, Masonry"
definition: >
  The steel reinforcement embedded within a masonry wall, horizontal joint
  reinforcement laid in the mortar joints, vertical rebar grouted into CMU
  cells, and the ties and anchors connecting a veneer wythe back to its
  structural backup, that gives masonry the tensile and lateral capacity plain
  unreinforced masonry lacks.
whyItMatters: >
  Unreinforced masonry performs poorly in seismic and high-wind events since
  masonry resists compression well but has almost no tensile strength on its
  own, reinforcement is what lets a masonry wall actually resist those lateral
  forces rather than simply crack and fail.
example: >
  A mason grouts vertical rebar into specific CMU cells called out in the
  structural drawings, and lays horizontal joint reinforcement in the mortar
  every other course, giving the wall the reinforced capacity the structural
  engineer designed it to have.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] specifies exactly where
  and how much reinforcement a given wall needs. A [[career-mason|Mason]]
  places it correctly as the wall goes up, since it's impossible to inspect
  once cells are grouted.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A mason skips vertical rebar in several CMU cells to keep the work moving,
  assuming a special inspector will catch it later. The inspector isn't present
  for that day's grouting, and the missing reinforcement isn't caught until the
  structural engineer, reviewing special inspection reports during closeout,
  notices no report exists at all for that day's work, prompting a
  ground-penetrating radar scan that confirms several cells were never filled
  or reinforced.
