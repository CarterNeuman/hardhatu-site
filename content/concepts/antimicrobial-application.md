id: concept-antimicrobial-application
title: Antimicrobial Application (Mold Remediation)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The step in mold remediation where an EPA-registered antimicrobial or biocide
  product is applied to a cleaned surface to kill remaining mold spores and
  inhibit regrowth before the area is closed back up.
whyItMatters: >
  Skipping or rushing this step is a common reason mold remediation jobs fail
  inspection or the mold comes back, since physical removal of visible growth
  alone doesn't kill microscopic spores embedded in porous material.
example: >
  A remediation crew removes moldy drywall down to the studs, HEPA-vacuums the
  framing, then applies an EPA-registered antimicrobial to the studs before a
  clearance test confirms the area is ready to rebuild.
careerAngle: >
  Mold remediation technicians need current product knowledge, since using an
  antimicrobial not registered for the specific substrate or mold type can
  fail a post-remediation verification test.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes think any household bleach or disinfectant is an acceptable
  substitute for a registered antimicrobial product in a documented
  remediation protocol, when bleach doesn't penetrate porous materials and
  isn't typically specified in IICRC-based protocols.
