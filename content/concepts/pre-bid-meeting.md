id: concept-pre-bid-meeting
title: Pre-Bid Meeting
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A meeting, often held on-site, before bids are due, where prospective contractors
  walk the project, review the plans, and ask the architect or owner questions about
  anything unclear before they commit to a price.
whyItMatters: >
  A contractor who prices a job without seeing the actual site is guessing at conditions
  that can swing a bid by a lot: tight access, existing utilities, an unusual site
  slope. The pre-bid meeting is what turns those unknowns into known facts before the
  number gets locked in.
example: >
  A dozen contractors walk an existing building together before submitting bids on a
  renovation. One contractor asks about a suspicious pipe in the ceiling, and the answer,
  asbestos abatement will be handled separately by the owner, changes what every
  bidder needs to include in their price.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] attends to see the real site conditions firsthand
  before pricing the work; photos and drawings only tell part of the story. A
  [[career-preconstruction-manager|Preconstruction Manager]] is often the one flagging
  which questions raised in the meeting are significant enough that the architect or
  owner needs to issue a formal [[concept-addendum|addendum]] to every bidder.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An estimator skips the pre-bid meeting to save time, assuming the drawings tell the
  whole story. After winning the job, the crew discovers the site has almost no room for
  material staging or truck access, a constraint every contractor who attended the
  walk-through already knew about and priced into their number, but this one didn't.
