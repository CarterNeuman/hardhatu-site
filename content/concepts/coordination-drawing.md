id: concept-coordination-drawing
title: Coordination Drawing
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  An overlay drawing that combines multiple trades' rough-in plans, plumbing,
  ductwork, electrical conduit, into a single view, used to catch physical
  conflicts between trades on paper before anyone actually installs pipe,
  duct, or conduit in the field.
whyItMatters: >
  Plumbing, HVAC, and electrical rough-in all compete for the same limited
  space inside a wall or ceiling cavity, and each trade's drawings are usually
  produced separately. A coordination drawing is the step where someone
  actually overlays all of them together and finds the conflicts before they
  become a physical collision in the field. The same idea done digitally in a
  3D model is what [[concept-clash-detection|Clash Detection]] covers.
example: >
  Before rough-in begins in a tight mechanical room, a project team overlays
  the plumbing, ductwork, and electrical conduit drawings for that space onto
  a single coordination drawing, catching a spot where a duct run and a pipe
  run were both planned to occupy the same physical space.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] often runs the coordination
  drawing process, since it requires actively comparing multiple trades' plans
  against each other rather than reviewing any single trade's drawings alone.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project skips coordination drawings for a tight mechanical space, assuming
  each trade's own drawings are sufficient. Two trades show up on the same day
  to rough in through the exact same physical space, and one of them has to
  reroute its work in the field instead of on paper.
