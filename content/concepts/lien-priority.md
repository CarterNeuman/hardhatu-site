---
id: concept-lien-priority
title: Lien Priority
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The order in which competing claims against a property, such as a construction
  lien, a mortgage, and a tax lien, get paid out of the proceeds if the
  property is sold or foreclosed on, when there isn't enough money to pay
  everyone in full.
whyItMatters: >
  Construction liens don't automatically jump ahead of an existing mortgage;
  depending on the state, priority can run from the date the lien is recorded,
  the date work actually started on site, or another rule entirely, which is
  why the timing of a lien filing matters as much as the filing itself.
example: >
  A property goes into foreclosure with both a bank mortgage and a contractor's
  mechanic's lien outstanding. Because the state's priority rule ties lien
  priority to the date construction visibly began on site, and that date
  predates the mortgage recording, the contractor's lien is found to have
  priority over the bank's mortgage for the proceeds of the sale.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] researches a
  state's specific lien priority rules before advising a client on how
  aggressively to pursue a lien claim, and the [[career-construction-claims-
  consultant|Construction Claims Consultant]] factors likely recovery given
  priority position into a claim's overall strategy.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor assumes filing a mechanic's lien guarantees getting paid ahead of
  the bank, without realizing that in many states a lien's priority date, not
  its filing date, determines where it actually ranks against an existing
  mortgage.
