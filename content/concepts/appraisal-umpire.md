---
id: concept-appraisal-umpire
title: Appraisal Umpire
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The neutral third party appointed to break a tie when the two party-appointed
  appraisers in an [[concept-appraisal-clause|appraisal clause]] proceeding can't
  agree on the amount of loss. The umpire's decision, combined with either
  appraiser's, becomes the final, binding award.
whyItMatters: >
  Umpire selection is where appraisal actually gets decided in practice. Each side's
  appraiser is expected to advocate somewhat for the side that appointed them, so the
  umpire is the only genuinely neutral decision-maker in the room.
example: >
  The two appraisers on a disputed claim each submit a list of umpire candidates,
  then a judge appoints one from the overlap, or by lot if there's no overlap at all,
  once it's clear the appraisers can't agree between themselves.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-public-adjuster|Public Adjuster]] serving as a policyholder's appointed
  appraiser has a direct stake in who gets selected as umpire, since that person's
  number is what actually settles a deadlocked claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Both sides' appraisers pick umpire candidates who lean toward the insurance
  industry's usual way of valuing claims, producing a "neutral" umpire who reliably
  sides with the carrier on close calls. A policyholder's attorney has to challenge
  the umpire selection process itself before the resulting appraisal award can be
  trusted as genuinely neutral.
