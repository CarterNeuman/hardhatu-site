---
id: concept-site-concrete
title: Site Concrete, Curbs & Sidewalks
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "32, Exterior Improvements"
definition: >
  Concrete work outside a building's footprint, curbs, gutters, sidewalks,
  site paving, and exterior stairs, distinct from structural building
  concrete, typically built to a civil site plan's grading and drainage
  design rather than a structural engineer's specifications.
whyItMatters: >
  Site concrete's slope and elevations are what actually make a site's
  stormwater drainage plan work in the field, a sidewalk or curb poured even
  slightly off the design grade can redirect water toward a building
  entrance instead of away from it, undermining the drainage design
  regardless of how well the design itself was engineered.
example: >
  A concrete crew sets curb and gutter forms using elevations shot directly
  from the civil engineer's grading plan, confirming each section's slope
  actually drains toward the designed catch basin rather than assuming a
  level curb line is close enough.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] forms and places site
  concrete. A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] designs the grading
  the concrete has to match.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew pours a section of sidewalk slightly out of the elevations shown on
  the grading plan, assuming a small deviation won't matter. The section
  ends up holding a shallow puddle of water after every rain instead of
  draining, and it has to be saw-cut and repoured once the standing water
  becomes a slip hazard complaint.
