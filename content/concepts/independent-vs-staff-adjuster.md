id: concept-independent-vs-staff-adjuster
title: Independent Adjuster vs. Staff Adjuster
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The distinction between a staff adjuster, a salaried employee of a single
  insurance company, and an independent adjuster, a contracted adjuster who
  works claims for multiple insurers through an independent adjusting firm,
  both still working on the insurer's behalf rather than the policyholder's.
whyItMatters: >
  Insurers lean heavily on independent adjusters during catastrophe surges, when
  claim volume far exceeds what their own staff adjusters can handle, so a
  homeowner's adjuster after a major storm is often an independent contractor
  seeing that carrier's claims for the first time rather than a long-tenured
  staff employee.
example: >
  After a hurricane generates thousands of claims at once, an insurer
  supplements its staff adjusters by contracting independent adjusters through
  a third-party adjusting firm, deploying them to the disaster area to keep
  claim response times from collapsing under the surge in volume.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors working catastrophe claims should expect to deal with independent
  adjusters more often than staff adjusters, and should know both are still
  working for the insurer, not the policyholder, whatever their employment
  arrangement.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume an independent adjuster is neutral or works for the
  policyholder because of the word 'independent,' when both staff and
  independent adjusters are retained by and paid by the insurance company,
  just through a different employment structure.
