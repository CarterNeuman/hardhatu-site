title: Water Conservation Strategies (Greywater Reuse & Low-Flow Fixtures)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Design strategies that reduce a building's potable water demand, combining
  low-flow     fixtures and efficient irrigation with greywater systems that
  capture and treat lightly used     water, such as from sinks and showers,
  for reuse in irrigation or toilet flushing rather than     sending it
  straight to the sewer.
whyItMatters: >
  Water efficiency credits are a significant scoring category in most green
  building     certifications, and a greywater system in particular can cut a
  building's potable water     demand substantially, which matters most in
  drought-prone regions where water costs and     availability are both under
  increasing pressure.
example: >
  An office building installs a greywater system that captures water from
  restroom sinks,     filters and disinfects it, and reuses it for toilet
  flushing throughout the building,     reducing the amount of potable water
  the building draws from the municipal supply for     non-drinking uses.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] installing a greywater system has to keep its
  piping     completely separate and clearly labeled from the potable water
  system, since cross-connecting     the two is a serious code violation and
  health hazard.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A greywater system is installed without clearly labeling its separate
  piping, and a later     plumbing repair accidentally cross-connects it to
  the potable water supply, a mistake that     can introduce contaminated
  water into the building's drinking water system.
