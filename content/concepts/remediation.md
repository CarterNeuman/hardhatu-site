---
id: concept-remediation
title: Remediation
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The process of removing mold, sewage contamination, or other hazardous residue
  from a structure following water damage or a similar loss, governed by industry
  protocols like the [[concept-iicrc-standards|IICRC's restoration standards]]
  rather than a specific insurance requirement, and distinct from the broader
  environmental hazard abatement (lead, asbestos) that happens on demolition and
  renovation projects.
whyItMatters: >
  Remediation that isn't documented and verified against an industry protocol is
  difficult to prove was done correctly if a dispute comes up later, since a property
  owner, a future buyer, or the insurer itself may all want proof that contamination
  was actually removed, not just that the visible symptoms were cleaned up.
example: >
  After a sewage backup, a restoration crew removes contaminated materials, applies
  antimicrobial treatment, and verifies the remaining structure is clean following
  IICRC protocol, documenting each step in case the work is ever questioned later.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]] oversees
  remediation crews to industry-standard protocol rather than a generic cleaning
  approach. An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] reviews remediation
  documentation to confirm the scope performed actually matches what was approved
  and necessary.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A remediation crew clears visible mold from a wall surface but doesn't test
  moisture levels inside the wall cavity before closing it back up. The remaining
  moisture allows mold to return within weeks, and the second remediation effort
  costs far more than properly verifying dryness the first time would have.
