id: concept-section-106-historic-review
title: Section 106 Historic Review
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A review process under the National Historic Preservation Act requiring federal
  agencies, or any project using federal funding, permits, or licenses, to consider
  a project's effect on historic properties before proceeding, coordinated with the
  State Historic Preservation Office and, when needed, the Advisory Council on
  Historic Preservation.
whyItMatters: >
  The trigger isn't whether a building looks historic, it's whether federal money,
  permits, or licenses touch the project at all, which means an otherwise ordinary
  renovation can suddenly owe a full Section 106 review the moment it accepts a
  federal grant or needs a federal permit.
example: >
  A city project renovating a downtown building uses a federal transportation
  grant for part of the funding, and that federal money alone is enough to trigger
  Section 106 review, requiring coordination with the State Historic Preservation
  Office before work can proceed.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-historic-preservation-specialist|Historic Preservation Specialist]]
  owns this process end to end, coordinating with the State Historic Preservation
  Office and, when needed, the Advisory Council on Historic Preservation.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team accepts federal grant funding without realizing that alone
  triggers Section 106 review, and construction begins before State Historic
  Preservation Office consultation happens. The agency later requires costly
  redesign, or in some cases reversal of work already completed, once the missed
  review surfaces.
