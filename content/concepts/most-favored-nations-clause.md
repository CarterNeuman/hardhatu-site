---
id: concept-most-favored-nations-clause
title: Most Favored Nations Clause (Pricing)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A contract provision guaranteeing a buyer that the price or terms it receives
  will be at least as good as those given to any other customer buying
  comparable volume or scope, protecting against the risk of unknowingly
  paying more than other, similarly situated buyers.
whyItMatters: >
  It's more common in long-term material supply agreements than in typical
  project-specific construction contracts, and it generally requires the buyer
  to actually know or be able to audit what other customers are being charged,
  which makes the clause's practical value dependent on real transparency into
  the supplier's broader pricing.
example: >
  A large general contractor negotiates a multi-year steel supply agreement that
  includes a most favored nations clause, guaranteeing that if the supplier
  later offers a better price to another contractor buying a comparable
  volume, the clause automatically extends that same better price to the
  agreement already in place.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] negotiates most favored
  nations language into major recurring material supply agreements, and the
  [[career-construction-accountant|Construction Accountant]] audits delivered
  pricing against the clause's guarantee when the company suspects better
  terms may be available elsewhere.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A buyer negotiates a most favored nations clause but never actually exercises
  any right to verify what other customers are being charged, leaving the
  protection effectively unused since nobody checks whether a better price was
  ever actually offered to someone else.
relatedIds:
