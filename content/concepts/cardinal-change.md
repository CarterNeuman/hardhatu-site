---
id: concept-cardinal-change
title: Cardinal Change
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A change to a contract so fundamental that it goes beyond what the parties
  originally bargained for, effectively creating a different project altogether,
  giving the contractor grounds to treat the original contract as breached
  rather than simply pricing yet another change order.
whyItMatters: >
  A cardinal change usually isn't one dramatic event, it's the cumulative effect
  of many individually reasonable change orders that, added together, turn the
  project into something the contractor never actually priced or agreed to build.
example: >
  A renovation contract for cosmetic upgrades to an office building accumulates
  so many structural, mechanical, and electrical change orders that the finished
  project bears little resemblance to the original scope, a pattern that can
  support treating the cumulative change as cardinal.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-construction-claims-consultant|Construction Claims Consultant]]
  analyzes a project's full change order history to determine whether its
  cumulative effect crossed the line from ordinary changes into a cardinal
  change.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner issues change order after change order, and the contractor keeps
  pricing and processing each one individually as routine, never stepping back
  to recognize the cumulative effect has become a fundamentally different
  project. By the time anyone raises a cardinal change argument, the contractor
  has already absorbed costs a breach claim could have recovered.
