id: concept-storm-drainage
title: Storm Drainage
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "33, Utilities"
definition: >
  The underground piping, catch basins, and detention or retention structures that
  collect stormwater runoff from a site's roofs and paved surfaces and convey it away,
  either to a municipal storm sewer system or to on-site detention that releases it
  slowly, depending on what the local jurisdiction requires.
whyItMatters: >
  Many jurisdictions require a site to detain stormwater on-site and release it no
  faster than the property's undeveloped runoff rate, meaning storm drainage design
  isn't just about moving water away from a building; it's a regulatory requirement
  that a site's own development doesn't push extra runoff onto neighboring
  properties or an already-stressed municipal system.
example: >
  A retail site's storm drainage system routes runoff from its roof and parking lot
  into an underground detention system sized specifically to release water at a
  controlled rate matching what the jurisdiction requires for pre-development
  conditions.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] excavates and installs storm
  drainage piping and structures to the civil engineering plans. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] verifies pipe slopes
  and structure elevations are within design tolerance before backfill, since a
  storm system that doesn't drain at the designed slope can pond water underground
  where the problem is invisible until it surfaces somewhere unexpected.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A storm drainage pipe is installed with a slightly flatter slope than the design
  called for, an error not caught before backfill covers the pipe. Water moves through
  the pipe more slowly than designed, and during a heavy storm the system backs up and
  surcharges through a catch basin onto the parking lot, a failure that traces back to
  a slope error that should have been verified before backfill.
