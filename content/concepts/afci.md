id: concept-afci
title: AFCI (Arc-Fault Circuit Interrupter)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  A protective device that detects the specific electrical signature of a
  dangerous arc, such as a nail accidentally driven through a cable, and shuts
  the circuit off before that arc can start a fire, required by code in most
  living spaces and, separately, on DC circuits for rooftop solar systems.
whyItMatters: >
  A standard breaker reacts to overload or a short, but a dangerous arc can
  occur without ever drawing enough current to trip a normal breaker. AFCI
  protection is built to recognize the specific pattern an arc creates and
  react to that pattern directly, closing a fire-risk gap that ordinary
  circuit protection doesn't cover.
example: >
  An electrician wiring a bedroom circuit installs an AFCI breaker at the
  panel, a requirement in most living spaces, so that a damaged cable behind
  the wall, one that might otherwise smolder undetected, trips the circuit
  before it can start a fire.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] installs AFCI protection on every
  circuit code requires it for, and a
  [[career-solar-installer|Solar Installer]] relies on the same AFCI principle
  on the DC side of a rooftop solar array, where a damaged panel connection
  creates a similar arc-fault risk.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A remodel adds a new bedroom circuit from an older subpanel that doesn't
  have AFCI protection, assuming the rest of the house's wiring sets the
  standard for what's required. The gap is caught during inspection, since
  current code requires AFCI on that new circuit regardless of what protection
  exists elsewhere in the house.
