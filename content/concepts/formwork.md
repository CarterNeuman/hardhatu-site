id: concept-formwork
title: Formwork
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  The temporary molds, built from wood, engineered panels, or steel, that hold
  freshly poured concrete in its designed shape and position until it cures enough to
  support itself, then get stripped away and often reused.
whyItMatters: >
  Formwork has to be strong enough to resist the substantial pressure of wet, unset
  concrete without bulging, leaking, or collapsing, and precise enough that the
  finished concrete comes out the exact size and shape the drawings call for; a
  formwork failure during a pour is one of the more dangerous jobsite incidents that
  can occur.
example: >
  A crew builds and braces plywood formwork for a foundation wall, checking it's
  plumb, square, and properly braced before the concrete pour begins, since any
  movement during the pour permanently distorts the finished wall.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] builds and sets formwork, a skill distinct from
  finish carpentry that requires understanding the forces wet concrete will exert on
  it. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] inspects formwork bracing before
  authorizing a pour to proceed, since a formwork failure mid-pour can injure workers
  and ruin the concrete element entirely.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew under schedule pressure skips a bracing step on a tall foundation wall's
  formwork, assuming it will hold for one pour. The formwork bulges under the pressure
  of the wet concrete partway through the pour, and the wall has to be demolished and
  re-poured once the compromised concrete cures out of shape.
