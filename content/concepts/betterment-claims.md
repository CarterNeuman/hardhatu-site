---
id: concept-betterment-claims
title: Betterment
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A deduction an insurer can sometimes apply when a repair unavoidably leaves a
  property in better condition than it was before the loss, distinct from a
  code-required upgrade covered separately under
  [[concept-ordinance-or-law-coverage|ordinance or law coverage]], which usually
  isn't treated as betterment.
whyItMatters: >
  Betterment is easy to apply incorrectly, since it's genuinely hard to draw the
  line between "this repair happened to improve the property" and "this upgrade was
  legally required and should be covered separately."
example: >
  A single outdated electrical panel is replaced along with damaged wiring during a
  covered repair, since code no longer allows the old panel type to be reused. The
  insurer treats the panel upgrade as ordinance-or-law coverage rather than deducting
  it as betterment, since it wasn't optional.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] has to decide, item by item,
  whether an upgrade counts as betterment or as a required code upgrade, since the
  two get handled completely differently on the claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An adjuster applies a betterment deduction to a code-required upgrade, like adding
  a required fire-rated door during a repair, that's actually covered separately
  under the policy's ordinance-or-law coverage. That incorrect deduction shortchanges
  the policyholder for an upgrade they were legitimately entitled to have paid for.
