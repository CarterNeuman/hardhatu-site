id: concept-choice-of-law-forum-selection
title: Choice of Law / Forum Selection Clause
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  Contract provisions specifying which state's law governs interpretation of the
  contract, and which specific court or location any resulting lawsuit must be
  filed in, agreed to in advance rather than left to be fought over after a
  dispute arises.
whyItMatters: >
  These clauses matter most on multi-state projects, since contract law, lien
  rights, and even which clauses are enforceable at all, like pay-if-paid
  provisions, can vary significantly by state, so a party that doesn't
  negotiate this language can end up litigating a dispute under an unfamiliar
  state's law, far from its own home base.
example: >
  A regional subcontractor signs onto a project in a different state without
  negotiating the prime contract's choice of law and forum selection clauses,
  which designate the owner's home state's law and courts. When a dispute
  arises, the subcontractor has to litigate far from home under legal rules
  less favorable to subcontractors than its own state's law would have been.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] negotiates choice
  of law and forum selection terms before a contract is signed, especially on
  multi-state work, and the [[career-contracts-administrator| Contracts
  Administrator]] flags unfavorable forum language for legal review during
  contract intake.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A company signs a multi-state contract without reading the choice of law and
  forum clauses closely, assuming disputes would naturally be handled locally,
  and only discovers the inconvenient and unfamiliar forum once a dispute
  actually requires filing suit.
