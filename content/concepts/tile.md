id: concept-tile
title: Tile
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  Ceramic, porcelain, or stone tile installed on floors, walls, or countertops, set
  into a bed of thin-set mortar or adhesive and finished with grout between the joints,
  a finish chosen as much for water resistance (bathrooms, kitchens) as for
  appearance.
whyItMatters: >
  Tile installed in a wet area depends on a properly waterproofed substrate
  underneath it: the tile and grout themselves are not the actual waterproofing
  layer in most modern assemblies, a common misconception that leads to water damage
  behind or beneath tile that looks perfectly intact on the surface.
example: >
  Before tiling a shower wall, the installer applies a waterproofing membrane over
  the substrate first, so the tile and grout serve as the finished surface while the
  actual water barrier is the membrane underneath, not the tile joints themselves.
careerAngle: >
  A specialty tile-setting crew installs tile to precise layout and leveling
  tolerances, since an uneven tile installation is highly visible in a finished
  space. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] confirms
  waterproofing is properly installed and tested in wet areas before tile covers it,
  since it's the last chance to catch a waterproofing gap.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A tile installer sets tile directly over a substrate in a shower without
  confirming a waterproofing membrane was installed underneath first, assuming the
  tile and grout alone will keep water out. Water gradually works through the grout
  lines over months of use, and because there's no membrane behind it, the moisture
  reaches the wall framing, causing hidden rot discovered only once it becomes
  extensive.
