title: Trade Permits (Electrical, Mechanical, Plumbing)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The separate permits, beyond the general building permit, typically required
  for electrical, mechanical (HVAC), and plumbing work, each reviewed against
  its own trade-specific code and usually requiring its own licensed trade
  inspector to sign off before that system can be closed in or turned on.
whyItMatters: >
  A general contractor can have a fully permitted building permit and still be
  in violation if the electrical, mechanical, or plumbing scope proceeds
  without its own separate trade-specific permit and inspection.
example: >
  A remodel's building permit covers the overall structural work, but the
  electrician and plumber each pull their own separate trade permits before
  starting rough-in, since the local jurisdiction inspects electrical and
  plumbing work against the National Electrical Code and plumbing code
  independently of the general building inspection.
careerAngle: >
  Project managers should track trade permits as their own separate line items
  in the schedule, since a missing electrical or plumbing permit can hold up a
  rough-in inspection even when the general building permit is in perfect
  order.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume one building permit covers all the work happening
  under it, when most jurisdictions require electrical, mechanical, and
  plumbing scopes to each be permitted and inspected separately by their own
  trade-specific inspector.
