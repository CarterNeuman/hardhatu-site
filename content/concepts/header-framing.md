id: concept-header-framing
title: Header (Framing)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics, and Composites"
definition: >
  A horizontal structural framing member sized, usually from an engineering
  span table, to carry the load above a wall opening, like a door or window,
  around that opening instead of down through it.
whyItMatters: >
  Cutting an opening into a load-bearing wall removes the studs that were
  carrying weight straight down at that spot, and a header is what replaces
  that lost load path, redirecting the weight from above around the opening
  and back down through the framing on either side.
example: >
  A framer installing a window opening in a load-bearing wall sizes the header
  using a span table matched to the opening's width and the load above it,
  rather than guessing at a lumber size, since an undersized header can sag or
  fail under load over time.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] sizes and installs headers over every
  opening in a load-bearing wall, checking the span table rather than relying
  on habit from a different job with different loads.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framer installs a header sized by eye rather than checked against a span
  table, assuming a size that worked on a similar-looking opening elsewhere.
  The header turns out to be undersized for this specific span and load, and
  the wall above the opening sags slightly as the house settles.
