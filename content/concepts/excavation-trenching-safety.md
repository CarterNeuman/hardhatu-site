title: Excavation & Trenching Safety
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The specific set of OSHA requirements (29 CFR 1926 Subpart P) governing protection for
  workers in an [[concept-excavation|excavation]] or trench, daily competent-person
  inspections, an appropriate protective system, atmospheric testing where needed, and a
  safe means of entering and exiting, distinct from the physical act of excavating
  itself.
whyItMatters: >
  Trench cave-ins can happen suddenly and with almost no warning, and a cubic yard of
  soil weighs enough to be fatal in seconds. Subpart P exists as its own dedicated
  standard, separate from general jobsite safety rules, specifically because this hazard
  is unusually fast and unusually deadly relative to how routine excavation work looks
  from the surface.
example: >
  A crew preparing to enter a utility trench deeper than five feet waits for the site's
  [[concept-competent-person|competent person]] to inspect the [[concept-shoring|shoring]]
  and confirm it matches the actual soil conditions before anyone climbs down.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] works inside and around
  excavations daily and depends on the protective system being correctly matched to
  actual site conditions before entering. A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]]
  verifies the required daily inspections are happening and documented, since
  excavation work remains one of construction's most survivable-if-prevented, fatal-if-not
  hazards.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew reuses yesterday's excavation inspection for today's work without a fresh
  competent-person check, assuming overnight conditions couldn't have changed. Overnight
  rain destabilizes the trench wall in a way the outdated inspection never accounted for,
  and the required daily re-inspection would have caught it before anyone went back in.
