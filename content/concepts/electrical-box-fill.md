id: concept-electrical-box-fill
title: Box Fill
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  The code-based limit on how many wires, and of what size, can legally
  terminate inside a given electrical box, calculated from the box's internal
  cubic-inch volume and a fixed volume allowance per conductor size.
whyItMatters: >
  An overfilled electrical box is a genuine fire and shock hazard, since
  overcrowded wires generate more heat and are harder to work with safely
  during future repairs. Box fill calculations are exactly the kind of code
  math that trips up new electricians, since it's easy to keep adding wires to
  a box without tracking the running total against its legal limit.
example: >
  An electrician wiring a junction box with several incoming and outgoing
  cables calculates the box fill before choosing the box size, confirming the
  box's volume is large enough for every conductor, device, and clamp inside
  it before closing it up.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] calculates box fill as a routine part
  of planning a rough-in, choosing a larger box whenever a location needs more
  conductors than a standard box's volume allows.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrician keeps adding wires to a box that's already at its code limit,
  without recalculating box fill as the design changes. An inspector catches
  the overfilled box during rough-in inspection, requiring the electrician to
  install a larger box and redo the connections.
