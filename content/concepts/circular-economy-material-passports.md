id: concept-circular-economy-material-passports
title: Circular Economy & Material Passports
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A sustainability approach that designs a building's materials and assemblies
  for future     disassembly and reuse rather than eventual demolition and
  landfill disposal, often supported     by a material passport, a digital
  record documenting a building's materials, their     composition, and how
  they're assembled, so a future deconstruction crew knows exactly what's
  recoverable and how to get it out intact.
whyItMatters: >
  [[concept-building-deconstruction|Building deconstruction]] at the end of a
  building's     life only works as well as the documentation available about
  what's actually in the     building and how it was put together; a material
  passport created during design and     construction is what makes an
  eventual deconstruction effort decades later actually     efficient instead
  of a slow process of discovery.
example: >
  A building's design team specifies bolted rather than welded structural
  connections and     maintains a digital material passport logging every
  major material's composition and     installation method, so that decades
  later, a deconstruction crew can disassemble and     recover the structural
  steel instead of demolishing and scrapping it.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]]
  develops a     project's material passport during design, working with the
  design team to favor     mechanically fastened assemblies that support
  future disassembly over adhesives and welds     that don't.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project claims circular economy credentials based on using some
  recycled-content     materials, when circular economy design also requires
  the assembly itself to be designed for     future disassembly; recycled
  input materials assembled in a way that can't be taken apart     again don't
  close the loop the concept is named for.
