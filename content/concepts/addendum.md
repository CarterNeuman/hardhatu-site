---
id: concept-addendum
title: Addendum
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  An official, written change to the bid documents, such as plans, specs, or bidding
  instructions, issued by the architect or owner before bids are due, correcting an
  error or adding information every bidder needs.
whyItMatters: >
  If one contractor finds out about a correction and others don't, they're no longer
  bidding on the same project, which isn't fair to anyone and can void the whole bid
  process. An addendum is what keeps every bidder working from the same, current
  information.
example: >
  A week before bids are due, the architect discovers a dimension error on a structural
  drawing. Rather than telling contractors informally, they issue a numbered addendum to
  every bidder correcting the drawing, and every bidder has to acknowledge receiving it
  in their bid.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] has to catch every addendum and fold its changes into
  the bid before it's submitted; missing one is one of the more common, avoidable bid
  mistakes. A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] often tracks addenda after
  award, since they become part of the official contract documents.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An estimator finishes a bid and submits it the morning it's due without checking for a
  late addendum that reduced the scope of work. The bid comes in artificially high
  because it still includes work the addendum removed, and the contractor loses the
  project to a competitor who caught the change.
