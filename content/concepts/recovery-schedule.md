---
id: concept-recovery-schedule
title: Recovery Schedule
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A revised project schedule developed specifically to make up lost time
  after a project has fallen behind, identifying which activities can be
  compressed, resequenced, or run with added resources to bring the project
  back toward its original completion date.
whyItMatters: >
  Simply updating an existing schedule to reflect that it's behind doesn't
  actually recover any time, a real recovery schedule requires deliberately
  identifying specific, resourced actions that will close the gap, not just
  documenting that one exists. Distinct from
  [[concept-acceleration|acceleration]], the broader decision and cost of
  deliberately compressing a schedule, a recovery schedule is the specific
  replanned schedule document itself, the resequenced and re-resourced plan
  that may or may not involve acceleration's added cost.
example: >
  A project falls three weeks behind after a permitting delay, and the
  scheduler builds a recovery schedule resequencing several finish activities
  to run in parallel instead of sequentially, closing most of the gap without
  extending the overall duration.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] builds the recovery schedule, identifying
  which specific activities can actually absorb compression or resequencing
  without just shifting the problem elsewhere.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project falls behind schedule, and the team keeps updating the existing
  schedule optimistically without ever building a real recovery schedule that
  identifies specific, resourced actions to actually make up the lost time.
  The gap between the optimistic update and reality keeps widening.
