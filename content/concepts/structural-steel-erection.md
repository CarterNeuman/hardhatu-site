id: concept-structural-steel-erection
title: Structural Steel Erection
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  The sequenced process of lifting, positioning, and connecting a building's
  fabricated steel members in the field, following an engineered erection plan
  that keeps the partially assembled frame stable and braced at every stage,
  distinct from the fabrication of the steel itself.
whyItMatters: >
  OSHA's steel erection standard sets specific requirements, such as a minimum
  of two bolts per connection before a member is considered self-supporting,
  because an erection sequence that gets ahead of itself leaves an unstable
  frame that can collapse under wind or its own weight before enough
  connections are made.
example: >
  An erection crew sets and connects each level's steel following the
  engineer's erection plan, keeping temporary guy cables and braces on each
  piece until enough permanent bolted or welded connections are complete to
  make that section self-supporting.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] makes the field connections and bolts up
  each piece. A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] or a
  specialty erection engineer designs the sequence and temporary bracing the
  crew follows.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An erection crew, pushing to keep the crane moving, fully bolts up several
  bays ahead of the engineer's approved sequence without waiting for the survey
  check the sequence calls for at that stage. By the time the surveyor catches
  the cumulative drift, three already-completed bays are out of tolerance, and
  the crew has to loosen and reset connections across all three rather than
  just the one where the drift started.
