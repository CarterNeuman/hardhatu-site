id: concept-fema-ia-vs-insurance-claim
title: FEMA Individual Assistance vs. Insurance Claim
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The distinction between FEMA's Individual Assistance program, federal disaster
  aid available to uninsured or underinsured disaster survivors as a
  supplement rather than a replacement for insurance, and a private insurance
  claim, which FEMA assistance generally requires applicants to pursue first
  if they have coverage.
whyItMatters: >
  Contractors working disaster-recovery jobs need to understand that FEMA aid is
  not insurance and comes with its own, often lower, funding caps and
  documentation requirements, which changes what repair scope a disaster
  survivor can actually afford.
example: >
  A flood victim without flood insurance applies for FEMA Individual Assistance,
  which covers a portion of basic habitability repairs up to a program cap,
  well short of full replacement cost, while a neighbor with NFIP flood
  coverage works through a traditional insurance claim process instead.
careerAngle: >
  Restoration contractors working disaster areas should ask early whether a
  homeowner is funding repairs through FEMA assistance or an insurance claim,
  since the available funding and required documentation differ substantially.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes expect FEMA Individual Assistance to function like insurance
  and fully fund a rebuild, when the program is designed to supplement meeting
  basic needs and typically caps assistance well below full repair or
  replacement cost.
