title: Scope Gap / Scope Overlap
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A coordination problem where the written scope of work for two different
  subcontractors either leaves a piece of work assigned to neither of them, a
  gap, or assigns the same piece of work to both of them, an overlap, usually
  only discovered once work is actually ready to begin in the field.
whyItMatters: >
  A scope gap means nobody shows up to do a piece of necessary work until
  someone notices it's missing, often at the worst possible moment in the
  schedule, while a scope overlap wastes money paying for the same work twice
  or triggers a dispute between two subs over who's actually responsible for
  doing it.
example: >
  A general contractor discovers, right as rough-in is about to start, that
  neither the electrical nor the low-voltage subcontractor's scope of work
  actually includes running conduit for a specific security system, a scope
  gap that has to be resolved with a change order to one of the subs before
  work can proceed on schedule.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] reviews every
  subcontract's scope of work against the others before award specifically
  looking for gaps and overlaps, and the [[career-project- manager|Project
  Manager]] resolves a gap discovered in the field quickly enough to avoid it
  becoming a schedule delay.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Subcontracts are written and awarded without cross-checking each scope of work
  against the others, and a gap between two subs' scopes isn't discovered
  until a crew is standing on site ready to work and nobody is actually
  contracted to do the piece in front of them.
