id: concept-tenant-improvement-allowance
title: Tenant Improvement Allowance
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A dollar amount, usually set per square foot, that a landlord agrees to
  contribute toward building out a tenant's leased space, negotiated as part of
  the lease itself before any construction starts. The tenant (or their
  contractor) typically still manages the actual construction, drawing against
  this allowance as costs are incurred.
whyItMatters: >
  The allowance sets a hard ceiling before a single wall gets built. A buildout
  that runs even modestly over the negotiated allowance means the tenant pays
  the difference out of pocket, so tracking actual costs against the allowance
  in real time, not just at the end, is what keeps a project from becoming an
  unpleasant surprise for the tenant.
example: >
  A lease sets a $50 per square foot tenant improvement allowance for a 5,000
  square foot office, giving the tenant a $250,000 budget. Change orders during
  construction push the actual buildout cost to $280,000, and the $30,000
  difference comes directly out of the tenant's own pocket since it exceeds
  what the landlord agreed to fund.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-tenant-improvement-construction-manager|Tenant Improvement
  Construction Manager]] tracks the allowance budget against actual costs
  throughout the project and flags early whenever a change order risks pushing
  the job over what the landlord agreed to fund, since catching it early gives
  the tenant time to adjust scope instead of absorbing the full overage at the
  end.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A tenant approves several seemingly small change orders during construction
  without checking a running total against the allowance. By substantial
  completion, the cumulative overage is tens of thousands of dollars more than
  expected, and the tenant is caught off guard by a bill they assumed the
  landlord's allowance had already covered.
