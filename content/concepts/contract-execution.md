---
id: concept-contract-execution
title: Contract Execution
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The point at which every party to a contract has signed it, making it
  legally binding, which is a different event from the work under that
  contract actually starting.
whyItMatters: >
  A contract can be fully executed, meaning signed and legally binding, and
  still sit for weeks before the schedule clock that governs the work actually
  starts, which is usually [[concept-notice-to-proceed|Notice to Proceed]]
  instead. Confusing execution with the start of work is a common source of
  schedule disputes.
example: >
  A subcontract gets fully executed, signed by both the general contractor and
  the subcontractor, on March 1st, but the subcontractor's actual notice to
  proceed doesn't arrive until April 15th, once the site is ready for that
  scope of work. The subcontractor's schedule obligations run from the notice
  to proceed date, not the execution date.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] tracks exactly
  when every contract on a project reaches execution, since that date starts
  the clock on certain contractual obligations even when physical work doesn't
  begin for weeks afterward.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A subcontractor assumes its schedule obligations start the day its contract
  is executed, rather than the later date it actually receives notice to
  proceed. It mobilizes a crew too early, sits idle waiting for the site to be
  ready, and disputes who should absorb that cost.
