id: concept-public-work-vs-private-work
title: Public Work vs. Private Work
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The fundamental distinction between government-funded construction, subject to
  [[concept-prevailing-wage|prevailing wage]],
  [[concept-certified-payroll|certified payroll]], bonding, and formal public bidding
  requirements; and privately funded construction, governed primarily by whatever
  terms the private parties negotiate into their own contract.
whyItMatters: >
  Because public work carries a distinct set of legal requirements that simply don't
  apply on private work, a contractor moving between the two has to operate under two
  different rulebooks: a mechanic's lien, for example, generally isn't available as a
  remedy against public property, so unpaid subcontractors on public work rely on a
  payment bond claim instead of the lien rights they'd have on a private project.
example: >
  A general contractor completes a private office building using a standard negotiated
  contract with no prevailing wage or public bidding requirements, then moves directly
  into a public library project that requires competitive public bidding, prevailing
  wage compliance, and certified payroll reporting the private project never involved.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] has to build fundamentally different cost
  assumptions into a public bid versus a private one: prevailing wage rates, bonding
  costs, and compliance overhead all add cost that isn't present on comparable private
  work. A [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] has to know which
  remedies (lien rights vs. bond claims) are even available to protect payment,
  depending on which category the project falls into.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A subcontractor on an unpaid public project attempts to file a mechanic's lien the
  same way they would on a private project, only to discover liens generally can't
  attach to government-owned property. Without that remedy available, the
  subcontractor has to pursue a claim against the project's payment bond instead, a
  different process with its own notice deadlines the subcontractor wasn't prepared for
  because they assumed the same rules applied as on private work.
