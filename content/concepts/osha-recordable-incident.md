id: concept-osha-recordable-incident
title: OSHA Recordable Incident
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A work-related injury or illness that meets OSHA's specific recordability criteria,
  medical treatment beyond basic first aid, days away from work, job restriction, or loss
  of consciousness among the triggers, and must be logged on the employer's OSHA 300
  injury and illness log.
whyItMatters: >
  Not every jobsite injury is recordable, and not every recordable incident feels serious
  in the moment: the criteria are specific and sometimes counterintuitive (a single
  prescription-strength dose of medication can trigger recordability where an
  over-the-counter one wouldn't). Getting the classification right matters because the
  count directly feeds a company's [[concept-total-recordable-incident-rate|TRIR]].
example: >
  A worker receives stitches for a cut that required more than basic first aid. That
  crosses OSHA's recordability threshold, and the incident goes on the OSHA 300 log even
  though the worker returns to full duty the same day.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] makes the recordability determination for
  every jobsite injury and maintains the OSHA 300 log accurately, since a
  misclassification in either direction creates real compliance exposure. A
  [[career-hr-manager-construction|Human Resources Manager]] coordinates the
  documentation with workers' compensation claims that often accompany a recordable
  incident.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A supervisor classifies an injury as "just first aid" to keep it off the log, without
  checking whether the actual treatment given crosses OSHA's specific recordability
  criteria. An audit later finds the injury should have been recorded, turning a
  classification mistake into a recordkeeping violation on top of the original incident.
