id: concept-limitation-of-liability-clause
title: Limitation of Liability Clause
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A contract provision capping the total amount one party can be required to pay
  the other for damages arising from the contract, often set at a multiple of
  the contract's fee or a specific dollar figure, regardless of how large the
  actual loss turns out to be.
whyItMatters: >
  It's especially common in design and engineering contracts, where a design
  professional's fee may be a small fraction of the total project cost, but a
  design error could theoretically expose them to damages many times larger
  than that fee without a cap in place.
example: >
  An engineering firm's contract for a water treatment plant includes a
  limitation of liability clause capping its total liability at the amount of
  its design fee. When a design error later causes costly rework, the owner's
  recovery against the engineer is capped at that amount, regardless of the
  actual rework cost.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] negotiates
  limitation of liability language on behalf of design professionals and
  contractors alike, and the [[career-risk-manager-construction|Risk Manager]]
  evaluates whether a proposed cap leaves the company adequately protected
  against realistic exposure on a given project.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner accepts a design contract's limitation of liability clause without
  really grasping how low the cap is relative to the project's total value,
  and later discovers that even a clear, costly design error can only be
  recovered up to that capped amount.
