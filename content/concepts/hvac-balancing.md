---
id: concept-hvac-balancing
title: HVAC Testing, Adjusting & Balancing (TAB)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "23, HVAC"
definition: >
  The process of measuring a completed HVAC system's actual airflow and water
  flow at every outlet and adjusting dampers and valves until it matches the
  values the mechanical design called for, performed by an independent
  balancing contractor after installation but before the system is considered
  complete.
whyItMatters: >
  An HVAC system that's fully installed and running isn't necessarily
  delivering the airflow the design actually calls for at every outlet, TAB is
  what verifies and corrects that, and skipping it means occupants can end up
  with rooms that are too hot, too cold, or under-ventilated despite the
  system apparently working.
example: >
  An independent balancing contractor measures airflow at every supply and
  return diffuser throughout a building, adjusting dampers until each one
  matches the specific value shown on the mechanical design's airflow
  schedule.
careerAngle: >
  An independent TAB technician performs the balancing, working separately
  from the HVAC installer specifically to provide an unbiased verification. A
  [[career-commissioning-agent|Commissioning Agent]] reviews the balancing
  report as part of overall building commissioning.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project skips independent TAB on the chilled water side to save schedule,
  relying on the installer's own valve settings. Zones at the far end of the
  hydronic loop never actually get the design flow through their coils, and
  persistent comfort complaints on the building's top floor trace back months
  later to balancing valves that were never actually set to the design
  report's values.
