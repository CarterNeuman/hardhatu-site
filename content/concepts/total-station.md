---
id: concept-total-station
title: Total Station
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A surveying instrument that combines an electronic theodolite and a distance
  meter to measure precise horizontal and vertical angles along with exact
  distances from a known point, used to establish survey control, including
  ground control points for drone mapping.
whyItMatters: >
  Before a site can be mapped, built on, or compared against its design, there
  has to be a precise, trustworthy set of known coordinates to measure
  everything else against. A total station is the standard tool that
  establishes that foundational survey-grade accuracy, which GPS-based methods
  alone generally can't match for the tightest tolerances.
example: >
  A surveyor sets up a total station over a known benchmark and shoots precise
  angles and distances to establish the exact coordinates of several ground
  control points across a site, work a drone mapping project later relies on
  for accuracy.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-surveyor|Surveyor]] operates a total station for the
  highest-precision survey work on a project, a level of accuracy drone or GPS
  methods alone typically can't achieve on their own.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project relies solely on GPS-based positioning for a layout task that
  actually requires survey-grade precision, skipping a total station
  measurement to save time. The resulting layout is off by a margin that GPS
  alone can't resolve, a discrepancy that only becomes obvious once the actual
  construction doesn't line up correctly.
