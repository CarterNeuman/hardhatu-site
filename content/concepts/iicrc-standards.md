id: concept-iicrc-standards
title: IICRC Standards (Water Damage Restoration)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  Technical standards published by the Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and
  Restoration Certification (IICRC), most notably the S500 standard for water
  damage restoration, that define accepted industry procedure for categorizing
  water contamination, drying structures, and monitoring progress, referenced by
  insurers, restoration contractors, and courts as the benchmark for whether
  restoration work was actually performed correctly.
whyItMatters: >
  When a dispute arises over whether a restoration job was done right, such as whether a
  structure was actually dry before it was closed back up or whether remediation
  actually removed contamination, IICRC standards are the reference point everyone
  from adjusters to expert witnesses uses to evaluate the work, not an informal
  judgment call.
example: >
  A restoration technician follows IICRC S500 protocol on a water loss, classifying
  the water's contamination category, documenting daily moisture readings throughout
  the drying process, and confirming the structure meets the standard's drying goals
  before reconstruction begins.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]] holds IICRC
  certification and follows its protocols on every water loss as standard practice,
  not just for higher-value claims. An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] references IICRC standards to evaluate whether a contractor's proposed
  remediation scope is actually necessary or goes beyond what the standard requires.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A restoration contractor dries a water-damaged structure but doesn't monitor and
  document daily drying progress the way IICRC S500 requires, relying instead on a
  final visual check. When a dispute later arises over whether the structure was
  actually fully dry before reconstruction began, there's no documentation to prove
  it either way.
