id: concept-s-curve-cost-schedule
title: S-Curve (Cost/Schedule Curve)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A graph plotting a project's planned or actual cumulative cost or progress
  against time, which typically forms an S shape: slow at the start as work
  ramps up, steep through the bulk of construction, then slow again as the
  project winds down toward completion.
whyItMatters: >
  Comparing the actual progress curve against the planned baseline S-curve at a
  glance shows whether a project is tracking ahead, behind, or on pace without
  requiring anyone to dig into individual activity data, which makes it one of
  the most common ways project status gets communicated to an owner or
  executive who doesn't need the full schedule detail.
example: >
  A monthly owner report includes an S-curve chart showing the planned
  cumulative percent complete against the actual cumulative percent complete
  to date. The two lines visibly diverge starting in month four, giving the
  owner an immediate, intuitive sense that the project has fallen behind
  without having to read a detailed schedule narrative.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-cost-engineer|Cost Engineer]] builds and updates the S-curve each
  reporting period, and the [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] uses it
  as the quick visual anchor for explaining overall project status in owner
  meetings.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project relies entirely on the S-curve's visual trend to judge project
  health without digging into which specific activities are actually driving
  the divergence, missing that one badly delayed trade can be masked by other
  trades running ahead of schedule in the same aggregate curve.
