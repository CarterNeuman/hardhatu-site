id: concept-carbon-offset-credits
title: Carbon Offset Credits
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A certificate representing one metric ton of carbon dioxide emissions
  reduced, avoided, or     sequestered elsewhere, such as through a
  reforestation or renewable energy project, that a     building owner can
  purchase to counterbalance emissions they can't eliminate directly,
  distinct from RECs, which represent renewable electricity generation
  specifically rather     than any emissions-reduction project.
whyItMatters: >
  A building performance standard's carbon cap can sometimes be satisfied, in
  part, by     purchasing qualifying carbon offset credits rather than only
  through direct building     retrofits, giving an owner facing a tight
  compliance timeline an additional, though often     more expensive,
  compliance pathway.
example: >
  An owner facing a Local Law 97 emissions overage in a compliance period
  purchases carbon     offset credits equal to the building's excess tons as
  an interim compliance measure while a     multi-year HVAC electrification
  retrofit is still being designed and permitted.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]]
  evaluates     whether a specific offset program is a legitimate, verified
  reduction before recommending it     as a compliance strategy, since not
  every offset marketed for sale represents a real,     additional emissions
  reduction.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner buys carbon offset credits assuming any certificate labeled carbon
  offset     satisfies a building performance standard's compliance pathway,
  when many jurisdictions only     accept offsets from specific, pre-approved
  registries and reject generic market offsets for     regulatory compliance
  purposes.
