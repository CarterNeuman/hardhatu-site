---
id: concept-agreed-value-coverage
title: Agreed Value Coverage
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A policy endorsement in which the insurer and policyholder agree in advance on
  a property's insured value, waiving the coinsurance penalty that would
  otherwise apply if the property turns out to be underinsured at the time of
  a loss.
whyItMatters: >
  Commercial property owners who get a professional appraisal and buy agreed
  value coverage protect themselves from a coinsurance penalty surprise that
  could otherwise cut a claim payout substantially.
example: >
  A warehouse owner gets the building appraised at $2 million and purchases an
  agreed value endorsement at that figure, so a $500,000 fire loss is paid in
  full even though the original policy limit assumptions would otherwise have
  triggered a coinsurance cut.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors advising commercial clients on coverage adequacy should flag when
  a recent renovation has likely increased the property's value past what the
  agreed value endorsement reflects.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Agreed value coverage is sometimes assumed to be permanent once set, but it
  typically needs periodic renewal or re-appraisal, and a lapsed agreed value
  endorsement reverts to standard coinsurance exposure.
