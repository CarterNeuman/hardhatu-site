---
id: concept-additional-living-expenses
title: Additional Living Expenses (ALE)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  Coverage reimbursing a homeowner for the extra cost of temporary housing, food, and
  related expenses above their normal cost of living while their home is
  uninhabitable after a covered loss. It's owner-occupant coverage specifically, not
  the same as rental income coverage for a landlord.
whyItMatters: >
  ALE carries both a dollar limit and a time limit, commonly 12 to 24 months, and a
  family displaced by a slow
  [[career-restoration-project-manager|restoration project]] can run past that time
  limit before repairs are actually finished.
example: >
  A family displaced for four months after a kitchen fire has their hotel bill and
  restaurant costs, above what they'd normally spend on groceries, reimbursed through
  ALE, up to the policy's stated limit.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] tracks ALE spending against both
  the policy's dollar limit and its time limit throughout a claim, since either one
  running out ends the reimbursement regardless of whether repairs are finished.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A restoration project stalls on material delays and a family's temporary housing
  runs past their policy's ALE time limit, commonly capped at 12 or 24 months
  regardless of the dollar amount still remaining, leaving the family to cover the
  gap themselves because the repair timeline wasn't managed against that ticking
  clock.
