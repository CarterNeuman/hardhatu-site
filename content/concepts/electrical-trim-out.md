id: concept-electrical-trim-out
title: Trim-Out (Electrical)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  The later phase of electrical work, happening after drywall is finished and
  painted, when switches, outlets, light fixtures, and other devices are
  finally installed onto the wiring that was roughed in earlier, closing out
  the electrical scope.
whyItMatters: >
  Rough-in and trim-out are separated by a long gap in the schedule, often
  weeks, while drywall, texture, and paint happen in between, which means the
  wiring sits hidden and untested inside finished walls for a significant
  stretch of the project before anyone ever sees whether it actually works
  correctly.
example: >
  Once painting is complete, an electrician returns to a house to trim out
  every rough-in location, installing switches, outlets, and light fixtures
  onto the wiring that was left capped off behind the walls weeks earlier
  during rough-in.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] returns for trim-out as a distinct
  visit from rough-in, often a different stage of the same job scheduled much
  later once finishes are complete.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A trim-out electrician discovers during final device installation that a
  rough-in connection behind the wall was never actually completed correctly.
  Since the wall has long since been finished and painted, tracing and fixing
  the problem now requires opening up a wall that was already considered done.
