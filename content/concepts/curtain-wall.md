id: concept-curtain-wall
title: Curtain Wall
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  A non-structural exterior wall system, typically a grid of aluminum framing
  infilled with glass, metal panels, or stone, that hangs off a building's
  structural frame rather than supporting any of the building's structural load
  itself, common on mid-rise and high-rise commercial buildings.
whyItMatters: >
  Because a curtain wall spans multiple floors and has to accommodate the building's
  structural movement, wind sway, seismic movement, thermal expansion, without
  cracking or leaking, its connections back to the structural frame are engineered
  specifically to allow that movement, and a curtain wall installed as if it were
  rigidly fixed can crack or fail as the building moves normally around it.
example: >
  A 20-story office tower's curtain wall is engineered with connections back to the
  structural frame that allow for the building's expected wind sway and thermal
  movement, so the glass and framing don't crack as the structure flexes under normal
  conditions.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] coordinates curtain wall shop
  drawings and mockup approval well in advance of installation, since a curtain wall
  system is highly engineered and manufactured to order. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] often requires a
  full-scale mockup panel tested for water and air infiltration before the full
  curtain wall order is released for fabrication.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A curtain wall subcontractor's shop drawings show a connection detail that doesn't
  actually accommodate the building's calculated movement, and the discrepancy isn't
  caught before fabrication. After installation, thermal cycling over the following
  seasons cracks sealant joints that a properly detailed connection would have
  tolerated, requiring an expensive re-seal of large sections of the building's
  exterior.
