title: Apprenticeship Ratio Requirement
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A rule, common on public and prevailing-wage projects, limiting how many
  apprentices a contractor can use on a job relative to the number of
  fully-credentialed journey-level workers, to keep apprentices from being
  substituted for skilled labor at a lower pay rate. The ratio is one of
  several compliance obligations tied to running a registered
  [[concept-apprenticeship-program|apprenticeship program]].
whyItMatters: >
  The specific ratio is typically set by the registered apprenticeship program's
  own standards, not an arbitrary number the contractor picks, and exceeding
  the allowed ratio on a public job can trigger a prevailing-wage violation
  even if every apprentice is legitimately registered.
example: >
  An electrical subcontractor's registered apprenticeship program allows one
  apprentice for every two journey-level electricians on a job. When the sub
  staffs a prevailing-wage project with three apprentices and only two
  journey-level electricians, the crew exceeds the allowed ratio, and the
  excess apprentices have to be paid the full journey-level prevailing wage
  rate instead of the lower apprentice rate.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-certified-payroll-specialist|Certified Payroll Specialist]]
  checks apprentice-to-journeyman ratios on certified payroll before it's
  submitted, and the [[career-hr-manager-construction|HR Manager]] tracks each
  worker's registered apprenticeship status and level to keep crew staffing
  within the allowed ratio.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor staffs a public job with more apprentices than its registered
  program's ratio allows, assuming the lower apprentice pay rate still
  applies, and ends up owing journey-level back wages for the apprentices
  staffed beyond the permitted ratio.
