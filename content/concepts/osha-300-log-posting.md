id: concept-osha-300-log-posting
title: OSHA 300 Log / 300A Summary Posting
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The recordkeeping requirement that covered employers maintain a running OSHA
  300 Log of recordable injuries and illnesses throughout the year, post the
  300A annual summary in a visible location from February 1 through April 30,
  and electronically submit certain data to OSHA's Injury Tracking Application
  by March 2 of each year if the establishment meets the size and industry
  thresholds for that requirement.
whyItMatters: >
  The 300 Log and 300A posting are a separate obligation from simply knowing
  which incidents were recordable, since even a company that classifies every
  incident correctly can still be cited for failing to post the required
  summary during the required window or missing the electronic submission
  deadline.
example: >
  A company finishes a clean year with its OSHA 300 Log accurately maintained,
  but still gets cited after an inspector notices the required 300A summary
  was never actually posted in a visible break-room location during the
  required February-through-April window.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers should treat the February 1 posting deadline and the March 2
  electronic submission deadline, where applicable, as fixed annual dates on
  the compliance calendar, separate from the ongoing task of logging incidents
  correctly all year.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Companies sometimes think keeping an accurate 300 Log all year is the whole
  requirement, when the posting of the 300A summary during a specific window,
  and electronic submission for covered establishments, are each their own
  separate, deadline-driven obligations.
