id: concept-osha
title: OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The federal agency, part of the U.S. Department of Labor, that writes and enforces
  workplace safety standards, its construction-specific regulations (29 CFR 1926) are
  the legal floor underneath nearly every safety practice on a U.S. jobsite, from fall
  protection to trench shoring to hazard communication.
whyItMatters: >
  Almost every safety term in construction traces back to a specific OSHA standard, and
  that standard is a legal minimum, not a suggestion; a company can be cited and fined
  for falling short of it regardless of how good its safety record otherwise looks.
  Understanding that distinction is what separates "best practice" language from an
  actual compliance obligation.
example: >
  An OSHA compliance officer arrives unannounced after a worker complaint about missing
  fall protection. The visit turns into either a quick walk-through or a serious citation
  depending entirely on what the site can actually show: current [[concept-job-hazard-analysis|job hazard analyses]],
  training records, and documented [[concept-toolbox-talk|toolbox talks]] on file.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] builds the entire jobsite safety program
  around meeting OSHA's specific requirements and surviving exactly this kind of
  inspection. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] is often OSHA 30-certified and
  is the one actually enforcing those requirements in the field every day, since most
  violations get caught, or missed, at the crew level, not in an office.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A company treats its one-time OSHA 10 training as a permanent box checked rather than
  an ongoing program. A minor injury occurs, an OSHA inspector arrives to investigate,
  and finds outdated hazard analyses and no toolbox talk records to show, turning what
  might have been a manageable incident into a serious citation with real fines attached.
