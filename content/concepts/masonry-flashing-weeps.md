id: concept-masonry-flashing-weeps
title: Masonry Flashing & Weeps
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "04, Masonry"
definition: >
  Metal or membrane flashing embedded within a masonry wall, at the base, above
  openings, and at shelf angles, that catches water getting past the outer
  wythe and directs it back out through small weep holes spaced along the
  wall's base, the drainage system that lets a masonry wall manage water
  intrusion rather than trying to stop it entirely.
whyItMatters: >
  Masonry isn't actually waterproof, water is expected to get behind the outer
  wythe, so the flashing and weeps are what actually keep that water from
  accumulating and soaking into the wall assembly, not the brick itself.
example: >
  A mason installs through-wall flashing at the base of a CMU backup wall,
  lapped continuously and turned up behind the brick veneer, with weep holes
  spaced every 24 inches along the flashing to let collected water drain back
  out.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mason|Mason]] installs the flashing and coordinates weep hole
  spacing during construction. A [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]]
  verifies flashing laps and weep spacing before the wall is closed in.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A mason skips weep holes at a shelf angle flashing to keep the coursing
  looking clean. Water collects on the flashing with nowhere to drain, and it
  eventually backs up and soaks into the wall assembly, showing up as
  efflorescence and staining on the interior finish months later.
