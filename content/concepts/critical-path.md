id: concept-critical-path
title: Critical Path
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The specific sequence of tasks in a project schedule that determines the shortest
  possible time the whole project can take: a delay to any task on this path delays the
  entire project's finish date, while delays to other tasks might not.
whyItMatters: >
  Not every delay is equal. Understanding which tasks are actually on the critical path
  is what tells a project team where to focus their attention when something starts
  slipping, instead of treating every schedule hiccup as equally urgent.
example: >
  A scheduler's software shows that framing, drywall, and final inspection form the
  critical path on a project, while landscaping, which can happen anytime before
  closeout, has weeks of flexibility. A three-day rain delay to framing pushes the
  whole project back three days; the same delay to landscaping might not move the
  finish date at all.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] builds and maintains the schedule logic that
  identifies exactly which tasks are critical, updating it as the project actually
  progresses. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] uses that critical path
  analysis to decide where to spend money on overtime or extra crews when the project
  needs to catch up.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project manager throws extra crews and weekend overtime at a task that's running
  behind, without checking whether it's actually on the critical path. The task catches
  up, but the project's finish date doesn't move at all, because a different task the
  team never looked at was the one actually driving the completion date the whole time;
  the extra money was spent on the wrong problem.
