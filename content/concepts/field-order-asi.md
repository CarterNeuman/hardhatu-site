---
id: concept-field-order-asi
title: Field Order / Architect's Supplemental Instruction (ASI)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A formal written direction from the design team clarifying or slightly
  adjusting the work, issued when the change is minor enough that it doesn't
  affect contract price or time, distinct from a
  [[concept-construction-change-directive|construction change directive]],
  which is used specifically when cost or schedule impact is expected.
whyItMatters: >
  Treating every minor clarification as if it needs the heavier process of a
  full change order would bog down a project in paperwork; an ASI or field
  order exists specifically so small adjustments can be directed quickly,
  though a contractor who believes a supposedly minor instruction actually
  does affect cost or time still has to raise that promptly rather than
  silently absorb it.
example: >
  The architect issues an ASI slightly repositioning a wall-mounted fixture a
  few inches to clear an unforeseen duct run, a minor field adjustment with no
  cost or schedule impact. The contractor implements it the same day, without
  it needing to go through a full change order process.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] logs and tracks every ASI
  issued on a project, and the [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts
  Administrator]] watches for an ASI that, despite being labeled minor, may
  actually carry a cost or time impact worth raising before simply complying
  with it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor receives an ASI that actually does add real cost or time to the
  work, but implements it without raising the issue, assuming the ASI label
  itself means no compensation is available, and loses the chance to recover
  costs it may have been entitled to if it had flagged the impact before
  proceeding.
