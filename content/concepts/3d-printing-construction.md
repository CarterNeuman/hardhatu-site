id: concept-3d-printing-construction
title: 3D Printing / Additive Manufacturing in Construction
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Building structural or architectural elements, most often concrete walls, by
  depositing     material layer by layer from a robotic printer following a
  digital model, rather than     forming and pouring concrete in traditional
  formwork.
whyItMatters: >
  3D printing eliminates the labor and material cost of building and stripping
  formwork     entirely, which matters most on complex curved geometry that
  would otherwise require     expensive custom formwork, though the technology
  still needs a structural engineer to verify     the printed wall actually
  performs the way a conventionally formed one would.
example: >
  A contractor 3D-prints a home's exterior walls directly from the
  architectural model     overnight using a gantry-mounted concrete printer,
  then has a crew install conventional roof     framing, windows, and MEP
  rough-in into the printed shell the next day.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] has to verify a
  3D-printed concrete     mix's layer-bonding strength meets the same
  structural requirements as conventionally formed     and vibrated concrete,
  since a weak bond between printed layers isn't something that can be
  assumed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project assumes 3D-printed walls are structurally equivalent to
  conventionally formed     concrete by default, when the layer-by-layer
  printing process can leave weaker bond lines     between passes that need
  their own engineering verification, not just a visual inspection.
