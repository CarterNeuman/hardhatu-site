id: concept-water-heater
title: Water Heater
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "22, Plumbing"
definition: >
  The equipment that heats a building's domestic hot water supply, tank-style or
  tankless, gas or electric, sized specifically for the building's expected hot
  water demand and installed with the venting, combustion air, and code-required
  safety devices (temperature-pressure relief valves) appropriate to its fuel type.
whyItMatters: >
  An undersized water heater is a comfort and functionality problem, not a safety
  one, but an improperly vented gas water heater is a genuine life-safety hazard:
  carbon monoxide from incomplete combustion has nowhere to safely go, which is why
  gas water heater venting is inspected closely rather than treated as routine.
example: >
  A commercial building's water heater is sized based on a calculated peak hot water
  demand for its occupancy type, with a gas-fired unit vented directly to the
  exterior according to the manufacturer's and code's specific venting requirements.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] sizes, installs, and vents water heaters to code, a
  task where the venting details for gas units carry real safety consequences if done
  incorrectly. A [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] may be involved on
  combination systems where water heating and space heating share equipment.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A plumber installs a gas water heater's vent pipe with insufficient slope and
  fewer support points than the manufacturer specifies, assuming the general shape of
  the venting run matters more than the exact detail. Over time, condensation
  collects in the improperly sloped vent, eventually causing a blockage that allows
  combustion gases to spill back into the building instead of venting outside, a
  hazard traced directly back to the installation detail.
