id: concept-stone-veneer
title: Stone Veneer
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "04, Masonry"
definition: >
  A thin layer of natural or manufactured, concrete-based, stone applied to a
  wall's exterior purely as a finish, attached with mechanical anchors, mortar,
  or an adhesive system depending on the stone type and substrate, distinct
  from full-thickness structural stone masonry.
whyItMatters: >
  Manufactured stone veneer is lightweight and far less expensive than natural
  stone, but it depends entirely on the water-resistive barrier and drainage
  plane behind it, since the veneer itself isn't waterproof, a detail that's
  easy to skip when an installer treats it as purely decorative.
example: >
  A builder installs manufactured stone veneer over a wood-framed wall, with a
  water-resistive barrier, metal lath, and a scratch coat installed first, so
  any water that gets behind the stone drains down and out rather than reaching
  the wood sheathing.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mason|Mason]] installs the stone veneer and its scratch coat. A
  [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] typically installs the water-resistive barrier
  the veneer depends on before the mason starts.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An installer applies manufactured stone veneer directly over sheathing
  without the required water-resistive barrier and weep screed, treating it as
  a simple decorative finish. Water gets behind the stone during the first
  heavy rain season and rots the wood sheathing, discovered only when a section
  of the veneer wall starts sagging.
