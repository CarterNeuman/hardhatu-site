---
id: concept-progress-photo-documentation
title: Progress Photo Documentation
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Systematic, dated photographs of the jobsite taken at regular intervals
  throughout construction, especially of work that will later be covered up, such
  as structural connections, MEP rough-in behind drywall, or waterproofing before
  backfill, building a visual record for closeout, disputes, and future
  maintenance.
whyItMatters: >
  Once drywall closes over a rough-in, the photos are the only record left of
  what's actually behind the wall, which makes them genuinely valuable evidence in
  a dispute, an insurance claim, or a future renovation years down the line.
example: >
  A project engineer photographs every MEP rough-in run before drywall closes it
  in, organized by date and location, and uploads the set to the project's
  document management software the same day.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] typically owns the task of
  capturing and organizing progress photos throughout construction.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew closes in a wall without photographing the plumbing rough-in behind it.
  Months later a leak requires locating a shutoff valve, and with no photo record
  of where the line actually runs, the facilities team has to open up drywall
  exploratively just to find it.
