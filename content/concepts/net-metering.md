id: concept-net-metering
title: Net Metering
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A utility billing arrangement that credits a building owner with on-site
  solar generation     for excess electricity sent back to the grid,
  offsetting their own electricity bill at a     rate set by the utility or
  state regulator, rather than requiring a separate battery system     to
  store every kilowatt-hour generated on-site for later use.
whyItMatters: >
  Net metering is what makes rooftop solar financially viable for most
  building owners     without also installing expensive battery storage, since
  excess midday generation gets     credited against the building's own
  nighttime grid draw instead of being wasted or requiring     on-site storage
  to capture.
example: >
  A warehouse's rooftop solar array generates more electricity than the
  building uses during     a sunny afternoon; the excess flows back onto the
  grid and appears as a credit on the     owner's next utility bill,
  offsetting the electricity the building draws from the grid after     dark.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-solar-installer|Solar Installer]] has to confirm the specific net
  metering     rate and rules of the local utility before sizing a system,
  since some utilities credit     excess generation at full retail rate while
  others pay a lower wholesale rate, which     significantly changes a
  system's payback calculation.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A solar installation is sized and sold assuming a 1:1 full-retail net
  metering credit,     when the actual utility's net metering policy pays a
  lower rate for excess generation, a gap     that can meaningfully extend the
  system's real payback period beyond what was originally     projected.
