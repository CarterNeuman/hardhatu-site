---
id: concept-reconstruction
title: Reconstruction
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The rebuild phase that follows emergency mitigation and remediation on a
  restoration project, repairing or replacing structural and finish components to
  return the property to its pre-loss condition, performed to the agreed
  [[concept-scope-of-loss|scope of loss]].
whyItMatters: >
  Starting reconstruction before drying or remediation is actually verified complete
  is one of the most common and costly sequencing mistakes in restoration work,
  since finishing over moisture that hasn't fully dried can trap it behind new
  materials where it causes the same problem all over again.
example: >
  A reconstruction crew waits for documented moisture readings to confirm a
  water-damaged wall cavity has fully dried before installing new drywall and
  insulation, rather than rebuilding on a timeline driven purely by the project
  schedule.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]] manages
  reconstruction crews and subcontractors through the rebuild, coordinating the
  sequence against verified drying and remediation completion. An
  [[career-insurance-restoration-estimator|Insurance Restoration Estimator]] writes
  the reconstruction estimate the crew actually builds from, based on the approved
  scope of loss.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A restoration company starts reconstruction on a water-damaged wall before
  confirming the wall cavity has fully dried, under pressure to keep the project
  moving. Moisture trapped behind the new drywall causes mold to develop within
  weeks, requiring the newly finished work to be torn out and redone.
