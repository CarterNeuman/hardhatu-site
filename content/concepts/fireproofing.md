---
id: concept-fireproofing
title: Fireproofing, Spray-Applied
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  A spray-applied cementitious or intumescent coating applied directly to
  structural steel to insulate it from heat, giving the steel enough time
  before it loses structural strength in a fire for occupants to evacuate and
  firefighters to respond, distinct from [[concept-firestopping|firestopping]],
  which seals openings rather than coating the structure itself.
whyItMatters: >
  Unprotected steel loses much of its strength at fire temperatures far lower
  than most people expect, so the fireproofing's thickness, specified in hours
  of fire rating, is calculated for the specific structural member's size and
  the rating the code requires for that part of the building, not applied at a
  single standard thickness everywhere.
example: >
  A fireproofing subcontractor sprays cementitious fireproofing onto
  structural steel beams and columns to a thickness verified with a gauge at
  multiple points on each member, confirming it meets the two-hour rating the
  code requires for that structure.
careerAngle: >
  A specialty fireproofing subcontractor applies the coating. A
  [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] verifies the applied thickness with
  a gauge before it's covered by other finishes.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A fireproofing crew sprays a steel column to what looks like an adequate
  coating by eye but skips the gauge verification the specification requires.
  The shortfall isn't found until the fire marshal's final walkthrough flags
  visibly thin coverage at one beam's flange, and the crew has to strip and
  reapply fireproofing across the whole corridor's steel before occupancy can
  be granted.
