---
id: concept-paint
title: Paint
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  The finish coating applied to interior and exterior surfaces, such as walls, trim,
  ceilings, and exposed metal, as one of the last steps in a construction sequence,
  chosen for both appearance and functional performance (durability, moisture
  resistance, fire rating in some applications).
whyItMatters: >
  Paint performance depends heavily on proper surface preparation and the substrate
  actually being ready for it: painting over a surface that's still curing,
  insufficiently primed, or not properly cleaned can cause the paint to fail (peeling,
  blistering) well before its expected service life, even if the paint product itself
  is high quality.
example: >
  A painter allows freshly finished drywall joint compound to fully dry and then
  primes it before applying finish coats, rather than painting directly over compound
  that still has residual moisture, which would trap moisture under the paint film
  and cause it to fail early.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-painter|Painter]] manages surface preparation and coating selection to
  match both the substrate and the project's performance requirements. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] may check paint film
  thickness or cure time on higher-performance coating systems, since some specialty
  coatings require specific application conditions to achieve their rated
  performance.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A painting crew, under schedule pressure, applies finish paint over drywall that
  hasn't fully dried from a recent joint compound application. Moisture trapped
  beneath the paint film causes blistering and peeling within months of occupancy, a
  failure that traces back to skipping adequate dry time rather than a defect in the
  paint itself.
