id: concept-fire-retardant-treated-wood
title: Fire-Retardant-Treated Wood
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics & Composites"
definition: >
  Lumber or plywood pressure-treated with fire-retardant chemicals to reduce
  its flame spread rating, required by code in specific applications, such as
  certain roof assemblies in combustible construction types near property
  lines, distinct from wood that's simply painted with an intumescent coating
  after the fact.
whyItMatters: >
  Some older fire-retardant treatments are known to chemically degrade the
  wood itself over time, especially in hot, poorly ventilated attic spaces,
  weakening roof sheathing enough to cause real structural concerns, which is
  why treated roof sheathing in older buildings sometimes needs inspection or
  replacement even without any fire ever having occurred.
example: >
  A builder specifies fire-retardant-treated plywood roof sheathing for a
  section of a multifamily building located close to the property line,
  meeting the code's flame spread requirement for that specific fire-separation
  condition.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] installs fire-retardant-treated wood the
  same as untreated lumber, but has to confirm each piece is actually stamped
  as treated where code requires it. A
  [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] accounts for the reduced
  allowable design values some treatments
  impose on the wood's structural capacity.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framing crew runs out of fire-retardant-treated plywood mid-installation
  and substitutes standard sheathing for the remaining few sheets in a
  code-required area, intending to swap it later. The substitution is
  forgotten, and drywall closes over the roof sheathing days afterward. It's
  the general contractor's own closeout documentation review, reconciling
  delivered stamped-panel counts against installed square footage, that turns
  up the shortfall, forcing the untreated panels to be located and replaced
  before final inspection.
