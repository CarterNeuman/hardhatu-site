id: concept-rough-in-plumbing
title: Rough-In Plumbing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "22, Plumbing"
definition: >
  The plumbing pipe and fitting installation performed inside walls, floors, and
  ceilings before they're closed up with drywall or flooring, such as supply lines,
  drain-waste-vent piping, and rough fixture connections, installed and
  pressure-tested while it's still visible and accessible for inspection.
whyItMatters: >
  Rough-in plumbing is one of the clearest examples of work that has to be inspected
  before it's covered, since a leak or code violation hidden behind finished walls
  can go undetected for years and cause serious water damage before anyone notices.
example: >
  A plumber installs supply and drain-waste-vent piping throughout a building's
  walls and floors, then pressure-tests the system and has it inspected before
  drywall and flooring close over it for good.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] installs rough-in plumbing to the plans and
  applicable plumbing code, coordinating pipe routing with framing and other trades
  to avoid conflicts. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] sequences other
  trades around plumbing rough-in inspection, since drywall and flooring crews can't
  close up walls until the plumbing inspection clears.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A plumber completes rough-in work on a floor, but the required pressure test and
  inspection get skipped in the rush to keep the schedule moving, and drywall closes
  over the piping before the inspection happens. A subsequent inspection requires
  cutting into the finished wall to verify the work, an expensive and avoidable step
  caused by inspecting out of the required sequence.
