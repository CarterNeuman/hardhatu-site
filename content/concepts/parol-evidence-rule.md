id: concept-parol-evidence-rule
title: Parol Evidence Rule
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A contract-law rule that bars a party from introducing outside oral or written
  statements to contradict or add to the terms of a final, complete written
  contract.
whyItMatters: >
  It means that promises made during negotiation but left out of the signed
  contract usually cannot be enforced later, which makes getting every
  material term into the written agreement, rather than relying on a verbal
  understanding, essential.
example: >
  A subcontractor claims the general contractor verbally promised extra
  compensation for overtime work, but the signed subcontract is silent on
  overtime pay and contains a clause stating it is the entire agreement
  between the parties. The parol evidence rule generally keeps that verbal
  promise out of the dispute.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] makes sure
  every negotiated term actually makes it into the final written contract, and
  the [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] relies on the
  parol evidence rule when defending against claims based on side
  conversations that never made it into the document.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team negotiates important changes to scope or price verbally and
  assumes they're binding, only to find the signed contract's integration
  clause keeps those verbal changes from being enforced later.
