id: concept-job-hazard-analysis
title: Job Hazard Analysis (JHA)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A written, step-by-step breakdown of a specific task's hazards and the controls used
  to address each one, completed before higher-risk work begins, also called a Job
  Safety Analysis (JSA) depending on the company.
whyItMatters: >
  A generic safety plan misses the hazards specific to today's actual task. A JHA forces
  a crew to think through a job's real steps in advance, so a hazard gets caught on paper
  before it gets caught the hard way in the field.
example: >
  Before a crew enters a [[concept-confined-space-entry|permit-required confined space]],
  they complete a JHA identifying atmospheric testing, ventilation, and rescue procedures
  as required controls, steps a generic safety orientation wouldn't have covered for
  that specific space.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] develops and reviews JHAs for a project's
  higher-risk activities. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] makes sure crews
  actually complete one before starting a task it applies to, rather than treating it as
  paperwork to fill out after the fact.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew fills out a JHA as a formality, copying a previous one without actually
  discussing the current task's real hazards. A step the generic form didn't anticipate,
  an underground utility line in the wrong place, causes an incident a genuine,
  task-specific analysis would likely have flagged in advance.
