id: concept-orthomosaic-mapping
title: Orthomosaic
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A single, seamless, geometrically corrected map created by stitching
  together many overlapping drone photos, with the correction process, called
  orthorectification, removing the distortion caused by camera angle and
  terrain so the result can actually be measured accurately rather than just
  viewed as a picture.
whyItMatters: >
  A regular photo distorts distances and angles depending on the camera's
  angle and the terrain's elevation changes, which makes it useless for
  actually measuring anything on it. Orthorectification is what turns a
  stitched set of drone photos from a nice-looking picture into a
  surveying-grade map someone can take real measurements from.
example: >
  A drone operator captures hundreds of overlapping photos of a construction
  site and processes them into a single orthomosaic, geometrically corrected
  against ground control points, giving the project team a map accurate enough
  to measure real distances and areas directly from it.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-drone-uav-specialist|Drone/UAV Specialist]] captures the raw
  photos and processes them into a finished orthomosaic, while a
  [[career-gis-specialist|GIS Specialist]] often works with the resulting
  geospatial data afterward.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A team treats a quickly stitched set of drone photos as measurement-accurate
  without it ever having been properly orthorectified against ground control
  points. Distances pulled from that unprocessed image end up noticeably off
  from reality, since the raw stitched photo was never actually geometrically
  corrected.
