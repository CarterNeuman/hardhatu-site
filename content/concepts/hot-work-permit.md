id: concept-hot-work-permit
title: Hot Work Permit
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  An authorization required before welding, cutting, grinding, or other spark- or
  flame-producing work begins, confirming the surrounding area is cleared of flammable
  materials, a fire extinguisher is on hand, and a fire watch is posted for a set period
  after the work is done.
whyItMatters: >
  Sparks and slag from hot work can travel and ignite flammable materials well outside
  the immediate work area, sometimes smoldering unnoticed until well after the work
  itself has stopped. The permit process exists specifically to catch what a worker
  focused on the weld itself might not think to check.
example: >
  Before welding near a stack of stored insulation material, a crew relocates the
  flammable material out of range, wets down the surrounding area, and posts a fire
  watch for thirty minutes after the welding stops, per the hot work permit's
  requirements.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-welder|Welder]] works with hot work permits as routine practice any time the
  job is performed outside a dedicated, fire-safe welding area. A
  [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] issues the permit after verifying the
  surrounding conditions actually meet the required precautions.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew skips the formal hot work permit for a "quick" cutting job, assuming the area
  looks clear enough without a real fire watch. A smoldering ember from the cut ignites
  hidden combustible material hours later, well after the crew has moved on and stopped
  watching the area at all.
