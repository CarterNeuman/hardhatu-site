---
id: concept-actual-cost
title: Actual Cost
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A cost that has genuinely been incurred, work actually performed, materials
  actually delivered, an invoice actually received, as opposed to a
  [[concept-committed-cost|committed cost]], which only reflects what's been
  legally obligated through a signed contract or purchase order regardless of
  whether the work has happened yet.
whyItMatters: >
  Actual cost is the only number that reflects reality rather than a legal
  promise. A budget comparison that only looks at actual cost against the
  original bid, without also tracking committed cost, can miss real financial
  exposure that's already locked in through signed subcontracts but hasn't
  shown up in an invoice yet.
example: >
  A concrete subcontractor has an $800,000 signed contract (committed cost),
  but after finishing the foundation and half the slab work, only $500,000 of
  actual invoices have come in so far, work performed and billed to date.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-cost-engineer|Cost Engineer]] compares actual cost against both
  the original budget and the current committed cost regularly, since a gap
  between the two is often the earliest real signal that a project's
  financial outcome is drifting from what was planned. A
  [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] uses actual cost, alongside
  committed cost, to decide whether a subcontractor's pay application should
  be approved as submitted.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project reports actual cost as its only measure of financial health, and
  it tracks close to budget for months, right up until a wave of
  already-committed subcontracts and change orders all convert to actual
  cost at once near the end of the job, revealing an overage that had
  actually been locked in long before anyone noticed.
