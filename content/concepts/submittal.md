id: concept-submittal
title: Submittal
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Product data, shop drawings, samples, or other documentation a contractor sends to the
  architect or engineer for review and approval before ordering or installing a material
  or system, confirming it actually matches what the design calls for.
whyItMatters: >
  Building from the wrong material or the wrong size because nobody checked first is
  expensive to undo. The submittal process is the checkpoint that catches a mismatch on
  paper, before it's ever poured, framed, or installed.
example: >
  A steel fabricator submits detailed shop drawings for a structural beam showing exact
  dimensions and connections. The structural engineer reviews them and returns them
  "approved as noted," with a few dimension corrections, before the fabricator cuts any
  steel.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] typically owns the submittal log day to
  day, tracking dozens of items through review and chasing down approvals that are
  taking too long. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] feels it from the field
  side: work can't safely start on an unapproved item without risking a costly redo.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor gets impatient waiting on a slow architect and installs a batch of windows
  before their submittal comes back approved. The approval finally arrives two weeks
  later with a rejection, because the wrong glazing was ordered, and the
  already-installed windows have to come back out, at the contractor's own cost, since
  installing ahead of approval was their choice.
