---
id: concept-suit-limitation-clause
title: Suit Limitation Clause
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A policy provision that sets a deadline, often one or two years from the date
  of loss, by which the policyholder must file a lawsuit against the insurer
  over a denied or underpaid claim, separate from and usually shorter than the
  general statute of limitations.
whyItMatters: >
  Homeowners and contractors who are still negotiating a dispute can lose the
  right to sue entirely if they wait too long, since this clause can expire
  while settlement talks are still dragging on.
example: >
  A policyholder disputes a storm-damage denial and spends 14 months going back
  and forth with the adjuster, not realizing the policy's one-year suit
  limitation clause already expired and cut off their ability to take the
  insurer to court.
careerAngle: >
  Restoration contractors working on disputed claims should flag this deadline
  early so the homeowner doesn't lose leverage while the insurer slow-walks a
  response.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor assumes the standard several-year state statute of limitations
  for breach of contract applies to insurance disputes, when the policy's own,
  much shorter suit limitation clause actually controls.
