---
id: concept-firestopping
title: Firestopping
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  Fire-rated sealants, caulks, and specialty devices installed at every
  penetration through a fire-rated wall or floor, pipes, conduit, cable trays,
  ductwork, to restore that assembly's fire rating at the point it's been
  penetrated, since even a small unsealed gap can let fire and smoke spread
  through an otherwise fire-rated barrier.
whyItMatters: >
  A fire-rated wall is only as good as its weakest penetration, and
  firestopping has to be installed using a system specifically tested and
  listed for that exact combination of penetrating item and wall type, an
  approach that works for a steel pipe doesn't automatically work for a bundle
  of plastic conduit.
example: >
  An electrician's conduit penetrations through a two-hour fire-rated wall are
  sealed with a firestop sealant system specifically tested and listed for
  that wall assembly and conduit type, referenced by a UL system number noted
  on the installer's documentation.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] or specialty firestop installer seals
  penetrations as trades finish their rough-in. A
  [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] verifies firestopping is installed
  correctly before the wall is
  closed in, since it's essentially impossible to inspect once finishes cover
  it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A general contractor's punch list catches several conduit penetrations
  through a fire-rated corridor wall that were never firestopped at all, the
  trade assumed someone else's scope covered it. The gaps aren't discovered
  until a life-safety inspection specifically probes penetrations behind the
  finished wall, requiring the wall to be opened back up to correct it.
