title: Construction Robotics & Automation
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Robotic equipment performing specific, repetitive physical construction
  tasks, such as autonomous layout robots that mark floor plans directly
  on a slab, bricklaying robots, or rebar-tying robots, distinct from
  [[concept-autonomous-equipment|autonomous heavy equipment]] since these
  robots typically perform a single specialized task rather than operating
  general earthmoving machinery.
whyItMatters: >
  Construction robots today mostly handle narrow, repetitive tasks reliably
  rather than replacing a skilled trade's full scope of work, a layout
  robot still needs an accurate digital model to work from and a
  tradesperson to handle the parts of the job that don't fit its narrow
  programmed task, so the realistic value is in speeding up specific
  repetitive steps, not eliminating the trade.
example: >
  A contractor uses an autonomous layout robot to mark wall locations
  directly on a concrete slab from the building's BIM model, a task that
  previously required a two-person layout crew working from printed
  drawings and a tape measure.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-construction-technology-manager|Construction Technology Manager]]
  evaluates and rolls out robotic equipment across a company's
  projects. A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] or layout crew still verifies
  the robot's marked layout against field conditions before framing begins.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew trusts an autonomous layout robot's markings without spot-checking
  them against an actual existing column location, since the BIM model it
  worked from hadn't been updated after a late structural change. Framing
  proceeds against the robot's incorrect layout for most of a floor before
  someone notices the walls don't actually line up with the columns.
