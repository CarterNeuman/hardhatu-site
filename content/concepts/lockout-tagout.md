id: concept-lockout-tagout
title: Lockout/Tagout (LOTO)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A procedure for de-energizing equipment or systems, locking out the energy source and
  tagging it as out of service, before servicing or maintenance work begins, so the
  equipment can't be unexpectedly re-energized or started while someone is working on it.
whyItMatters: >
  Equipment that unexpectedly powers on or discharges stored energy while someone is
  servicing it is one of the more preventable, and more severe, categories of jobsite
  injury. LOTO exists specifically to remove the possibility of that happening by
  physically controlling the energy source itself, not just relying on a warning.
example: >
  Before servicing a rooftop unit's electrical components, an HVAC technician locks out
  the breaker feeding the unit and tags it, confirming with a test that the unit is
  actually de-energized before opening any panel.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] applies lockout/tagout constantly when working on
  panels and circuits that would otherwise be energized. An
  [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] does the same before servicing equipment
  with both electrical and mechanical stored-energy hazards, like a pressurized system or
  a unit with a flywheel that keeps moving after power is cut.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A technician tags out a breaker but skips physically locking it, assuming the tag alone
  will keep anyone else from restoring power. A coworker unaware of the tag re-energizes
  the circuit to test something unrelated, and the equipment the first technician was
  servicing unexpectedly comes back to life.
