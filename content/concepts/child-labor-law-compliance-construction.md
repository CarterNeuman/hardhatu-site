title: Child Labor Law Compliance (Construction)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  Federal rules under the Fair Labor Standards Act that bar anyone under 18 from
  working in a set of occupations specifically declared hazardous, including
  roofing, excavation, and demolition work, regardless of state-level minimum
  work age rules that might otherwise allow a 16- or 17-year-old to work.
whyItMatters: >
  A state's general minimum working age for a particular job doesn't override
  these federal hazardous-occupation restrictions, so a 17-year-old legally
  allowed to work under state law can still be barred from specific
  construction tasks federal rules consider too dangerous for anyone under 18.
example: >
  A construction company hires a 17-year-old general laborer, legal under the
  state's minimum work age, but has to keep the worker off the roofing and
  excavation crews entirely, since federal hazardous-occupation rules bar
  anyone under 18 from those specific tasks no matter what the state allows.
careerAngle: >
  HR and site supervisors hiring younger workers need to check task assignments
  against the federal hazardous-occupation list specifically, since a worker
  legally hired under state rules can still be barred from entire categories
  of jobsite tasks.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Employers sometimes assume meeting a state's minimum working age for
  construction is the whole compliance picture, when federal
  hazardous-occupation rules impose a separate, stricter under-18 bar on
  specific tasks like roofing, excavation, and demolition that no state
  minimum-age rule can override.
