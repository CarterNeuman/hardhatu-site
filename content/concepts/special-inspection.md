---
id: concept-special-inspection
title: Special Inspection
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A code-required inspection of specific critical or life-safety-related work, such as
  structural welding, high-strength bolting, concrete placement, or soil compaction,
  performed by an independent, qualified third-party inspector rather than the
  contractor's own crew or the local building inspector alone.
whyItMatters: >
  Some construction work becomes invisible or nearly impossible to verify once it's
  covered: structural welds inside a column, rebar inside a concrete pour. Special
  inspections exist specifically because building codes recognize that self-certification
  isn't good enough for the work that holds a building up.
example: >
  Before a structural steel connection is welded, a special inspector observes and
  documents the welding procedure and inspects the completed weld, submitting a report
  to the building department separate from the contractor's own quality documentation.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] coordinates scheduling
  special inspections around the construction sequence, since work often can't
  proceed, or can't be covered up, until the inspector signs off. A
  [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] has to hold up the schedule for trades
  waiting on a special inspection result, which makes timely coordination with the
  inspector a real scheduling factor, not just a formality.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A superintendent lets a concrete crew proceed with the next pour before the required
  special inspection of the rebar placement happens, assuming it will pass anyway. The
  inspector arrives too late to verify the rebar, which is now buried in cured concrete,
  and the project has to bring in additional testing, or in the worst case, remove the
  concrete, to prove the structure is actually code-compliant.
