---
id: concept-tilt-up-concrete
title: Tilt-Up Concrete
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  A concrete wall construction method where large wall panels are cast
  horizontally on the building's own floor slab, used as a casting bed, then
  tilted upright into their final position using cranes and temporarily braced
  until permanently connected to the structure's frame and to each other.
whyItMatters: >
  The bracing engineering and lift timing are safety-critical since a panel is
  extremely heavy and genuinely unstable until it's braced and connected, and
  concrete has to reach a specified minimum strength before a panel can be lifted
  at all without risking it cracking under its own weight.
example: >
  A warehouse developer uses tilt-up construction, casting dozens of wall panels
  flat on the building's own slab, then a crane lifts each panel into place over
  several days, with each one braced immediately until the roof structure ties
  the panels together.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] forms and finishes the panels
  while they're still flat. A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]]
  designs the lifting and bracing engineering each panel needs before and after
  erection.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew lifts a panel before concrete cylinder breaks confirm it reached the
  specified lifting strength. The panel cracks under its own weight during the
  lift, forcing the panel to be scrapped and re-poured, which delays the entire
  erection sequence behind it.
