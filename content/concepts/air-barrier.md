---
id: concept-air-barrier
title: Air Barrier
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  A continuous material or system installed across a building's envelope
  specifically to stop uncontrolled air leakage into and out of the building, a
  distinct function from a [[concept-vapor-barrier|vapor barrier]], which blocks
  moisture vapor diffusion; an assembly can control air leakage without controlling
  vapor diffusion, or vice versa, and a well-designed envelope addresses both.
whyItMatters: >
  Air leakage wastes far more energy than vapor diffusion alone and can also carry
  moisture along with the air, so a continuous air barrier, sealed at every seam,
  penetration, and transition, is now a core energy code requirement on most
  commercial projects, not just a nice-to-have.
example: >
  A building's air barrier is installed as a continuous membrane across the wall
  sheathing, with every seam taped, every window and door opening sealed, and every
  penetration for pipes or conduits carefully sealed back to the membrane, so the
  whole envelope acts as one continuous air-tight boundary.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] or a specialty air-barrier crew installs and seals
  the membrane, a task that requires real attention to continuity, since a single
  unsealed gap can undermine the whole system's performance. A
  [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]] may require
  a blower-door test to verify the building's actual measured air leakage rate against
  the design target.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew installs the air barrier membrane carefully across the wall field but
  doesn't properly seal it where it transitions around a rooftop mechanical
  penetration, assuming that detail is someone else's scope. That one gap becomes a
  significant point of uncontrolled air leakage, and a blower-door test after
  completion reveals the building falls short of its target air-tightness, with the
  failure traced back to that single unsealed transition.
