id: concept-percent-complete-units-method
title: Percent Complete (Units-Complete Method)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  One specific way of measuring how much of an activity is actually done:
  counting physical units already installed, like linear feet of pipe or
  square feet of drywall, against the total units required, rather than
  estimating progress by cost spent or by a supervisor's subjective judgment.
whyItMatters: >
  The units-complete method produces a harder, more verifiable percentage than a
  subjective estimate, since anyone can walk the site and count installed
  units against the total, which is exactly why owners and architects often
  prefer it for certifying progress on repetitive, measurable scope items over
  taking a contractor's own estimate at face value.
example: >
  A drywall subcontractor claims forty percent completion on a pay application.
  Using the units-complete method, the architect counts actual installed
  square footage against the total required square footage and confirms the
  claimed percentage matches what's physically measurable on site, supporting
  certification of that line item.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-quantity-surveyor|Quantity Surveyor]] measures installed units
  against total required quantities to verify a claimed percent complete, and
  the [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] relies on that
  measurement when deciding whether to certify a disputed pay application line
  item.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A pay application line item's percent complete is accepted based on the
  contractor's own estimate alone, without anyone actually counting installed
  units against the total required, and a later dispute reveals the claimed
  percentage was significantly overstated relative to what was physically in
  place.
