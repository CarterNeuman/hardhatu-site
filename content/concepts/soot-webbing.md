---
id: concept-soot-webbing
title: Soot Webbing (Fire Damage)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A pattern of thin, stringy soot deposits, resembling spiderwebs, that forms on
  walls and ceilings from a smoldering, oxygen-starved fire, distinct from the
  heavier, oily deposits left by a fast-burning synthetic-material fire.
whyItMatters: >
  The pattern and type of soot deposit helps restoration estimators determine
  which cleaning method and chemical approach will actually work, since the
  wrong cleaning agent can smear soot into a surface rather than lifting it
  off.
example: >
  A restoration estimator identifies fine, web-like soot webbing across a
  ceiling from a smoldering furniture fire and specifies a dry-sponge cleaning
  approach rather than the solvent-based method that would be used for a
  heavier synthetic-fire soot deposit.
careerAngle: >
  Fire-restoration estimators need to read soot patterns correctly, since
  misdiagnosing the soot type leads to choosing a cleaning method that smears
  rather than removes the residue, requiring the work to be redone.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People unfamiliar with fire restoration sometimes assume all soot is the same
  and can be cleaned with one general method, when different combustion types
  leave chemically different residues that call for different techniques.
