---
id: concept-retaining-wall
title: Retaining Wall
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "32, Exterior Improvements"
definition: >
  A structural wall engineered to hold back soil at a change in grade, built as
  cast-in-place concrete, segmental concrete block, or other systems depending on the
  wall's height and the load behind it, always designed with drainage built in,
  since a retaining wall's biggest structural enemy is water pressure building up
  behind it, not the soil itself.
whyItMatters: >
  A retaining wall without proper drainage behind it can fail even if the wall itself
  is built to the correct structural design, because water trapped behind the wall
  adds hydrostatic pressure the design never accounted for; drainage isn't an
  optional detail, it's part of what makes the structural design actually work as
  intended.
example: >
  A retaining wall is built with a perforated drain pipe and free-draining backfill
  behind it, directing water that accumulates behind the wall out through weep holes
  rather than letting it build up pressure against the wall's back face.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] forms and pours cast-in-place
  retaining walls (or a mason builds a segmental block wall), following the
  engineered design's exact reinforcing and drainage details. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] inspects the drainage
  system behind the wall before backfill covers it, since it's effectively impossible
  to verify or fix once the wall is backfilled.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A retaining wall is backfilled before its drainage system is fully inspected,
  under pressure to keep the site schedule moving. Over the following rainy season,
  water builds up behind the wall without the intended drainage path, and the wall
  begins to bow and crack under pressure the original design assumed would be
  relieved by drainage that was never properly verified.
