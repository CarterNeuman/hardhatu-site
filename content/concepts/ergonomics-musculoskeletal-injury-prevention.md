title: Ergonomics / Musculoskeletal Injury Prevention
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  Workplace practices aimed at reducing injuries from repetitive motion, awkward
  postures, and manual material handling, addressing a category of injury that
  builds up gradually over time rather than from a single sudden event, and so
  isn't governed by one specific OSHA standard the way many acute-injury
  hazards are.
whyItMatters: >
  Musculoskeletal injuries account for a significant share of construction
  workers' compensation claims, but because they develop slowly, they're often
  harder to trace to a single preventable cause than a fall or a struck-by
  injury would be.
example: >
  A masonry crew rotates workers between tasks requiring heavy repetitive
  lifting and lighter work throughout the day, and uses mechanical lifting
  aids for the heaviest material, specifically to reduce the kind of
  cumulative strain that leads to back and shoulder injuries over time.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers should look at task rotation and mechanical lifting aids as
  real prevention tools for this injury category, since there's no single OSHA
  standard to point to the way there is for fall protection, making proactive
  ergonomic planning the main available lever.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Companies sometimes only address musculoskeletal risk after an injury claim
  already comes in, when the injury itself usually reflects weeks or months of
  cumulative strain that proactive task rotation and lifting aids could have
  addressed well before the injury ever happened.
