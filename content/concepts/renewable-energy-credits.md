id: concept-renewable-energy-credits
title: Renewable Energy Credits (RECs)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A tradable certificate representing the environmental benefit of one
  megawatt-hour of     renewable electricity generated and delivered to the
  grid, which a building owner can     purchase separately from their actual
  electricity supply to claim renewable energy use for     sustainability
  reporting or green building certification credit.
whyItMatters: >
  A building doesn't need its own solar panels to claim renewable energy use;
  purchasing     RECs lets an owner claim credit toward a green building
  certification's renewable-energy     requirement even while physically
  drawing standard grid power, since the REC represents     renewable
  generation that happened somewhere else on the same grid.
example: >
  A commercial building purchasing RECs equal to 100% of its annual
  electricity consumption     can claim its operations are powered by
  renewable energy for its LEED application, even     though the building's
  actual electrical service still comes from the local utility's mixed
  generation grid.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]]
  sources RECs     to help a project meet a green building certification's
  renewable energy credit when on-site     generation isn't feasible for that
  building.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner assumes buying RECs is the same as actually reducing their
  building's energy     consumption, when RECs only address the source of the
  electricity on paper; a building still     needs real efficiency measures to
  reduce how much energy it actually uses in the first     place.
