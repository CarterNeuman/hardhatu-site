id: concept-first-notice-of-loss
title: First Notice of Loss (FNOL)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The initial report a policyholder makes to their insurer that a covered loss has
  occurred, the event that officially starts the claims process and the clock on
  several policy deadlines, including the later, more formal
  [[concept-proof-of-loss|proof of loss]].
whyItMatters: >
  FNOL is often the very first moment a restoration company, an adjuster, or an
  emergency services crew learns a loss happened at all, so how quickly and
  accurately that initial report gets relayed can directly affect how fast mitigation
  starts and how much additional damage occurs before help arrives.
example: >
  A homeowner calls their insurer immediately after discovering a burst pipe,
  giving a brief description of the damage over the phone. That call becomes the
  claim's official first notice of loss, starting the timeline the rest of the
  process runs on.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] is typically assigned to a
  claim right after FNOL is filed, before ever seeing the property in person. A
  [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]] often gets
  dispatched based on FNOL details alone, well before a full inspection or scope of
  loss exists.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A policyholder delays calling their insurer for several days after a leak starts,
  not realizing that FNOL timing itself can matter to the claim, since many policies
  require prompt notice of a loss. By the time the claim is filed, both the physical
  damage and the insurer's willingness to fully cover it have gotten worse than a
  same-day report would have produced.
