id: concept-drywall
title: Drywall
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  Gypsum board panels installed over framing (wood or [[concept-metal-studs|metal studs]]) to form a building's interior wall and ceiling surfaces, then taped and
  finished with joint compound to create a smooth, paintable surface.
whyItMatters: >
  Drywall installation quality shows up immediately and unavoidably once painted:
  every framing flaw, seam, or fastener that isn't properly finished telegraphs
  through the paint under the right lighting, making drywall finish one of the most
  visible measures of overall construction quality to an occupant.
example: >
  A drywall crew hangs gypsum board over metal stud framing, then applies multiple
  coats of joint compound over the taped seams, sanding between coats until the wall
  reads as one smooth, continuous surface once primed and painted.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-drywall-installer|Drywall Installer]] hangs and finishes drywall, work
  that depends heavily on the framing underneath already being straight and properly
  spaced. A [[career-painter|Painter]] inherits whatever quality the drywall finish
  left behind, since paint doesn't hide flaws; it reveals them under raking light.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A drywall crew works over metal stud framing that's slightly out of plumb in
  several spots, and rather than flagging the framing issue, they try to compensate by
  building up extra joint compound to hide the unevenness. Under certain lighting
  after painting, the wall's surface still reads as visibly wavy, a flaw traceable
  back to framing that should have been corrected before drywall ever went up.
