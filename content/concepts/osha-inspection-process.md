id: concept-osha-inspection-process
title: OSHA Inspection Process / Walkaround
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The standard sequence an OSHA compliance officer follows during a jobsite
  inspection: an opening conference explaining the inspection's scope, a
  physical walkaround of the site with employer and employee representatives
  present, employee interviews, and a closing conference summarizing any
  apparent violations found.
whyItMatters: >
  An employer's rights and obligations during this process, like which employee
  representative can accompany the inspector and what can and can't be
  withheld, directly affect what ends up on the resulting citation, so knowing
  the process in advance changes how an inspection actually goes.
example: >
  When a compliance officer arrives unannounced after a complaint, the site
  superintendent asks to see credentials, holds a brief opening conference to
  learn the inspection's scope, and designates both a management and an
  employee representative to accompany the officer during the walkaround.
careerAngle: >
  Superintendents and safety managers should have a plan for handling an OSHA
  inspection before one ever happens, since scrambling to figure out rights
  and procedures in the moment tends to go worse for the employer.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Employers sometimes think they have no say in how an inspection unfolds, when
  they have specific rights, like limiting the inspection's scope to what
  triggered it or questioning whether a warrant is needed, that a prepared
  employer can actually exercise.
