id: concept-integrated-project-delivery
title: Integrated Project Delivery (IPD)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A project delivery approach where the owner, architect, and key contractors sign a
  single multi-party contract and share risk and reward collectively, structured
  specifically to align everyone's financial incentives toward the project's overall
  success rather than each party protecting only their own piece.
whyItMatters: >
  Traditional delivery methods can put the owner, architect, and contractor in
  adversarial positions, each protecting their own contract and their own risk. IPD
  tries to remove that friction by making everyone's profit genuinely tied to the same
  shared outcome, encouraging real collaboration instead of each party just managing
  their own exposure.
example: >
  On an IPD hospital project, the owner, architect, and general contractor sign one
  shared agreement with a pooled profit tied to hitting the overall project budget and
  schedule together, rather than each party protecting only their own separate
  contract.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-preconstruction-manager|Preconstruction Manager]] works far more
  collaboratively with the design team under IPD than under a traditional delivery
  method, since design and construction decisions are made jointly rather than handed
  off sequentially. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] has to manage a
  genuinely shared risk pool instead of just their own company's separate contract.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team adopts an IPD contract structure but keeps operating with the same
  adversarial habits as a traditional delivery method, protecting individual scope
  and documenting disputes defensively instead of collaborating. The shared risk pool
  the contract was designed around doesn't actually change behavior, since a contract
  structure alone can't force genuine collaboration if the team culture doesn't shift
  along with it.
