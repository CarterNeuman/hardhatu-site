---
id: concept-environmental-product-declaration
title: Environmental Product Declaration (EPD)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A standardized, third-party-verified document reporting a specific
  building product's environmental impacts, including its embodied carbon,
  over its life cycle, allowing a design team to compare the actual
  environmental footprint of competing products rather than relying on
  generic industry-average assumptions.
whyItMatters: >
  An EPD reports a specific manufacturer's actual product, made at a
  specific plant, not a generic industry average, so two concrete mixes
  with the same strength rating can carry meaningfully different embodied
  carbon numbers depending on their actual EPDs, a difference an
  industry-average estimate would completely miss.
example: >
  A structural engineer compares the EPDs of two competing concrete
  suppliers bidding on the same project, selecting the mix with a
  documented lower embodied carbon figure for the same specified strength,
  rather than assuming both mixes have equivalent environmental impact.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]]
  reviews EPDs to support a project's [[concept-embodied-carbon|embodied carbon]]
  reduction goals. An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] increasingly
  has to source and compare EPDs when a project specification requires
  them for material selection.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project's specification requires EPDs for major structural materials,
  but the general contractor's buyout process doesn't verify subcontractors
  actually collected them until closeout. Several suppliers never had a
  product-specific EPD available at all, and the project has to accept
  industry-average default values instead, missing the actual comparison
  the specification was meant to support.
