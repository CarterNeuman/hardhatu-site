id: concept-bolted-vs-welded-connections
title: Bolted vs. Welded Connections
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  The two primary methods of joining structural steel members in the field,
  high-strength bolts torqued to a specified tension, or field welds joining
  pieces directly, each with different inspection methods, cost, and schedule
  implications, and a single project typically uses both depending on the
  connection.
whyItMatters: >
  A bolted connection can be visually and mechanically verified relatively
  quickly by checking bolt tension, while a welded connection's actual quality
  is hidden inside the weld itself and requires nondestructive testing, such as
  ultrasonic testing, to verify, which affects both inspection cost and
  schedule.
example: >
  A structural engineer specifies bolted connections for a steel frame's
  repetitive beam-to-column connections, since they can be erected and
  inspected quickly, but calls out field welds at a few specific moment
  connections where the design requires a continuous, rigid joint bolts alone
  can't achieve.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] makes bolted connections in the field. A
  [[career-welder|Welder]] makes field welds, a distinct skill requiring its
  own certification. A [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] verifies both,
  using different inspection methods for each.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew substitutes field welds for several connections the structural
  drawings specified as bolted, to save time sourcing the correct bolts. The
  substitution isn't caught until the fabricator's own steel detailer,
  preparing as-built documentation for the closeout package, notices the site
  photos don't match the approved connection design, triggering an
  engineering review before the connections can be accepted or have to be
  redone.
