---
id: concept-fire-watch
title: Fire Watch
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A dedicated person assigned to monitor an area for fire hazards during and
  after hot work like welding or cutting, equipped with fire extinguishing
  equipment and trained to recognize and respond to a fire, typically required
  to remain in place for a set period after the hot work itself stops.
whyItMatters: >
  Many fires from hot work don't ignite during the work itself but smolder
  undetected in nearby combustible material and flare up later, which is
  exactly why a fire watch has to stay posted well after the welding torch is
  actually turned off.
example: >
  After finishing a welding task near exposed wood framing, the crew's
  designated fire watch stays posted at the location for the full 30 minutes
  required after hot work ends, specifically to catch any smoldering material
  before it turns into an active fire.
careerAngle: >
  Supervisors issuing hot work permits need to confirm a fire watch is actually
  assigned and stays through the required post-work monitoring period, not
  just during the active welding or cutting itself.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Crews sometimes treat the fire watch as done the moment the hot work itself
  stops, when the requirement specifically covers a monitoring period after
  the work ends, since smoldering combustible material is often the real
  delayed fire risk.
