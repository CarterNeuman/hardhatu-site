---
id: concept-ofci
title: OFCI (Owner-Furnished, Contractor-Installed)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Equipment or material purchased directly by the owner, such as kitchen
  equipment, specialty security systems, or furniture, but installed by the
  general contractor's crews, splitting the buying and installing responsibilities
  between the two parties and requiring careful coordination of delivery timing
  against the contractor's schedule.
whyItMatters: >
  The contractor has no control over the owner's own purchasing timeline, so an
  OFCI item that arrives late, or wrong, can delay work the contractor is
  contractually on the hook for finishing, making the risk allocation around OFCI
  items a real point of negotiation in the contract.
example: >
  A hospital owner directly purchases specialized imaging equipment as an OFCI
  item, coordinating delivery dates with the general contractor's rough-in
  schedule so the electrical and structural support are ready exactly when the
  equipment arrives.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] or
  [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] coordinates the owner's
  purchase and delivery timing against the general contractor's install schedule.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner purchases OFCI furniture without coordinating delivery against the
  general contractor's move-in schedule. The furniture arrives eight weeks late,
  the contractor's crews sit idle waiting to place and assemble it, and the
  contractor seeks a schedule extension and standby costs since the delay traces
  back to the owner's own purchasing timeline.
