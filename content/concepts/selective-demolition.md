---
id: concept-selective-demolition
title: Selective Demolition
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "02, Existing Conditions"
definition: >
  The targeted removal of specific building elements -- a single wall, a
  section of roofing, interior finishes -- while leaving the surrounding
  structure intact, as opposed to full demolition that clears an entire
  building or structure.
whyItMatters: >
  Selective demolition lets a renovation or addition proceed without
  destroying parts of the building the owner wants to keep, but it requires
  more care than full demolition since the crew has to protect what's staying
  while removing what isn't.
example: >
  A contractor renovating a single floor of an occupied office building
  selectively demolishes interior partition walls and ceiling tile on that
  floor only, using dust barriers and negative-air equipment to keep the work
  from disrupting tenants on other floors.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-demolition-supervisor|Demolition Supervisor]] sequences selective
  demolition to avoid compromising structural elements, and a
  [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] signs off when removal
  touches anything load-bearing.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew removes a wall during selective demolition without confirming it
  isn't load-bearing, and the floor above sags before the error is caught,
  turning a planned interior renovation into an unplanned structural repair.
