---
id: concept-passive-house
title: Passive House
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  An extremely energy-efficient building standard, certified either by the
  Germany-based Passive House Institute or, on most US projects, by PHIUS,
  a separate American certifier using its own climate-zone-specific
  targets, achieved primarily through an airtight building envelope, high
  levels of continuous insulation, and heat-recovery ventilation, rather
  than relying on renewable energy generation or efficient mechanical
  equipment alone to hit its energy targets.
whyItMatters: >
  Passive House's airtightness requirement is verified through a mandatory
  blower-door test with a genuinely demanding numeric target, far stricter
  than typical energy code requirements, so a building pursuing Passive
  House certification can't rely on "close enough" envelope detailing the
  way a conventional project sometimes can.
example: >
  A multifamily developer pursuing Passive House certification requires a
  mid-construction blower-door test specifically to catch air leakage while
  the envelope is still accessible for repair, rather than waiting for the
  final test after finishes are complete.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] installs the continuous, carefully
  detailed air barrier and insulation Passive House depends on. A
  [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]]
  manages the certification process and required testing.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project targeting Passive House certification waits until the building
  is fully finished to conduct its first blower-door test, discovering the
  envelope falls well short of the required airtightness. With finishes
  already installed, tracking down and sealing the source of the leakage
  becomes far more disruptive and expensive than it would have been during
  rough framing.
