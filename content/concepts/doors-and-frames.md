id: concept-doors-and-frames
title: Doors & Frames
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  The door leaf and the frame it hangs in, specified together as a coordinated
  assembly, material (wood, hollow metal, aluminum), fire rating where required, and
  hardware preparation all have to match between the door and its frame for the
  assembly to function and, where applicable, to carry its rated fire performance.
whyItMatters: >
  A fire-rated door assembly only carries its rating as a complete, labeled unit,
  door, frame, and hardware all rated and installed together, so substituting an
  unrated frame under a rated door, or vice versa, invalidates the fire rating even if
  each individual piece looks correct on its own.
example: >
  A stairwell requires a 90-minute fire-rated door assembly. The door, frame, and
  hardware are all ordered and labeled together as a matched, rated assembly, since
  mixing a rated door with an unrated frame would defeat the fire rating regardless of
  the door's own label.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] installs doors and frames to the manufacturer's
  tolerances, since a frame installed out of square makes the door bind or fail to
  latch properly. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]]
  verifies fire-rated assemblies carry matching labels on the door, frame, and
  hardware before accepting the installation, since a missing or mismatched label is a
  life-safety code violation.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor installs a fire-rated door in a frame salvaged from a different,
  unrated assembly to save on material cost, assuming a rated door alone satisfies the
  code requirement. A code inspector catches the mismatched labeling during a
  life-safety inspection, and the entire assembly has to be replaced with a properly
  matched, labeled set before occupancy can be approved.
