id: concept-construction-trust-fund-statute
title: Construction Trust Fund Statute
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A state law, where one exists, that treats payments made to a contractor or
  subcontractor on a construction project as held in trust for the
  subcontractors, laborers, and suppliers who are owed money from that
  project, rather than as the contractor's general business funds.
whyItMatters: >
  These statutes vary significantly by state. Some states have an explicit trust
  fund statute with civil and even criminal penalties for misusing the funds;
  other states have no such statute on the books at all and instead rely on
  ordinary breach-of-contract or fraud claims to address misused construction
  payments.
example: >
  A general contractor in a state with a construction trust fund statute
  receives a large progress payment but uses part of it to cover unrelated
  company payroll instead of paying the subcontractors who earned that money.
  Because the statute treats that payment as trust funds, the contractor's
  principal can face personal civil liability, and in some states even
  criminal exposure, for diverting it.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] advises contractors
  on whether their state has a trust fund statute and what it requires, and
  the [[career-construction-accountant|Construction Accountant]] helps
  structure how project payments are tracked and disbursed to avoid running
  afoul of it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor assumes that because no state agency is actively monitoring how
  project payments get spent, commingling and redirecting those funds carries
  no real legal risk, when a trust fund statute can expose the company's
  principals personally.
