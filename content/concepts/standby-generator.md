id: concept-standby-generator
title: Emergency/Standby Generator
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  An engine-driven generator, typically diesel or natural gas, installed to
  automatically provide backup electrical power to a building during a
  utility outage, sized to carry either the whole building's load or a
  smaller, code-defined subset of life-safety and critical loads, and
  connected through an automatic transfer switch that detects the outage and
  switches the building's power source.
whyItMatters: >
  Code distinguishes between an emergency system, required life-safety loads,
  such as exit lighting and fire alarm systems, that must be powered within
  seconds, and a standby system, other critical loads that can tolerate a
  longer transfer time, and a generator's sizing and transfer switch
  configuration have to match which category of loads it's actually serving.
example: >
  A hospital's emergency generator is sized and configured to restore power
  to its life-safety loads within the ten-second window code requires, with a
  separate, larger standby generator covering less time-critical loads like
  general HVAC on a longer transfer sequence.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] installs the generator's electrical
  connections and the automatic transfer switch. A
  [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] designs which loads the generator
  serves and the required transfer time for each category.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor wires several general building loads into a circuit intended
  only for code-required life-safety loads on the emergency generator,
  exceeding the generator's designed capacity for that circuit. During the
  required annual load-bank test, the overloaded emergency circuit trips
  under full life-safety load, and the electrical inspector flags the
  miswired circuit before it's ever needed during a real outage.
