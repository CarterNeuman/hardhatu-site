id: concept-demand-response-load-shedding-automation
title: Demand Response & Load Shedding Automation
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "25, Integrated Automation"
definition: >
  Automated systems that reduce a building's electrical demand during
  utility-requested demand response events or periods of peak pricing,
  automatically adjusting HVAC setpoints, dimming non-critical lighting, or
  shedding specific loads without staff intervention.
whyItMatters: >
  Many utilities offer financial incentives for buildings that can
  automatically reduce demand during grid stress events, but only buildings
  with automation already in place to respond within the utility's required
  timeframe can actually participate in these programs.
example: >
  A controls contractor programs a building's automation system to
  automatically raise cooling setpoints by a few degrees and dim non-critical
  lobby lighting whenever the utility sends a demand response signal, allowing
  the building to participate in the utility's incentive program.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] designs which loads can be
  automatically shed without affecting occupant comfort or safety, coordinated
  with the [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]]'s operational
  priorities.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A building's automation is wired to respond to demand response signals but
  was never actually enrolled in the utility's program, so the automated
  load-shedding capability sits unused while the building misses out on the
  financial incentive it was built to capture.
relatedIds:
  - career-mep-engineer
  - career-facilities-manager
---
