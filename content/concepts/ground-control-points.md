---
id: concept-ground-control-points
title: Ground Control Points
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Physical markers placed on the ground and precisely surveyed before a drone
  flight, used to anchor the resulting photos or 3D model to real-world
  coordinates rather than leaving the result floating in an arbitrary,
  unanchored coordinate system.
whyItMatters: >
  A drone's own onboard GPS isn't precise enough on its own for survey-grade
  work. Ground control points give the processing software known, highly
  accurate reference locations to calibrate against, which is what actually
  makes the resulting map or model trustworthy enough to take real
  measurements from.
example: >
  A surveyor sets several ground control point markers across a site using a
  total station to establish their exact coordinates before a drone flight,
  giving the drone mapping software fixed, known reference points to calibrate
  the final orthomosaic against.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-surveyor|Surveyor]] establishes ground control points with
  survey-grade precision, work a
  [[career-drone-uav-specialist|Drone/UAV Specialist]] then relies on to
  anchor the drone data accurately.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A drone mapping project skips setting ground control points to save time,
  relying only on the drone's onboard GPS for positioning. The resulting map
  looks fine visually but is measurably less accurate than survey-grade work,
  a problem that only surfaces once someone tries to use it for a precise
  measurement.
