id: concept-delay-claim
title: Delay Claim
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A formal request by a contractor (or sometimes an owner) for additional time and money
  because of a schedule delay attributed to the other party or to a defined "excusable"
  cause, distinct from an ordinary schedule slip because it comes with a documented
  argument for who's responsible and what it cost.
whyItMatters: >
  A project can run late for a dozen reasons, but only some of them entitle the
  contractor to extra time or money; the rest are just the contractor's own problem to
  absorb. A delay claim is how that distinction actually gets argued and resolved,
  usually with the [[concept-baseline-schedule|baseline schedule]] and daily records as
  the evidence.
example: >
  A contractor's schedule slips six weeks after the owner takes four months to approve a
  design change that should have taken two weeks. The contractor files a delay claim for
  the excess time and the added [[concept-acceleration|acceleration]] costs incurred
  trying to make some of it back up.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] builds the initial delay claim from the
  project's own records, arguing why a specific delay wasn't the contractor's fault. A
  [[career-construction-claims-consultant|Construction Claims Consultant]] often gets
  brought in on larger or disputed claims to independently analyze the schedule and
  build a more rigorous, defensible case.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor waits until the very end of the project to assemble a delay claim
  covering issues that happened months earlier, relying on memory to reconstruct what
  caused each slip. Without contemporaneous [[concept-daily-report|daily reports]]
  clearly tying each delay to its cause, the owner disputes the claim's accuracy, and a
  case that should have been straightforward becomes a drawn-out argument over
  incomplete evidence.
