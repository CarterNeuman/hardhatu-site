---
id: concept-allowance
title: Allowance
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A specific dollar amount built into a contract for an item that hasn't been fully
  selected yet, like flooring or light fixtures, so a project can be priced and started
  before every finish decision has been made.
whyItMatters: >
  Waiting until every tile and fixture is picked before signing a contract would stall
  projects for months. An allowance lets a contract move forward with a placeholder
  number, adjusted up or down once the owner actually makes their selection.
example: >
  A kitchen remodel contract includes a $3,000 allowance for countertops. The owner later
  selects a countertop that costs $4,200 installed, so the contract price increases by
  $1,200 to cover the difference.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] sets realistic allowance amounts during bidding; set
  one too low and it becomes a source of disputes later. A
  [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] tracks allowances during construction and
  processes the price adjustment, usually via a [[concept-change-order|change order]],
  once the owner's actual selection is known.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An estimator sets a flooring allowance far below realistic market prices just to make
  the overall bid number look more competitive against other contractors. The owner picks
  an ordinary, mid-range floor and is blindsided by a large change order to cover the
  real cost, a number the estimator likely knew from the start was unrealistic.
