id: concept-kilowatt-hour
title: Kilowatt-Hour (kWh)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  The standard unit utilities use to bill electricity, equal to using one
  kilowatt of power continuously for one hour, and the same unit a net
  metering program uses to credit solar power sent back to the grid against
  power drawn from it.
whyItMatters: >
  Nearly every number on a utility bill, and every number describing a solar
  system's output or net metering credit, is expressed in kilowatt-hours. It's
  a unit most people have seen without ever being told exactly what it
  measures, which matters once someone has to actually evaluate a solar
  proposal or a net metering credit.
example: >
  A solar system is sized to offset a household's typical monthly usage of
  roughly nine hundred kilowatt-hours, and net metering credits the homeowner
  one kilowatt-hour against their bill for every kilowatt-hour of solar power
  the system sends back to the grid.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-solar-installer|Solar Installer]] sizes a solar system against a
  customer's actual kilowatt-hour usage history, pulled from past utility
  bills, rather than a rough guess at the household's needs.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A solar proposal quotes a system's output in a way that sounds impressive
  but doesn't actually match the customer's real kilowatt-hour usage from
  their own utility bills. The system ends up oversized or undersized relative
  to what the household actually needs to offset.
