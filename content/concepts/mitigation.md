---
id: concept-mitigation
title: Mitigation
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The immediate steps taken right after a loss to prevent further damage, such as
  covering a damaged roof, extracting standing water, or shutting off a leaking
  supply line, required by most insurance policies as a condition of coverage, not
  an optional courtesy to the property owner.
whyItMatters: >
  A policyholder who fails to take reasonable mitigation steps can have their claim
  reduced or partially denied for the additional damage that resulted from the
  delay, since the policy's mitigation requirement exists specifically to keep a
  small loss from turning into a much larger one.
example: >
  Within hours of a storm damaging a roof, a restoration crew installs an emergency
  tarp to stop water intrusion, well before the full scope of loss is written or the
  claim is even settled, since delaying mitigation would let the damage keep
  spreading.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]] dispatches
  emergency mitigation crews immediately after a loss, often before a full inspection
  is even scheduled. An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] evaluates
  whether adequate mitigation steps were taken in a reasonable timeframe before
  approving the full claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A property owner delays calling a restoration company for several days after
  discovering a leak, hoping it would dry out on its own. By the time mitigation
  finally starts, mold has developed that wouldn't have grown with a prompt
  response, and the insurer denies coverage for the mold portion of the claim citing
  the policy's mitigation requirement.
