---
id: concept-continuous-insulation
title: Continuous Insulation
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  A layer of rigid or semi-rigid insulation installed continuously across a
  building's exterior wall assembly, uninterrupted by framing members such as
  studs, which would otherwise create a direct thermal path, a thermal bridge,
  around the insulation between the framing cavity's insulation and the
  outside.
whyItMatters: >
  Energy codes now require a minimum amount of continuous insulation in most
  climate zones specifically because cavity insulation alone, insulation
  placed only between studs, leaves the studs themselves as an uninsulated
  thermal bridge, and continuous insulation is the standard way of closing that
  gap.
example: >
  A wall assembly is designed with both cavity insulation between the studs
  and a separate continuous layer of rigid foam insulation installed over the
  sheathing, meeting the energy code's combined requirement rather than relying
  on cavity insulation alone.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] or specialty insulation crew installs
  continuous insulation over the sheathing, and coordinates its thickness with
  whoever installs the cladding attachment system, since thicker continuous
  insulation means longer fasteners and different attachment details.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A general contractor specifies continuous insulation to meet the energy code
  on paper, but the cladding attachment subcontractor uses standard-length
  fasteners that don't account for the added insulation thickness, compressing
  the insulation at every attachment point and creating dozens of small thermal
  bridges the code requirement was specifically meant to eliminate.
