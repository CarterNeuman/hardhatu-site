title: Concrete Finishing Techniques
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  The sequence of hand and power tool passes, screeding to level fresh
  concrete to the form height, bull floating to close the surface right after
  screeding, and then a final trowel finish for a smooth interior floor or a
  broom finish for a slip-resistant exterior surface, that turns a wet pour
  into a usable, finished slab.
whyItMatters: >
  Each pass has to happen at the right point in the concrete's curing window.
  Finishing too early pulls water and fine material to the surface, weakening
  it, while finishing too late means the concrete has already stiffened too
  much to work. Choosing the wrong final finish for the setting, a slick
  trowel finish on an exterior walkway, for instance, can also create a real
  safety hazard.
example: >
  A crew screeds a freshly poured interior slab level with the form boards,
  bull floats it to close the surface, and finishes with a steel trowel for a
  smooth, hard surface, while a separate exterior slab poured the same day
  gets a broom finish instead to provide slip resistance in wet weather.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] times each finishing pass to
  the concrete's actual set, a skill that comes largely from experience rather
  than a fixed schedule, since set time changes with temperature and humidity.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew applies a smooth trowel finish to an exterior walkway slab to match
  the interior floors nearby, without considering that the finish will be
  dangerously slick once it's wet. The mistake isn't caught until after the
  concrete has cured, and the surface has to be mechanically roughened
  afterward to make it safe.
