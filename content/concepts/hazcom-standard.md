id: concept-hazcom-standard
title: Hazard Communication Standard
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard, aligned with the UN's Globally Harmonized
  System of chemical classification, requiring properly labeled containers,
  accessible [[concept-safety-data-sheet|Safety Data Sheets]], and worker training
  for any hazardous chemical present on a jobsite, regardless of which company
  brought it there.
whyItMatters: >
  The standard applies to every hazardous chemical on a shared jobsite, not just
  the ones a company brings itself, so a general contractor's HazCom compliance
  depends on every subcontractor's chemicals being properly labeled and documented
  too.
example: >
  A subcontractor bringing a solvent-based adhesive onto a jobsite provides its
  Safety Data Sheet to the general contractor's site office and ensures the
  container itself carries a GHS-compliant label before any worker uses it.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] maintains the jobsite's SDS system and
  trains crews on reading labels and safety data sheets for whatever hazardous
  chemicals are actually present that week.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A worker mixes an unlabeled solvent left behind by another trade with a
  cleaning product to strip a stubborn adhesive residue, not realizing the
  two are chemically incompatible, and the combination produces a burst of
  fumes strong enough to force the area to be evacuated. Because the solvent
  was never labeled or documented on an SDS as the standard requires, no one
  on site had any way to know the two products shouldn't be combined.
