id: concept-concealment-fraud-clause
title: Concealment or Fraud Clause
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A standard policy provision voiding an entire claim, not just the fraudulent
  portion of it, if the policyholder intentionally conceals or misrepresents a
  material fact at any point in the claims process.
whyItMatters: >
  Unlike most claim disputes, which are only ever about the amount owed, a proven
  concealment or fraud finding can forfeit the whole claim, including any legitimate,
  undisputed portions that would otherwise have been paid without a fight.
example: >
  A policyholder files a claim listing several electronics as stolen that were never
  actually owned, padding an otherwise legitimate burglary claim. The insurer's
  special investigation unit uncovers the fabricated items and voids the entire
  claim, not just the padded portion.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] watches for inconsistencies
  between a policyholder's statements, documentation, and physical evidence that can
  trigger a special investigation unit referral before a claim is ever paid.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor pads a scope of loss with unrelated pre-existing damage to make a
  small claim "worth it," not realizing that if the insurer's investigators catch
  even one fabricated line item, the concealment or fraud clause can void the entire
  claim, including the legitimate damage that would otherwise have been paid without
  any dispute at all.
