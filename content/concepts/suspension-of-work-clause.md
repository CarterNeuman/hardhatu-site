id: concept-suspension-of-work-clause
title: Suspension of Work Clause
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A contract provision giving the owner the right to temporarily halt all or
  part of the construction work for a stated period, for the owner's own
  convenience or for cause, while generally entitling the contractor to an
  adjustment of time and sometimes cost for the resulting delay and
  remobilization expense.
whyItMatters: >
  Unlike termination, which ends the contract entirely, a suspension is meant to
  be temporary, and a well-drafted clause spells out how long a suspension can
  last before the contractor gains the right to treat it as a termination for
  convenience instead, which protects the contractor from being left
  indefinitely in limbo.
example: >
  An owner suspends work on a public project for several weeks while resolving a
  funding issue. The contractor demobilizes its crew and equipment during the
  suspension, then invokes the suspension clause to recover both the schedule
  extension and the documented cost of demobilizing and remobilizing once work
  resumes.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] documents demobilization and
  remobilization costs during a suspension to support a later claim, and the
  [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] tracks the
  suspension's duration against the contract's conversion-to-termination
  threshold.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor absorbs the cost of an owner-directed suspension without formally
  invoking the suspension clause or documenting its added cost, assuming it
  will simply be compensated later, and loses leverage to recover those costs
  once work resumes and the issue is no longer fresh.
