id: concept-access-doors-panels
title: Access Doors & Panels
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  Small hinged or removable panels installed in walls and ceilings to provide
  access to concealed equipment behind a finished surface, valves, dampers,
  junction boxes, cleanouts, sized and located based on what specific
  equipment needs to be reached for future maintenance, not placed arbitrarily.
whyItMatters: >
  Access panel locations have to be coordinated before drywall closes in,
  since a panel added after the fact means cutting into a finished wall or
  ceiling, and a panel placed without input from whoever actually maintains the
  equipment behind it can end up too small or in the wrong spot to be useful.
example: >
  A general contractor coordinates access panel locations with the mechanical
  and electrical rough-in drawings before drywall installation, placing a
  panel exactly where it lines up with a concealed valve the plumber will need
  to service after occupancy.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-drywall-installer|Drywall Installer]] frames the opening and
  installs the panel. A [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]]
  relies on access panels being in the right places to actually reach
  equipment during maintenance after occupancy.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Drywall closes in over a concealed valve without an access panel because the
  location wasn't coordinated ahead of time. When the valve needs servicing
  after occupancy, facilities staff have to cut an opening into the finished
  wall, and coordinate with a drywall crew to patch it back afterward.
