id: concept-pre-engineered-metal-building
title: Pre-Engineered Metal Building
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "13, Special Construction"
definition: >
  A structural system, often called a "PEMB" or "metal building system",
  engineered and manufactured as a complete kit of steel framing, roofing, and wall
  panels, designed by the manufacturer's own engineers to the project's specific
  loads and dimensions, then shipped to the site for erection.
whyItMatters: >
  Because a PEMB is engineered as a complete, integrated system by the manufacturer
  rather than designed independently by the project's structural engineer, changes to
  the building's dimensions or loading after the manufacturer's engineering is
  finalized can require the entire structural package to be re-engineered, not just a
  simple field adjustment.
example: >
  A warehouse owner orders a pre-engineered metal building sized and engineered by
  the manufacturer for the specific roof snow load and wind load at that site, with
  the entire steel frame, roof, and wall panel package manufactured as a coordinated
  kit and shipped for erection.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] coordinates the manufacturer's
  engineering submittals and shop drawings early, since a PEMB's long manufacturing
  lead time means design decisions have to be locked in well before erection. An
  [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] erects the steel frame following the
  manufacturer's specific erection drawings, which are often more prescriptive than a
  conventional structural steel erection sequence.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team decides to add a mezzanine level to a pre-engineered metal building
  after the manufacturer's engineering package has already been finalized and
  fabrication has started. The added load requires the manufacturer to re-engineer
  and potentially re-fabricate structural components, creating a costly delay that a
  decision made earlier, before the engineering was locked in, would have avoided.
