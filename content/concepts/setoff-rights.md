---
id: concept-setoff-rights
title: Set-Off Rights (Contractual Setoff)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A contractual or legal right allowing one party to reduce what it owes another
  party by deducting an amount the other party separately owes back, rather
  than paying the full amount and then separately pursuing collection on the
  counter-debt.
whyItMatters: >
  Construction contracts often address set-off explicitly, either allowing an
  owner or GC to offset backcharges and defective-work costs against an
  otherwise-due payment, or specifically restricting set-off rights to protect
  a subcontractor's cash flow, so the actual contract language, not just
  general legal principle, usually controls how broadly it can be used.
example: >
  A general contractor incurs a significant backcharge after having to hire
  another trade to correct a subcontractor's defective work. Rather than
  paying the subcontractor's next invoice in full and separately pursuing
  reimbursement, the GC exercises its contractual set-off right and deducts
  the backcharge directly from that payment.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] tracks
  backcharges eligible for set-off against upcoming payments, and the
  [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] evaluates whether a
  specific contract's set-off language actually supports a disputed deduction
  before it's applied.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A party exercises set-off against a payment without confirming the contract's
  language actually permits it for that type of claim, and ends up facing its
  own breach-of-contract exposure for withholding money it wasn't actually
  entitled to offset.
