id: concept-spud-wrench
title: Spud Wrench
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  A hand wrench with a long, tapered handle, used by ironworkers to align
  mismatched bolt holes between two steel members by inserting the tapered end
  and levering the holes into alignment before a bolt can actually be driven
  through them.
whyItMatters: >
  Steel connections almost never line up with perfect precision on the first
  try, and the spud wrench's tapered point is specifically designed to fit
  into a slightly misaligned hole and lever the connecting plate into the
  position needed for a bolt to pass through cleanly.
example: >
  An ironworker setting a beam finds the bolt holes on the connecting plate
  slightly offset from the column's holes, and uses the tapered end of a spud
  wrench to lever the plate into alignment before driving the first bolt
  through to hold the connection temporarily.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] carries a spud wrench as one of the most
  used hand tools on a steel erection crew, since nearly every connection
  needs some amount of hole alignment before it can be bolted.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An ironworker forces a bolt through a misaligned hole with a hammer instead
  of taking the time to lever it into true alignment with a spud wrench first.
  The forced bolt doesn't seat properly in the hole, leaving a connection
  that's weaker than it was designed to be.
