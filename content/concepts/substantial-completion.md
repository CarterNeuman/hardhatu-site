id: concept-substantial-completion
title: Substantial Completion
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The point in a project when the work is complete enough for the owner to use the
  building for its intended purpose, even though minor items, the punch list, still
  need to be finished.
whyItMatters: >
  It's a specific legal and financial milestone, not just a feeling that things are
  "mostly done." Substantial completion is usually when warranties start counting down
  and retainage starts releasing, though actually moving in still depends on a
  separate [[concept-certificate-of-occupancy|certificate of occupancy]], so the exact
  substantial completion date matters even before anyone can legally use the space.
example: >
  A new office building's walls, systems, and finishes are all complete and the space is
  fully usable, but a handful of touch-up paint spots and one slow-closing door remain.
  The architect certifies substantial completion, and the punch list becomes the
  remaining short list of work.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] pushes hard for an official substantial
  completion date, since it usually triggers a big milestone payment. A
  [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] has to be confident the building is actually
  safe and usable before agreeing to it; the date isn't just a formality.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor pressures the architect into certifying substantial completion so
  retainage releases and the warranty clock starts, even though the fire alarm system
  isn't fully functional yet. The certification turns out to be premature: the fire
  marshal's own inspection catches the same problem before a certificate of occupancy
  can be issued, delaying the owner's actual move-in and leaving the rushed
  certification looking dishonest in hindsight.
