id: concept-shear-wall
title: Shear Wall
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics, and Composites"
definition: >
  A wall section built with a tighter nailing schedule and specific hold-down
  hardware anchoring it to the framing below, engineered specifically to
  resist the sideways, or lateral, forces a building experiences from wind or
  seismic activity.
whyItMatters: >
  Most framing is designed to carry vertical loads, weight pressing straight
  down. A shear wall is built to resist a completely different kind of force,
  sideways push, which is why it needs its own specific engineering, tighter
  fastening, and hardware rather than just being a normal wall that happens to
  be load-bearing.
example: >
  An engineer designates specific wall sections in a house as shear walls,
  calling for a tighter nailing pattern on the sheathing and steel hold-down
  hardware bolting the wall to the foundation, specifically to help the house
  resist lateral wind and seismic loads.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] designates which walls
  in a building are shear walls and specifies their exact nailing and hardware
  requirements, which a [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] then has to follow
  exactly, since a shear wall built to the wrong specification doesn't perform
  as designed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framing crew builds a designated shear wall using the same nailing
  schedule as an ordinary wall nearby, not realizing the engineered drawings
  called for a tighter pattern specific to that wall. The wall looks identical
  to any other once finished, but it doesn't actually provide the lateral
  resistance the design was counting on.
