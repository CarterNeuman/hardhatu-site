id: concept-wetlands-permit-404
title: Wetlands Permit / Clean Water Act Section 404
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A permit from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers required before discharging
  dredged or fill material into wetlands or other waters of the United States,
  ranging from a streamlined nationwide permit for minor impacts to a lengthier,
  more involved individual permit for larger ones.
whyItMatters: >
  A low, wet-looking area on a site plan isn't automatically a jurisdictional
  wetland, and a genuinely dry-looking area sometimes is, the actual determination
  requires a real wetland delineation, not a visual guess made from the grading
  plan.
example: >
  A site's civil engineer commissions a formal wetland delineation before finalizing
  the grading plan, identifying a jurisdictional area the design then routes around
  rather than filling, avoiding the need for a 404 permit altogether.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] designs the site plan around any
  identified wetlands, while an
  [[career-environmental-remediation-specialist|Environmental/Remediation Specialist]]
  may assist with the delineation and permitting process itself.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A site's grading plan fills a low, wet area that a formal delineation, done only
  after grading is already underway, identifies as a jurisdictional wetland. The
  project receives a stop-work order and a required restoration order well after
  the fill work is finished.
