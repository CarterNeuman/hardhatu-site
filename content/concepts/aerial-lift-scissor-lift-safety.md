id: concept-aerial-lift-scissor-lift-safety
title: Aerial Lift / Scissor Lift Safety
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA requirements for operating boom lifts, scissor lifts, and similar aerial
  work platforms, covering operator training, fall protection, and the
  critical distinction that a boom lift's basket requires a harness tied off
  to the boom while a scissor lift's platform generally relies on its
  guardrails instead.
whyItMatters: >
  Workers sometimes transfer fall-protection habits from one lift type to the
  other incorrectly, and the harness requirement specifically depends on which
  type of lift is being used, which is a distinction that trips up even
  experienced crews.
example: >
  A worker trained primarily on scissor lifts moves to a boom lift job and
  initially skips the required harness and lanyard, since the scissor lift's
  guardrails alone had always been sufficient, before the site's safety
  manager corrects the oversight before work begins.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers should verify which specific type of aerial lift a crew is
  using each day and confirm the correct fall-protection setup for that type,
  rather than assuming one lift's safety rules apply across the board.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Workers sometimes assume any elevated platform's guardrails alone satisfy fall
  protection, when a boom lift specifically requires a tied-off harness
  because the basket can tip or eject an occupant in ways a scissor lift's
  vertically-rising platform generally can't.
