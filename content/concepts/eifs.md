---
id: concept-eifs
title: EIFS (Exterior Insulation & Finish System)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  An exterior wall cladding built up from rigid foam insulation board adhered
  or mechanically attached to the wall, then coated with a reinforced base coat
  and a textured finish coat, distinct from stucco since EIFS's insulation and
  finish are a manufactured system rather than a traditional plaster assembly.
whyItMatters: >
  Early "barrier" EIFS systems had no drainage plane and trapped any water
  that got behind the cladding, a problem that caused widespread moisture
  damage and lawsuits in the 1990s, which is why current EIFS installations
  require a "drainage" EIFS design with a water-resistive barrier and drainage
  channel behind the insulation, not the older barrier approach.
example: >
  A contractor installs a drainage EIFS system over a water-resistive barrier
  with a continuous drainage plane behind the insulation board, so any water
  that gets past the finish coat drains down and out at the base rather than
  becoming trapped against the sheathing.
careerAngle: >
  A specialty EIFS subcontractor installs the system. A
  [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] verifies the water-resistive
  barrier and drainage plane are properly installed before the insulation board
  covers them.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An installer applies an EIFS system without confirming the required drainage
  plane is continuous behind the insulation board, leaving a section where the
  insulation is adhered directly to the sheathing with no drainage path. The
  trapped moisture isn't discovered until a tenant reports a persistent musty
  smell near that section of wall years later, and an invasive moisture probe
  finally traces it back to the missing drainage path, exposing sheathing
  damage no one suspected from the exterior.
