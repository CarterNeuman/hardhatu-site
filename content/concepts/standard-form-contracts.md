id: concept-standard-form-contracts
title: Standard Form Contracts (AIA & ConsensusDocs)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  Pre-drafted, industry-standard contract templates, most commonly published by the
  American Institute of Architects (AIA) and ConsensusDocs, that project parties adapt
  and fill in for a specific project instead of drafting a custom contract from scratch
  for every job.
whyItMatters: >
  Because these forms are widely used and heavily litigated over decades, their terms
  are well understood across the industry: an owner, architect, and contractor working
  from an AIA A101 or a ConsensusDocs 200 already share a common baseline vocabulary for
  risk allocation, payment, and disputes, which speeds up negotiation compared to
  reviewing a one-off custom document line by line.
example: >
  A general contractor and owner use AIA Document A101 (Standard Form of Agreement
  Between Owner and Contractor) paired with A201 (General Conditions of the Contract for
  Construction) instead of drafting their own agreement, filling in project-specific
  terms, such as contract sum, schedule, and insurance requirements, into the standard
  template.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] needs to know these
  standard forms cold, since so much of the job is reviewing and negotiating
  modifications against a familiar baseline rather than reading an unfamiliar document
  from page one. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] relies on knowing which
  standard form governs a project to quickly answer questions about notice deadlines,
  payment timing, and dispute procedures without re-reading the whole contract.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team assumes AIA and ConsensusDocs forms are interchangeable and treats
  contract review as a formality since "it's just the standard form." The two families
  allocate risk differently in places, for example around indemnification scope and
  dispute resolution defaults, and a party that skips careful review because the
  document looks familiar can agree to unfavorable terms buried in the specific edits
  made to that project's version of the template.
