id: concept-daily-report
title: Daily Report
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A superintendent's or foreman's daily written log of what happened on-site, crews
  present, work completed, weather, deliveries, equipment used, and any problems or
  delays, kept for essentially every day of a project.
whyItMatters: >
  Memory fades, but a daily report doesn't. Months or years later, when a dispute comes
  up about who was on-site on a specific date or why a task took longer than planned,
  the daily report is often the only reliable record anyone has.
example: >
  A daily report from a rainy Tuesday notes that concrete work was postponed due to
  weather, that a specific delivery of rebar arrived late, and that only six of the
  scheduled eight workers were on-site, details nobody would remember accurately
  without having written them down that same day.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] writes the daily report every single day,
  making it one of the most routine but important habits of the job. A
  [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] relies on that running record whenever a
  delay claim or dispute requires reconstructing exactly what happened and when.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A superintendent gets busy and skips daily reports for a stretch of two weeks, meaning
  to fill them in later from memory. When a delay dispute comes up months afterward
  about exactly which days were lost to weather during that period, there's no
  contemporaneous record to point to, only a reconstruction from memory that the other
  side has no reason to simply accept.
