title: Tempered and Laminated Safety Glass
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  Two different types of safety glass: tempered glass is heat-treated so that
  if it breaks, it shatters into small, relatively harmless pieces instead of
  large sharp shards, while laminated glass bonds two glass layers around a
  plastic interlayer so that even if it breaks, the pieces stay held together
  rather than falling away.
whyItMatters: >
  Code requires safety glass in specific hazardous locations, like doors and
  low windows, precisely because ordinary glass breaks into dangerous shards.
  Tempered and laminated glass solve that problem in different ways, and a
  project specifies the right one based on the actual application, since
  they're not interchangeable: laminated glass, for instance, is also used
  where keeping broken glass in place matters, like overhead glazing.
example: >
  A glazier installs tempered glass in a storefront door, a code-required
  hazardous location, while a separate overhead skylight on the same project
  uses laminated glass instead, since the design calls for broken glass to
  stay held in place rather than fall through if it's ever damaged.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-glazier|Glazier]] installs the specific safety glass type a
  location requires, confirming it matches the design and code requirement for
  that exact spot rather than treating all safety glass as interchangeable.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A glazier installs standard annealed glass in a code-required hazardous
  location, rather than tempered or laminated safety glass, either by mistake
  or because the correct material wasn't on hand. The substitution isn't
  caught until a final inspection specifically checks glazing type at
  hazardous locations.
