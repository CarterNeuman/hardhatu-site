---
id: concept-qualifications-based-selection
title: Qualifications-Based Selection (QBS)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A procurement method, most common for design and engineering services, that
  selects a firm based on qualifications, experience, and technical approach
  first, and negotiates fee or price only after that firm has already been
  chosen.
whyItMatters: >
  It's required by law for most publicly funded architecture and engineering
  contracts in the United States, on the reasoning that competing design firms
  primarily on price can push them toward cutting corners on a service where
  quality is hard to judge until the project is already built.
example: >
  A public agency selects a structural engineering firm for a new bridge based
  on a review of comparable past projects and the proposed project team's
  qualifications, and only begins negotiating the engineering fee after that
  firm has already been selected as the top-ranked candidate.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] runs the
  qualifications-based selection process and resulting fee negotiation, and
  the [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] or other design professional
  prepares the qualifications package that QBS evaluations are actually scored
  against.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An agency tries to speed up a QBS process by asking firms to submit fee
  proposals alongside their qualifications from the start, which can violate
  the qualifications-first sequence that QBS laws for public design contracts
  typically require.
