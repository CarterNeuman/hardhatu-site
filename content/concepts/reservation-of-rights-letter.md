---
id: concept-reservation-of-rights-letter
title: Reservation of Rights Letter
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A letter an insurer sends a policyholder after a claim is filed, stating that the
  insurer will investigate and handle the claim while explicitly reserving the right
  to later deny coverage, in whole or in part, if the investigation shows the loss
  isn't actually covered.
whyItMatters: >
  The letter doesn't mean the claim is denied, it means it's still genuinely open,
  and a policyholder who misreads it as a denial and stops cooperating can
  accidentally hand the insurer real grounds to deny the claim later.
example: >
  A homeowner files a water damage claim and receives a reservation of rights
  letter noting the insurer needs to confirm whether the source was a covered
  plumbing failure or an excluded long-term seepage issue before paying anything.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] sends the letter as a
  standard part of investigating a claim with an open coverage question, while a
  [[career-public-adjuster|Public Adjuster]] explains to the policyholder what it
  actually means and what to do next.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A policyholder receives a reservation of rights letter, assumes their claim has
  effectively been denied, and stops returning the adjuster's calls or providing
  requested documentation. That lack of cooperation is itself a policy violation the
  insurer can later cite to support an actual denial.
