---
id: concept-transformer
title: Transformer
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  Equipment that steps electrical voltage up or down between a utility's supply
  voltage and the lower voltage a building's internal distribution system actually
  uses, typically set on a concrete pad outside the building or in a dedicated
  electrical room, depending on the utility's requirements and the building's size.
whyItMatters: >
  A transformer's location and clearances are dictated by the utility company and
  electrical code, not just site convenience, so a project that places one without
  confirming those requirements early can end up relocating a heavy, already-ordered
  piece of equipment late in the project.
example: >
  A utility company reviews a project's site plan early in design and specifies the
  required pad location, clearances, and access for the transformer that will serve
  the building, coordinating the timeline for utility interconnection well before
  construction reaches that point.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] terminates the building's side of the
  transformer connection, working closely with the utility on interconnection
  requirements. A [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] treats the
  transformer as critical infrastructure requiring coordination with the utility for
  any future service changes or capacity upgrades.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project finalizes its site layout without confirming the utility's required
  transformer clearances, placing it too close to a planned loading dock. The utility
  flags the conflict during final review, well after the pad was poured, forcing an
  expensive relocation of both the transformer pad and the loading dock design.
