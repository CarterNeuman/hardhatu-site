id: concept-rough-in-electrical
title: Rough-In Electrical
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  The conduit, wiring, and box installation performed inside walls, floors, and
  ceilings before they're closed up with drywall or flooring, installed and inspected
  while it's still visible, the electrical equivalent of plumbing and HVAC rough-in
  happening in the same building at the same time.
whyItMatters: >
  Electrical rough-in that's covered before inspection creates the same kind of hidden
  risk as uninspected plumbing, except with electrical work the consequence of a
  hidden defect can be a fire rather than a leak, which is why code requires
  electrical rough-in inspection before it's ever covered.
example: >
  An electrician installs conduit and pulls wire through a floor's framed walls, then
  has the completed rough-in inspected against the electrical drawings and code before
  drywall closes over it.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] installs rough-in wiring to the electrical
  drawings and the National Electrical Code, coordinating box and conduit placement
  with other trades roughing in the same walls. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]]
  sequences drywall and ceiling closure around the electrical inspection, since no
  wall can close until that inspection clears.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrical crew completes rough-in work on a floor, but the required inspection
  gets scheduled late and drywall crews, working to their own schedule, close up the
  walls before the inspector arrives. The inspection has to happen through
  spot-opened sections of the finished wall instead, an expensive and avoidable
  consequence of covering work before it was cleared.
