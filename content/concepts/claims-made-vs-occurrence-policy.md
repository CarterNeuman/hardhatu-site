id: concept-claims-made-vs-occurrence-policy
title: Claims-Made vs. Occurrence Policy
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  Two different ways a liability insurance policy can be triggered: an
  occurrence policy covers an incident that happens during the policy period
  no matter when the claim is later filed, while a claims-made policy only
  covers claims actually filed while the policy is active or during a defined
  extended-reporting period.
whyItMatters: >
  Most commercial general liability policies in construction are
  occurrence-based because construction defects often surface years after work
  is finished, but professional liability and some specialty policies are
  claims-made, which means a lapse in continuous coverage can leave a past
  incident with no policy available to respond to a late-filed claim.
example: >
  A structural engineer's errors-and-omissions policy is claims-made. The firm
  switches insurers without buying tail coverage, and a design-error claim
  surfaces two years later tied to work performed under the old policy.
  Because no policy is active to receive that claim, the firm has a coverage
  gap.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-risk-manager-construction|Risk Manager]] tracks which of a
  company's policies are claims-made versus occurrence-based to make sure
  coverage gaps don't open up during a carrier change, and the
  [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] determines which policy
  period actually applies to a given claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A firm switches insurance carriers and assumes its old claims-made policy
  still protects it against claims filed later, without buying the extended
  reporting endorsement that keeps that protection in place.
