id: concept-workplace-violence-prevention-plan
title: Workplace Violence Prevention Plan
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A written program addressing the risk of violence on a jobsite, from conflicts
  between workers to incidents involving the public or site visitors, covering
  hazard assessment, incident reporting, and response procedures, an area of
  growing state-level regulation beyond what federal OSHA specifically
  requires.
whyItMatters: >
  Construction sites bring together workers from multiple companies, often under
  stress from schedule pressure or disputes, and the site is frequently
  accessible to the public in ways an office building isn't, both of which
  raise workplace violence risk in ways a generic safety plan may not
  specifically address.
example: >
  A general contractor's workplace violence prevention plan specifically
  addresses how site security handles an agitated member of the public who
  wanders onto an active jobsite, a scenario the company's general safety
  orientation never used to cover in detail.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers on sites with public access or history of worker conflict
  should treat this as its own distinct written plan, since workplace violence
  risk on a construction site looks different from the risk profile most
  generic safety programs were built around.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Companies sometimes assume their general safety program already covers
  workplace violence simply because it covers safety broadly, when this is an
  increasingly separately regulated area, in some states now with its own
  specific written-plan requirement, distinct from general jobsite safety
  planning.
