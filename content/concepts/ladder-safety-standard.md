id: concept-ladder-safety-standard
title: Ladder Safety Standard
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's construction standard governing ladder use, covering required load
  capacity, the proper setup angle, the three-points-of-contact rule for
  climbing, and the prohibition on using the top two rungs of a stepladder or
  standing on the very top of an extension ladder.
whyItMatters: >
  Falls from ladders are one of the most common and most preventable types of
  construction injury, and most ladder-fall citations trace back to a handful
  of repeat issues: wrong angle, missing extension above the landing, or a
  worker simply standing somewhere on the ladder the standard prohibits.
example: >
  A worker sets an extension ladder against a wall at roughly a 75-degree angle,
  extends it at least three feet above the roofline they're climbing onto, and
  maintains three points of contact the whole way up rather than carrying
  tools in both hands.
careerAngle: >
  Superintendents doing jobsite walkthroughs should check ladder angle and
  extension height specifically, since these are easy to verify at a glance
  and are two of the most common ladder-safety violations.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Workers sometimes treat the top of a stepladder or the very top rungs of an
  extension ladder as just another step, when the standard specifically
  prohibits standing there because of how unstable the ladder becomes at that
  point.
