---
id: concept-hurricane-tie
title: Hurricane Tie
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics, and Composites"
definition: >
  A formed metal strap or clip that physically connects roof framing members
  to the wall's top plate, engineered specifically to resist the upward force,
  or uplift, that high winds create as they try to lift a roof off a building.
whyItMatters: >
  Nails alone resist a roof sliding sideways reasonably well, but they resist
  being pulled straight out, which is what wind uplift does, far less
  effectively. A hurricane tie adds a mechanical connection specifically
  engineered to resist that uplift force, which is why it's required in many
  wind-prone regions regardless of how well the framing is otherwise nailed
  together.
example: >
  A framing crew installs a hurricane tie at every rafter-to-top-plate
  connection on a roof, in addition to the normal nailing, specifically to
  resist the uplift forces the structure is expected to see during a high-wind
  event.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] installs hurricane ties according to the
  engineered drawings, since skipping even a few connections can create a weak
  point where a roof is more likely to fail first during a severe wind event.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew installs hurricane ties on most, but not all, of a roof's
  rafter-to-wall connections, treating the hardware as a general best practice
  rather than following the engineered layout exactly. The missing connections
  become the weak points where roof uplift failure is most likely to start
  during a severe storm.
