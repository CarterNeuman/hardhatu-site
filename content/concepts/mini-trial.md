---
id: concept-mini-trial
title: Mini-Trial
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  An alternative dispute resolution process in which each party presents a
  condensed version of its case, evidence included, to a neutral advisor and
  to senior decision-makers from both companies who have authority to settle,
  aiming to produce a realistic settlement without the cost of full
  litigation.
whyItMatters: >
  It differs from mediation because actual evidence and arguments are formally
  presented, almost like a compressed trial, but it differs from arbitration
  because the neutral advisor's opinion typically isn't binding; the real goal
  is to give the parties' own decision-makers, who often haven't been close to
  the day-to-day dispute, a realistic picture of how a real trial might go.
example: >
  Two companies in a costly delay dispute agree to a one-day mini-trial before
  pursuing full litigation. Each side's attorneys present a condensed case to
  a retired judge acting as neutral advisor and to each company's own senior
  executives, who then use the advisor's non-binding reaction to negotiate a
  settlement that evening.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] prepares and
  presents the condensed case at a mini-trial, and the
  [[career-construction-claims-consultant|Construction Claims Consultant]]
  helps assemble the technical evidence and damages analysis presented during
  the session. A neutral advisor, often a
  [[career-construction-mediator-arbitrator|Construction Mediator / Arbitrator]],
  hears both condensed presentations and offers a non-binding opinion on how
  the case would likely resolve.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A company sends only its litigation attorneys to a mini-trial without anyone
  who actually has settlement authority in the room, defeating the format's
  purpose of giving real decision-makers a realistic basis to settle the
  dispute that same day.
