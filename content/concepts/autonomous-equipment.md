---
id: concept-autonomous-equipment
title: Autonomous & Semi-Autonomous Equipment
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Heavy construction equipment, such as bulldozers, compactors, or haul
  trucks, capable of operating with reduced or no direct human control,
  using GPS positioning, sensors, and onboard software to perform
  repetitive earthmoving tasks, distinct from
  [[concept-gps-machine-control|GPS machine control]], which assists a human
  operator rather than replacing them.
whyItMatters: >
  Autonomous equipment today is generally limited to controlled, repetitive
  tasks on a well-defined site, such as compacting a large fill area,
  rather than the full range of judgment-heavy decisions an experienced
  operator makes, so the realistic near-term value is freeing operators for
  more complex tasks rather than eliminating the role entirely.
example: >
  A large earthwork contractor deploys an autonomous compactor to
  repeatedly pass over a large fill area overnight, freeing its operators
  during the day for the more judgment-heavy excavation and grading work
  the autonomous equipment isn't suited for.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] increasingly oversees
  and troubleshoots autonomous equipment rather than directly operating
  every machine on site, a shift in the role's day-to-day work rather than
  its elimination.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor deploys an autonomous compactor on a fill area without
  adequately fencing off the zone from other site traffic, assuming the
  equipment's sensors would reliably detect and avoid an unexpected
  obstacle. A delivery truck enters the zone unexpectedly, and only the
  truck driver's own quick reaction, not the autonomous system, prevents a
  collision, prompting the contractor to add hard site controls around
  future autonomous work zones.
