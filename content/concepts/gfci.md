---
id: concept-gfci
title: GFCI (Ground-Fault Circuit Interrupter)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  A protective device that continuously monitors a circuit for current leaking
  to ground and shuts off power within a fraction of a second if it detects a
  leak, protecting a person from electric shock, required by code near water
  and in other specific locations.
whyItMatters: >
  A standard circuit breaker protects wiring and equipment from overload, but
  it doesn't react fast enough to protect a person from shock. A GFCI is built
  specifically to detect the much smaller current imbalance that happens when
  electricity starts flowing through a person's body instead of its intended
  path, and to cut power before serious injury occurs.
example: >
  An electrician installs a GFCI outlet in a bathroom, a location code
  requires it, so that if a hair dryer plugged into it ever comes into contact
  with water, the device cuts power almost instantly rather than relying on a
  standard circuit breaker that wouldn't react in time.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] installs GFCI protection in every
  code-required location, including kitchens, bathrooms, and exterior outlets,
  without exception.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A remodel adds an outlet near a sink without GFCI protection, since the
  original circuit it taps into wasn't GFCI protected either. The oversight
  isn't caught until an inspection specifically tests for GFCI protection at
  every required location.
