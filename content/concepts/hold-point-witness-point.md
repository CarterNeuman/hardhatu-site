---
id: concept-hold-point-witness-point
title: Hold Point / Witness Point
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Two levels of required inspection stops built into an inspection and test
  plan: a hold point is a mandatory stop where work legally cannot continue
  until a specific party signs off, while a witness point simply requires that
  party be notified and given the opportunity to observe, without actually
  stopping the work if they don't show up.
whyItMatters: >
  The distinction matters because a missed witness point is a documentation gap,
  while proceeding past a missed hold point without the required sign-off is a
  real compliance violation, which means the ITP has to clearly label which
  specific inspections are hold points and which are only witness points.
example: >
  An ITP designates rebar inspection before a structural pour as a hold point,
  meaning the crew legally cannot pour until the inspector signs off, but
  designates the inspector's observation of formwork stripping as only a
  witness point, meaning the crew can proceed on schedule even if the
  inspector isn't available to watch that specific step.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] confirms which designated hold
  points actually require a sign-off before work proceeds, and the
  [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] schedules crews around hold points
  to avoid a costly standstill waiting on inspection availability.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew treats a designated hold point like an ordinary witness point and
  proceeds with work when the required inspector doesn't show up on schedule,
  not realizing the work now has to be exposed again later for the sign-off
  that should have happened before it continued.
relatedIds:
