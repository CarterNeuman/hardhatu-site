---
id: concept-responsible-bidder-ordinance
title: Responsible Bidder Ordinance
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A local or state law requiring public project bidders to meet specific
  qualification standards, such as apprenticeship program participation,
  minimum experience, or a clean safety record, before they're even eligible
  to bid, separate from being simply the lowest responsible number submitted.
whyItMatters: >
  These ordinances shift part of the contractor-qualification process to before
  bidding even opens, rather than leaving it entirely to post-bid
  responsiveness review, which means a contractor can be disqualified from
  bidding at all if it doesn't meet the listed criteria, regardless of what
  price it might have offered.
example: >
  A city's responsible bidder ordinance requires any contractor bidding on a
  public building project to operate a state-approved apprenticeship program
  and show no serious safety violations within the past three years. A
  low-price bidder lacking an approved apprenticeship program is found
  ineligible to bid at all, regardless of its proposed price.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-bid-coordinator|Bid Coordinator]] confirms a company meets a
  jurisdiction's responsible bidder criteria before submitting a bid package,
  and the [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] maintains the safety-record
  documentation these ordinances commonly require.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor submits a competitive bid without first checking a jurisdiction's
  responsible bidder prequalification criteria, and gets the bid rejected as
  ineligible regardless of price because a listed requirement, like an
  approved apprenticeship program, wasn't met.
