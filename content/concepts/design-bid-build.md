title: Design-Bid-Build
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The traditional project delivery method where an owner hires an architect to
  complete the design first, then bids the finished design out to general
  contractors, and finally hires the winning contractor to build it, three separate,
  sequential contracts instead of one combined one.
whyItMatters: >
  It's still the most common and most straightforward delivery method precisely
  because the design is fully complete before pricing starts, giving the owner price
  certainty from a bid. The tradeoff is a longer overall timeline than
  [[concept-design-build|design-build]] or [[concept-fast-tracking|fast-tracking]],
  since construction can't start until design is finished.
example: >
  A school district hires an architect to fully design a new building, then issues an
  [[concept-invitation-for-bid|invitation for bid]] to general contractors once the
  design is complete, selecting a contractor based on the resulting bids.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] prices a design-bid-build project against a
  complete, finished design, which makes for a more straightforward bid than pricing
  an unfinished design under [[concept-design-build|design-build]]. A
  [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] inherits a design they had no input into,
  which means RFIs uncovering design gaps are a more routine part of the job on this
  delivery method than on others.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A design-bid-build project's design turns out to have significant coordination
  errors between the architectural and structural drawings, discovered only after
  construction starts. Because the design was already complete and bid before the
  contractor had any input, the fixes have to go through a slower change order process
  instead of being caught and resolved during a design phase the contractor never
  participated in.
