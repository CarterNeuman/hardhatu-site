---
id: concept-functional-replacement-cost
title: Functional Replacement Cost
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A coverage basis that pays to repair or rebuild a damaged property using less
  expensive, code-compliant, but functionally equivalent materials and methods
  rather than replicating the original construction exactly, often used for
  older or ornate buildings.
whyItMatters: >
  This matters because a historic building with original materials like plaster
  or old-growth lumber can't always be rebuilt to its exact prior
  specification cost-effectively, and functional replacement cost coverage
  sets different expectations than standard replacement cost.
example: >
  An insurer settles a fire claim on a 1920s building by paying to rebuild the
  walls with modern drywall and dimensional lumber instead of the original
  lath-and-plaster and full-dimension timber, since that meets the functional
  replacement cost standard.
careerAngle: >
  Restoration contractors bidding historic-property jobs need to know which
  replacement cost basis applies before pricing out period-accurate materials
  the claim may not fund.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Property owners sometimes expect an exact like-for-like historical restoration
  under any replacement cost policy, not realizing functional replacement cost
  intentionally funds a modern equivalent rather than a true restoration.
