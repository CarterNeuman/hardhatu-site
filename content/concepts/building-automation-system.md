id: concept-building-automation-system
title: Building Automation System (BAS)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "25, Integrated Automation"
definition: >
  The centralized software and hardware platform that monitors and controls a
  building's mechanical, electrical, and life-safety systems, HVAC, lighting, and
  sometimes access control, from a single interface, built on top of the
  individual controllers described by
  [[concept-direct-digital-control|direct digital control]] but adding the
  building-wide dashboard, scheduling, and
  trend-logging a facilities team actually uses day to day.
whyItMatters: >
  A BAS is only as good as the points and sequences programmed into it, and an
  owner who inherits a poorly commissioned BAS often ends up running systems
  manually or fighting a dashboard that doesn't actually reflect what's installed.
example: >
  A hospital's BAS lets facilities staff view and adjust every air handler's
  setpoint, schedule after-hours setbacks across the whole building, and receive
  an alarm the moment a critical space's temperature drifts out of range, all from
  one screen.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] designs the sequences of operation the
  BAS executes. A
  [[career-construction-technology-manager|Construction Technology Manager]]
  or the owner's facilities staff manages the platform once it's turned
  over. A [[career-commissioning-agent|Commissioning Agent]] verifies the BAS
  actually controls equipment the way the sequences describe.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner takes over a newly commissioned BAS, but the trend logs and alarm
  thresholds were never tuned past the controls contractor's default settings.
  Facilities staff get flooded with hundreds of nuisance alarms in the first week,
  can't distinguish a real failure from noise, and end up disabling alarms
  altogether rather than sorting through them.
