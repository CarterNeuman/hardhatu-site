title: Thermal Imaging / Infrared Building Diagnostics
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Using an infrared camera to visualize surface temperature differences across
  a building's     envelope or systems, revealing heat loss, moisture
  intrusion, and missing or compressed     insulation that are invisible to
  the naked eye, distinct from     [[concept-reality-capture|reality capture]]
  laser scanning, which measures physical     geometry rather than
  temperature.
whyItMatters: >
  A wall can look perfectly finished on both the interior and exterior while
  still having a     gap in the insulation behind the drywall; a thermal scan
  reveals that defect directly as a     visible temperature anomaly, whereas a
  conventional visual inspection has no way to detect a     problem it can't
  actually see.
example: >
  An energy auditor scans a home's exterior walls with a thermal camera and
  identifies a     cold streak running vertically between two stud bays,
  revealing a section of missing wall     insulation that passed a standard
  visual inspection because the drywall and siding both     looked completely
  normal.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] uses thermal imaging as part
  of a broader     energy audit, often paired with a
  [[concept-envelope-air-tightness-testing|blower door     test]], since air
  leaks and missing insulation often show up together but aren't the same
  defect.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An inspector treats a thermal scan as conclusive on its own, when a thermal
  anomaly can     also result from something harmless like a stud or framing
  member behind the wall, not     necessarily a defect; a thermal finding
  usually needs a secondary check, like moisture     metering, to confirm
  what's actually causing it.
