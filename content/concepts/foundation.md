id: concept-foundation
title: Foundation
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  The structure, usually concrete, that transfers a building's weight into the ground
  beneath it. Everything above it depends on it being level, sized correctly, and
  built on soil that can actually support it.
whyItMatters: >
  A mistake in the foundation is one of the few construction mistakes that's nearly
  impossible to fix later without extraordinary cost. Foundations are inspected more
  heavily than almost any other part of a building for exactly this reason.
example: >
  Before a single wall goes up, a geotechnical engineer tests the soil, an engineer
  designs a foundation sized for that soil and the building's weight, and it gets
  inspected before it's ever covered up with a slab.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] does the actual placement and finishing,
  but it's the [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] who has to catch a problem
  before it's covered up; once a foundation is poured and backfilled, nobody can see
  it again, which makes that inspection window one of the least forgiving moments on
  the whole project.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew pours a foundation slightly out of level, and because it's covered with a
  slab within the same week, nobody notices until the framing crew starts hanging
  walls months later and everything is fighting to stay plumb. Fixing a foundation
  problem after the fact can mean underpinning or even partial demolition, costs
  that dwarf what it would have taken to catch the issue during the pre-pour
  inspection.
