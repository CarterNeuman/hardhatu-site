id: concept-foodservice-equipment
title: Foodservice Equipment
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "11, Equipment"
definition: >
  The commercial-grade cooking, refrigeration, and prep equipment, ranges,
  walk-in coolers, exhaust hoods, dish machines, installed in a restaurant,
  cafeteria, or institutional kitchen, requiring close coordination between the
  equipment's specific utility requirements, gas, water, drainage, dedicated
  electrical circuits, makeup air for the exhaust hood, and the building's
  rough-in.
whyItMatters: >
  Foodservice equipment often isn't finally selected until late in design, since
  the kitchen consultant is a specialty separate from the base architect, and a
  late equipment change can leave a utility rough-in that no longer matches what's
  actually going to be installed.
example: >
  A restaurant's kitchen consultant finalizes the exact make and model of a
  commercial range and exhaust hood, and the general contractor coordinates gas
  line sizing, makeup air, and hood fire suppression rough-in against that
  specific equipment's cut sheets before drywall closes the space in.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] designs the utility rough-in the
  equipment depends on. An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] and
  [[career-plumber|Plumber]] install the dedicated circuits and gas and drain
  connections.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A general contractor roughs in utilities based on an early foodservice
  equipment schedule, but the kitchen consultant later substitutes a range model
  with a larger gas demand. The rough-in gas line is undersized for the equipment
  that actually arrives, forcing rework before the final connection can be made.
