id: concept-leed
title: LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  The most widely used
  [[concept-green-building-certification|green building certification]] system in the
  U.S., run by the U.S. Green Building Council (USGBC) and
  its credentialing body, GBCI. A project earns points ("credits") across categories
  like energy, water, and materials, and the total determines the certification level
  (Certified, Silver, Gold, or Platinum).
whyItMatters: >
  Many owners, especially institutional and government ones, require or strongly prefer
  LEED certification on new projects, which makes it a real business requirement on a
  meaningful share of construction work, not just an optional environmental gesture.
  Understanding the credit system matters because a design decision made without it in
  mind can quietly cost a project a certification level it was targeting.
example: >
  A project targeting LEED Gold tracks its credits throughout design, and a late change
  to a specified material threatens to drop a materials credit the project was counting
  on, a gap the sustainability consultant catches and resolves with a substitute
  material before the design is finalized.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]] manages the
  entire credit-tracking and certification submission process from design through
  construction. An [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] often makes
  the initial call on which certification level to pursue, weighing the cost of chasing
  additional credits against the owner's actual goals for the building.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A design team assumes a project is on track for its targeted certification level
  without tracking credits formally throughout design. Late in construction, a credit
  review reveals several assumed credits were never actually documented, and the project
  falls short of its target level with no time left to recover the gap.
