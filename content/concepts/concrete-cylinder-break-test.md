id: concept-concrete-cylinder-break-test
title: Concrete Cylinder Break Test
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  A quality control test where cylindrical concrete samples are cast from a
  pour, cured alongside it, and then crushed in a lab press at set intervals,
  typically seven and twenty-eight days, to verify the concrete actually
  reached its specified compressive strength.
whyItMatters: >
  Concrete doesn't reach its full design strength the moment it's poured, it
  gains strength over weeks as it cures, which is why the test happens at two
  checkpoints: the seven-day break gives an early read on whether the mix is
  on track, and the twenty-eight day break is the one that actually confirms
  the concrete met its specified design strength.
example: >
  A testing lab casts six cylinders from the same batch of concrete used in a
  foundation pour, breaks two at seven days to get an early strength
  indication, and breaks the rest at twenty-eight days to confirm the pour
  meets its specified design strength before the project relies on that
  foundation structurally.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] oversees cylinder sampling and
  tracks the lab results against each pour's specified strength requirement.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A pour's twenty-eight day cylinder break comes back below the specified
  strength, but by that point the concrete has already been in place for a
  month and other work has already built on top of it. The low result forces
  an expensive structural evaluation to determine whether the pour is still
  acceptable.
