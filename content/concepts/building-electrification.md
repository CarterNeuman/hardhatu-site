id: concept-building-electrification
title: Building Electrification (Heat Pump Transition)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Replacing a building's fossil-fuel-burning equipment, primarily gas
  furnaces, boilers,     and water heaters, with electric heat pump equipment
  that can both heat and cool using the     same system, a core strategy for
  reducing a building's operational carbon footprint,     though the federal
  Section 25C tax credit that had subsidized residential heat pump
  purchases expired for installations after December 31, 2025.
whyItMatters: >
  A gas furnace can only ever be as clean as the fuel burning inside it, but a
  heat pump's     emissions depend entirely on how clean the electricity grid
  supplying it is, which means     electrification's carbon benefit grows
  automatically as the regional grid itself gets     cleaner over time,
  something a gas system can never do.
example: >
  A building owner replacing an aging gas furnace chooses an electric heat
  pump system     instead of a like-for-like gas replacement, accepting a
  higher upfront equipment cost with no     federal tax credit available for a
  2026 installation, but anticipating lower long-term     operating costs as
  the regional grid's electricity generation mix continues shifting toward
  renewables.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] sizing a heat pump replacement for a
  cold-climate     building has to verify the specific equipment's rated
  performance at the region's actual     design temperature, since not every
  heat pump maintains its full heating capacity in very     cold outdoor
  conditions.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A homeowner or contractor budgets a 2026 heat pump installation assuming the
  federal     25C tax credit still applies, when that credit expired for any
  equipment placed in service     after December 31, 2025, leaving only state
  and utility rebate programs, which vary     significantly by location, as
  the remaining incentive pathways.
