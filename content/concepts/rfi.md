---
id: concept-rfi
title: RFI (Request for Information)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A formal question sent from the field or a subcontractor to the design team when
  the drawings or specs are unclear, missing, or contradictory.
whyItMatters: >
  Building from a guess is expensive and sometimes dangerous. An RFI creates a paper
  trail showing who asked what, when, and what the official answer was, which matters
  later if there's a dispute about who's responsible for a mistake.
example: >
  A framing crew notices the drawings show a wall in two different places on two
  different pages. Instead of picking one, the superintendent files an RFI, and the
  architect issues an official answer within a few days.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] usually owns the RFI log directly;
  tracking dozens of open questions and their response deadlines is exactly the kind
  of detail-heavy responsibility new engineers get handed early in their career. A
  [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] cares from the other direction: every open
  RFI can mean a crew standing around waiting on an answer before they can keep working.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framing crew hits a discrepancy between two drawings and, rather than waiting on
  an official answer, the foreman just picks the interpretation that seems obvious and
  keeps building. Weeks later, the architect's actual intent turns out to be the other
  option, and the finished wall has to be torn out and rebuilt, a cost and delay that
  a two-day RFI turnaround would have avoided entirely.
