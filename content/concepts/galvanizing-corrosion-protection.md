id: concept-galvanizing-corrosion-protection
title: Galvanizing & Corrosion Protection
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  A zinc coating applied to steel, most commonly by hot-dip galvanizing,
  dipping fabricated steel into molten zinc, to protect it from corrosion, used
  on steel that will be exposed to weather or moisture, such as exterior
  stairs, guardrails, and structural steel in coastal or industrial
  environments.
whyItMatters: >
  Galvanizing has to happen after fabrication, cutting, welding, or drilling a
  galvanized piece afterward breaks the coating and exposes bare steel at
  exactly that spot, so the fabrication sequence has to treat galvanizing as
  one of the last steps rather than something done to raw stock before
  fabrication.
example: >
  A fabricator completes all welding, cutting, and drilling on a set of
  exterior stair stringers before sending them out for hot-dip galvanizing, so
  the entire finished piece, including every cut edge and weld, gets fully
  coated.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] or specialty installer sets the
  galvanized steel in the field, field-touching up any coating damaged during
  installation with a zinc-rich repair compound.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An installer field-drills a bolt hole into an already-galvanized guardrail
  post to fit an unexpected field condition, exposing bare steel at the hole
  with no zinc coating protecting it. Rust begins forming at that exact spot
  within the first year, well before the rest of the galvanized piece shows any
  corrosion.
