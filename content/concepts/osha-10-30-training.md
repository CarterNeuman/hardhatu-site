title: OSHA 10/30-Hour Training
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's Outreach Training Program, delivering a 10-hour safety awareness course
  for entry-level construction workers or a 30-hour course for supervisors, through
  authorized trainers. The program itself is voluntary at the federal level, but
  many states, municipalities, and general contractors' own site-access policies
  require the card in practice.
whyItMatters: >
  Because the card isn't universally mandated by federal law, whether it's actually
  required depends entirely on the state, the municipality, and the specific
  general contractor's own site-access rules, which is exactly why a worker can't
  assume it's optional just because federal OSHA itself doesn't require it
  everywhere.
example: >
  A new construction laborer completes a 10-hour OSHA outreach course before their
  first day on a jobsite, not because federal law required it everywhere, but
  because the general contractor's own site-access policy requires the card of
  every worker regardless of what state law does or doesn't mandate.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] tracks which workers hold current OSHA
  10 or 30 cards and enforces whatever combination of state law and company
  site-access policy applies to a given jobsite.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A new hire shows up to a jobsite without an OSHA 10 card, assuming it wasn't
  legally required, without realizing the general contractor's own site-access
  policy requires it regardless of state law. They're turned away at the gate on
  their first day.
