id: concept-concrete-mix-design
title: Concrete Mix Design
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  The specific recipe, cement, water, sand, aggregate, and any admixtures, and
  their exact proportions, that an engineer specifies for a particular pour to
  hit a required strength and performance, rather than concrete being a single
  one-size-fits-all material.
whyItMatters: >
  Different parts of a building need concrete with very different properties:
  a foundation might need high strength, while a decorative slab might
  prioritize a specific finish or reduced cracking. The mix design is what
  translates those requirements into an actual, batchable recipe a concrete
  plant can produce.
example: >
  A structural engineer specifies a mix design calling for a higher cement
  content and a specific admixture to achieve a required compressive strength
  for a foundation, while a separate mix design with different proportions is
  specified for an interior slab that doesn't carry the same structural load.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] specifies the mix
  design a pour must meet, while a [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]]
  places and finishes concrete batched to that design.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A concrete supplier substitutes a different mix design than the one
  specified, assuming the difference is minor. The substituted mix doesn't
  reach the required strength, and the error isn't caught until cylinder break
  testing weeks after the pour, by which point the concrete is already in
  place.
