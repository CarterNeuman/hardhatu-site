id: concept-hepa-filtration-in-restoration
title: HEPA Filtration in Restoration
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The use of High-Efficiency Particulate Air filters, which capture at least
  99.97 percent of particles 0.3 microns in size, in vacuums and air scrubbers
  used throughout mold, fire, and biohazard restoration work to remove fine
  particulates from the air and surfaces.
whyItMatters: >
  A standard shop vacuum or box fan can actually make a contamination problem
  worse by blowing fine particles back into the air, which is why HEPA-rated
  equipment is specified in most restoration protocols for mold, soot, and
  biohazard work.
example: >
  A fire-restoration crew uses a HEPA-filtered air scrubber to run negative air
  during soot cleanup and a HEPA vacuum for surface cleaning, rather than a
  standard vacuum that would exhaust fine soot particles right back into the
  room.
careerAngle: >
  Restoration technicians should be able to confirm their equipment is genuinely
  HEPA-rated, since using non-HEPA equipment on a job that calls for it can
  fail a clearance test and void the documented protocol compliance.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Marketing terms like 'HEPA-type' or 'HEPA-like' on consumer equipment don't
  meet the actual HEPA filtration standard, and using such equipment on a
  documented remediation job can undermine the protocol.
