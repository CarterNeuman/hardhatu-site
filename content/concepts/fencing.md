id: concept-fencing
title: Fencing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "32, Exterior Improvements"
definition: >
  Permanent perimeter fencing installed as part of a finished site, for security,
  screening, or code-required enclosure of features like pools and utility yards,
  distinct from the temporary construction fencing that goes up around a jobsite
  during construction itself.
whyItMatters: >
  Confusing permanent and temporary fencing scope is a common and costly mistake on a
  bid or contract: temporary construction fencing is usually a general conditions
  cost the contractor carries throughout the project, while permanent fencing is a
  finish item specified and installed near the end, and treating one as covering the
  other leaves a gap someone has to pay for after the fact.
example: >
  A project's contract documents clearly separate the temporary construction fencing
  the general contractor maintains throughout the project from the permanent
  perimeter fencing specified as a finish item near the end, so there's no ambiguity
  about which budget and schedule line each falls under.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-laborer|Construction Laborer]] or specialty fencing subcontractor
  installs permanent fencing to the site plan's layout and the manufacturer's
  installation requirements. A [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]]
  inherits permanent fencing as long-term site security infrastructure requiring
  periodic maintenance and repair.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project's bid documents describe "site fencing" without clearly distinguishing
  temporary from permanent scope, and the general contractor assumes the permanent
  perimeter fencing is included in a subcontractor's temporary fencing scope. The gap
  isn't discovered until near project closeout, when neither party has budgeted for
  the permanent fencing the owner was expecting.
