---
id: concept-appraisal-award
title: Appraisal Award
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The final, usually binding dollar amount reached through a policy's appraisal
  process, issued jointly by the policyholder's and insurer's appraisers, or
  by the umpire if the two appraisers disagree, settling a dispute over the
  amount of a covered loss.
whyItMatters: >
  Once issued, an appraisal award typically ends the dispute over how much a
  covered loss is worth, so contractors scoping repair work tied to a disputed
  claim should wait for this figure before finalizing a scope that assumes a
  different number.
example: >
  After a roofing contractor's scope and the insurer's adjuster disagree sharply
  on storm-damage repair costs, the dispute goes to appraisal, and the
  resulting appraisal award sets the final payout figure that both sides are
  bound to.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors involved in an appraisal dispute should understand the award
  typically resolves the dollar amount only, not whether the loss is covered
  at all, which can remain a separate dispute.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes expect an appraisal award to also resolve coverage questions,
  like whether a particular cause of damage is covered, when appraisal under
  most policies is limited strictly to valuing the amount of a loss already
  agreed to be covered.
