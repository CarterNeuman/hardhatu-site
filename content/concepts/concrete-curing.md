id: concept-concrete-curing
title: Concrete Curing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  The process of keeping freshly poured concrete at the right moisture and temperature
  for a period after placement so it can fully hydrate and reach its designed strength,
  not the same as "drying," since concrete that dries out too fast before it fully
  hydrates can end up significantly weaker than designed.
whyItMatters: >
  Concrete strength develops over weeks, not hours, and inadequate curing (too little
  moisture, too much heat or cold too soon) can permanently reduce the concrete's final
  strength even though it looks fully hardened on the surface, a defect that isn't
  visible until the concrete is tested or, worse, until it fails to perform under load.
example: >
  On a hot, windy day, a concrete crew covers a freshly poured slab with wet burlap and
  plastic sheeting to slow moisture loss, rather than letting the surface dry out and
  crack before the concrete has had time to properly cure.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] manages curing conditions immediately
  after a pour, since the first few days determine much of the concrete's final
  strength. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] oversees
  concrete strength testing, breaking test cylinders taken during the pour, to
  confirm the cured concrete actually meets its specified strength.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew pours concrete in cold weather without protecting it from freezing during the
  critical early curing period. The water inside the fresh concrete freezes before it
  can hydrate properly, permanently damaging the concrete's internal structure in a way
  that isn't obvious until strength tests come back low weeks later.
