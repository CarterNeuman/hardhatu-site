---
id: concept-vacancy-clause
title: Vacancy Clause (Insurance)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A policy provision that reduces or suspends certain coverages, especially for
  vandalism, theft, and water damage, once a property has sat vacant beyond a
  specified period, commonly 30 to 60 days.
whyItMatters: >
  Contractors doing renovation work on properties between tenants or owners need
  to know whether a vacancy clause has already kicked in, because a loss that
  happens during an uncovered vacancy period can leave no insurance money to
  pay for the repair at all.
example: >
  A flip investor's gutted, unoccupied house sits vacant for 75 days during a
  renovation, and when a pipe bursts the insurer denies water-damage coverage
  because the vacancy clause's 60-day threshold had already passed.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors and investors doing vacant-property renovations should confirm a
  vacant-home or builder's risk policy is in place rather than relying on a
  standard homeowner's policy that assumes occupancy.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes think boarding up windows or leaving utilities on proves a
  home isn't vacant for insurance purposes, when most vacancy clauses turn on
  absence of furnishings and occupants, not utility status.
