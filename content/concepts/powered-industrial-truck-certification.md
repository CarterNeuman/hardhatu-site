title: Powered Industrial Truck (Forklift) Certification
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's requirement that any employee operating a forklift or other powered
  industrial truck complete formal training and a hands-on evaluation specific
  to the type of truck and the conditions it will be operated in, refreshed if
  the operator is seen operating unsafely or is involved in an accident.
whyItMatters: >
  A forklift operator certified on one type of truck isn't automatically
  certified on another, and conditions matter too, so an operator trained only
  on a warehouse sit-down forklift isn't automatically qualified to run a
  rough-terrain forklift on an active jobsite.
example: >
  A worker certified to operate a sit-down counterbalance forklift in a
  warehouse gets additional training and a hands-on evaluation before being
  assigned to operate a rough-terrain forklift on an outdoor jobsite, since
  the equipment type and site conditions both differ from the original
  certification.
careerAngle: >
  Site superintendents should check that a forklift operator's certification
  actually covers both the specific truck type and the site conditions being
  worked in, not just that the operator holds a certification card of some
  kind.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Companies sometimes treat forklift certification as a one-time, lifetime
  credential, when OSHA requires refresher training after a near-miss or
  accident, a change in equipment type, or observed unsafe operation,
  regardless of how long ago the original training happened.
