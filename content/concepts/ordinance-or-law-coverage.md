id: concept-ordinance-or-law-coverage
title: Ordinance or Law Coverage
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  An insurance endorsement that covers the additional cost of bringing a damaged
  building up to current building code requirements during a covered repair, a gap
  a standard policy leaves open, since it otherwise only pays to restore a building
  to its original, possibly now-outdated condition.
whyItMatters: >
  A building damaged badly enough to require rebuilding a significant portion of it
  often has to meet current code in that rebuilt area, even if the rest of the
  building was legally built to an older standard; without ordinance or law
  coverage, that code-driven cost gap falls entirely on the property owner.
example: >
  A fire severely damages one floor of an older building, and current code requires
  the rebuilt floor to include upgraded fire-rated construction the original floor
  never had. The property's ordinance or law coverage pays for that additional
  code-required cost, which a standard policy wouldn't have covered on its own.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] identifies when code-driven
  upgrades trigger this specific coverage rather than treating them as a standard
  repair cost. A [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]]
  coordinates with the local building department to determine exactly which
  upgrades are actually code-required versus optional.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A property owner's building sustains fire damage requiring code-mandated
  electrical upgrades during reconstruction, but their policy has no ordinance or
  law coverage. The insurer pays only to restore the original electrical
  configuration, leaving the property owner to cover the entire cost of the
  code-required upgrade out of pocket.
