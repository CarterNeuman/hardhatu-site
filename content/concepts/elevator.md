id: concept-elevator
title: Elevator
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "14, Conveying Equipment"
definition: >
  A mechanized car system that moves people or freight vertically between floors,
  installed by a specialized, licensed elevator contractor working under its own
  dedicated code, in most states based on the ASME A17.1 Safety Code for Elevators
  and Escalators, separate from the general building code.
whyItMatters: >
  Elevator installation and inspection follow their own regulatory track, often with
  a state or local elevator inspector separate from the general building inspector,
  and the elevator shaft has to be built to precise structural and dimensional
  tolerances well before the elevator contractor's equipment even arrives, since a
  shaft built out of tolerance can be extremely costly to correct after the fact.
example: >
  A general contractor builds an elevator shaft to the precise dimensions and
  tolerances the elevator manufacturer requires, coordinating the schedule so the
  specialized elevator contractor can begin installing rails, cab, and equipment as
  soon as the shaft is structurally complete.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] coordinates the elevator
  contractor's long lead time and shaft tolerance requirements well in advance, since
  elevators are typically ordered and engineered months before installation begins. A
  [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] manages the elevator's required
  ongoing inspections and maintenance long after construction, since elevators are
  subject to recurring code-mandated testing throughout a building's life.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A general contractor builds an elevator shaft slightly out of the tolerance the
  elevator manufacturer specified, not realizing the shaft's plumbness had to be more
  precise than typical structural tolerances. The elevator contractor can't install
  the guide rails correctly until the shaft is corrected, creating a costly,
  schedule-damaging delay right before the project's planned completion.
