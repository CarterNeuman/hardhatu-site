title: Windows
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  Manufactured window units set into rough openings in a building's exterior wall,
  sized and specified for the project's structural, thermal, and code requirements,
  including the flashing and sealant details around each unit that keep water from
  getting into the wall through the opening.
whyItMatters: >
  A window's own manufactured quality matters far less to long-term performance than
  how well it's flashed and sealed into the rough opening: most window-related water
  intrusion problems trace back to the installation detail, not a defective window
  unit.
example: >
  A window installer sets a window into a rough opening prepared with flashing at the
  sill first, then the window itself, then flashing at the jambs and head, following
  the same shingle-lap sequence that keeps water shedding outward at every layer.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] sets windows into prepared rough openings and
  installs the flashing around them, a task where the sequence matters as much as the
  window itself. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]]
  inspects window flashing details before siding or other finishes cover them, since
  it's one of the last chances to catch a sequencing error.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew sets a batch of windows without properly flashing the sill first, assuming
  the window's own integrated flashing flange is sufficient on its own. Water that
  gets past the window during a storm has no flashing underneath to redirect it back
  out, and it leaks into the wall cavity below the window, discovered only once
  damage shows up on the interior finish.
