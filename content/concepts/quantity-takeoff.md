---
id: concept-quantity-takeoff
title: Quantity Takeoff
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The process of measuring and counting exact quantities of materials, such as cubic
  yards of concrete, linear feet of wall, or number of doors, directly off the
  architectural and engineering drawings, forming the foundation every cost estimate is
  built on.
whyItMatters: >
  A bid is only as accurate as the quantities it's priced against. Get the takeoff
  wrong, say by miscounting linear feet of drywall or missing a wall entirely, and
  every downstream number in the estimate is wrong too, no matter how accurate the unit
  pricing is.
example: >
  An estimator uses takeoff software to measure every wall on a floor plan, generating
  an exact linear footage of drywall partition, which then gets multiplied by a
  per-linear-foot cost to build that portion of the estimate.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] performs the quantity takeoff as one of the first
  and most fundamental steps of building a bid, often the single most time-consuming
  part of estimating a large project. A [[career-senior-estimator|Senior Estimator]]
  spot-checks takeoffs on major cost items before a bid goes out, since a takeoff error
  on a big-ticket item like concrete can be expensive to catch after the fact.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An estimator's takeoff software misreads a drawing revision and counts an entire wing
  of a building's floor area only once instead of twice, since the plans showed it as a
  mirrored duplicate. The resulting bid is missing roughly half the actual square
  footage of work, and the error isn't caught until well after the contract is signed.
