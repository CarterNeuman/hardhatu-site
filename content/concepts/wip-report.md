id: concept-wip-report
title: Work-in-Progress (WIP) Report
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A monthly schedule, one line per active job, comparing how much of a
  contract has actually been earned based on progress against how much has
  actually been billed, surfacing every project as either over-billed (billed
  ahead of progress) or under-billed (billed behind progress).
whyItMatters: >
  A contractor can look profitable on a single invoice and still be
  quietly losing money, or look behind on billing and still be in fine shape,
  depending on how its billing compares to its real progress. The WIP report
  is the one document that catches this directly, which is exactly why a
  surety or bank reviewing a contractor's financial health asks for it by name.
example: >
  A $1,000,000 contract is 60 percent complete, meaning $600,000 of revenue has
  been earned, but the contractor has only billed $500,000 so far. The WIP
  report flags this job as under-billed by $100,000, a real asset the
  contractor hasn't collected yet, not a problem, but something worth billing
  for soon.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-construction-accountant|Construction Accountant]] prepares the WIP
  report every month across all active jobs, and a
  [[career-controller-cfo-construction|Controller/CFO]] reviews it company-wide
  for anomalies, since a job that's badly over-billed can mean the company is
  about to run out of cash on it once the remaining work doesn't generate
  enough new billing to cover costs.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project manager keeps billing ahead of actual progress to improve cash
  flow, assuming the work will eventually catch up. It doesn't, and by the time
  the WIP report flags how far over-billed the job has become, there's no
  remaining contract value left to bill against the work still unfinished,
  turning what looked like healthy cash flow into a real loss on paper.
