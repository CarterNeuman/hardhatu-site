id: concept-building-permit
title: Building Permit
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A government authorization required before most construction work can legally begin,
  confirming submitted plans comply with applicable
  [[concept-building-code|building code]] and zoning requirements, issued after a
  [[concept-plan-review|plan review]] and followed, at completion, by the
  [[concept-certificate-of-occupancy|certificate of occupancy]] that confirms the
  finished work matches what was actually permitted.
whyItMatters: >
  Starting work without the required permit isn't just a paperwork gap: it exposes an
  owner and contractor to a [[concept-stop-work-order|stop work order]], fines, and
  potentially having to undo completed work that a building department never had the
  chance to review in progress.
example: >
  A contractor begins a structural renovation before the permit revision covering that
  specific scope is approved, and a building inspector issues a stop work order on the
  affected portion of the site until the missing permit documentation is submitted and
  approved.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] tracks permit status closely because so
  much of the schedule depends on it: work often legally can't start, or can't be
  inspected, until the right permit is in hand. An
  [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] often manages the permitting
  process directly on the owner's behalf, especially on projects where permitting
  timelines are a major schedule risk.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project manager assumes a scope change is minor enough not to need a permit revision
  and proceeds with the work anyway. A routine inspection catches the unpermitted change,
  and the resulting stop work order costs far more time than simply submitting the
  revision up front would have.
