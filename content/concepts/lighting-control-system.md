id: concept-lighting-control-system
title: Lighting Control System
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  The switches, occupancy sensors, daylight sensors, and central control
  panels that automatically turn lighting on, off, or dim it based on
  occupancy, time of day, or available daylight, increasingly required by
  energy code rather than simply offered as a convenience feature in most
  commercial buildings.
whyItMatters: >
  Energy codes now require automatic lighting controls, such as occupancy
  sensors in most spaces, in most commercial construction, making a properly
  commissioned lighting control system a code compliance issue, not an
  optional add-on the owner can skip to save budget.
example: >
  An office building's lighting control system uses occupancy sensors in
  individual offices and conference rooms combined with daylight sensors
  along the building's perimeter, automatically dimming perimeter lighting
  when enough natural daylight is already present.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] installs the lighting control
  system's wiring and devices. A
  [[career-commissioning-agent|Commissioning Agent]] verifies the sensors and
  programmed sequences actually function as designed before turnover.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A lighting control system is installed and wired correctly, but the
  occupancy sensors are never actually programmed with the correct time
  delays before the building's energy code inspection. Several offices go
  dark mid-meeting because the sensor's default delay is too short for the
  actual occupancy pattern, and the programming has to be revisited after
  complaints from occupants.
relatedIds:
