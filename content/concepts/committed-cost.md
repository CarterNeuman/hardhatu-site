---
id: concept-committed-cost
title: Committed Cost
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A cost that has become legally obligated, through a signed subcontract,
  purchase order, or executed change order, whether or not the associated work
  has actually happened yet, distinct from an [[concept-actual-cost|actual cost]],
  which reflects only what has genuinely been spent or invoiced so far.
whyItMatters: >
  A budget can look healthy by committed cost alone while hiding real risk,
  since committed cost only shows what's been legally locked in, not whether
  the work underlying it will actually come in on, under, or over that
  committed number. Tracking committed cost separately from actual cost is
  what lets a project team catch a budget problem while there's still time to
  react to it.
example: >
  A general contractor signs a $400,000 drywall subcontract in March,
  committing that cost immediately, even though the drywall crew won't
  actually start work, and begin generating actual cost, until June.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-cost-engineer|Cost Engineer]] tracks committed cost the moment a
  subcontract or purchase order is executed, so the project's true financial
  exposure is visible well before any of that work is actually performed. A
  [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] is often the one
  whose subcontract execution is what actually creates a committed cost in the
  first place.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team tracks only actual cost, spent-to-date, and assumes the
  budget is fine because spending looks on pace. They miss that several large
  subcontracts have already been signed for amounts well above what was
  budgeted, committed cost nobody's watching, and the overage only becomes
  visible once that work actually starts and the invoices begin arriving.
