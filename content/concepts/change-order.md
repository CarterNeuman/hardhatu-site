id: concept-change-order
title: Change Order
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A formal, written change to the original contract, adjusting the scope of work,
  the price, the schedule, or all three, that both the owner and contractor agree to.
whyItMatters: >
  Nothing changes on a project's price or timeline without one. A change order is what
  protects a contractor from doing extra work for free, and protects an owner from
  paying for something they didn't actually approve.
example: >
  An owner decides mid-project they want upgraded countertops instead of the ones in
  the original plans. The contractor prices the difference, and both sides sign a
  change order before the new countertops get ordered.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] lives inside this process day to day:
  chasing signatures, pricing changes, and keeping the schedule honest is one of the
  most constant parts of the job. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] cares
  just as much from the field side, since they're often the one deciding whether work
  can actually start before the paperwork has caught up.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A subcontractor gets a verbal "yes, go ahead" on a $12,000 scope change and starts
  work immediately. Three weeks later the owner disputes the number, insisting they
  remember a lower figure, and nobody can find anything in writing to settle it. The
  subcontractor either eats the difference or spends weeks fighting for money that a
  signature up front would have secured in thirty seconds.
