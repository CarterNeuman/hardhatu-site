title: Green Building Certification
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Third-party verification that a building meets a defined standard for environmental
  performance, health, or energy efficiency. [[concept-leed|LEED]] is the best-known
  system in the U.S., but WELL (occupant health), Energy Star (energy performance), and
  Passive House (extreme energy efficiency through building envelope design) each certify
  a different, more specific set of goals.
whyItMatters: >
  These systems aren't interchangeable versions of the same thing; a project pursuing
  WELL certification for occupant wellness is optimizing for meaningfully different
  design choices than one pursuing Passive House's strict energy targets, even though
  both fall under "green building certification." Picking the right system for the
  owner's actual goals matters more than picking the most well-known one.
example: >
  An owner initially assumes their new office should pursue LEED by default, but after
  discussing their actual priority, employee wellness and air quality, their
  sustainability consultant recommends targeting WELL certification instead, since it's
  built specifically around those goals.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]] helps an
  owner choose the certification system that actually matches their goals, not just the
  most recognizable one. An [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]]
  weighs that recommendation against the project's budget and timeline before committing
  to a specific certification target.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project commits to a specific certification system early based on its market
  recognition rather than what the owner actually wants the building to achieve. Partway
  through design, the owner realizes the system being pursued doesn't actually address
  their real priority, forcing a costly mid-design pivot to a different certification
  path.
