---
id: concept-spearin-doctrine
title: Spearin Doctrine / Implied Warranty of Plans
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A foundational legal doctrine, from a 1918 U.S. Supreme Court case, holding
  that when an owner provides a contractor with design plans and specifications,
  the owner impliedly warrants those plans are adequate, and the contractor isn't
  liable for defects that result from following them exactly as designed.
whyItMatters: >
  The doctrine shifts real financial liability away from whoever built the
  design and onto whoever designed it, so identifying whether a defect came from
  faulty design or faulty construction isn't just a technical question, it decides
  who actually pays.
example: >
  A retaining wall fails after being built exactly to the structural engineer's
  specified dimensions and reinforcement, and the Spearin doctrine places
  responsibility on the design professional whose plans were inadequate, not on
  the contractor who built precisely what was designed.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] relies on the Spearin
  doctrine constantly when a contractor is blamed for a defect that traces back
  to the plans it was given rather than to its own workmanship.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor swaps in a slightly different fastener spacing than the plans
  specify, assuming the substitution is close enough not to matter, and the
  resulting failure traces back to that deviation rather than to the original
  design. Because the contractor didn't actually follow the plans as designed,
  the Spearin doctrine's protection doesn't apply, and the contractor bears the
  liability it assumed the design would have covered.
