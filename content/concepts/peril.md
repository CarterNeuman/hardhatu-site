---
id: concept-peril
title: Peril
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The specific cause of a loss that a policy either covers or excludes, such as fire,
  wind, hail, water, or theft, the foundational concept an insurance policy is actually built
  around, since coverage never applies to "damage" in the abstract, only to damage
  caused by a peril the policy names as covered.
whyItMatters: >
  Two properties can look identically damaged and be handled completely differently
  by insurance depending on the peril that caused it; flood damage and wind-driven
  rain damage, for instance, are often covered under entirely separate policies,
  which is why determining the actual peril is usually the very first step in any
  claim.
example: >
  An adjuster inspecting a water-stained ceiling determines the water entered
  through wind-damaged roof shingles rather than rising floodwater, since the two
  causes are covered under completely different types of policies with different
  claim processes.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] identifies which peril
  actually caused the damage before evaluating anything else about the claim, since
  it determines which policy, if any, applies. A [[career-public-adjuster|Public Adjuster]] argues for the covered peril when the actual cause is genuinely
  ambiguous, since insurers and policyholders don't always agree on it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An adjuster initially attributes roof damage to ordinary wear and tear, an
  excluded cause, without fully investigating whether a specific recent wind event
  actually caused it instead. A public adjuster brings in additional evidence, a
  documented storm date and matching damage pattern, that shifts the determination
  to a covered peril, reversing the initial denial.
