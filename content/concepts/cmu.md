---
id: concept-cmu
title: CMU (Concrete Masonry Unit)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "04, Masonry"
definition: >
  Standardized, precast concrete blocks, commonly called "concrete block" or "cinder
  block", laid in a mortar-bonded pattern to build walls, both structural
  (load-bearing) and non-structural (partition or veneer backup).
whyItMatters: >
  CMU walls can be built either as the actual structural system of a building or
  purely as a backup wall behind a different exterior finish, and knowing which role a
  given CMU wall plays changes everything about how it's reinforced, inspected, and
  detailed.
example: >
  A single-story retail building uses reinforced CMU walls as its actual structural
  system, with vertical steel reinforcing bars grouted solid inside certain block
  cells according to the structural engineer's spacing requirements.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mason|Mason]] lays CMU to precise coursing and plumb tolerances, since a
  wall that's out of plumb becomes increasingly difficult to correct as it rises. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] verifies reinforcing
  steel placement and grout consolidation in structural CMU walls, since the
  reinforcing, like rebar in concrete, is hidden once the wall is finished.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A mason crew builds a load-bearing CMU wall without grouting the reinforced cells as
  frequently as the structural drawings specify, trying to save material cost. The
  wall passes a casual visual inspection since grouted and ungrouted cells look
  identical from the outside, but the wall doesn't have the load capacity the engineer
  designed for until the error is caught by a more careful special inspection.
