title: Ceiling Tile (ACT)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  Acoustic Ceiling Tile: lightweight, sound-absorbing panels set into a suspended
  grid system hung below a building's structural ceiling, and the standard finished
  ceiling in most commercial office and institutional spaces, chosen for easy access
  to the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems hidden above it.
whyItMatters: >
  Because everything above an ACT ceiling, such as ductwork, conduit, sprinkler piping,
  cabling, has to be roughed in and inspected before the ceiling grid closes over it,
  the ceiling installation sequence is one of the clearer markers of a project
  reaching substantial completion of its overhead systems.
example: >
  Before installing ceiling tile, the contractor confirms that mechanical,
  electrical, and fire sprinkler rough-in above the ceiling line have all passed
  inspection, since access to that space becomes far more difficult once the finished
  ceiling grid and tiles are in place.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] or specialty ceiling installer sets the suspended
  grid and tiles, coordinating around light fixtures, diffusers, and sprinkler heads
  that penetrate the ceiling plane. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] verifies overhead rough-in work is complete and inspected before
  authorizing the ceiling grid to close over it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A ceiling crew installs the grid and tiles before an electrical inspection above
  the ceiling line is actually complete, assuming it would pass and wanting to stay
  ahead of schedule. The inspection later reveals a deficiency that requires
  reopening a section of the finished ceiling to correct, costing more time than
  simply waiting for the inspection to clear first would have.
