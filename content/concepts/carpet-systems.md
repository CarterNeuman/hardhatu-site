id: concept-carpet-systems
title: Carpet Systems
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  Carpet installed either as broadloom, large rolls seamed together on site,
  or as modular carpet tile, individual squares that can be replaced one at a
  time, each suited to different traffic patterns and maintenance approaches,
  and each installed with a specific method, direct glue-down or stretched
  over padding, that affects durability and replaceability.
whyItMatters: >
  Carpet tile's big advantage over broadloom is that a single damaged or
  stained tile can be pulled and replaced without disturbing the surrounding
  floor, which is exactly why it's become the standard choice for offices,
  where staining and heavy point-traffic wear happen in localized, predictable
  spots.
example: >
  An office tenant specifies carpet tile with a random, non-directional
  pattern specifically so replacement tiles installed later, after a spill or
  heavy wear in one spot, blend in without a visible mismatch or the pattern
  lining up wrong.
careerAngle: >
  A specialty flooring installer lays out and installs the carpet system,
  coordinating pattern direction and seam placement. A
  [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] keeps a small stock of
  spare tiles on hand from the original installation for future spot
  replacement.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A facilities team needs to replace a stained carpet tile two years after
  installation but discovers no spare tiles were ordered or stored from the
  original run. The manufacturer has since changed dye lots, and the
  replacement tile visibly doesn't match the surrounding carpet's color.
