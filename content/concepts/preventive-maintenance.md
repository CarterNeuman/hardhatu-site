id: concept-preventive-maintenance
title: Preventive Maintenance
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Scheduled inspection, servicing, and part replacement performed on a
  fixed interval, by calendar time or by actual usage (engine hours, cycles),
  before equipment or a building system actually fails, rather than repairing
  it only after it breaks.
whyItMatters: >
  A failure that happens on a predictable maintenance schedule is cheap and
  planned; the same failure happening unexpectedly, in the middle of a job or
  in the middle of a building's operating hours, is expensive, disruptive, and
  sometimes dangerous. Preventive maintenance trades a small, scheduled cost
  now for avoiding a much larger, unscheduled one later.
example: >
  A crane's manufacturer specifies an inspection and service interval every 250
  operating hours. An owner that follows this schedule catches worn components
  during a planned service window; an owner that skips it risks the same
  component failing mid-lift, with far higher cost and real safety
  consequences.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-fleet-manager|Equipment/Fleet Manager]] schedules
  preventive maintenance across a company's fleet using actual engine-hour
  data rather than guessing, catching wear before it becomes a breakdown on an
  active jobsite. A [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] runs the
  same discipline for a building's HVAC, electrical, and life-safety systems
  once it's occupied, since a building's preventive maintenance schedule is
  what keeps it running smoothly for the decades after construction ends.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A company defers a piece of equipment's scheduled service to avoid pulling it
  off a busy jobsite, planning to catch up later. The deferred wear compounds,
  and the equipment fails during a critical phase of a different project, costing
  far more in lost schedule and emergency repair than the original service
  would have, the exact outcome the schedule existed to prevent.
