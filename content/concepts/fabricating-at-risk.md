id: concept-fabricating-at-risk
title: Fabricating at Risk
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The practice of a fabricator starting to build custom components before
  formal submittal approval is complete, done to save schedule time, which
  means the fabricator risks having to redo the work if the submittal comes
  back with required changes.
whyItMatters: >
  Custom fabricated items, millwork, structural steel, specialty glazing,
  often have long lead times that can threaten a project's schedule if the
  fabricator waits for every approval before starting. Fabricating at risk
  trades that schedule protection for real financial risk, since rework on an
  already-built custom component is far more expensive than correcting a
  drawing.
example: >
  A custom millwork fabricator begins cutting material for a reception desk
  before the submittal is formally approved, in order to meet a tight
  schedule, accepting the risk that any required revision in the approval
  comments will mean redoing some of the work already completed.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] tracks which fabricators are
  working at risk on a given project, since it changes how urgently a
  submittal review needs to move and what's actually at stake if changes come
  back.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A fabricator building at risk finishes a custom component just before the
  submittal comes back with a required dimensional change. The
  already-completed work has to be partially redone, costing far more in time
  and material than if the fabricator had simply waited for approval before
  starting.
