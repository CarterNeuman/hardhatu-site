---
id: concept-finish-carpentry
title: Finish Carpentry
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics, Composites"
definition: >
  The visible, decorative wood trim and millwork installed after rough framing and
  finishes are largely complete, baseboards, door and window casings, crown molding,
  built-in cabinetry, where precision and appearance matter far more than in
  [[concept-rough-carpentry|rough carpentry]].
whyItMatters: >
  Finish carpentry is one of the most visible signs of overall construction quality to
  an untrained eye, so tight, consistent joints and clean installation reflect
  directly on the perceived quality of the whole project, even though it's one of the
  last trades to touch the space.
example: >
  A finish carpentry crew installs baseboard, door casings, and crown molding
  throughout a building's finished spaces, mitering corners precisely and filling nail
  holes so the trim reads as a single continuous, seamless element rather than
  assembled pieces.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] specializing in finish work brings a different
  skill set than rough framing, precision joinery and an eye for a clean, consistent
  finished appearance. A [[career-painter|Painter]] typically follows finish
  carpentry, since trim usually gets caulked and painted as one of the last steps
  before a space is considered complete.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A finish carpentry crew installs trim throughout a building before the walls have
  fully settled and dried out from an unusually wet construction period. As the
  building continues to dry and settle over the following months, gaps open up at the
  trim joints that a properly timed installation, waiting for the framing to
  stabilize, would have avoided.
