title: Utility Locating & Potholing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "02, Existing Conditions"
definition: >
  The process of identifying the exact location and depth of buried utility
  lines before excavation -- first through locating services that mark lines
  on the surface, then through potholing, small hand-dug or vacuum-excavated
  test holes that expose a line to confirm its position before heavier
  equipment digs nearby.
whyItMatters: >
  Striking a buried gas, electric, or fiber line with an excavator can cause
  injuries, outages, and six-figure repair bills; potholing confirms what a
  surface locate only estimates, which matters most on sites with old or
  poorly documented utility records.
example: >
  Before trenching for a new water line, a crew calls in utility locates, then
  vacuum-excavates a test hole at the point where a marked gas line crosses
  the planned trench route, confirming its actual depth is deeper than the
  surface markings suggested.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-surveyor|Surveyor]] or civil crew coordinates locating services,
  and the [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] digs the test holes
  and adjusts excavation once depths are confirmed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew trusts the surface-marked depth from a utility locate and skips
  potholing before trenching, then strikes a gas line that was buried
  shallower than the old utility map indicated, triggering an evacuation and
  an emergency utility response.
