id: concept-rough-carpentry
title: Rough Carpentry
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics, Composites"
definition: >
  The structural and semi-structural wood framing work on a project, including framing
  walls, floors, roofs, and blocking, as distinct from
  [[concept-finish-carpentry|finish carpentry]], which covers the visible, decorative
  wood trim and millwork installed after the rough structure is complete.
whyItMatters: >
  Rough carpentry sets the actual structural skeleton everything else attaches to, so
  mistakes made here, such as out-of-plumb walls or missing blocking for fixtures
  installed later, become much harder and more expensive to fix once drywall, siding, or other
  finishes cover the frame.
example: >
  A framing crew completes the rough carpentry for a building, including walls, floor
  framing, roof framing, and blocking for future cabinet and fixture installations, all
  inspected before insulation and drywall cover it for good.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] performs rough carpentry framing, often the first
  trade physically shaping the building. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]]
  walks the completed rough framing before it's covered, checking for missing blocking
  or out-of-plumb walls that are dramatically easier to fix now than after finishes go
  up.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framing crew forgets to install blocking for a wall-mounted cabinet that isn't
  installed until months later, during the finish phase. The installer discovers
  there's nothing solid to anchor into behind the drywall, and fixing it means cutting
  into finished drywall to add blocking that should have gone in during rough framing.
