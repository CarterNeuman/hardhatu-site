id: concept-fema-flood-zone
title: FEMA Flood Zone
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A federally mapped designation, published by FEMA, rating a specific area's
  risk of flooding, used to determine flood insurance requirements and to
  inform site feasibility and design decisions before a project ever breaks
  ground.
whyItMatters: >
  A site's flood zone designation can affect whether a lender requires flood
  insurance, what elevation a building's lowest floor has to be built at, and
  whether a project is financially feasible at all, which is why checking a
  site's FEMA flood zone happens early, often during feasibility, rather than
  being discovered as a surprise later.
example: >
  A developer evaluating a site during feasibility checks FEMA's published
  flood zone map and finds the property sits in a high-risk zone, a finding
  that changes both the required foundation elevation and the project's
  expected insurance costs before design even begins.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] factors a site's FEMA flood zone
  designation into grading and drainage design from the earliest stages of a
  project.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project proceeds through design without checking the site's FEMA flood
  zone designation early, assuming it isn't a concern. The flood zone
  requirement surfaces later during permitting, forcing a costly redesign of
  the building's foundation elevation well after the design was otherwise
  considered finished.
