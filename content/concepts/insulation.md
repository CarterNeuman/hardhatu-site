id: concept-insulation
title: Insulation
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  Material installed within a building's walls, roof, and floor assemblies to resist
  heat flow, keeping conditioned indoor air from escaping and outdoor temperatures
  from penetrating, available in many forms (batt, rigid board, spray foam,
  blown-in) chosen based on the specific assembly and performance requirement.
whyItMatters: >
  Insulation performance depends heavily on correct, continuous installation, not just
  the material's rated R-value; gaps, compression, or missing insulation at corners
  and penetrations create thermal bridges that can undermine a significant portion of
  the assembly's intended performance, invisible once finishes cover it.
example: >
  A wall assembly is insulated with batt insulation cut and fitted carefully around
  every electrical box and pipe penetration, avoiding the gaps and compression that
  would otherwise reduce its effective performance in exactly those spots.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] or specialty insulation installer fits insulation
  into wall and ceiling cavities, a task that looks simple but is easy to do sloppily
  in ways that aren't obvious once drywall covers it. A
  [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]] may require
  blower-door testing or thermal imaging to verify actual installed performance on a
  project targeting specific energy goals.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An insulation crew installs batt insulation quickly, leaving small gaps and
  compressed sections around electrical boxes and wall corners rather than carefully
  fitting each piece. The wall is covered with drywall before anyone catches the gaps,
  and the building's actual energy performance falls noticeably short of what the
  design calculated, discovered only after occupancy through higher-than-expected
  utility bills.
