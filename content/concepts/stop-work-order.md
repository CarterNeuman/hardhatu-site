id: concept-stop-work-order
title: Stop Work Order
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A formal directive halting some or all work on a project, issued by the owner, a
  government building or safety authority, or occasionally the contractor's own safety
  team, most often in response to an unpermitted change, a serious safety hazard, or a
  code violation serious enough that continuing work would make the problem worse.
whyItMatters: >
  Some problems get more expensive and more dangerous the longer work continues around
  them. A stop work order exists to force an immediate pause specifically so the
  underlying issue gets addressed before more work gets built on top of it.
example: >
  A building inspector discovers structural work proceeding without an approved permit
  revision and issues a stop work order on that portion of the site. Work can't resume
  until the contractor submits the missing permit documentation and the inspector signs
  off.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] has the authority to halt work directly for
  an imminent safety hazard, without waiting for anyone else's approval, since immediate
  danger doesn't wait for paperwork. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] has to
  manage the practical fallout of a stop work order on-site: repositioning crews,
  protecting exposed work, and keeping the rest of the project moving wherever the order
  doesn't apply.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A superintendent lets a crew keep working in an area adjacent to where a stop work
  order was issued, assuming it doesn't apply to them. The inspector returns to find
  work has continued near the cited area, and the stop work order gets expanded to cover
  a much larger portion of the site, a broader shutdown that a stricter reading of the
  original order would have avoided.
