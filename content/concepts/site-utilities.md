id: concept-site-utilities
title: Site Utilities
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "33, Utilities"
definition: >
  The underground water, sewer, storm drainage, electrical, and gas infrastructure
  installed to connect a building to the municipal or utility systems it depends on,
  coordinated across multiple utility trades and typically installed before paving
  locks the site's final surface into place.
whyItMatters: >
  Site utility work happens underground and gets buried almost immediately, so
  coordination between the different utility trades has to happen on paper before
  digging starts: discovering a conflict between a sewer line and an electrical duct
  bank after one of them is already installed means excavating back into ground
  that's already been backfilled.
example: >
  A project's civil engineer produces a utility coordination drawing showing every
  underground utility's planned location before any excavation begins, letting each
  utility trade verify their work won't physically conflict with another trade's
  buried infrastructure.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] excavates and backfills for
  each utility trench to the coordinated utility plan. A
  [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] tracks the different utility
  subcontractors' installation sequencing, since site utilities often involve more
  separate trades working in the same confined area than almost any other project
  phase.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A sewer line is installed at the depth shown on its own trade's drawing without
  cross-checking a nearby electrical duct bank's drawing, and the two are installed at
  crossing elevations that violate the required separation between utilities. The
  conflict isn't discovered until an as-built survey catches it, requiring one of the
  already-backfilled utilities to be re-excavated and re-routed.
