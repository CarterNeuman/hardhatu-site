id: concept-building-performance-standards
title: Building Performance Standards (Carbon Caps)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Local laws, most prominently New York City's Local Law 97, that set a
  maximum allowable     carbon emissions limit for existing buildings over a
  certain size, calculated from a     building's reported annual energy use
  rather than direct emissions monitoring, with     financial penalties for
  buildings that exceed their limit.
whyItMatters: >
  Local Law 97 took effect with its first compliance year in 2024 and carries
  a penalty of     $268 per metric ton of carbon dioxide equivalent over a
  building's limit, which turns a     building's energy performance from an
  operating-cost question into a direct, recurring     regulatory liability
  for the owner.
example: >
  A commercial office tower's annual energy benchmarking data shows it will
  exceed its 2024     to 2029 Local Law 97 emissions limit, so the owner
  commissions an HVAC electrification     retrofit rather than accept the
  ongoing per-ton penalty for every year it remains     noncompliant.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]]
  models a     building's projected emissions against its jurisdiction's
  specific carbon cap schedule,     since the allowable limit tightens in
  stages over multiple compliance periods rather than     staying fixed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A building owner assumes energy benchmarking and a carbon performance cap
  are the same     requirement, when benchmarking is simply the reporting
  mechanism the carbon cap's own     emissions calculation depends on; filing
  the benchmarking data doesn't by itself mean the     building is under its
  emissions limit.
