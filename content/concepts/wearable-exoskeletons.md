---
id: concept-wearable-exoskeletons
title: Wearable Exoskeletons
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Powered or passive wearable support frames worn on the back, shoulders, or
  legs that     reduce the physical strain of repetitive lifting, overhead
  work, or prolonged kneeling,     distinct from
  [[concept-wearable-safety-technology|wearable safety technology]], which
  monitors conditions and alerts rather than physically assisting the body.
whyItMatters: >
  Musculoskeletal injuries from repetitive strain are one of construction's
  most common,     slow-building injury types, and an exoskeleton addresses
  the physical load itself rather     than just tracking or warning about it
  after the strain has already accumulated.
example: >
  A drywall crew wears passive shoulder-support exoskeletons during a week of
  overhead     ceiling installation, reporting noticeably less shoulder
  fatigue at the end of each shift     compared to the same task performed
  without the devices.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] evaluating ergonomic injury
  trends may pilot     exoskeletons for tasks with the highest reported
  strain, such as overhead drywall or rebar     tying, rather than issuing
  them site-wide without a specific use case.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A company issues exoskeletons to a crew without adjusting them to each
  worker's body and     task, assuming any unit helps any job; a poorly fitted
  exoskeleton can actually alter posture     in ways that create a new strain
  pattern instead of reducing the original one.
