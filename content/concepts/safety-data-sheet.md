id: concept-safety-data-sheet
title: Safety Data Sheet (SDS)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A standardized document, supplied by a chemical's manufacturer, detailing its hazards,
  safe handling and storage requirements, and the personal protective equipment needed to
  work with it safely, formerly called a Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) under the
  older hazard communication standard.
whyItMatters: >
  A can of solvent or an adhesive doesn't come with its real hazards printed on the
  label in enough detail to work safely. OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard requires
  every hazardous chemical on a jobsite to have a readily accessible SDS, so workers can
  actually find out what they're handling before something goes wrong, not after.
example: >
  A worker gets an unexpected reaction from a construction adhesive and a supervisor
  pulls the product's SDS on the spot to confirm first-aid steps and whether the exposure
  requires further medical attention, information the product's label alone wouldn't
  have provided in enough detail.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-painter|Painter]] works with solvents and coatings regularly and needs to
  know an SDS's ventilation and [[concept-personal-protective-equipment|PPE]]
  requirements before opening a new product. A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]]
  maintains the site's full SDS binder (or digital equivalent) and makes sure it's
  actually accessible to every crew using a listed chemical.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A jobsite's SDS binder is out of date, missing sheets for several products crews are
  currently using. A worker has an adverse reaction to a chemical and the crew can't
  quickly find the correct first-aid response, delaying appropriate care during exactly
  the window when it matters most.
