title: Value Engineering Change Proposal (VECP)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A formal mechanism, common on public and federal contracts, letting a
  contractor propose a specific change to the contract's own requirements that
  would reduce cost without sacrificing required performance, with any
  resulting savings shared between the contractor and the owner under a
  pre-agreed formula.
whyItMatters: >
  Unlike general [[concept-value-engineering|value engineering]] done during
  design, a VECP is submitted after the contract is already signed and
  specifically proposes changing what the contract itself requires, which is
  why it needs owner approval through a formal process rather than just being
  implemented as a normal construction decision.
example: >
  A contractor on a federal building project identifies a specified structural
  system that could be substituted for a less expensive alternative without
  reducing performance. The contractor submits a formal VECP documenting the
  proposed substitution and projected savings, and once the owner approves it,
  the contract is formally modified and the savings are split between the
  contractor and the owner under the contract's VECP sharing formula.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-value-engineering-consultant|Value Engineering Consultant]] helps
  develop and document a VECP's technical justification, and the
  [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] processes the
  formal contract modification once the VECP is approved.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor implements a cost-saving substitution without going through the
  formal VECP approval process, assuming it's simply a normal construction
  decision, and loses the right to share in savings the mechanism would
  otherwise have entitled it to, since the change was never actually processed
  as a VECP.
