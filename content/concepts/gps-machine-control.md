id: concept-gps-machine-control
title: GPS Machine Control
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A system that feeds GPS positioning data and a digital design surface directly into a
  piece of earthmoving equipment's controls, guiding the operator, or, on some
  equipment, automatically adjusting the blade or bucket, to the correct cut, fill, or
  grade elevation in real time without relying on staked grade markers.
whyItMatters: >
  Traditional grading relies on a survey crew setting physical stakes an operator then
  works between, which takes real time to set and is easy to disturb once equipment is
  moving around the site. GPS machine control lets an operator work directly against the
  digital design surface, cutting the time spent staking and restaking as work
  progresses.
example: >
  An excavator equipped with GPS machine control shows its operator, in real time, how
  close the bucket is to the design grade elevation at that exact point on the site,
  work that would otherwise require stopping to check a physical stake and grade rod.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] running GPS-guided equipment reads
  the in-cab display to hit design grade directly, instead of working between physical
  stakes. A [[career-surveyor|Surveyor]] prepares and loads the digital design surface
  the system references, so its accuracy depends entirely on the surface being correct
  before equipment starts moving.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew loads an outdated design surface into a GPS-guided machine after a late grading
  revision, without confirming the file was actually updated first. The equipment grades
  precisely and confidently to the wrong elevation, since the system has no way to know
  the surface it was given is out of date.
