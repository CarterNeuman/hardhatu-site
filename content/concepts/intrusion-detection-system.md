---
id: concept-intrusion-detection-system
title: Intrusion Detection System
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "28, Electronic Safety and Security"
definition: >
  Door and window contacts, motion sensors, and a central control panel that
  detect unauthorized entry into a building after hours and signal an alarm,
  either locally or to a monitoring service, distinct from
  [[concept-access-control-system|access control]], which manages authorized
  entry rather than detecting unauthorized entry.
whyItMatters: >
  An intrusion detection system's sensor placement has to account for how the
  specific space is actually used, a motion sensor placed without considering
  after-hours cleaning crews or scheduled deliveries generates enough false
  alarms that staff start ignoring or disabling the system entirely,
  defeating its purpose.
example: >
  A warehouse's intrusion detection system is programmed with a separate
  arming schedule for its loading dock area, accounting for a scheduled
  early-morning delivery that would otherwise trigger a false alarm every time
  it occurred.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] or specialty security integrator
  installs the sensors and control panel. A
  [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] manages the arming
  schedule and monitoring service relationship after occupancy.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An installer sets a building's intrusion detection system with a single
  arming schedule that doesn't account for a cleaning crew's actual
  after-hours schedule. The system triggers a false alarm nearly every night
  the crew works, and facility staff eventually disable the zone entirely
  rather than dealing with the constant false alarms, leaving that area
  effectively unmonitored.
