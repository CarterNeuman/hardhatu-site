id: concept-osha-general-duty-clause
title: OSHA General Duty Clause
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The provision of the Occupational Safety and Health Act requiring every
  employer to keep a workplace free of recognized hazards likely to cause
  death or serious harm, used by OSHA to cite a hazard that has no specific
  standard written for it.
whyItMatters: >
  Most OSHA citations point to a specific numbered standard, but a surprising
  number of serious hazards, especially new or unusual ones, aren't covered by
  any specific standard at all, and the General Duty Clause is how OSHA still
  holds an employer accountable for those.
example: >
  After a worker is injured by a hazard from a piece of equipment so new that no
  OSHA standard yet addresses it directly, OSHA cites the employer under the
  General Duty Clause instead, arguing the hazard was recognized in the
  industry and the employer failed to address it.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers need to understand that having no specific standard for a
  hazard doesn't mean there's no citation risk, since a documented,
  industry-recognized hazard can still draw a General Duty Clause citation.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Employers sometimes assume that if no OSHA standard specifically addresses a
  hazard, there's nothing to cite them for, when the General Duty Clause
  exists specifically to cover exactly that gap.
