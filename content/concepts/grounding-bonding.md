id: concept-grounding-bonding
title: Grounding & Bonding
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  The system of conductors connecting a building's electrical system,
  equipment, and metal components to the earth, grounding, and to each other,
  bonding, a code-required safety system that gives fault current a
  deliberate, low-resistance path back to its source so a circuit breaker
  trips quickly instead of energizing exposed metal during a fault.
whyItMatters: >
  Grounding and bonding are what actually make overcurrent protection
  devices, like circuit breakers, work correctly during a ground fault,
  without a proper low-resistance path back to the source, a fault current
  might not be large enough to trip the breaker, leaving energized metal that
  a breaker was supposed to protect against.
example: >
  An electrician bonds a building's metal water piping, structural steel, and
  equipment grounding conductors all together and back to the electrical
  service's grounding electrode, giving any fault current a clear,
  low-resistance path back to the source.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] installs the grounding and bonding
  system, work that's inspected closely since it's essentially invisible once
  concealed but critical to how the rest of the electrical system actually
  protects people.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrician misses bonding a piece of metal ductwork back to the
  building's grounding system during rough-in, assuming its connection to
  grounded structural steel elsewhere is sufficient. Months after occupancy, a
  maintenance worker touching that ductwork section during a minor repair
  gets a noticeable shock from a small ground fault upstream that never had a
  low-resistance path to trip the breaker, and only then does a continuity
  test trace the gap back to the missed bonding jumper.
