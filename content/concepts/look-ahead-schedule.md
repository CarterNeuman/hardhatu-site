---
id: concept-look-ahead-schedule
title: Look-Ahead Schedule
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A short-term schedule, usually covering the next two to three weeks, pulled from the
  full project schedule and reviewed regularly with the crews and subcontractors
  actually doing the near-term work.
whyItMatters: >
  A full project schedule can run hundreds of lines long and cover a year or more,
  useful for planning, but not something a foreman can act on day to day. The
  look-ahead schedule translates that into "here's exactly what needs to happen in the
  next three weeks," which is the timeframe people can actually plan around.
example: >
  Every Friday, a superintendent reviews the coming three weeks of work with every
  subcontractor on-site, confirming the electrician will be ready to start rough-in the
  same week the framing crew finishes, rather than everyone discovering a conflict when
  it's too late to adjust.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] pulls the look-ahead schedule from the master
  schedule and keeps it current as work actually progresses. A
  [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] runs the weekly meeting where it actually
  gets used; it's one of their main tools for keeping every trade coordinated in real
  time.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A superintendent stops updating the look-ahead schedule for a few weeks because the
  job feels like it's running smoothly. When an inspection unexpectedly delays one
  trade, nobody had visibility into how it would ripple into the next three weeks of
  other trades' work, and crews show up with no task ready for them to actually do.
