id: concept-conceptual-estimate
title: Conceptual Estimate
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A very early, rough cost projection built before a design is finished,
  usually based on square footage or a project's general type, accurate only
  within a wide range, often fifty to one hundred percent, rather than a
  precise number.
whyItMatters: >
  A conceptual estimate exists to answer one question early: is this project
  even in the right ballpark financially, before anyone spends real money
  designing it in detail. Treating a conceptual estimate as a firm budget,
  rather than the rough placeholder it actually is, is one of the most common
  sources of friction later in a project.
example: >
  Before any drawings exist, an estimator gives an owner a conceptual estimate
  for a new warehouse based on a typical dollar-per-square-foot cost for
  similar buildings, explicitly labeled as accurate only within a wide range,
  to help the owner decide whether to proceed into actual design.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] builds a conceptual estimate at the very
  start of a project, long before enough detail exists for a tighter number.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner takes a conceptual estimate's wide range and quotes only the low
  end of it to investors as the expected cost. When the real, detailed
  estimate comes in higher, the owner blames the estimator for being wrong,
  when the actual mistake was treating a rough early number as a firm
  commitment.
