id: concept-top-and-bottom-plate
title: Top Plate and Bottom Plate
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics, and Composites"
definition: >
  The horizontal framing members that run along the very top and very bottom
  of a stud wall, with the vertical studs nailed in between them, forming the
  basic frame a wall is built from before it's ever stood up.
whyItMatters: >
  The top and bottom plates are what actually tie a wall's studs together into
  a single unit and transfer the wall's loads down into the floor framing or
  foundation below. A double top plate is also commonly used specifically to
  overlap and tie intersecting walls together at the corners.
example: >
  A framing crew lays out a bottom plate on the subfloor, nails studs to it at
  regular spacing, caps the studs with a top plate, and only then stands the
  assembled wall section upright and secures it in place.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] assembles a wall's plates and studs on the
  flat ground or subfloor before standing the whole wall section up as one
  unit, which is faster and more accurate than building a wall stud by stud
  while it's already vertical.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew fails to properly overlap the top plates where two walls intersect at
  a corner, leaving the connection weaker than it should be. The gap isn't
  obvious once drywall covers it, but it leaves that corner less structurally
  tied together than the rest of the framing.
