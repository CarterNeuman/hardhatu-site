title: "Design Phases: SD, DD, and CD"
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The three stages a building's design passes through on its way to being
  biddable: Schematic Design (SD), which sets the basic layout and look,
  Design Development (DD), which works out real systems and materials, and
  Construction Documents (CD), the fully detailed drawings a contractor can
  actually build and price from.
whyItMatters: >
  An estimate's accuracy depends heavily on which of these three stages the
  design has actually reached. A number pulled from schematic design is a
  rough placeholder, while a number pulled from completed construction
  documents reflects real, buildable detail, and knowing which stage a set of
  drawings represents is essential to understanding how much an estimate can
  be trusted.
example: >
  An estimator pricing a project at the schematic design stage flags the
  number as preliminary, since walls, systems, and finishes are still subject
  to change. By the time the same project reaches construction documents, the
  estimate tightens considerably because every detail that affects cost has
  finally been drawn.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] prices a project differently, and with
  different confidence, depending on which design phase the drawings
  represent.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A client is given a single number early in schematic design and treats it as
  a final budget, without being told how much the design will still change
  before construction documents are complete. By the time CD drawings are
  finished, the real cost has grown well past that early number, and the
  client feels misled even though the estimator never claimed that number was
  final.
