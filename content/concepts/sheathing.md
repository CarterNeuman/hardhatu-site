id: concept-sheathing
title: Sheathing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics & Composites"
definition: >
  Structural panels, plywood or oriented strand board (OSB) most commonly,
  fastened to the outside of a building's wall or roof framing to tie the frame
  together, provide a nailing surface for the exterior finish, and, on walls,
  often serve as the structure's lateral bracing against wind and seismic
  loads.
whyItMatters: >
  Sheathing's nailing pattern, fastener spacing and edge distance, is
  engineered for the specific lateral loads the wall or roof has to resist, not
  just to hold the panel in place, so a crew that skips fasteners or
  over-spaces them can leave the structure without the shear capacity it was
  actually designed for.
example: >
  A framing crew installs OSB wall sheathing following the nailing schedule
  called out on the structural drawings, with tighter fastener spacing at panel
  edges specifically required for the wall's designated shear resistance.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] installs sheathing following the specified
  nailing pattern. A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]]
  specifies that pattern based on the lateral loads the wall or roof has to
  resist.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framing crew installs wall sheathing quickly using a wider fastener spacing
  than the structural drawings specify, assuming any reasonable nailing pattern
  is close enough. A later inspection catches the shortfall, and the crew has
  to add fasteners throughout the wall to bring it up to the specified shear
  capacity.
