id: concept-wind-vs-water-causation
title: Wind vs. Water Causation Dispute
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The recurring coastal-claims fight over whether damage was caused by wind,
  typically covered under a standard homeowners policy, or by storm-surge flooding,
  typically excluded and requiring separate
  [[concept-flood-insurance-nfip|flood coverage]], made harder because both often hit
  the same property during the same storm.
whyItMatters: >
  This dispute reached the national spotlight after Hurricane Katrina, when large
  numbers of coastal claims turned entirely on proving whether damage happened
  before or after storm surge reached a property, sometimes deciding whether a
  homeowner was covered at all.
example: >
  A beachfront condo's windows fail under sustained hurricane winds, letting
  wind-driven rain soak the interior for hours before storm surge ever reaches the
  building. Establishing that timeline is what determines whether the interior water
  damage is covered as wind-driven rain, even though the same storm also caused
  excluded flood damage once the surge arrived.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-construction-claims-consultant|Construction Claims Consultant]]
  investigates the physical sequence and timing of damage on a coastal loss,
  reconstructing what happened and when from structural evidence, weather data, and
  witness accounts.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A homeowner's engineer and the insurer's engineer inspect the same interior
  water staining but reach opposite conclusions about whether wind-driven
  rain or storm surge caused it first, since the same physical evidence,
  staining height, water lines, debris patterns, can genuinely support
  either timeline. With the two forensic opinions in direct conflict, the
  coverage dispute ends up litigated rather than settled at the adjuster
  level, each expert testifying to a different sequence of events from the
  same physical scene.
