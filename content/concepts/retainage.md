---
id: concept-retainage
title: Retainage
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A percentage, commonly 5 to 10 percent, withheld from each progress payment and paid
  out only once the project reaches substantial completion, giving the owner leverage to
  make sure the contractor actually finishes the job.
whyItMatters: >
  Without it, a contractor could get paid nearly in full while a project is only mostly
  done, with little financial incentive left to finish the remaining, less profitable
  details. Retainage keeps a meaningful chunk of money tied to actual completion.
example: >
  A contractor bills $200,000 for a month's work, but the owner pays only $180,000; the
  other $20,000, 10 percent, is held as retainage and won't be released until the
  project reaches substantial completion.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] has to plan cash flow around retainage
  being withheld throughout the entire project, then plan how to actually collect it at
  the end. A [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] tracks exactly
  how much retainage is owed and pushes to make sure it's released promptly once it's
  earned.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner keeps finding reasons to delay releasing retainage well after substantial
  completion, using it as leverage over unrelated, minor disputes. The contractor has
  already paid their subcontractors and suppliers in full months earlier, meaning the
  delay squeezes only the contractor's own cash flow while everyone they owed money to
  has already been paid.
