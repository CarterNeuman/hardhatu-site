id: concept-dewatering
title: Dewatering
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  The process of removing groundwater from an excavation, using well points,
  sump pumps, or deep wells, to keep a construction area dry enough to work
  in, required whenever an excavation extends below the local water table and
  often triggering its own separate discharge permit for where the removed
  water actually goes.
whyItMatters: >
  Dewatering doesn't just make an excavation dry, done incorrectly it can
  draw down the groundwater table enough to cause settlement in nearby
  existing structures, so a dewatering plan on an urban site has to account
  for its effect well beyond the excavation's own footprint.
example: >
  A contractor excavating for a below-grade parking structure installs a ring
  of well points around the excavation's perimeter, pumping continuously to
  keep groundwater below the working elevation while monitoring nearby
  buildings for any settlement the dewatering might cause.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] designs the dewatering plan
  based on the site's geotechnical conditions. An
  [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] or specialty dewatering
  contractor installs and runs the well points and pumps.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor's dewatering plan underestimates how far the pumping will
  actually draw down the groundwater table, based on a drawdown radius
  calculated from limited geotechnical data. Within weeks, a private well
  serving a home two lots away runs dry, well outside the radius the original
  plan accounted for, forcing the contractor to supply temporary water to the
  homeowner while the well is investigated and the dewatering plan is
  revised.
