---
id: concept-constructive-acceleration
title: Constructive Acceleration
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A claim arising when an owner refuses to grant a contractor a time extension
  it was genuinely entitled to, for a legitimate excusable delay, while still
  expecting the original completion date, effectively forcing acceleration
  without formally directing it or agreeing to pay for it.
whyItMatters: >
  The owner never has to say the word "accelerate" for this claim to apply, the
  acceleration is "constructive" precisely because it happens by the owner's
  refusal to act, not by any explicit directive.
example: >
  A contractor documents a legitimate weather-related delay and formally
  requests a corresponding time extension. The owner denies the request without
  real justification while continuing to hold the contractor to the original
  completion date.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-scheduling-delay-consultant|Scheduling & Delay Consultant]] builds
  the forensic schedule analysis a constructive acceleration claim depends on,
  proving both that the original delay was excusable and that the owner's
  refusal forced real acceleration costs.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor's legitimate time extension request is denied, and rather than
  formally documenting the acceleration that follows, the contractor just quietly
  adds overtime shifts to protect the schedule. Without that documentation, the
  later constructive acceleration claim has no paper trail to prove the extra
  costs were the owner's doing.
