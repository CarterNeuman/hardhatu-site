---
id: concept-rebar
title: Rebar (Reinforcing Steel)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  Steel bars embedded within concrete before it's poured, giving the finished concrete
  the tensile strength (resistance to bending and pulling forces) that plain concrete
  lacks on its own, since concrete is strong in compression but weak in tension, and
  rebar fills exactly that gap.
whyItMatters: >
  Concrete without adequate reinforcement can crack and fail under loads it would
  easily handle if properly reinforced, so rebar placement, meaning size, spacing, and
  position within the concrete, is engineered specifically for each structural
  element and inspected before the pour, since it's completely hidden once the
  concrete goes in.
example: >
  A structural engineer's drawings specify the exact size, spacing, and placement of
  rebar within a foundation wall. An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] crew ties the
  rebar cage to those exact specifications, and it's inspected and approved before the
  concrete truck is allowed to pour.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] cuts, bends, and ties rebar into place according
  to the structural drawings. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] performs a rebar
  inspection, checking size, spacing, and concrete cover, that's often a required
  [[concept-special-inspection|special inspection]] before the pour can proceed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A rebar cage is tied with less concrete cover (the distance between the steel and
  the outer surface) than the drawings specify, and the error isn't caught before the
  pour. Years later, moisture reaches the under-covered steel and causes it to corrode
  and expand, cracking and spalling the concrete around it from the inside out.
