id: concept-fire-sprinkler-system
title: Fire Sprinkler System
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "21, Fire Suppression"
definition: >
  A network of piping and sprinkler heads installed throughout a building, designed
  to automatically detect and suppress a fire with water before it spreads,
  engineered to a specific hazard classification and density requirement based on the
  building's occupancy and contents, not a one-size-fits-all layout.
whyItMatters: >
  A fire sprinkler system's design directly affects whether a building can even
  legally reduce other fire-protection requirements, like allowable building area or
  required fire-rated separations, under the building code, so the sprinkler design
  isn't just a safety feature, it's often load-bearing for the entire code
  compliance strategy of the building.
example: >
  A warehouse's high-piled storage occupancy requires a specific sprinkler density
  and coverage designed for that hazard classification, since a standard
  light-hazard sprinkler design used in an office building wouldn't provide adequate
  protection for the denser combustible storage.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] coordinates fire watch procedures if the
  sprinkler system has to be temporarily out of service during construction or
  renovation. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] witnesses
  the required flow and pressure testing of the completed system before it's
  accepted, since a system that looks complete can still fail to deliver the
  required water flow.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A tenant improvement project changes a warehouse space from light storage to
  high-piled combustible storage without updating the existing fire sprinkler
  system's design for the new, more demanding hazard classification. The existing
  system doesn't provide adequate protection for the new use, a mismatch that isn't
  caught until a fire marshal's review flags it during permitting for the change of
  use.
