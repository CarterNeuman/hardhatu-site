title: Crane Operator & Signal Person Certification
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's requirement, under 29 CFR 1926.1427, that operators of most cranes with
  a rated capacity over 2,000 pounds be certified by an accredited testing
  organization or qualified through an audited employer program, paired with a
  separate, less formal qualification requirement for the signal person who
  directs the crane's movements from the ground.
whyItMatters: >
  Certification alone doesn't fully satisfy the rule: an employer also has to
  document a separate evaluation confirming the certified operator is actually
  qualified on the specific machine being operated, a documentation step that
  catches out a lot of otherwise compliant-looking operations.
example: >
  A crane operator holds a valid certification from an accredited testing
  organization, but the operator's employer still has to complete and keep on
  file a separate, machine-specific evaluation confirming that operator is
  qualified on the exact crane model being used that day.
careerAngle: >
  Site superintendents should confirm both pieces exist before a lift: the
  operator's underlying certification and the employer's own documented,
  machine-specific evaluation, since OSHA has cited employers for having a
  certified operator but no evaluation documentation on file.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Employers sometimes assume a valid crane operator certification card alone
  fully satisfies the rule, when the standard also separately requires the
  employer to document a machine-specific evaluation confirming that
  operator's qualification on the particular crane actually being used.
