---
id: concept-weather-day
title: Weather Day
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A day of lost work time caused by weather conditions that prevent safe or effective
  construction, commonly used as the basis for a contractor's request for additional
  contract time, though usually only when the weather is unusually severe compared to
  the historical average for that location and time of year.
whyItMatters: >
  Most construction contracts allow schedule extensions for "abnormal" weather, but not
  for ordinary weather a region should expect: a rainy week in a rainy climate
  typically isn't grounds for extra time on its own. Whether a weather day actually
  qualifies for a schedule extension depends on comparing it against historical weather
  data for the site, not just whether work stopped.
example: >
  A contract in a region with a well-documented average of six rain days in April allows
  a schedule extension only for rain days beyond that average. A project experiences
  eleven rain days that April, and the contractor documents and claims a five-day
  extension for the days beyond the historical norm.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] logs weather conditions and their impact on
  the day's work in the [[concept-daily-report|daily report]], since that documentation
  is what a weather day claim depends on later. A [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] tracks
  weather days against the contract's allowance and factors legitimate extensions into
  schedule updates.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A superintendent doesn't bother logging weather conditions on days when work slows
  down but doesn't fully stop, assuming it won't matter later. Months later, when the
  project is behind schedule and the contractor wants to claim weather-related time
  extensions, there's no daily report documentation to support which specific days were
  actually lost to weather versus other causes.
