---
id: concept-contract
title: Contract
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A legally binding agreement between two or more parties, an owner and a
  contractor, a contractor and a subcontractor, that defines the scope of work,
  price, schedule, and the rights and obligations each side owes the other if
  something goes wrong.
whyItMatters: >
  Nearly every relationship on a construction project runs through a contract of some
  kind, and disputes almost always come back to what a specific contract actually
  says, not what anyone remembers agreeing to verbally. Reading and understanding
  contract language is a foundational skill across nearly every construction career,
  not just for lawyers.
example: >
  A general contractor's prime contract with the owner sets the overall price,
  schedule, and terms for the whole project, while separate subcontracts between the
  GC and each trade set the terms for each piece of the work underneath it.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-contracts-administrator|Contracts Administrator]] works with contract
  language every day, drafting and reviewing agreements before they're signed. A
  [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] refers back to the actual contract
  constantly during a dispute, since a verbal understanding that isn't reflected in
  the signed document usually doesn't hold up.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Two parties have a clear verbal understanding about how a specific ambiguous
  situation should be handled, but never put it in writing as a contract amendment.
  When a dispute arises months later, neither side can prove what was actually
  agreed to, and the written contract, silent on the issue, ends up governing
  instead, in a way neither party actually intended.
