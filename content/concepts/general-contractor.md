id: concept-general-contractor
title: General Contractor
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The company holding the main contract with the project owner, responsible for
  delivering the finished project, usually by hiring and coordinating subcontractors
  who do the specialized work (electrical, plumbing, concrete, and so on).
whyItMatters: >
  The general contractor (GC) is the single point of accountability. If a subcontractor
  falls behind or does bad work, it's the GC's job to catch it and fix it. The owner
  typically has one contract, with the GC, not dozens of contracts with every trade.
example: >
  A hospital owner signs one contract with a general contractor to build a new wing.
  The GC then signs its own contracts with an electrical subcontractor, a plumbing
  subcontractor, and a dozen others, and coordinates all of their schedules.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] and [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]]
  typically work for the general contractor directly; it's their employer, not just a
  name on the org chart. An [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] sits
  on the opposite side of that relationship: hired specifically to watch the GC's
  performance on the owner's behalf, which only matters because the owner has exactly
  one contract, with the GC, to hold accountable.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrical subcontractor falls badly behind schedule, but the general contractor
  doesn't catch it until the delay has already pushed back the drywall crew waiting
  behind them. Because the owner's contract is with the GC, not with each individual
  trade, it's the GC who owns that delay in the eyes of the contract, even though a
  subcontractor caused it. Staying on top of every trade's progress, not just the GC's
  own crews, is the job.
