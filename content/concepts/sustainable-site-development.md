id: concept-sustainable-site-development
title: Sustainable Site Development
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Site design and construction practices that reduce a project's environmental impact
  outside the building itself (managing stormwater naturally, minimizing disturbed
  area, protecting existing vegetation, and choosing paving and landscaping that reduce
  heat and runoff), as distinct from the building's own energy or materials performance.
whyItMatters: >
  A highly efficient building on a site that generates excessive stormwater runoff or
  destroys existing natural drainage still creates real environmental impact: several
  green building certification systems specifically award credits for site practices
  separately from building performance, because the two aren't the same problem.
example: >
  Instead of a conventional paved parking lot, a project uses permeable paving and
  bioswales to let stormwater filter naturally into the ground on site, reducing the
  volume of runoff the surrounding municipal storm system has to handle during heavy
  rain.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-environmental-remediation-specialist|Environmental/Remediation Specialist]]
  is often involved early in site planning to identify environmentally sensitive areas
  worth protecting or working around. A
  [[career-sustainability-leed-consultant|Sustainability/LEED Consultant]] tracks
  site-specific credits (reduced disturbed area, stormwater management, reflective
  paving) as part of the project's overall certification strategy.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project clears more of a site than the design actually requires, assuming extra
  cleared area simplifies construction logistics. The additional disturbed area increases
  stormwater runoff beyond what the site's drainage design accounted for, and the project
  has to add stormwater management measures after the fact that a smaller initial
  footprint would have avoided.
