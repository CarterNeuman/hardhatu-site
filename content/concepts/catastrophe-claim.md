id: concept-catastrophe-claim
title: Catastrophe (CAT) Claim
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A claim arising from a large-scale, officially declared disaster event, such as a
  hurricane, wildfire, or major hailstorm, handled differently from an everyday
  claim, typically through a surge workforce of CAT adjusters temporarily deployed
  from out of state and emergency claim-handling procedures insurers activate
  specifically for these events.
whyItMatters: >
  The sheer volume of claims filed simultaneously after a catastrophe changes how
  quickly any individual claim gets attention, so understanding that a CAT claim
  follows a different timeline than a routine claim helps set realistic expectations
  for how fast an inspection or payment will actually happen.
example: >
  After a hurricane makes landfall, an insurer activates its catastrophe response
  plan, deploying CAT adjusters from across the country to a temporary claims
  office in the affected region to handle the surge of claims filed in the storm's
  aftermath.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]] can take on CAT deployments as
  an independent or catastrophe adjuster, higher-paying, travel-heavy work that
  concentrates around major disaster events. A [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]] scales crews up rapidly to meet the surge in
  restoration demand a CAT event creates in a region.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A homeowner's claim after a major hurricane sits unassigned for weeks because
  thousands of claims were filed in the same region simultaneously, and the
  out-of-state CAT adjuster eventually assigned isn't familiar with local
  construction costs or common regional damage patterns, slowing the inspection and
  scoping process further.
