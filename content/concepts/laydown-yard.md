---
id: concept-laydown-yard
title: Laydown Yard
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A designated on-site or nearby off-site area for storing materials, equipment,
  and prefabricated assemblies before they're needed for installation, planned as
  part of site logistics on projects where the building footprint itself leaves
  little room for staging.
whyItMatters: >
  A poorly planned laydown yard causes real double-handling, materials get moved
  out of the way and moved again, and on a tight site it can create the kind of
  congestion that slows down every trade trying to move through the same limited
  space.
example: >
  On a tight urban high-rise site with no room to store materials, the general
  contractor leases a laydown yard several blocks away and trucks materials to
  site on a tightly scheduled, just-in-time basis rather than stockpiling on the
  site itself.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] plans and manages day-to-day use of
  the laydown yard. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] negotiates the
  lease when the yard sits off site.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A general contractor underestimates how much structural steel needs staging for
  an accelerated erection sequence. The laydown yard fills up mid-erection,
  incoming deliveries have to wait off site for space to open up, and the crane
  crew sits idle waiting on material that's stuck in a queue outside the gate.
