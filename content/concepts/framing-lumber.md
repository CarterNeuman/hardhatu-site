---
id: concept-framing-lumber
title: Framing Lumber
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "06, Wood, Plastics, Composites"
definition: >
  Dimensional wood lumber (like standard 2x4s and 2x6s) used to build a building's
  structural frame, such as walls, floors, and roof, graded and stamped for strength so a
  builder knows exactly what structural loads a given piece of lumber is rated to
  carry.
whyItMatters: >
  Not all lumber of the same dimension is structurally equivalent. Grading stamps
  indicate species, strength, and moisture content, and using ungraded or improperly
  graded lumber in a structural application can mean the actual frame doesn't have the
  load capacity the design assumed.
example: >
  A residential framing crew builds floor joists using lumber graded and stamped to
  the specific structural rating the span tables require for that joist spacing and
  length, rather than whatever lumber happens to be available.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] selects and installs framing lumber according to
  the span and spacing requirements in the plans, since substituting a lower grade or
  wrong species for a structural member can silently undermine its load capacity. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] on larger residential or
  light commercial projects checks framing against the structural plans before it's
  covered by insulation and drywall.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A framing crew runs short on the specified lumber grade partway through a job and
  substitutes a lower grade for a batch of floor joists, assuming the difference won't
  matter over a short span. The substitution goes uncaught, and the affected floor
  later develops a noticeable bounce under normal foot traffic that a properly graded
  joist wouldn't have.
