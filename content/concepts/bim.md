id: concept-bim
title: Building Information Modeling (BIM)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A 3D digital model of a project, structural, architectural, and MEP systems modeled
  together with real data attached to every element, used to design, coordinate, and
  increasingly build a project, rather than a purely visual 3D rendering with no
  underlying data behind it.
whyItMatters: >
  A 2D drawing set can show a duct and a structural beam in the same location on two
  separate sheets without anyone noticing until the conflict shows up in the field. A
  coordinated BIM model surfaces that same conflict, a [[concept-clash-detection|clash]],
  on a screen during design, where it costs a model update instead of cutting into
  installed work.
example: >
  Before steel erection begins, the project team runs a coordination review across the
  structural, mechanical, and electrical models and finds a duct routed directly through
  a structural beam, a conflict resolved with a model revision instead of a field rework
  order once the beam is already up.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-bim-vdc-specialist|BIM/VDC Specialist]] builds and maintains the model as
  the project's single coordinated source of truth across every trade. A
  [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] relies on the model's accuracy for
  everything from quantity takeoffs to resolving field conflicts before they become
  change orders.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team treats the 3D model as a visualization tool rather than a coordination
  tool, skipping a scheduled clash detection pass to save time before a milestone review.
  A duct-versus-beam conflict the pass would have caught instead surfaces in the field
  after the beam is already installed, turning a model fix into an expensive rework.
