id: concept-sump-pump
title: Sump Pump / Sewage Ejector
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "22, Plumbing"
definition: >
  A pump installed in a pit below a building's lowest drainage level (a sump pump
  for groundwater that would otherwise flood a basement, a sewage ejector pump for
  wastewater from fixtures located below the level the main sewer line can drain to by
  gravity), mechanically lifting water or waste up to where it can flow out normally.
whyItMatters: >
  Any fixture or drain installed below the building's gravity sewer connection
  depends entirely on a functioning pump to get its water or waste out of the
  building, so a pump failure in this specific location isn't a minor inconvenience:
  it can mean a flooded basement or a sewage backup with no gravity-driven backup
  path.
example: >
  A finished basement bathroom sits below the level the main sewer line can reach by
  gravity, so its fixtures drain into a sewage ejector pit, where a pump
  automatically lifts the wastewater up to the level of the gravity sewer line
  whenever the pit fills.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] sizes and installs the pump and pit for the specific
  fixture load and expected water volume. A [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] treats sump pumps and sewage ejectors as critical equipment worth a backup power plan, since a power outage during a storm is exactly when a sump pump
  is needed most.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A basement renovation adds a bathroom below the main sewer line's gravity reach,
  and the sewage ejector pump installed for it is sized for the original, smaller
  fixture load rather than the renovation's actual new demand. The undersized pump
  struggles to keep up during heavy use, and the pit occasionally overflows before
  the undersizing is diagnosed and corrected.
