id: concept-fire-rated-assembly
title: Fire-Rated Assembly
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A wall, floor, ceiling, or door assembly tested and listed by an independent
  laboratory to resist fire for a specific duration, commonly one or two hours,
  required by code between certain occupancies, exits, and structural elements.
whyItMatters: >
  The rating belongs to the entire tested assembly exactly as listed, not to the
  individual materials in it, so substituting a different fastener spacing, layer
  count, or even a different brand of drywall than the tested assembly specifies can
  invalidate the rating even though the wall looks identical.
example: >
  A one-hour rated corridor wall is built using the exact layer count, fastener
  type, and fastener spacing specified in its UL listing, since deviating from any
  one of those details, even slightly, means the wall no longer matches a tested
  and approved assembly.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-drywall-installer|Drywall Installer]] has to build fire-rated
  assemblies to the listed assembly's exact specifications, not to a general sense
  of "close enough," since inspectors check against the specific listing.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrician cuts an opening through a fire-rated wall to run conduit and
  doesn't install a listed firestop system to seal it afterward. Weeks later,
  the general contractor's own QA/QC walkthrough, cross-checking penetration
  locations against the fire-stopping subcontractor's completed-work log,
  finds no record of that penetration ever being sealed, catching the gap
  before it reaches final inspection.
