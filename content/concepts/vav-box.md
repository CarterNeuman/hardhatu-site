id: concept-vav-box
title: VAV Box (Variable Air Volume)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "23, HVAC"
definition: >
  A mechanical device installed in a building's ductwork that modulates the
  amount of conditioned air delivered to a specific zone based on that zone's
  actual temperature, allowing a single central air handling system to serve
  many zones with different, changing heating and cooling needs rather than
  delivering a constant volume of air everywhere.
whyItMatters: >
  A VAV box has to be balanced correctly for its specific zone's airflow
  requirement during commissioning, since a box that's stuck fully open or
  fully closed either wastes energy overcooling a zone or leaves it without
  adequate airflow, a problem that shows up as an occupant comfort complaint
  rather than an obvious equipment failure.
example: >
  A building's mechanical engineer designs a VAV system with a separate box
  serving each perimeter zone, since each zone's solar exposure and occupancy
  create different, independently varying heating and cooling loads
  throughout the day.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] or specialty controls
  technician installs and balances VAV boxes. A
  [[career-mep-engineer|MEP Engineer]] designs the zoning and airflow each box
  has to deliver.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A controls technician commissions a VAV box but doesn't verify its minimum
  airflow setpoint against the zone's actual ventilation requirement. The box
  modulates down to a setting that technically satisfies temperature but
  delivers less outside air than code requires for that occupancy, a
  deficiency that isn't caught until an indoor air quality complaint prompts a
  closer review.
