id: concept-ada-title-ii-vs-title-iii
title: ADA Title II vs. Title III
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The distinction between the two parts of the Americans with Disabilities Act
  that govern the built environment: Title II covers facilities owned or
  operated by state and local government, while Title III covers places of
  public accommodation operated by private businesses, each with its own
  enforcement agency and compliance nuances.
whyItMatters: >
  A renovation project's accessibility obligations can differ depending on which
  title applies, since Title II facilities face additional program-access
  requirements beyond physical construction that a privately owned Title III
  building generally doesn't.
example: >
  A contractor renovating a city-owned community center works under Title II's
  broader program-access standard, while the same contractor's next job
  renovating a privately owned retail store next door falls under Title III's
  public-accommodation requirements instead.
careerAngle: >
  Design and construction teams should confirm early which title governs a given
  project, since a government-owned facility can carry accessibility
  obligations beyond what a privately owned building of the same type would
  face.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes treat ADA compliance as one uniform set of rules, when a
  facility's ownership, government versus private, determines which title
  actually applies and what its specific obligations are.
