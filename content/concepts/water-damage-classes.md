id: concept-water-damage-classes
title: Water Damage Classes (1-4)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  The IICRC S500 standard's four-tier classification of how much water was absorbed
  and how hard it will be to dry, Class 1 (minimal absorption) through Class 4
  (water trapped in low-permeance materials like hardwood, plaster, or masonry),
  distinct from [[concept-water-damage-categories|Category]], which measures
  contamination rather than drying difficulty.
whyItMatters: >
  Class determines the drying equipment and timeline a restoration job actually
  needs, a Class 4 loss requires specialized, longer-duration drying methods that a
  Class 1 loss never does, even when both involve the same amount of standing water
  initially.
example: >
  A small kitchen leak affecting only part of one room (Class 1) dries in a day or
  two with standard equipment, while the same volume of water soaking into a home's
  hardwood floors and plaster walls (Class 4) can take a week or more of specialized
  low-evaporation drying.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-insurance-restoration-estimator|Insurance Restoration Estimator]] uses
  the class, not just the category, to price the equipment count and drying
  timeline for a job, since class drives labor and equipment cost more directly than
  contamination level does.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An estimator prices a water loss using a Class 2 drying timeline and equipment
  count, but the crew actually finds a Class 4 situation, water trapped in hardwood,
  plaster, and concrete requiring specialized low-evaporation drying. The
  under-scoped estimate leaves the restoration company absorbing the extra cost, or
  forces a disruptive supplement mid-job.
