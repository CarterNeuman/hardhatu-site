id: concept-joint-sealants
title: Joint Sealants
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  Flexible sealant compounds, such as silicone or polyurethane, installed at
  building joints, control joints, expansion joints, and the perimeter of
  windows and doors, engineered to accommodate the specific amount of movement
  that joint is expected to experience without losing its seal, distinct from a
  rigid material that would crack under the same movement.
whyItMatters: >
  Different sealant chemistries are rated for different amounts of joint
  movement and different substrates, using a sealant rated for less movement
  than the joint actually experiences, or one incompatible with the specific
  substrate, leads to a bond failure well before the sealant's rated service
  life is up.
example: >
  A glazing contractor seals a curtain wall's expansion joints with a
  structural silicone sealant specifically rated for the joint's calculated
  range of movement, confirmed compatible with the specific glass and frame
  substrates through the manufacturer's adhesion testing.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-glazier|Glazier]] or specialty caulking crew installs sealants at
  window and curtain wall joints. A [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]]
  may require a field adhesion test, a pull test, before a sealant installation
  is approved for the whole building.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor uses a lower-cost general-purpose sealant at a building's
  primary expansion joints instead of the higher-movement-rated sealant
  specified. The joint's actual seasonal movement exceeds what the substituted
  sealant can accommodate, and the bond fails within two years, well before the
  original sealant's warranted service life would have ended.
