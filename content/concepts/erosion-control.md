id: concept-erosion-control
title: Erosion Control
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  Temporary measures, silt fence, inlet protection, sediment basins, installed to
  keep disturbed soil from washing off a construction site into storm drains,
  streams, or neighboring properties, typically required by a site's Stormwater
  Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) under federal and state stormwater regulations.
whyItMatters: >
  Sediment leaving a construction site isn't just a housekeeping issue: it's a
  regulated environmental discharge, and a project that lets erosion control measures
  fail can face real regulatory fines from the EPA or a state environmental agency,
  not just a cleanup bill.
example: >
  A site installs silt fence along its downhill perimeter and inlet protection at
  every nearby storm drain before any significant earthwork begins, inspecting and
  maintaining those measures throughout construction as the SWPPP requires.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-laborer|Construction Laborer]] installs and maintains erosion control
  measures like silt fence throughout the project, work that has to continue as long
  as soil stays disturbed. An [[career-environmental-remediation-specialist|Environmental/Remediation Specialist]] oversees SWPPP compliance and documents the required periodic
  inspections that regulators can request at any time.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A site's silt fence is installed correctly at the start of a project but never
  inspected or repaired after a storm damages a section of it. Sediment washes through
  the gap into a storm drain during a later rain event, and a state inspector cites
  the project for a stormwater violation that a routine inspection would have caught
  before it became a discharge.
