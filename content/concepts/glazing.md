id: concept-glazing
title: Glazing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  The glass (or glass-like material) installed into windows, curtain walls, and
  storefront systems, specified for its performance properties, such as insulating
  value, solar heat control, and safety rating (tempered or laminated), rather than
  just its transparency.
whyItMatters: >
  Code often requires specific glazing types in specific locations, tempered or
  laminated safety glass near doors, stairways, and low sills, for example,
  precisely because ordinary annealed glass in those locations can shatter into
  dangerous shards if broken, and a glazing substitution in the wrong location is both
  a safety hazard and a code violation.
example: >
  Glazing installed within the swing arc next to a door, or low enough near a floor
  or walking surface to be at risk of impact, is specified as tempered safety glass,
  which breaks into small, relatively harmless pieces rather than large, sharp
  shards, meeting the code requirement for glazing in these hazardous locations.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] verifies glazing type
  against the drawings before installation is accepted, particularly in code-mandated
  safety glazing locations, since the glass itself is often visually indistinguishable
  from the wrong type once installed. A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]]
  tracks glazing submittals closely, since performance specifications, such as solar
  heat gain and insulating value, vary significantly between similar-looking glass
  products.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A glazing subcontractor installs standard annealed glass in a location near a
  stairway landing where code requires tempered safety glass, and the substitution
  isn't caught during a rushed installation. A code inspector flags the
  non-compliant glazing during a life-safety walk-through, and the panel has to be
  removed and replaced with the correct safety-rated glass before the project can
  proceed to occupancy.
