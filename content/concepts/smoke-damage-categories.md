id: concept-smoke-damage-categories
title: Smoke Damage Categories (Protein, Wet, Dry Smoke)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A classification system restoration professionals use to describe smoke
  residue by its source and behavior: protein smoke from a low-heat, often
  kitchen-related fire leaves a nearly invisible but strong-smelling residue;
  wet smoke from a slow, smoldering fire leaves sticky, smearing residue; and
  dry smoke from a fast, high-heat fire leaves a powdery residue that's easier
  to clean.
whyItMatters: >
  Each category calls for a different cleaning chemistry and approach, so
  correctly identifying the smoke type up front prevents wasted labor on a
  method that doesn't work for that residue.
example: >
  A restoration estimator identifies protein smoke residue after a stovetop
  grease fire, despite little visible soot, and specifies an enzyme-based
  cleaning approach targeted at the invisible but odor-intensive protein
  residue rather than a standard soot-removal method.
careerAngle: >
  Fire-restoration technicians need to correctly classify smoke type before
  choosing cleaning chemistry, since using a dry-smoke method on a wet-smoke
  residue typically smears it rather than removing it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes judge smoke damage severity by how much soot is visible, when
  protein smoke from a low-heat fire can leave minimal visible residue but an
  intense, hard-to-remove odor.
