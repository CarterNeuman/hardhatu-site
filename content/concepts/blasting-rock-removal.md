id: concept-blasting-rock-removal
title: Blasting & Rock Removal
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  The controlled use of explosives to fracture and remove solid rock that
  can't practically be excavated with conventional earthmoving equipment, a
  specialized scope requiring its own licensed blaster, a detailed blast
  plan, and careful monitoring of vibration and flyaway debris to protect
  nearby structures and people.
whyItMatters: >
  Blasting vibration is regulated by specific limits, often expressed as
  peak particle velocity, to protect nearby structures, and a contractor who
  blasts without proper seismic monitoring and pre-blast surveys of nearby
  buildings has no defense against a legitimate damage claim, even a small
  one, since there's no baseline documentation showing the structure's
  condition before blasting began.
example: >
  A highway contractor conducts a pre-blast survey documenting the condition
  of nearby homes before beginning rock removal, then monitors vibration
  levels at each blast with seismographs to confirm they stay within the
  permitted limit.
careerAngle: >
  A licensed blaster plans and executes the blast. A
  [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] or
  [[career-heavy-civil-estimator|Heavy Civil Estimator]] accounts for
  blasting's real cost and schedule impact when rock is identified during
  the geotechnical investigation.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor skips the pre-blast survey of a nearby building to save time
  and cost, assuming the vibration limits alone are protection enough. A
  homeowner later reports new cracking and claims it was caused by the
  blasting, and without a documented pre-blast condition survey, the
  contractor has no way to prove the cracks weren't already there before
  work began.
