title: Lead-Based Paint Remediation
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "02, Existing Conditions"
definition: >
  Under the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Rule, any work that
  disturbs painted surfaces in housing or child-occupied facilities built
  before 1978 -- when lead-based paint was banned -- must be performed by an
  EPA lead-safe certified firm using specific containment and cleanup
  practices, unless testing proves the paint isn't lead-based.
whyItMatters: >
  Lead dust created by sanding, cutting, or demolishing painted surfaces is a
  serious health hazard, especially for children, and the RRP Rule makes using
  certified, lead-safe practices a legal requirement rather than a best
  practice on covered buildings.
example: >
  A contractor renovating a kitchen in a 1960s house uses plastic sheeting to
  contain the work area, wet-sands rather than dry-sands the old trim, and
  HEPA-vacuums the debris, documenting the lead-safe practices required
  because the home predates the 1978 ban.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-environmental-remediation-specialist|Environmental Remediation
  Specialist]] or [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] confirms RRP
  certification is current and containment practices are followed before
  renovation work begins on a pre-1978 structure.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A remodeling crew dry-sands painted trim in a pre-1978 home without
  confirming the paint's lead content or using RRP-required containment,
  spreading lead dust through the home's ventilation system and triggering a
  costly re-cleaning and disclosure obligation.
