---
id: concept-motor-grader
title: Motor Grader
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "31, Earthwork"
definition: >
  A piece of heavy equipment with a long, adjustable blade mounted between its
  front and rear axles, used to cut precise, exact final elevations and slopes
  across a site, the finishing step after rougher equipment has already moved
  most of the material into place.
whyItMatters: >
  A bulldozer gets material roughly into position quickly, but a motor grader
  is what actually achieves the exact elevations and slopes a civil design
  calls for. Its long blade and fine-adjustment controls let an operator cut a
  surface flat and true within a fraction of an inch, which is impossible with
  equipment built for speed instead of precision.
example: >
  Once a bulldozer has rough-graded a parking lot area, an operator uses a
  motor grader with GPS machine control to cut the final surface to the exact
  elevations and slopes the civil drawings specify, so that stormwater drains
  correctly once paving goes down.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] runs a motor grader
  during final grading, where the tolerances are tight enough that small
  errors can cause real drainage problems later.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A motor grader operator cuts a lot's final grade slightly off the design's
  specified slope, assuming it's close enough. Once paving is complete, water
  doesn't drain the way the design intended, pooling in a spot that wasn't
  supposed to collect water at all.
