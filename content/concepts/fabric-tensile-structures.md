---
id: concept-fabric-tensile-structures
title: Fabric & Tensile Structures
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "13, Special Construction"
definition: >
  Lightweight structures using a tensioned fabric membrane supported by a
  steel or cable frame, used for canopies, stadium roof sections, and
  large-span open-air coverings, engineered for wind and snow load differently
  than a rigid roof assembly.
whyItMatters: >
  A tensile membrane's engineering depends on the fabric staying under the
  correct tension across its whole span, and a structure designed for one wind
  or snow load region can fail if built somewhere with different conditions
  without re-engineering the tensioning.
example: >
  A structural engineer re-calculates a tensile canopy's cable tensioning and
  anchor loads for a project's actual local wind zone, rather than reusing the
  tensioning specs from a similar structure built in a different region.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] engineers the
  tensioning and anchor system, and a specialty fabric-structure subcontractor
  typically self-installs the membrane under that engineering.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A tensile canopy's anchor points are installed using a generic spec sheet
  rather than site-specific wind load calculations, and the fabric membrane
  develops visible slack and flutter during the first windstorm after
  installation.
relatedIds:
  - career-structural-engineer
