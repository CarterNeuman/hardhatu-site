---
id: concept-cm-as-advisor
title: Construction Manager as Advisor (Agency CM)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A project delivery approach where a construction manager is hired purely as the
  owner's paid advisor, providing scheduling, cost, and constructability advice
  throughout design and construction, without ever holding the actual construction
  contract or taking on financial risk for the work, unlike a
  [[concept-cm-at-risk|CM at Risk]].
whyItMatters: >
  Because an agency CM never converts into the contractor and never guarantees a
  price, they have no financial incentive pulling against the owner's interests:
  their only job is to advise. That's different from CM at Risk, where the CM
  eventually takes on construction risk themselves, which can create a subtle
  conflict between advising the owner and protecting their own upcoming exposure.
example: >
  A university hires a construction manager purely in an advisory role to help plan a
  multi-building campus expansion, while separate general contractors are hired to
  actually hold the construction contracts and perform the work.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] role overlaps closely
  with agency CM: both work purely on the owner's behalf without taking on
  construction risk themselves. A
  [[career-preconstruction-manager|Preconstruction Manager]] on the contractor side
  interacts with an agency CM as an independent advisor reviewing their numbers,
  rather than as a construction manager who might eventually become the GC.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An owner assumes an agency CM's cost estimates carry the same weight as a
  guaranteed maximum price, since the terminology sounds similar to CM at Risk. When
  actual construction costs come in higher than the agency CM's advisory estimate,
  the owner has no contractual recourse against the CM: an agency CM never
  guaranteed the number in the first place, unlike a CM at Risk who would be
  financially on the hook for the difference.
