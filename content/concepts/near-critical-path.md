---
id: concept-near-critical-path
title: Near-Critical Path
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Activities that aren't technically on the critical path but carry only a small
  amount of float, putting them at real risk of becoming critical if even a
  minor delay eats up that cushion.
whyItMatters: >
  A schedule that only tracks the single critical path can miss a second
  sequence of work quietly running out of room, so experienced schedulers
  watch a band of near-critical activities too, since any of them can become
  the new critical path with surprisingly little warning.
example: >
  A project's official critical path runs through structural steel, but a
  separate mechanical rough-in sequence is running with only three days of
  float. A minor equipment delivery delay eats up that float entirely,
  instantly making mechanical rough-in the new critical path the schedule
  update didn't see coming until it had already happened.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] flags near-critical activities in every
  schedule update so the [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] can watch
  them as closely as the official critical path, since either one slipping
  threatens the finish date.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team focuses exclusively on protecting the official critical path
  shown on the schedule, while a near-critical sequence quietly loses its
  remaining float and becomes the real threat to the finish date without
  anyone noticing until it's already causing delay.
relatedIds:
  - career-scheduler
  - career-project-manager
---
