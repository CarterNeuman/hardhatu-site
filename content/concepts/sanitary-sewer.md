---
id: concept-sanitary-sewer
title: Sanitary Sewer
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "33, Utilities"
definition: >
  The underground gravity or pressure piping that carries a building's wastewater
  from its connection point to the municipal sewer system (or a septic system where no
  municipal sewer is available), sized and sloped to the local plumbing code's exact
  requirements for gravity flow.
whyItMatters: >
  A sanitary sewer line that isn't sloped correctly won't fully self-clean during
  normal flow, letting solids settle and eventually clog the line, a problem that's
  completely invisible from the surface and often isn't discovered until a backup
  happens well after the site is paved over.
example: >
  A site's sanitary sewer line is installed at the minimum slope the plumbing code
  requires for its pipe size, verified with a laser level during installation and
  confirmed again with a video inspection before backfill covers the pipe for good.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] or utility contractor installs and tests the
  underground sanitary sewer connection between the building and the municipal main.
  An [[career-equipment-operator|Equipment Operator]] excavates and backfills the
  sewer trench to the depth and bedding the plumbing code and manufacturer both
  require.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A sanitary sewer line is installed slightly flatter than the code-required minimum
  slope to avoid a deeper, more expensive excavation, and the discrepancy isn't
  caught before backfill. Within the building's first year of occupancy, solids
  settle in the under-sloped section and cause a backup that requires excavating back
  into the finished site to locate and correct.
