id: concept-violation-classification
title: Violation Classification (Willful, Serious, De Minimis)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The system OSHA uses to categorize citations by severity, from de minimis, a
  technical violation with no real safety impact, up through
  other-than-serious, serious, and willful, a knowing and intentional
  violation, each carrying a different penalty ceiling, with serious and
  other-than-serious violations capped at $16,550 and willful or repeated
  violations capped far higher at $165,514 per violation as of 2026.
whyItMatters: >
  The classification an inspector assigns, not just the underlying hazard,
  determines whether a citation is a minor paperwork item or a six-figure
  liability, so understanding how OSHA draws these lines matters as much as
  understanding the hazard itself.
example: >
  Two jobsites have the identical missing-guardrail violation, but one gets
  cited as serious because the employer had no fall protection program at all,
  while the other gets cited as willful because a prior inspection had already
  flagged and fined the exact same hazard and the employer never fixed it.
careerAngle: >
  Safety managers should document every corrective action taken after a
  citation, since an uncorrected repeat violation is exactly what turns a
  serious citation into a far more expensive willful or repeated one on the
  next inspection.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Employers sometimes assume a violation's classification is just about how
  dangerous the hazard looks, when OSHA actually weighs the employer's
  knowledge and intent heavily, meaning the same physical hazard can be cited
  very differently depending on what the employer knew and did about it.
