title: Statute of Limitations vs. Statute of Repose
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  Two different legal deadlines for construction defect claims. A statute of
  limitations runs from when a defect is discovered, or reasonably should have
  been, while a statute of repose runs from a fixed event, typically
  [[concept-substantial-completion|substantial completion]], and cuts off claims
  after that period regardless of when a defect is later discovered.
whyItMatters: >
  A statute of repose can bar a claim even when the statute of limitations would
  still allow it, because the repose period doesn't care when the defect was
  actually found, only how long ago the building was substantially complete.
example: >
  A structural defect is discovered eight years after a building's substantial
  completion. The statute of limitations would still allow a claim since the
  defect was just discovered, but the state's statute of repose cuts off
  construction claims entirely after seven years from substantial completion,
  barring the claim regardless.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-construction-attorney|Construction Attorney]] checks both deadlines
  before pursuing any defect claim, since missing either one, not just the more
  familiar limitations period, can bar an otherwise valid case.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A property owner discovers a serious latent defect and assumes they have time
  to sue simply because they just found it, within the statute of limitations'
  discovery window. They don't realize the state's statute of repose already
  expired years earlier, cutting off the claim entirely regardless of when the
  defect was found.
