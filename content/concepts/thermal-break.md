---
id: concept-thermal-break
title: Thermal Break
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  A strip of low-conductivity material, often a rigid polymer, set into an
  aluminum window or curtain wall frame to physically interrupt the metal's
  path, since aluminum conducts heat and cold extremely efficiently on its
  own.
whyItMatters: >
  Without a thermal break, an aluminum frame carries outside temperature
  straight through to the interior surface, which in cold weather drops that
  interior surface cold enough for moisture in the room's air to condense on
  it. The thermal break is what keeps a window or curtain wall's interior
  frame surface closer to room temperature.
example: >
  A curtain wall system uses extruded aluminum framing with a polyamide
  thermal break set between its exterior and interior sections, preventing the
  cold exterior aluminum from conducting directly through to the interior
  frame surface during winter.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-glazier|Glazier]] installs curtain wall and window systems
  engineered with thermal breaks, and has to maintain that thermal separation
  carefully during fabrication and installation rather than bridging it
  accidentally with a metal fastener.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An installer uses a metal fastener that accidentally bridges straight across
  a frame's thermal break while securing a piece of hardware. That single
  bridge point conducts outside temperature through anyway, creating a cold
  spot where condensation forms even though the rest of the frame performs
  correctly.
