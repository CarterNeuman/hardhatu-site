id: concept-supplement-claims
title: Supplement (Claims)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  An additional claim submitted to an insurer after the original scope of loss was
  approved, covering damage that was hidden, discovered mid-repair, or otherwise
  missed during the initial inspection, the insurance claim world's version of a
  [[concept-change-order|change order]].
whyItMatters: >
  Concealed damage (behind drywall, under flooring, inside a wall cavity) is
  common enough in restoration work that supplements aren't unusual exceptions,
  they're a routine part of the process, and a contractor who doesn't document and
  submit them promptly risks doing extra work without ever getting paid for it.
example: >
  During demolition for a water damage repair, a contractor discovers additional
  mold growth behind a wall that wasn't visible during the original inspection,
  documents it with photos, and submits a supplement request to the insurer before
  proceeding with that additional scope.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-restoration-project-manager|Restoration Project Manager]] documents
  concealed damage the moment it's found and prepares the supplement request before
  covering the area back up. An [[career-insurance-adjuster|Insurance Adjuster]]
  reviews supplement requests against photo documentation to confirm the additional
  damage is both real and actually related to the original covered loss.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A restoration crew discovers additional water damage during demolition but keeps
  working without pausing to document it or submit a supplement request, assuming
  it will get sorted out later. By the time the supplement is submitted, the area
  has already been repaired, and the insurer disputes paying for work it can no
  longer independently verify.
