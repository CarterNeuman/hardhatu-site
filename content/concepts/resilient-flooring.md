id: concept-resilient-flooring
title: Resilient Flooring
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  Non-textile, non-masonry floor coverings that stay somewhat flexible
  underfoot, vinyl composition tile (VCT), luxury vinyl tile or plank (LVT and
  LVP), rubber, and linoleum among the common types, each with different
  durability, moisture tolerance, and maintenance requirements, distinct from
  carpet or hard tile.
whyItMatters: >
  Resilient flooring's performance depends heavily on the subfloor's moisture
  condition, installing it over a concrete slab that hasn't cured long enough
  or hasn't been tested for moisture emission can cause the adhesive to fail
  and the flooring to bubble or delaminate within months.
example: >
  A flooring contractor tests a new concrete slab's moisture emission rate
  before installing VCT, confirming it falls within the flooring
  manufacturer's specified limit rather than assuming the slab is dry enough
  simply because it looks and feels dry to the touch.
careerAngle: >
  A specialty flooring installer places resilient flooring, testing subfloor
  moisture before starting. A [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] may
  require documented moisture test results before flooring installation is
  approved to proceed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor installs LVT flooring over a concrete slab without testing its
  moisture emission rate, assuming enough time has passed since the pour.
  Trapped moisture vapor breaks down the adhesive bond within a few months, and
  large sections of flooring begin bubbling and lifting, requiring the
  flooring to be removed and reinstalled once the slab actually dries out.
