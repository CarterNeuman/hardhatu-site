---
id: concept-stop-work-authority
title: Stop Work Authority
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The right, and expectation, that any worker on a jobsite, regardless of position or
  seniority, can halt work on the spot when they see an imminent safety hazard, without
  fear of reprisal. Distinct from a [[concept-stop-work-order|Stop Work Order]], which is
  a formal directive typically issued by an owner, inspector, or safety authority rather
  than exercised by an individual worker in the moment.
whyItMatters: >
  A hazard spotted in real time is only useful if someone actually feels safe stopping
  the work to address it. Stop work authority only functions as intended if the culture
  genuinely backs up a worker who uses it: a policy that exists on paper but punishes
  people for invoking it isn't real stop work authority at all.
example: >
  A laborer notices a scaffold missing a guardrail section and stops work on that
  scaffold immediately, before anyone else climbs it, rather than waiting to flag it
  through normal channels first.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-laborer|Construction Laborer]] is exactly the kind of worker this authority
  is designed to empower, someone close enough to the hazard to see it first, regardless
  of their position on the crew. A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] sets and
  reinforces the culture that makes stop work authority something workers actually feel
  safe using.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A worker notices a hazard but hesitates to invoke stop work authority, worried about
  being seen as slowing down the schedule or causing friction with a supervisor. The
  hesitation costs exactly the time that using the authority immediately would have
  saved, and a preventable incident happens in the gap.
