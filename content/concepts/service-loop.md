---
id: concept-service-loop
title: Service Loop
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  A deliberate extra length of slack wire coiled up behind an electrical box
  or device during rough-in, left there specifically so a future repair, a bad
  connection, or a device replacement, doesn't come up a few critical inches
  short.
whyItMatters: >
  Once drywall closes a wall in, there's no easy way to pull more wire into a
  box if the original length turns out to be too short. A service loop is
  cheap insurance against that problem, costing almost nothing to leave in
  during rough-in but potentially saving a wall from having to be opened up
  later.
example: >
  An electrician installing an outlet box leaves several extra inches of slack
  wire coiled behind the box rather than cutting the wire to the exact minimum
  length needed, so that if the device ever needs to be reconnected or
  replaced, there's enough wire to work with.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] builds service loops into rough-in
  work as standard practice, since it's a small habit that prevents a much
  bigger problem after walls are finished.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrician cuts wire to the bare minimum length needed at every box to
  save material, with no service loop left behind. When a device later needs
  to be reconnected after a repair, there isn't enough wire left to work with,
  and the wall has to be opened up to extend it.
