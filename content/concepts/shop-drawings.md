id: concept-shop-drawings
title: Shop Drawings
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  Detailed drawings prepared by a subcontractor, supplier, or fabricator showing exactly
  how they intend to build or install their specific piece of the project, the
  fabrication-level detail that goes well beyond what the architect's original drawings
  show.
whyItMatters: >
  An architect's drawings show design intent; they don't show every bolt, weld, and
  connection a fabricator actually needs to build something. Shop drawings are the
  translation from "here's what it should look like" to "here's exactly how we're going
  to build it," and someone on the design team has to catch mistakes here before
  fabrication starts.
example: >
  A structural steel fabricator produces shop drawings showing the exact size, length,
  and connection details for every beam and column on a project, far more specific than
  the architect's original structural drawings, for the structural engineer to review
  before any steel gets cut.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] works directly from approved shop drawings on-site,
  since they show the exact connection details the original design drawings never
  specified. A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] tracks the shop drawing
  review as part of the broader [[concept-submittal|submittal]] process, chasing down
  approvals before fabrication can start.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A fabricator starts cutting steel from their own shop drawings before the structural
  engineer actually approves them, trying to save time. The engineer's review catches an
  error in a connection detail after the pieces are already fabricated, and the
  fabricator has to eat the cost of correcting steel that was built to unapproved
  drawings.
