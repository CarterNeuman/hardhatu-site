---
id: concept-flashing
title: Flashing
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "07, Thermal & Moisture Protection"
definition: >
  Thin sheet material, such as metal, rubberized asphalt, or plastic, installed at any point
  in a building's exterior envelope where two different materials or planes meet
  (roof-to-wall intersections, window and door openings, wall penetrations),
  specifically to direct water away from the joint instead of letting it collect and
  find its way inside.
whyItMatters: >
  Nearly every serious water intrusion problem in a building traces back to a flashing
  detail that was missing, installed in the wrong sequence, or installed backward.
  Flashing works almost entirely on the principle of layering pieces so water always
  flows down and out over the next piece below it, and getting that sequence wrong
  defeats the whole system even if every individual piece looks correctly installed.
example: >
  At a window opening, flashing is installed at the sill first, then the window
  itself, then flashing at the jambs and finally the head, each piece overlapping the
  one below it like shingles, so water draining down the wall always sheds over the
  next flashing piece rather than behind it.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-carpenter|Carpenter]] or [[career-roofer|Roofer]] installs flashing at
  the specific location and has to follow the correct lapping sequence exactly, since
  flashing installed out of order can direct water inward rather than outward. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] inspects flashing details
  at windows, roof intersections, and other critical junctions before they're covered
  by siding, roofing, or other finishes.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew installs window flashing out of the correct sequence: the siding above the
  window gets installed first and tucked behind the head flashing instead of the
  flashing lapping over it, and the mistake isn't visible once the siding is
  finished. Water that runs down the wall during a storm gets directed behind the
  flashing instead of over it, leaking into the wall cavity around that window for
  years before the resulting damage becomes visible inside.
