---
id: concept-architect
title: Architect
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The design professional, licensed by the state, responsible for a project's design
  and construction documents, and often for administering the contract on the
  owner's behalf during construction by reviewing submittals, answering RFIs, and
  certifying payment applications and substantial completion.
whyItMatters: >
  The architect isn't just the designer: on most traditional projects, they also act
  as a kind of referee during construction, interpreting the drawings and specs and
  certifying to the owner that the work actually matches what was designed. That dual
  role shapes how RFIs, submittals, and change orders all get resolved.
example: >
  A contractor submits an RFI asking how a detail should work where two different wall
  types meet. The architect's answer becomes the official interpretation of the
  drawings for that condition, not just a suggestion, but a binding clarification.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] routes RFIs and submittals to the
  architect constantly, since keeping that back-and-forth moving is a core part of the
  job. A [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] sometimes takes over
  parts of the architect's traditional contract-administration role on a project,
  depending on how the owner structures their team.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor proceeds with a field decision without waiting for the architect's
  official answer to an open RFI, assuming their own interpretation is obviously
  correct. The architect's eventual answer contradicts what was already built, and the
  contractor has to redo the work at their own cost, since acting ahead of an official
  interpretation was their choice.
