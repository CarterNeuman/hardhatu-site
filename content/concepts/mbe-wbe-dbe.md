---
id: concept-mbe-wbe-dbe
title: MBE/WBE/DBE (Minority/Women/Disadvantaged Business Enterprise)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  Certification categories, Minority Business Enterprise (MBE), Women Business
  Enterprise (WBE), and Disadvantaged Business Enterprise (DBE), that qualify a
  business for participation goals and set-asides on
  [[concept-public-work-vs-private-work|public]] and some private projects, intended
  to increase contracting opportunities for historically underrepresented business
  owners.
whyItMatters: >
  Many public projects (and some private owners) set specific participation goals
  requiring a percentage of the contract value to go to certified MBE/WBE/DBE firms, so
  a general contractor bidding that work has to actively identify and include qualified
  certified subcontractors in their bid; failing to meet the goal, or failing to
  document a genuine good-faith effort to meet it, can disqualify a bid entirely on
  public work.
example: >
  A general contractor bidding on a publicly funded transit project has to demonstrate
  that at least a specified percentage of the subcontracted work will go to certified
  DBE firms, requiring the contractor to identify and solicit bids from certified
  subcontractors as part of putting the bid together.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] maintains relationships with
  certified MBE/WBE/DBE subcontractors specifically so the company can meet
  participation goals on public bids without scrambling to find qualified firms at the
  last minute. An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] has to build documented outreach to
  certified firms into the bid preparation timeline on any project with a participation
  goal, since the outreach itself is part of what has to be proven.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor submits a bid on a public project with a DBE participation goal but
  can't document a genuine good-faith effort to solicit certified subcontractors,
  having only reached out to one firm at the last minute. The awarding agency rejects
  the bid as non-responsive for failing to meet the participation requirement, even
  though the contractor's base price was otherwise competitive.
