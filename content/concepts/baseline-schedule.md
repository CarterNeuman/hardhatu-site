---
id: concept-baseline-schedule
title: Baseline Schedule
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The original, approved project schedule established near the start of construction,
  kept unchanged afterward specifically so actual progress can be measured against it,
  the fixed reference point for "were we ahead of or behind where we planned to be."
whyItMatters: >
  A schedule that gets silently rewritten every time the project falls behind stops
  being useful as a way to measure performance: there's no "behind" if the goalposts
  keep moving. The baseline schedule is what lets everyone agree on how much of a delay
  is real and who's responsible for it.
example: >
  A project's baseline schedule shows framing complete by week 12. When framing actually
  finishes in week 15, that three-week gap against the baseline becomes the starting
  point for figuring out what caused the delay and whether it qualifies for a schedule
  extension.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-scheduler|Scheduler]] establishes the baseline schedule early in the
  project and formally tracks the working schedule against it every reporting period,
  rather than replacing it. A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] relies on the
  baseline as the reference point in any dispute over who caused a delay, since it's the
  agreed-upon plan both sides signed off on.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A scheduler quietly resets the baseline schedule every time the project falls behind,
  rather than tracking variance against the original plan. By the end of the project,
  there's no clear record of how much total delay occurred or when it happened, which
  becomes a serious problem when the owner disputes a request for additional time and
  money.
