---
id: concept-structural-bay
title: Structural Bay
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  A rectangular section of a building's structural frame bounded by a set of
  columns on each side, the basic repeating unit a steel or concrete frame is
  built from, one bay at a time, across the whole structure.
whyItMatters: >
  This is a construction term of art, not the everyday meaning of "bay" as a
  body of water, and it's the unit a steel erection crew actually thinks and
  plans in: once one bay's columns, beams, and bracing are plumbed and
  connected, the crew moves on and repeats the same sequence in the next bay
  across the building.
example: >
  A steel erection crew fully plumbs, aligns, and permanently bolts up every
  connection in one structural bay before moving the crane and crew to erect
  the next bay over, rather than setting loose steel across the whole building
  before finalizing any of it.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] thinks of steel erection in terms of
  bays, since a crew typically can't move on to permanently connecting the
  next bay until the current one is fully plumbed and squared.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew sets steel loosely across several bays at once to keep the crane
  moving efficiently, planning to go back and plumb and finalize each bay
  afterward. Without one bay locked into its final, true position first,
  errors compound across the loosely set bays and become much harder to track
  down and fix.
