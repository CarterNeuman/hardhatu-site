id: concept-climate-resilience-design
title: Climate Resilience Design
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Designing a building to withstand more frequent or more severe
  climate-related events,     such as flooding, extreme heat, high winds, or
  wildfire, than the historical baseline the     original building code's
  requirements were set against, anticipating conditions the     structure is
  likely to face over its service life rather than only the conditions it was
  originally coded for.
whyItMatters: >
  Building codes are typically written against historical climate data, which
  means a     structure built to the current code minimum may already be
  under-designed for the more     extreme conditions it's statistically likely
  to face partway through its service life,     especially in coastal,
  wildfire-prone, or flood-prone regions.
example: >
  A coastal multifamily building's design team elevates the mechanical
  equipment above the     code-minimum flood elevation and adds an additional
  freeboard margin, anticipating that     projected sea-level rise over the
  building's service life will make today's minimum flood     elevation
  inadequate well before the building is due for its next major renovation.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] incorporates resilience design
  above code     minimums when a client's insurance underwriter or a
  jurisdiction's updated climate     projections indicate the baseline code
  requirement is already outdated for that specific     site.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A design team treats current code minimums as the ceiling of what's
  reasonable to design     for, when a code minimum reflects historical
  climate data and a site's actual risk can     already exceed it, especially
  for a building expected to remain in service for 50 years or     more.
