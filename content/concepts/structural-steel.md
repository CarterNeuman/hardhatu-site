id: concept-structural-steel
title: Structural Steel
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "05, Metals"
definition: >
  The steel beams, columns, and connections that form a building's structural frame,
  engineered, fabricated off-site to precise dimensions, then erected and connected
  (bolted or welded) on the jobsite in a sequence that has to keep the partially built
  frame stable at every stage.
whyItMatters: >
  A steel frame is only as good as its connections, and steel erection is one of the
  more hazardous phases of construction: pieces are heavy, the frame is unstable
  until enough of it is connected, and OSHA has specific fall protection and erection
  sequencing requirements just for structural steel work.
example: >
  A steel fabricator produces beams and columns from approved shop drawings, and an
  ironworker erection crew bolts and welds them into place following an engineered
  erection sequence that keeps the partially assembled frame braced and stable at each
  stage.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-ironworker|Ironworker]] connects steel members in the field, working at
  height on a structure that isn't fully stable until enough connections are made. A
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] oversees structural
  [[concept-special-inspection|special inspections]] of critical bolted and welded
  connections, since a bad connection is a life-safety issue, not just a quality one.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An erection crew gets ahead of the engineered sequence to save time, connecting
  upper-level steel before enough of the lower frame is fully braced. A wind gust
  catches the undersupported frame, and a section shifts out of plumb before it can be
  caught, a hazard and a costly correction that following the engineered sequence
  would have prevented.
