---
id: concept-fast-tracking
title: Fast-Tracking
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A scheduling strategy of starting construction on early phases of a project before the
  design for later phases is fully finished, overlapping design and construction
  instead of waiting for one to completely finish before the other begins.
whyItMatters: >
  Waiting for 100% complete design before starting any construction is the safest
  approach, but also the slowest. Fast-tracking can save real time on a schedule-driven
  project, but it trades that speed for real risk, building against a design that isn't
  finished yet.
example: >
  A developer under pressure to open a building by a fixed date has the contractor start
  foundation and structural steel work while the architect is still finalizing interior
  finish selections for upper floors, construction and design running in parallel
  instead of sequentially.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] weighs whether fast-tracking a project is
  worth the added coordination risk, usually at the owner's request to hit an aggressive
  deadline. A [[career-preconstruction-manager|Preconstruction Manager]] has to
  structure the [[concept-buyout|buyout]] packages differently on a fast-tracked job,
  since trades get bought out and started in phases rather than all at once against a
  complete design.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A team fast-tracks foundation work before the structural engineer finalizes the final
  steel design. The completed foundation doesn't match a late structural revision that
  adds additional column loads, forcing an expensive and disruptive foundation
  modification after the concrete has already cured.
