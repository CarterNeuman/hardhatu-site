---
id: concept-icra
title: Infection Control Risk Assessment (ICRA)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A formal, written assessment, required by the Joint Commission and most state
  health departments for construction or renovation inside an occupied healthcare
  facility, that rates the infection-control risk of the work and specifies the
  containment measures (barriers, negative-air machines, HEPA filtration, air
  pressure monitoring) the project has to use before it starts.
whyItMatters: >
  Ordinary construction dust is mostly a cleanup problem. In a hospital, the same
  dust can carry mold spores or bacteria directly into the air that
  immunocompromised patients breathe, so the containment has to be engineered and
  verified in advance, not improvised once work is already underway.
example: >
  Before cutting into a ceiling two floors above an oncology unit, the project
  team completes an ICRA that rates the work as high-risk given its proximity to
  immunocompromised patients, requiring sealed construction barriers and
  continuously monitored negative-air pressure for the duration of the work.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-healthcare-construction-manager|Healthcare Construction Manager]]
  develops and enforces ICRA procedures for every phase of work near clinical
  areas, coordinating directly with the hospital's own infection control staff
  before anything starts. A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] on a
  healthcare project verifies containment barriers and air pressure monitoring
  stay in place and functioning throughout construction, not just on day one.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew sets up dust barriers for a ceiling renovation but doesn't maintain
  negative air pressure continuously, since the monitoring equipment keeps
  tripping a false alarm and gets unplugged to stop the noise. Airborne
  particulates drift past the barrier into an adjacent patient corridor for
  several days before anyone notices the monitor was disconnected, triggering a
  hospital-wide infection control investigation.
