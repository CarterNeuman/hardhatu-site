id: concept-national-electrical-code
title: National Electrical Code (NEC)
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  The model electrical safety code, published and updated every three years,
  that individual states and municipalities adopt, sometimes with local
  amendments, as the legal standard electrical work in their jurisdiction has
  to meet.
whyItMatters: >
  Nearly every specific electrical requirement discussed on a jobsite, from
  box fill limits to GFCI and AFCI placement to solar interconnection rules,
  traces back to a specific section of the NEC. Electricians and inspectors
  both work from the same code, which is what makes electrical inspection
  consistent and enforceable from one jurisdiction to the next.
example: >
  An electrician references the current NEC edition adopted by the local
  jurisdiction when planning a rough-in, since an older or newer edition can
  have different requirements for the same type of installation.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] works to the NEC on every job, and a
  building inspector checks electrical work specifically against the NEC
  edition the local jurisdiction has formally adopted.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrician used to working in a jurisdiction on an older NEC edition
  takes a job in a jurisdiction that has already adopted a newer edition with
  stricter requirements, and plans the rough-in to the older rules out of
  habit. An inspector cites several code violations that wouldn't have been
  violations under the edition the electrician was used to.
