---
id: concept-job-order-contracting
title: Job Order Contracting (JOC)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  An indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity procurement method where an
  owner pre-negotiates unit prices with a contractor through a competitively
  bid coefficient, then issues individual job orders drawing from that
  pre-priced catalog for a stream of smaller repair and renovation projects
  over a multi-year term, without rebidding each one.
whyItMatters: >
  The coefficient bid a contractor submits to win the JOC contract locks in
  its pricing for every job order issued over the entire contract term, so an
  aggressively low bid to win the contract can turn every individual job order
  that follows into a loss.
example: >
  A school district awards a three-year JOC contract to a contractor after a
  competitive coefficient bid, then issues individual job orders for
  classroom renovations and repairs throughout that term, each priced against
  the pre-negotiated catalog rather than separately bid.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-estimator|Estimator]] prices the coefficient bid that determines
  profitability for the entire contract term, while a
  [[career-procurement-manager|Procurement Manager]] administers the ongoing
  stream of individual job orders issued against it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor wins a JOC coefficient bid too aggressively low just to secure
  the multi-year contract, then loses money on nearly every individual job
  order priced against that catalog for years, since the coefficient is locked
  in for the full contract term regardless of how the market shifts.
