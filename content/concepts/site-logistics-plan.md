id: concept-site-logistics-plan
title: Site Logistics Plan
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A drawing and plan, developed before or during mobilization, showing exactly how the
  physical jobsite will be organized during construction, such as crane locations,
  material staging areas, site access routes, parking, and
  [[concept-temporary-facilities|temporary facilities]], so the site functions safely
  and efficiently once work is underway.
whyItMatters: >
  A cramped urban site with one delivery gate and nowhere to stage steel is a very
  different logistics problem than an open suburban lot, and getting it wrong doesn't
  show up until dozens of trucks and crews are already trying to use the space at once.
  Planning it on paper first avoids that chaos.
example: >
  A site logistics plan for a tight downtown high-rise project designates a single
  curb-side loading zone with a strict delivery schedule, since there's no room on-site
  to stage more than a day's worth of material at a time.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] develops and enforces the site logistics
  plan, since they're the one who has to make the actual site work day to day once
  trades are on-site. A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] often helps draft
  the plan and coordinates getting it approved by the owner or local authorities when
  public space (like a street closure) is involved.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A superintendent doesn't finalize a site logistics plan before mobilization, assuming
  the crew will "figure it out." Once framing starts, the material delivery trucks and
  the concrete pump truck end up needing the same access point at the same time, and the
  resulting standoff burns half a day that a five-minute plan could have prevented.
