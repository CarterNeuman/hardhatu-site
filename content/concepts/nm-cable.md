id: concept-nm-cable
title: NM Cable (Romex)
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  Nonmetallic sheathed cable, commonly known by the brand name Romex, with
  multiple insulated conductors bundled together inside a single plastic
  jacket, as distinct from conduit, where individual conductors are pulled
  separately through a protective metal or plastic tube.
whyItMatters: >
  Which wiring method a job requires, NM cable or conduit, depends heavily on
  the building type and local code: NM cable is common and economical in
  residential framing, while commercial work and many jurisdictions require
  conduit instead, since conduit offers more physical protection and makes
  future wire changes easier.
example: >
  An electrician wiring a wood-framed house runs NM cable through drilled
  holes in the studs to reach each outlet and switch location, while a
  commercial electrician on a different job pulls individual conductors
  through metal conduit instead, since the commercial building's code requires
  it.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] chooses between NM cable and conduit
  based on the building type, local code, and the specific application, not
  out of simple preference.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrician runs NM cable in an application where local code actually
  requires conduit, assuming the two methods are interchangeable. An inspector
  rejects the rough-in, and the cable has to be pulled out and replaced with
  properly conduited wiring before the job can pass inspection.
