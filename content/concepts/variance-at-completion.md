---
id: concept-variance-at-completion
title: Variance at Completion (VAC)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  The difference between a project's original total budget and its currently
  forecasted final cost, calculated as budget minus Estimate at Completion,
  showing in dollar terms whether the project is now expected to finish under
  or over its original budget.
whyItMatters: >
  Unlike a simple month-to-date budget comparison, VAC looks all the way to the
  project's forecasted finish, so it can flag a cost problem that hasn't shown
  up in spending yet but is already baked into the current trend, giving the
  team time to react before the problem is actually realized in cash spent.
example: >
  A project's original budget is ten million dollars, but its current
  [[concept-cost-performance-index|CPI]] trend puts its Estimate at Completion
  at eleven million. The resulting negative one-million-dollar VAC tells
  leadership the project is on pace to run over budget by that amount if
  nothing changes, well before that overrun actually shows up in a monthly
  spending report.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-cost-engineer|Cost Engineer]] calculates VAC as part of routine
  cost reporting, and the [[career-operations-manager|Operations Manager]]
  uses a negative VAC trend across multiple projects to decide where
  leadership attention is most urgently needed.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project team watches monthly spending stay within budget and assumes
  everything is fine, without calculating VAC against the current cost
  performance trend, missing a forecasted overrun that spending reports alone
  won't reveal until it's already too late to correct.
