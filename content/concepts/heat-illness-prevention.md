id: concept-heat-illness-prevention
title: Heat Illness Prevention
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  Procedures, such as scheduled water and rest breaks, shade access, and acclimatization
  periods for workers new to heat exposure, that protect outdoor construction workers from
  heat-related illness, ranging from heat exhaustion to potentially fatal heat stroke.
whyItMatters: >
  Construction work happens outdoors, often in direct sun and with heavy PPE or physical
  exertion layered on top of the ambient temperature, conditions that can push a worker
  toward heat illness faster than the air temperature alone would suggest, and heat
  illness can progress from manageable to life-threatening quickly if the early warning
  signs are ignored.
example: >
  On a high-heat-index day, a crew shifts to a water-rest-shade schedule with breaks
  every twenty minutes, and a new hire who hasn't yet acclimatized to outdoor summer work
  is assigned lighter tasks during the hottest part of the day.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-laborer|Construction Laborer]] often does the most physically demanding
  outdoor work with the least ability to self-pace around the heat, making employer-set
  breaks and hydration policy directly protective. A [[career-roofer|Roofer]] works on a
  surface that absorbs and radiates heat on top of the ambient temperature, compounding
  the exposure.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew pushes through a scheduled break on a high-heat day to stay on schedule,
  treating the water-rest-shade protocol as a suggestion rather than a requirement. A
  worker's early heat exhaustion symptoms go unrecognized until they progress to a more
  serious condition that a timely break would likely have prevented.
