---
id: concept-storefront-system
title: Storefront System
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "08, Openings"
definition: >
  A ground-floor aluminum-framed glazing system, similar in construction to a
  [[concept-curtain-wall|curtain wall]] but supported from the floor below rather than
  hanging off the structure above, commonly used for retail entrances and
  ground-floor commercial glazing.
whyItMatters: >
  Storefront systems sit at the most heavily trafficked, most visible part of a
  building and are supported differently than a curtain wall, so a design or
  contractor that treats the two as interchangeable can end up detailing structural
  support incorrectly for a system that's actually held up from below rather than
  suspended from above.
example: >
  A retail building's ground-floor storefront system is framed and glazed as its own
  supported assembly bearing on the floor slab below, distinct from the curtain wall
  system above it that hangs from the building's structural frame at each floor.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-engineer|Project Engineer]] coordinates storefront submittals
  and shop drawings similarly to curtain wall, but has to confirm the support
  condition, floor-bearing versus structure-hung, is detailed correctly for this
  specific system. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]]
  inspects the sealant joints and flashing at the base of a storefront system, since
  ground-level glazing takes far more physical abuse and water exposure than
  upper-floor curtain wall.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A storefront system is installed using connection details copied directly from the
  building's curtain wall system one floor above, without adjusting for the fact
  that the storefront actually bears on the floor slab rather than hanging from the
  structure. The mismatched support detail creates unexpected stress on the frame as
  the building settles, cracking sealant joints within the first year.
