title: Window Analysis (Schedule Delay)
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A delay analysis technique that breaks a project's full duration into a series
  of shorter time periods, or windows, and examines how the critical path and
  schedule actually changed within each window, rather than comparing only the
  original baseline schedule to the final as-built result.
whyItMatters: >
  Looking only at the start and end dates can hide which specific events,
  happening in which specific windows, actually caused delay, since several
  different issues may have each eaten up float at different points in the
  project; a window analysis attributes delay to the period and cause where it
  actually occurred.
example: >
  A delay dispute covers an eighteen-month project with several different
  issues: a permit delay early on, a design change mid-project, and a weather
  event near the end. A window analysis breaks the project into several
  shorter periods and shows how the critical path shifted in each one,
  attributing specific delay days to each separate cause rather than lumping
  them all together.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-scheduling-delay-consultant|Scheduling Delay Consultant]]
  performs the window analysis to attribute delay to its actual cause and time
  period, and the [[career-construction-claims-consultant|Construction Claims
  Consultant]] uses that attribution to support or defend a delay claim.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A party tries to prove delay by simply comparing the original baseline
  schedule to the final as-built finish date, without a window analysis
  breaking down which specific period and cause the delay actually came from,
  leaving the claim vulnerable to a challenge over whether a different party
  or event was really responsible.
