---
id: concept-demolition
title: Demolition
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "02, Existing Conditions"
definition: >
  The controlled removal of existing structures, materials, or site improvements to
  prepare a site for new construction, ranging from selective interior demolition to
  full structural teardown, sequenced carefully so remaining structures, utilities, and
  adjacent properties aren't damaged in the process.
whyItMatters: >
  Demolition often uncovers hazardous materials or unknown structural and utility
  conditions the design team didn't know about, and a demolition plan has to account
  for those risks before the first wall comes down, not after.
example: >
  A renovation project's demolition scope requires selectively removing interior
  partitions and finishes while keeping the building's structural steel frame and
  exterior envelope intact for reuse.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] sequences demolition carefully to avoid
  compromising structural elements that need to stay standing, especially on a
  renovation where new work depends on what's kept. An
  [[career-environmental-remediation-specialist|Environmental Remediation Specialist]]
  gets involved before demolition starts on any older building, since hazardous
  material testing has to happen first.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A demolition crew removes a wall assumed to be non-structural based on old drawings,
  only to discover partway through that it was actually load-bearing, requiring
  emergency shoring to keep the floor above from sagging, a mistake a pre-demolition
  structural survey would have caught.
