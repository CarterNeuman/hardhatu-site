id: concept-excess-surplus-lines-insurance
title: Excess & Surplus Lines Insurance
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A segment of the insurance market made up of carriers not licensed as standard
  (admitted) insurers in a given state, who write coverage for risks that
  standard carriers decline, such as high-hazard trades, coastal catastrophe
  exposure, or contractors with a thin claims history.
whyItMatters: >
  Specialty and higher-risk contractors often can't get affordable general
  liability or property coverage in the standard market at all, so
  understanding the excess and surplus lines market is how they get insured.
example: >
  A roofing contractor who was dropped by two standard insurers after
  storm-season claims gets general liability coverage instead through a
  surplus lines carrier, at a higher premium and without the same state
  guaranty-fund protection.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors in high-risk trades, or working in catastrophe-prone regions,
  should expect to shop the surplus lines market and budget for higher
  premiums than standard-market quotes.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume all insurance companies operate under the same state
  guaranty-fund safety net, but non-admitted surplus lines carriers generally
  aren't backed by that fund if the carrier becomes insolvent.
