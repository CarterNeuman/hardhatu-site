---
id: concept-value-engineering
title: Value Engineering
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A systematic review of a project's design looking for ways to reduce cost, through a
  different material, system, or method, without sacrificing the function or quality
  the owner actually needs.
whyItMatters: >
  A project that comes in over budget doesn't always need to be redesigned from scratch
  or scaled back in ways the owner will notice. Value engineering is what finds the
  specific substitutions that save real money while still delivering what the owner
  actually cares about.
example: >
  A project's estimated cost comes in $400,000 over budget. During value engineering,
  the team proposes switching from a custom stone facade to a manufactured stone veneer
  that looks nearly identical but costs far less, closing most of the budget gap without
  changing the building's appearance.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-preconstruction-manager|Preconstruction Manager]] typically leads value
  engineering sessions, bringing in the right people to evaluate each proposed change. An
  [[career-estimator|Estimator]] prices each option quickly so the team can compare real
  savings against real trade-offs before deciding anything.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A team under budget pressure value-engineers a building's insulation down to the bare
  code minimum to hit a savings target, without modeling the long-term energy cost
  impact. The owner saves money upfront but ends up with utility bills far higher than
  expected for the life of the building, a savings decision that quietly cost more than
  it saved.
