title: Elevation Certificate (NFIP)
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  A FEMA document certified by a licensed surveyor or engineer that records a
  building's elevation relative to the base flood elevation for its zone, used
  by the National Flood Insurance Program to rate flood insurance premiums and
  by some communities to verify floodplain building compliance.
whyItMatters: >
  An accurate elevation certificate can substantially lower a property's flood
  insurance premium, so contractors building or elevating structures in flood
  zones should know this document exists and when it's required.
example: >
  After elevating a home's lowest floor above the base flood elevation during a
  flood-damage rebuild, the contractor's client has a new elevation
  certificate completed, which the homeowner's insurer uses to substantially
  reduce the flood policy's premium at renewal.
careerAngle: >
  Contractors doing flood-zone construction or elevation work should coordinate
  with a licensed surveyor on the elevation certificate, since it's what
  unlocks the insurance savings the elevation work is often meant to achieve.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume any elevation work automatically lowers flood
  insurance costs, when the actual premium benefit depends on a new elevation
  certificate being completed and submitted to the insurer.
