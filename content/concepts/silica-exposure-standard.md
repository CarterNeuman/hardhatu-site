id: concept-silica-exposure-standard
title: Silica Exposure Standard
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's regulation (29 CFR 1926.1153) limiting worker exposure to respirable crystalline
  silica dust, the fine dust released when cutting, grinding, or drilling concrete,
  masonry, or stone, and requiring specific engineering controls like wet cutting or
  vacuum dust collection to keep exposure below the legal limit.
whyItMatters: >
  Silica dust is fine enough to lodge deep in the lungs and cause silicosis, an
  irreversible lung disease, along with other serious respiratory harm, damage that
  builds up from repeated exposure over years rather than from any single incident. The
  standard exists because this hazard is easy to underestimate when the dust itself looks
  like an ordinary jobsite nuisance.
example: >
  A crew cutting concrete slab uses a saw with an integrated water suppression system
  instead of cutting dry, keeping the airborne dust, and the crew's silica exposure,
  well below what an unprotected dry cut would produce.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] regularly cuts and finishes concrete in
  ways that generate silica dust and has to use the required wet-cutting or vacuum
  controls as routine practice, not an occasional precaution. A [[career-mason|Mason]]
  faces the same exposure when cutting brick, block, or stone to size.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew cuts concrete dry with a standard saw to save setup time, treating dust
  suppression as optional for a "quick cut." Repeated exposure over many such shortcuts
  adds up to a documented overexposure well before anyone treats the dust itself as a
  serious hazard rather than a cleanup nuisance.
