id: concept-masonry-control-joint
title: Masonry Control Joint
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "04, Masonry"
definition: >
  A vertical joint built into a masonry wall at planned intervals, designed to let the
  wall expand, contract, or crack in a controlled, hidden location rather than
  randomly wherever stress happens to build up.
whyItMatters: >
  Masonry naturally moves with temperature and moisture changes, and without control
  joints placed at the right intervals, that movement shows up as random, unsightly
  cracking through the brick or block itself instead of at an intentional, nearly
  invisible joint designed to absorb it.
example: >
  A long masonry wall includes control joints spaced according to industry guidelines,
  placed at changes in wall height and near corners, so the wall's natural movement
  concentrates at those joints instead of cracking through the field of brick or
  block.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-mason|Mason]] has to build control joints into the wall at the right
  locations as the wall goes up, since retrofitting one into an already-built wall
  isn't really possible. An [[concept-architect|Architect]] specifies control joint
  locations during design, coordinating them with the wall's actual geometry rather
  than leaving it to be figured out in the field.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A long masonry wall is built without control joints at the spacing industry
  guidelines recommend, in an effort to keep the wall's appearance uniform. Within a
  year or two of temperature cycling, the wall develops a random diagonal crack
  through the brick itself, a crack a properly placed control joint would have
  redirected to an intentional, far less visible location.
