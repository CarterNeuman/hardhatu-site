title: Level of Development (LOD) Scale
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A numbered scale, running roughly from LOD 100 to LOD 500, that describes
  how finished and reliable a specific element inside a BIM model actually is,
  from a rough conceptual shape at the low end to a verified as-built element
  at the high end.
whyItMatters: >
  Not every element in a BIM model is equally trustworthy at every stage of a
  project, and the LOD scale is what communicates that difference precisely: a
  team coordinating around a model needs to know whether a given element
  reflects a rough placeholder or verified as-built detail before relying on
  it for a decision.
example: >
  Early in design, a mechanical system appears in the BIM model at LOD 200, a
  rough approximate shape and size, and is progressively refined until it
  reaches LOD 400 for fabrication-level detail and finally LOD 500, reflecting
  the system's actual as-built condition once construction is complete.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-bim-vdc-specialist|BIM/VDC Specialist]] tracks and communicates
  the LOD of different model elements throughout a project, since relying on
  an element before it's reached the appropriate LOD for that decision can
  lead a team astray.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A trade contractor pulls exact dimensions from a model element that's still
  only at LOD 200, a rough placeholder shape, and fabricates components based
  on it as if it were final. The real dimensions change as the model develops
  further, and the fabricated components no longer fit correctly.
