---
id: concept-osha-multi-employer-citation-policy
title: OSHA Multi-Employer Citation Policy
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  An OSHA enforcement policy allowing more than one employer at a shared,
  multi-employer worksite to be cited for the same hazardous condition,
  regardless of which company's employees were actually exposed to it or which
  company created the hazard.
whyItMatters: >
  On a typical construction site, several employers work side by side, and this
  policy means a general contractor with no employees anywhere near a hazard
  can still be cited as the controlling employer if it had the authority and
  opportunity to catch and correct the hazard but didn't.
example: >
  A subcontractor's employee is exposed to an unguarded floor opening the sub
  itself created, but OSHA cites both the subcontractor as the creating
  employer and the general contractor as the controlling employer, since the
  GC had supervisory authority over the site and the means to have caught the
  hazard during its own walkthroughs.
careerAngle: >
  General contractors need routine jobsite walkthroughs specifically to catch
  subcontractor hazards, since this policy means a GC's own safety liability
  doesn't stop at its own direct-hire crews.
whatGoesWrong: >
  Contractors sometimes assume they can't be cited for a hazard their own
  employees never encountered, when a controlling or correcting employer can
  still be held responsible under this policy even with zero of its own
  employees exposed.
