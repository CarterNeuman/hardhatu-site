id: concept-stormwater-detention-retention
title: Stormwater Detention & Retention
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "33, Utilities"
definition: >
  Underground vaults, surface ponds, or other structures designed to
  temporarily hold stormwater runoff on site and release it slowly through a
  controlled outlet, detention, or hold it long-term behind a permanent
  pool, releasing it gradually through evaporation, infiltration, and a
  controlled outlet, retention, reducing the peak volume and rate of water
  leaving a site so it doesn't overwhelm the municipal storm drainage system
  downstream.
whyItMatters: >
  Most municipalities require a site's post-development stormwater runoff
  rate to not exceed what the same site produced before development,
  detention and retention systems are what let a project meet that
  requirement even though paving and roofs replace absorbent natural ground
  with impervious surface.
example: >
  A shopping center's site design includes an underground detention vault
  sized to hold the calculated increase in stormwater runoff from the new
  building and parking lot, releasing it into the municipal storm system at
  a controlled rate that matches the site's pre-development runoff.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-civil-engineer|Civil Engineer]] designs the detention or
  retention system's sizing based on the site's runoff calculations. A
  [[career-qaqc-inspector|QA/QC Inspector]] verifies the system is
  installed to the specified volume and outlet configuration before it's
  buried and inaccessible.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor installs an underground detention vault but the outlet
  control structure is built with a larger orifice than the design
  specifies, letting water drain out faster than the calculated controlled
  rate. The error isn't caught until the next heavy storm, when downstream
  flooding complaints prompt a review that traces the problem back to the
  oversized outlet.
