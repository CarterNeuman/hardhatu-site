id: concept-conduit
title: Conduit
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "26, Electrical"
definition: >
  Rigid or flexible tubing that protects and routes electrical wiring through a
  building's walls, floors, and ceilings, sized specifically for the number and gauge
  of conductors it will carry, a code-governed calculation called conduit fill, not
  just whatever size happens to be on the truck.
whyItMatters: >
  Overfilling a conduit with more or larger wire than its fill rating allows makes it
  harder for heat generated by the conductors to dissipate, a code violation that can
  create a real fire risk hidden inside a wall long after the work is inspected and
  covered.
example: >
  An electrician calculates conduit fill for a run carrying several circuits, sizing
  the conduit to the code-required capacity rather than assuming a commonly used size
  will be sufficient, before pulling wire through it.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-electrician|Electrician]] selects and installs conduit sized correctly
  for its conductor load, a calculation that's easy to get wrong under schedule
  pressure. A [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] spot-checks
  conduit fill on larger electrical rough-in scopes, since an overfilled conduit looks
  identical to a correctly filled one from the outside.
whatGoesWrong: >
  An electrical crew pulls more circuits through an existing conduit run than its fill
  rating allows, reasoning that the conduit still has physical room for the wire. The
  overfill isn't caught during a routine visual inspection, since fill violations
  aren't visible without doing the calculation, and it isn't discovered until a later
  code review flags the as-built conditions.
