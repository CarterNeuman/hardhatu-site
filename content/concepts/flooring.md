id: concept-flooring
title: Flooring
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "09, Finishes"
definition: >
  The finished floor surface installed over a building's structural floor or
  [[concept-slab-on-grade|slab-on-grade]], such as carpet, resilient (vinyl), tile, or
  wood, selected for the space's use and installed over a subfloor that has to be flat,
  clean, and at the right moisture level for that specific flooring type.
whyItMatters: >
  Different flooring materials have very different moisture sensitivity, and
  installing moisture-sensitive flooring over a concrete slab that hasn't cured long
  enough or hasn't been tested for moisture content is one of the most common,
  expensive flooring failures; the flooring can bubble, delaminate, or grow mold
  long after the rest of the project looks finished.
example: >
  Before installing moisture-sensitive vinyl flooring over a new concrete slab, the
  flooring contractor tests the slab's moisture emission rate and confirms it's below
  the flooring manufacturer's maximum threshold before proceeding with installation.
careerAngle: >
  A specialty flooring crew installs the finished floor, but a
  [[career-quality-control-manager|Quality Control Manager]] often requires moisture
  testing on a new slab before flooring installation is allowed to proceed, since a
  flooring failure traced back to slab moisture is expensive to remediate after the
  fact. A [[career-facilities-manager|Facilities Manager]] deals with the long-term
  maintenance and eventual replacement of flooring long after the original
  installation.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A flooring contractor installs vinyl flooring over a concrete slab that was poured
  only a few weeks earlier, skipping the moisture testing step to keep the schedule
  on track. Residual moisture still trapped in the curing slab migrates up through
  the flooring over the following months, causing it to bubble and delaminate across
  large sections that then have to be torn out and replaced.
