title: Domestic Water Piping Materials
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "22, Plumbing"
definition: >
  The range of pipe materials used for a building's domestic water supply,
  copper, PEX (cross-linked polyethylene), and CPVC among the most common,
  each with different cost, installation method, and code-allowed
  applications, and each required to meet lead-free standards for potable
  water use.
whyItMatters: >
  PEX has become the dominant choice for much residential and light
  commercial work because it's faster to install and more resistant to freeze
  damage than rigid copper, but it isn't approved for every application, some
  jurisdictions and some specific uses still require copper or CPVC, so the
  choice isn't purely about cost and installation speed.
example: >
  A plumber installs PEX piping for a multifamily building's domestic water
  distribution, using push-fit or crimp connections that install
  significantly faster than soldering copper joints, while confirming the
  local jurisdiction's plumbing code allows PEX for that specific application.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-plumber|Plumber]] selects and installs the piping material
  appropriate for each application and jurisdiction, since the fastest or
  cheapest material isn't always the one code actually allows for a given use.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A plumbing contractor installs PEX piping throughout a project without
  checking that the local jurisdiction restricts PEX to specific applications
  and requires copper elsewhere in the system. The inspector rejects several
  runs during rough-in inspection, and the contractor has to remove and
  replace the noncompliant sections with copper.
