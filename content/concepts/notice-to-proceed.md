id: concept-notice-to-proceed
title: Notice to Proceed
tier: free
category: Project Process & Lifecycle
definition: >
  A formal, written notice from the owner authorizing the contractor to begin work, the
  document that officially starts the contract clock and the project schedule.
whyItMatters: >
  A contract can be fully signed and still not actually started: the notice to proceed
  is the specific trigger that starts counting toward the contract completion date.
  Starting work before it's issued, or an owner delaying it, both have real schedule and
  payment consequences.
example: >
  A contract calls for substantial completion 300 calendar days after notice to proceed.
  The owner issues that notice on March 1st, which means the completion date isn't tied
  to the contract signing date back in January; it's tied to March 1st.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-project-manager|Project Manager]] treats the notice to proceed date as the
  official start of the schedule they're accountable to. An
  [[career-owners-representative|Owner's Representative]] is often the one who actually
  issues it, timing it around financing, permits, or other conditions the owner needs
  cleared first.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A contractor starts [[concept-mobilization|mobilizing]] and ordering materials as soon
  as the contract is signed, assuming that's the same as being authorized to start. The
  owner's financing falls through and the notice to proceed is delayed by two months,
  time and money the contractor already spent getting ready, with no formal
  authorization yet in place to bill against.
