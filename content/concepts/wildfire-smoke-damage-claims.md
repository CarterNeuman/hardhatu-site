title: Wildfire Smoke Damage Claims
tier: free
category: Insurance & Restoration
definition: >
  Insurance claims for smoke, soot, and ash contamination from a nearby wildfire
  that affected a structure without the fire itself reaching the property, a
  category that has grown and drawn increased insurer scrutiny as wildfire
  frequency has increased.
whyItMatters: >
  These claims can be contentious because smoke damage is sometimes invisible to
  the naked eye but still contaminates HVAC systems, insulation, and porous
  materials, so documenting the damage thoroughly matters more than on an
  obvious structural loss.
example: >
  A restoration company uses air-quality testing and HVAC inspection to document
  smoke particulate contamination throughout a home located miles from an
  active wildfire, supporting a claim for cleaning and remediation despite no
  visible fire damage to the structure.
careerAngle: >
  Restoration contractors working wildfire-adjacent claims should document
  contamination with testing data, not just visual inspection, since insurers
  increasingly push back on wildfire smoke claims that lack objective evidence
  of contamination.
whatGoesWrong: >
  People sometimes assume no visible soot means no claim, when wildfire smoke
  can contaminate a structure's air systems and absorbent materials in ways
  that require testing to detect and document.
