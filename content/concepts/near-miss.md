---
id: concept-near-miss
title: Near Miss
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  An incident that could have caused injury, illness, or property damage but didn't,
  treated in a strong safety program as an early-warning signal worth investigating, not
  a "no harm, no foul" event to shrug off.
whyItMatters: >
  A near miss and an actual injury are often separated only by luck or timing, not by any
  meaningful difference in the underlying hazard. Investigating near misses catches the
  same root causes an injury investigation would, without anyone having to get hurt
  first.
example: >
  A dropped tool from an upper floor lands in a cleared area with no one below at the
  time. A safety manager treats it as a near miss worth investigating rather than a
  non-event, and traces it to a missing toe board that a fall-protection inspection
  should have caught.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] investigates reported near misses the same
  way they would an actual injury, looking for the root cause before it produces a real
  one. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] is often the first to hear about a
  near miss in the field and decides whether it gets reported up or dismissed as nothing.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew doesn't report a near miss because nothing actually happened, treating it as not
  worth the paperwork. The underlying hazard that caused it goes uncorrected, and the
  same condition later causes an actual injury that a reported near miss would likely
  have prevented.
