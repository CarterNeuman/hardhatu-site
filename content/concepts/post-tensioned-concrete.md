---
id: concept-post-tensioned-concrete
title: Post-Tensioned Concrete
tier: free
category: Materials & Systems
csiDivision: "03, Concrete"
definition: >
  A concrete construction method where steel tendons are threaded through ducts
  cast into a slab or beam and then stressed, tensioned, and anchored after the
  concrete cures, putting the concrete permanently into compression, distinct from
  conventional [[concept-rebar|reinforced concrete]], which simply resists tension
  passively as the structure is loaded.
whyItMatters: >
  Because the tendons are stressed to specific, engineered forces after the pour,
  the stressing sequence and timing are safety-critical: concrete has to reach a
  minimum strength before stressing begins, and an under-strength pour stressed
  too early risks a tendon anchor pulling through the concrete.
example: >
  A parking structure's post-tensioned slab is stressed by a specialty PT
  subcontractor only after concrete cylinder breaks confirm the slab reached its
  required minimum compressive strength, following a stressing sequence the
  structural engineer specified.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] designs the tendon layout
  and required stressing sequence. A [[career-concrete-worker|Concrete Worker]] or
  specialty PT crew places and stresses the tendons.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A PT crew begins stressing tendons ahead of schedule to keep the job moving,
  before the concrete's cylinder breaks confirm it reached the required strength.
  An anchor pulls partially through the still-undercured concrete, requiring the
  structural engineer to evaluate and repair the slab before it can be safely
  loaded.
