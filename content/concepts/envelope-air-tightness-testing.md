title: Envelope Air-Tightness Testing (Blower Door Test)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  A diagnostic test that depressurizes a building to 50 Pascals using a
  calibrated fan     sealed into a doorway, measuring the resulting air
  leakage rate in air changes per hour     (ACH50) to verify the building
  envelope meets code-required air-tightness, required under     the 2021 IECC
  at 3 ACH50 for most climate zones or 5 ACH50 in warmer zones 0 through 2.
whyItMatters: >
  A building can have excellent insulation and still perform poorly if the
  envelope itself     leaks air at every penetration and seam; the blower door
  test is what actually measures     whether the air barrier was executed
  correctly in the field, rather than just assumed     correct because the
  insulation was installed per the drawings.
example: >
  A new home in a cold climate zone fails its blower door test at 4.1 ACH50,
  above the     required 3.0 ACH50 limit, sending the crew back to seal gaps
  around electrical penetrations     and the attic hatch before a passing
  retest clears the home for its final energy inspection.
careerAngle: >
  An [[career-hvac-technician|HVAC Technician]] or building inspector performs
  the blower     door test as part of the final energy code inspection, a
  required step most jurisdictions     won't issue a certificate of occupancy
  without.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A crew treats insulation installation as the end of their air-sealing
  responsibility,     when gaps around electrical boxes, top plates, and
  mechanical penetrations are common     failure points a blower door test
  catches that a purely visual insulation inspection would     miss entirely.
