---
id: concept-hierarchy-of-controls
title: Hierarchy of Controls
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  OSHA's ranked order for addressing a workplace hazard, from most effective
  to least: eliminate the hazard entirely, replace it with something safer,
  engineer the risk out with equipment or design, use administrative controls
  like training and procedures, and only then rely on personal protective
  equipment as the last layer of defense.
whyItMatters: >
  PPE sits deliberately last in this order, not because it doesn't matter, but
  because it's the least reliable control: it only works if it's worn
  correctly every time, while eliminating or engineering out a hazard protects
  a worker even if they make a mistake. Understanding this order explains why
  safety programs push so hard on the controls that come before PPE.
example: >
  Rather than relying solely on respirators to protect workers from concrete
  dust, a crew first tries to control the hazard higher up the hierarchy by
  using a vacuum-attached saw that captures dust at the source, an engineering
  control, and only relies on the respirator as a backup layer.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-safety-manager|Safety Manager]] applies the hierarchy of controls
  when designing a safety program, prioritizing fixes that remove a hazard
  over fixes that just protect the worker from it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A jobsite handles a persistent hazard, like excessive noise from a piece of
  equipment, entirely through hearing protection rather than considering
  whether the equipment itself could be replaced or isolated. Workers
  inconsistently wear the protection correctly, and the underlying hazard
  never actually goes away.
