id: concept-drone-mapping
title: Drone/UAV Mapping (Photogrammetry)
tier: free
category: Technology, Data & Sustainability
definition: >
  Using a drone to capture overlapping aerial photographs, then stitching them together
  with photogrammetry software into an accurate map, 3D model, or
  [[concept-reality-capture|point cloud]] of a site, commonly used for topographic data,
  earthwork volume calculations, and ongoing progress documentation.
whyItMatters: >
  Measuring a large site's topography or tracking earthwork progress by hand is slow and
  requires someone physically on the ground at every point measured. Drone mapping
  captures the entire site at once from the air, turning a task that once took a survey
  crew days into a flight that takes hours.
example: >
  A drone pilot flies weekly missions over an active earthwork site, and the resulting
  photogrammetry model lets the project team calculate exactly how many cubic yards of
  material have been moved that week without a ground crew measuring stockpiles by hand.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-drone-uav-specialist|Drone/UAV Specialist]] plans and flies the missions and
  processes the captured imagery into usable maps and volume calculations. A
  [[career-surveyor|Surveyor]] often verifies drone-captured data against ground control
  points to confirm its survey-grade accuracy before it's used for anything requiring
  precise measurement.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A project relies on drone-captured earthwork volumes without establishing ground
  control points to calibrate the data's accuracy. The resulting volume calculations are
  off by an amount that only becomes apparent when they're compared against a
  traditional survey later, after decisions were already made based on the uncorrected
  numbers.
