---
id: concept-seismic-design-category
title: Seismic Design Category
tier: free
category: Safety & Regulatory Compliance
definition: >
  A classification, A through F, assigned to a building under the structural code
  based on its site's seismic hazard, the soil conditions at that specific site,
  and the building's occupancy or risk category, determining how much lateral
  seismic force the structural design actually has to resist.
whyItMatters: >
  The category depends on the site's actual soil conditions, not just its
  geographic location, so two buildings a few blocks apart can land in different
  seismic design categories if their soil reports come back differently, and
  skipping a real geotechnical investigation means designing to a guess instead of
  the site's real hazard.
example: >
  Two similarly-sized buildings in the same city end up with different seismic
  design categories because one sits on soft, unstable soil that amplifies ground
  shaking, and the other sits on solid bedrock that doesn't, a difference the
  geotechnical report reveals, not the address alone.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-structural-engineer|Structural Engineer]] assigns the seismic design
  category and designs the building's lateral force-resisting system to match it.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A structural engineer designs a building to a seismic design category based on
  an assumed, rather than an actually investigated, site soil class. An outside
  plan reviewer catches the mismatch against the real geotechnical report, forcing
  a redesign of the lateral system before the permit can be issued.
