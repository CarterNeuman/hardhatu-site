id: concept-union-vs-open-shop
title: Union vs. Open Shop (Merit Shop)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  Two different labor models in construction. A union shop staffs its workforce through
  union hiring halls under a collective bargaining agreement that sets wages, benefits,
  and work rules; an open shop (also called a merit shop) hires workers directly without
  union affiliation, setting its own wages and benefits independently. Many markets and
  even individual companies operate under a mix of both depending on the trade and the
  project.
whyItMatters: >
  The choice (or the market a contractor operates in) affects labor cost structure,
  workforce availability, and even which projects a contractor can bid competitively on:
  some large public or unionized markets effectively require union labor to be
  competitive, while other regions and trades are predominantly open shop, and a
  company built around one model can struggle to compete in a market dominated by the
  other.
example: >
  A general contractor bidding work in a heavily unionized metropolitan market staffs
  the project through union hiring halls under a project labor agreement, while the same
  company's projects in a different, predominantly open-shop region hire workers
  directly without any union affiliation.
careerAngle: >
  A [[career-hr-manager-construction|HR Manager]] manages fundamentally different labor
  relations depending on which model a project operates under: union grievance
  procedures and hiring hall dispatch rules on one side, direct hiring and retention
  strategy on the other. A [[career-superintendent|Superintendent]] running a union
  project has to work within collectively bargained work rules (jurisdiction between
  trades, break requirements) that don't apply the same way on an open shop job.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A company accustomed to open shop hiring practices takes on a project in a market
  that's effectively all-union and staffs it the way they normally would, without
  accounting for union jurisdiction rules over which trade performs which task. Disputes
  over work assignments between trades slow the project down and create friction the
  company didn't anticipate, since their usual open shop practices don't map onto a
  union jobsite's rules.
