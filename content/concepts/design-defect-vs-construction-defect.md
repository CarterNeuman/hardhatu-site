title: Design Defect vs. Construction Defect
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  The legal distinction between a problem caused by a flaw in how a building
  was designed, a design defect, and a problem caused by the building not
  being built the way it was correctly designed, a construction defect, a
  split that determines which party is actually liable when something goes
  wrong.
whyItMatters: >
  Whether a problem traces back to the design or the construction changes
  who's responsible: under the [[concept-spearin-doctrine|Spearin Doctrine]],
  a contractor that builds exactly to a flawed design generally isn't liable
  for the design's own flaw, while a contractor that fails to build to a
  correct design is liable for that failure regardless of how good the design
  was.
example: >
  A wall develops water intrusion after construction, and an investigation
  finds the waterproofing detail was built exactly as the architect specified
  it, which points to a design defect in the original drawings rather than a
  construction defect in how the contractor executed them.
careerAngle: >
  A
  [[career-expert-witness-construction-litigation|Construction Litigation Expert Witness]]
  is often brought in specifically to determine, through forensic
  investigation, whether a given failure traces back to design or
  construction, since that determination usually decides who's actually
  liable.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A dispute over a failed building system assumes the contractor is at fault
  simply because the contractor built it, without a proper forensic
  investigation into whether the failure actually traces back to the design
  itself. The real cause, and the real liability, only becomes clear once an
  expert witness investigates the failure in enough technical depth to
  separate the two.
