id: concept-sunset-clause-bid-validity
title: Sunset Clause (Bid Validity Period)
tier: free
category: Contracts & Business
definition: >
  A provision in a bid or proposal stating the period of time, often 30, 60, or
  90 days, during which the submitted price remains valid and open for
  acceptance, after which the bidder is no longer bound to honor it without
  agreeing to an extension.
whyItMatters: >
  Material and labor costs can shift meaningfully over a long bid review period,
  so a sunset clause protects a bidder from being locked into pricing that's
  gone stale by the time an owner finally decides to award, while forcing an
  owner who wants to keep evaluating past that window to formally request an
  extension instead of assuming the price still holds.
example: >
  A subcontractor submits a bid with pricing valid for 60 days. When the general
  contractor doesn't finalize the award until 90 days later, the subcontractor
  points to the sunset clause and requires the GC to accept updated pricing
  reflecting the material cost increases that occurred during the delay.
careerAngle: >
  The [[career-estimator|Estimator]] sets a bid's validity period based on how
  volatile current material pricing is, and the [[career-bid-coordinator|Bid
  Coordinator]] tracks which outstanding bids are approaching their sunset
  date so the company can decide whether to extend or let pricing expire.
whatGoesWrong: >
  A bidder assumes an old quote is still honored indefinitely just because no
  one has said otherwise, without noticing the bid's own sunset clause already
  expired weeks earlier, leaving the bidder free to reprice but also giving up
  any claim to rely on the original number.
