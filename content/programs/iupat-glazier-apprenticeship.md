whatToExpect: >
  Apprentices work full-time, paid, under a journeyman glazier while
  attending related classroom instruction covering glass selection,
  handling, cutting, installation, and the metal framing systems that
  support curtain walls, storefronts, and skylights. Many glazier
  apprenticeships are registered as roughly 8,000-hour programs — about
  four years of on-the-job training alongside classroom instruction —
  though exact length varies by local district council. Apprentices
  finish by earning an Architectural Glass and Metal Technician
  certification. Pay rises on a set schedule and training is free to
  the apprentice. Work spans everything from residential glass and
  mirrors to the glass exteriors of high-rise towers.
