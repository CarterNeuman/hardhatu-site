whatToExpect: >
  Apprentices work full-time, paid, under journeyman ironworkers while
  attending related classroom and shop instruction at a local training
  center. Most Ironworkers Joint Apprenticeship and Training Committee
  programs run four years — commonly around 6,000-8,000 hours of
  on-the-job training plus 576-800 hours of classroom instruction —
  though some locals run three-year tracks. Admission is competitive;
  locals screen applicants and often favor those with some welding
  experience or strong mechanical aptitude. Pay starts below journeyman
  scale and rises on a set schedule as apprentices complete hours, and
  training carries no tuition cost. Program length, screening process,
  and recruitment windows are set locally, so specifics vary by region.
