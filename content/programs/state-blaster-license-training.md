whatToExpect: >
  Because blasting is regulated at the state level, requirements vary.
  Most states require documented blasting experience (often one to two
  years working under a licensed blaster), classroom instruction commonly
  running 24 to 32 hours, and a written exam covering explosives safety,
  storage, and handling. States including Montana, Kentucky, and
  Pennsylvania run their own licensing boards and renewal cycles, typically
  every one to three years with continuing education required. Where a
  state has no program of its own, the federal Office of Surface Mining
  Reclamation and Enforcement (OSMRE) blaster certification is often
  accepted as a reciprocal credential.
