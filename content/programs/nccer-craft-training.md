tagline: Standardized, nationally portable craft credentials in over 70 construction trades, taught through community colleges, career centers, and employer training programs.
whatToExpect: >
  NCCER doesn't train students directly — it publishes a standardized
  curriculum and credentialing system that community colleges, high
  school career centers, and employer training programs use to teach
  and certify craft skills, from the general NCCER Core module
  (safety, tools, construction math, employability skills) through
  trade-specific levels in carpentry, electrical, HVAC, welding, and
  dozens of other crafts. Because credentials are recorded in
  NCCER's national registry, they transfer between employers and
  training sponsors in a way an individual school's own certificate
  may not. Program length, cost, and whether it leads toward a formal
  apprenticeship or associate degree all depend on where you take it —
  a community college program may run one to two years and charge
  standard tuition, while an employer-sponsored training program may
  be free. Check with the specific accredited training sponsor for
  cost and schedule.
